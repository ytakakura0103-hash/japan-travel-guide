Sapporo Snow Festival

Hokkaido

Running for about a week or so every February, the Sapporo Snow Festival brings together large-scale snow sculptures, some built by the Japanese military, alongside international sculpting teams, and ice carvings lit up after dark along Odori Park and in Susukino. A separate, more family-oriented site at Tsudome adds snow slides and hands-on activities aimed at children, and is reachable by a shuttle bus rather than being walkable from downtown.

The festival is one of Hokkaido's single biggest tourism draws, and the crowds reflect that; the main Odori site in particular gets extremely dense in the evenings when the sculptures are illuminated. It's worth deciding in advance whether the goal is photography, in which case the quieter, colder early morning hours before crowds build are worth the trade-off, or the evening atmosphere, in which case accepting the crowds is part of the deal.

Because dates shift slightly year to year and hotel prices in Sapporo spike heavily during the festival, booking accommodation well in advance is genuinely necessary rather than just advisable. Temperatures during the festival regularly sit well below freezing, so serious winter clothing, not just a warm jacket, is worth the investment for anyone planning to spend real time outdoors after dark.
