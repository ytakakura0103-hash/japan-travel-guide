Biei Patchwork Road

Sapporo

The gently rolling hills around Biei are ordinary farmland, growing potatoes, wheat, beets and other crops, but the undulating terrain combined with the different colors and textures of neighboring fields creates a patchwork effect that has made the area a magnet for photographers, particularly from a car or bicycle moving slowly along the back roads. A handful of individual trees along the route, including the so-called Ken and Mary Tree and the Mild Seven Hill trees, have become minor landmarks in their own right thanks to old advertising campaigns.\n\nThe scenery changes dramatically by season: green and gold crop patterns through summer, muted harvest tones in autumn, and a stark, near-monochrome snowscape from December through March that has its own quiet appeal for winter photographers. Because the whole point is the driving or cycling route itself rather than a single fixed site, a rental car or bicycle from Biei town makes the experience considerably better than trying to piece it together on foot.\n\nSummer weekends bring a lot of rental car and tour bus traffic to the most famous individual trees, some of which sit on private farmland and should be photographed from designated pull-offs only, out of respect for the farmers. Visiting on a weekday, or focusing on less-famous stretches of the loop, tends to give a calmer, more solitary version of the same scenery.
