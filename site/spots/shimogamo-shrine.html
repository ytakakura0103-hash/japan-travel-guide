Shimogamo Shrine

Kyoto

Shimogamo Shrine, one of Kyoto's two oldest shrines alongside Kamigamo, sits at the confluence of the Kamo and Takano rivers and is reached by walking through Tadasu no Mori, a forest of old-growth trees, some centuries old, that has survived largely intact within the boundaries of a modern city. The walk through the forest, along a gravel path lined by a small stream, is as much a part of the experience as the shrine buildings themselves and gives a sense of what much of the Kamo river basin may have looked like before Kyoto grew up around it.

The shrine's vermilion halls, rebuilt periodically over the centuries in keeping with Shinto tradition even as the site's history stretches back well over a thousand years, sit at the far end of the forest path, and the whole complex, along with Kamigamo Shrine, holds UNESCO World Heritage status. A walk through the forest and shrine grounds takes around thirty to forty-five minutes, and there's no fee to enter the main precinct.

Shimogamo is involved in the Aoi Matsuri procession each May and hosts a well-known used book fair in the forest each summer, both of which bring larger crowds than usual. Outside those events the shrine sees a fairly steady, moderate flow of visitors and rarely feels crowded, particularly deep in the forest section.

59 Shimogamo Izumigawacho, Sakyo Ward, Kyoto
