Enryakuji

Kyoto

Spread across the summit and slopes of Mount Hiei, straddling the border between Kyoto and neighboring Shiga, Enryakuji has been the headquarters of Tendai Buddhism since its founding by the monk Saicho in 788, and for centuries it functioned as one of the most powerful religious institutions in Japan, home to thousands of monks and, at times, its own armed warrior-monk forces. Much of that early power ended in 1571, when Oda Nobunaga's forces burned the complex to the ground during his campaign to break the temple's political influence; most of what stands today was rebuilt in later centuries, though the site's UNESCO World Heritage status reflects its historical and religious significance rather than the age of any single building. The complex is divided into three separate precincts spread across the mountain, connected by shuttle buses rather than easy walking distance, so seeing more than the main Todo area realistically takes a half day or more. A cable car from Sakamoto on the Shiga side is one of the more scenic ways up, climbing through forest with occasional views over Lake Biwa. The mountain setting keeps temperatures noticeably cooler than central Kyoto, pleasant in summer but genuinely cold in winter, when some paths can be closed. Autumn colors across the forested slopes are a major draw, and weekday visits generally avoid the larger tour groups that arrive on weekends.
