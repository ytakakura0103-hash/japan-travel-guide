Rairaikyo Gorge

Sendai

Rairaikyo is a narrow gorge carved by the Natori River through pale volcanic rock, and a walking trail follows the wall of the canyon roughly twenty meters above the water for about a kilometer, giving a continuous, changing view down into the ravine without any real elevation gain. Smooth, pale rock formations along the riverbed give the gorge a distinct look from most other river valleys in the area.

Because it sits right next to the Akiu Onsen hot spring town, it's an easy add-on for anyone visiting for a soak, a short, scenic walk before or after bathing rather than a full day trip on its own. The trail is unpaved in sections but generally manageable, and walking the full length out and back takes roughly forty to fifty minutes at a relaxed pace.

There's no admission fee and no closing time, which makes it flexible to fit into an Akiu day. It's rarely crowded even on weekends, since most Akiu visitors come for the onsen rather than the gorge, though early morning still guarantees near-total solitude on the trail. The gorge is especially striking with autumn color reflected in the river, typically peaking in early-to-mid November.

Akiu-machi Yumoto, Taihaku-ku, Sendai, Miyagi

About 50 to 55 minutes by bus from Sendai Station on the Akiu Line, getting off near the Nozokibashi or Akiu Sato Center stop.
