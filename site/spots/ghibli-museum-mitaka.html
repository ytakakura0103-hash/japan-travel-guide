Ghibli Museum

Tokyo

The Ghibli Museum in Mitaka was designed with direct input from Hayao Miyazaki himself, and it shows in details that resist a conventional museum layout: there is no marked route through the building, curved stairways and hidden passages encourage wandering, and small surprises — a stained-glass window, a hidden reading nook — reward visitors who explore rather than follow signage. A short original animated film, shown only inside the museum and unavailable anywhere else, screens on a rotating basis in the museum's small theater. Outside, a rooftop garden is watched over by a life-size robot soldier statue from Laputa: Castle in the Sky, one of the most photographed spots in the museum.

Tickets are sold for specific entry times and must be purchased well in advance, since the museum limits daily visitor numbers and tickets for popular dates, especially weekends and school holidays, sell out quickly; this is one of the few Tokyo attractions where advance planning is close to essential rather than optional. Most visits last two to three hours, and because there is no rush to move through in order, an unhurried pace suits the museum's design better than trying to see everything.

The museum is reachable either directly from Kichijoji Station or via a pleasant walk through Inokashira Park, which is worth doing if weather allows, as it extends naturally into a visit to the park itself.

1-1-83 Shimorenjaku, Mitaka, Tokyo

Open in Google Maps

15- to 20-minute walk from Kichijoji Station (JR Chuo Line) through Inokashira Park; alternatively, a 5-minute community bus ride from the South Exit of JR Mitaka Station (JR Chuo Line)
