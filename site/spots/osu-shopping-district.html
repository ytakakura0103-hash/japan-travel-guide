Osu Shopping District

Nagoya

Osu is often described as Nagoya's answer to a mash-up of Akihabara and Ameyoko, and that captures the spirit fairly well: narrow covered arcades packed with used-electronics stalls, retro game shops, cosplay costume stores, imported grocery shops, and racks of vintage American clothing, all within a few minutes' walk of a centuries-old temple. It has none of the polish of a shopping mall, and that's exactly the appeal.

Browsing costs nothing, and street snacks like fried skewers, taiyaki, and steamed buns can turn a wander into an impromptu meal for a modest amount. A couple of hours is enough to cover the main arcades, though it is easy to lose track of time ducking into side alleys.

Weekends bring a festive but crowded atmosphere, with occasional street performances near the temple entrance; a weekday visit lets you actually browse the smaller shops without queuing. The district layers eras oddly well — a shop selling rare vinyl records might sit beside a Nepalese curry counter or a shrine selling paper charms, and that unpredictability is part of what makes it worth returning to.

Osu, Naka-ku, Nagoya, Aichi
