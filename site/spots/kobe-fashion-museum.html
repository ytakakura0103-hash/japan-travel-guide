Kobe Fashion Museum

Kobe

Kobe has long branded itself as Japan's fashion capital, thanks to its history as an early hub for Western-style tailoring and imported clothing during the port's opening in the late 19th century, and this museum is the physical monument to that identity. Its distinctive saucer-shaped building on Rokko Island houses rotating exhibitions on historical Western costume, textile techniques and contemporary design, drawn from both its own collection and touring shows.

Because Rokko Island sits somewhat apart from Kobe's main sightseeing loop, largely residential and commercial rather than touristy, the museum sees a fraction of the visitors of Kobe's downtown attractions, which makes for a relaxed, unhurried visit even during exhibition changes. The building's interior spaces, with sweeping curved walls following the exterior shape, are worth noting on their own.

Exhibition themes and admission fees change with each rotating show, so it's worth checking what's currently on display before making the trip out, since the permanent collection isn't always on view. The island itself, a planned residential and port development from the 1970s, is an interesting, slightly surreal contrast to Kobe's older neighborhoods if you have time to wander after the museum.

Rokko Island, Higashinada Ward, Kobe

Open in Google Maps

From Sannomiya, take the Port Liner automated train to Island Center Station on Rokko Island (about 20 minutes); the museum is a short walk from the station.
