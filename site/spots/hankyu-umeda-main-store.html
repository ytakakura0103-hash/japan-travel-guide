Hankyu Umeda Main Store

Osaka

Hankyu Umeda's flagship store has anchored this corner of Umeda for roughly a century, and while the upper floors carry the fashion and homeware you'd expect from a major department store, most visitors come for the basement food hall, known locally as a depachika. Dozens of counters line the space, selling everything from delicate wagashi sweets and French-style pastries to elaborate bento boxes, prepared side dishes, and regional specialties from across Japan, many of them beautifully packaged and suited to bringing back to a hotel room for dinner.

The sheer density of counters can be overwhelming on a first visit, and it's worth wandering a full loop before committing to a purchase, since prices and specialties vary a lot between stalls. Some counters offer small samples, which is a good way to narrow down choices without overcommitting. Early evening, roughly the hour or two before closing, is when some prepared-food counters mark down unsold items, though popular ones sell out well before then.

Weekday late mornings tend to be quieter than weekend afternoons, when the basement floor gets genuinely crowded with shoppers. Beyond the food hall, the store connects directly into Osaka-Umeda Station, making it an easy stop to fold into a day of exploring the Umeda area rather than a dedicated destination in itself. The upper floors are also worth a look for their department-store architecture and seasonal window displays, particularly around New Year and midsummer gift-giving seasons.

8-7 Kakudacho, Kita Ward, Osaka
