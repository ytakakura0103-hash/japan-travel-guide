Starbucks Dazaifu Omotesando

Fukuoka

This is a fully functioning Starbucks, but the building itself is the real attraction: architect Kengo Kuma designed the interior around a diagonal lattice of roughly 2,000 cedar sticks, assembled using a traditional joinery technique called kigumi that locks the wood together without nails. The effect is a tunnel-like space that Kuma has described as making customers feel like they're drinking coffee inside a forest.

It sits directly on Dazaifu's main pedestrian approach to the shrine, wedged between the more traditional shopfronts selling umegae-mochi and souvenirs, which makes the contrast between old and new even more striking. The menu itself is standard Starbucks fare, so the appeal here is purely architectural rather than culinary.

Because it's a working cafe on a very high-traffic pilgrimage street, expect a line for both ordering and seating, especially on weekends and during plum blossom season. A quick photo of the exterior lattice is possible even without buying anything, useful if you're short on time or the queue looks long.

3-2-43 Saifu, Dazaifu, Fukuoka
