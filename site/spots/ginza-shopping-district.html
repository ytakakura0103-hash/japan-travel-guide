Ginza

Tokyo

Ginza has been Tokyo's premier shopping district since the late 19th century, and it still carries that reputation today, with flagship department stores, architect-designed boutiques for international luxury brands, and long-established Japanese retailers lining its main avenue and side streets. Beyond shopping, the district is worth a look simply for its architecture: many global fashion houses have commissioned striking standalone buildings here, making a walk down the main street as much an architecture tour as a shopping trip. The clock tower atop the Wako building, at the district's central intersection, is one of Ginza's most photographed landmarks.

On Saturday and Sunday afternoons, the main avenue, Chuo-dori, closes entirely to vehicle traffic and becomes a pedestrian zone, with cafe tables set up in the street and a noticeably more relaxed atmosphere than the weekday rush. This is generally the most pleasant time to visit if the goal is strolling rather than serious shopping, since weekday crowds tend to be office workers passing through rather than browsers.

Ginza isn't only about high-end brands; side streets and basement food halls (depachika) inside the department stores offer more affordable options, from beautifully packaged sweets to prepared food stalls worth a visit on their own. A couple of hours covers the main avenue comfortably, though gallery-hoppers and dedicated shoppers can easily spend a full day. The district also has a concentration of small art galleries tucked into upper floors of ordinary-looking buildings, open to browse for free.
