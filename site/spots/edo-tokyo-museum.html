Edo-Tokyo Museum

Tokyo

The Edo-Tokyo Museum is built around a bold architectural centerpiece: a life-size replica section of the old Nihonbashi bridge, which visitors walk across to enter a series of galleries tracing the city's transformation from the Edo period through the Meiji era, wartime destruction, and postwar reconstruction. Detailed scale models recreate street scenes, townhouses, and landmarks as they looked at different points in that history, alongside artifacts, woodblock prints, and reconstructed interiors that give a tangible sense of daily life in each era.

The museum closed for a major renovation and reopened on March 31, 2026 after roughly four years of work, with architect Kiyonori Kikutake's original building, including its distinctive elevated piloti, preserved and updated. It now operates on a normal daily schedule; when visiting, a thorough walk through the permanent exhibits typically takes a couple of hours, given how much ground the collection covers.

Because the museum sits right next to the Ryogoku sumo district and the Sumida Hokusai Museum, it fits naturally into a broader day of sightseeing in that part of Sumida ward. English-language panels are generally available for major exhibits. There's no particular seasonal element to a visit here, since it's an entirely indoor museum, making it a reasonable option regardless of weather.
