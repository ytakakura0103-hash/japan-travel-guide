Iwadeyama Castle Town

Sendai

After serving under Toyotomi Hideyoshi, Date Masamune was granted this domain in 1591, rebuilt its castle and renamed it Iwadeyama, and used the following thirteen years to develop it into a significant political and economic base before eventually founding Sendai itself. When he moved on, he left one of his sons to govern Iwadeyama in his place, and much of the old castle town layout and atmosphere has survived in a way that's largely disappeared from busier Sendai.

The castle ruins are free to explore, and a small local museum nearby covers the town's history in more depth. A relaxed visit, including a walk through the old samurai-era streets, takes two to three hours.

Despite its direct connection to Date Masamune's rise to power, Iwadeyama rarely appears on tourist itineraries focused on Sendai and Matsushima, leaving its historic streets and castle hill genuinely quiet. Cherry blossoms on the castle hill in spring are a modest but pleasant seasonal draw.
