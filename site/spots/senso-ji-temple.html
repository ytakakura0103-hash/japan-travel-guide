Senso-ji Temple

Tokyo

Senso-ji is Tokyo's oldest Buddhist temple, its origins traced to the 7th century, and it remains the spiritual anchor of the old Asakusa neighborhood. Most visitors start at the Kaminarimon ("Thunder Gate"), instantly recognizable for its huge red lantern and flanking guardian statues, then follow the pedestrian approach known as Nakamise-dori, a few hundred meters of stalls selling rice crackers, yukata, and other souvenirs on the way to the temple itself. Beyond the second gate, Hozomon, stands the main hall and a five-story pagoda, with a large incense burner where visitors waft smoke over themselves for good health.

A full loop through the grounds, including a stop to draw an omikuji fortune slip, takes roughly an hour, longer if the approach street is busy, which it usually is. Nakamise-dori and the temple courtyard get very crowded from mid-morning through the afternoon, especially on weekends; arriving soon after the gates open in the early morning is the most reliable way to see the buildings without a wall of people in every photo, and many of the food stalls are also open earlier than shoppers expect.

The area changes with the seasons: cherry blossoms bloom nearby along the Sumida River in spring, and the Sanja Matsuri festival in May fills the streets around the temple with portable shrines and crowds for three days. Senso-ji is free to enter, and combining it with a walk along the river or a rickshaw ride through the backstreets rounds out a half-day visit to the neighborhood.
