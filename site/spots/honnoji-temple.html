Local Pick

Honnoji Temple

Kyoto

Honnoji is best known not for its buildings, which are relatively modest and modern in appearance, but for an event that took place on a different site nearby in 1582: the betrayal of warlord Oda Nobunaga by one of his own generals, Akechi Mitsuhide, whose surprise attack forced Nobunaga to take his own life inside the temple rather than be captured, in what remains one of the most consequential single moments in Japanese history, cutting short Nobunaga's campaign to unify the country under his rule. The original temple complex, located elsewhere in the city at the time, burned down during the attack, and the version standing today was rebuilt at its current location in the following century, meaning visitors are seeing a later reconstruction rather than the actual site of Nobunaga's death, a detail worth knowing to set expectations correctly. The grounds include a small museum and a memorial to Nobunaga, along with information connecting the temple to the broader Sengoku-period history that culminated in the unification of Japan under his successors. A visit typically takes well under an hour, longer for visitors with a strong interest in the historical detail on display. The temple sits in the middle of downtown Kyoto, making it an easy stop to combine with shopping or dining in the Kawaramachi and Teramachi areas nearby. It's generally quiet year-round, without the seasonal crowd surges that affect the city's larger temples and gardens.

Teramachi-dori Oike, Nakagyo Ward, Kyoto
