Suntory Museum of Art

Tokyo

The Suntory Museum of Art relocated to Tokyo Midtown some years ago, and its exhibitions are built around the founding idea of "art for daily life" — a deliberate focus on lacquerware, ceramics, textiles, glasswork, and other traditionally functional crafts rather than the Western-style fine art paintings and sculpture found at many other Tokyo museums. Exhibitions rotate regularly, often drawing on the museum's own long-held collection alongside loaned pieces, and shows are frequently organized around a specific craft technique, historical period, or region rather than a single artist.

The museum's interior, designed with input from architect Kengo Kuma, uses wood latticework and understated materials meant to evoke a traditional Japanese aesthetic, a deliberate contrast to the sleeker glass-and-steel architecture of Tokyo Midtown surrounding it. A visit typically takes about an hour, depending on the size of the current exhibition, and admission is charged at the gate.

Because it focuses on craft rather than blockbuster fine-art exhibitions, the museum tends to draw smaller, more specialized crowds than Tokyo's larger art institutions, making it a comparatively calm visit even on weekends. It connects directly to Roppongi Station via Tokyo Midtown, and pairs naturally with a visit to 21_21 Design Sight or a walk through the Midtown Garden, both within the same complex.
