Shiroishi Castle

Sendai

Shiroishi Castle served for centuries as the stronghold of the Katakura family, top-ranking retainers of the Date clan entrusted with guarding Sendai's southern border, an unusually large degree of autonomy for a vassal family under Japan's strict castle regulations of the era. The original wooden keep was dismantled in the Meiji period, but a 1995 reconstruction rebuilt it using traditional joinery and materials rather than the reinforced concrete common at many 'reconstructed' Japanese castles, giving it a more authentic look, feel, and smell than most.

The compact three-story keep is climbable, with displays inside on the Katakura family and the castle's construction techniques, and the surrounding park includes a small samurai residence museum that adds context on daily life for the retainer class rather than just the lords themselves.

Admission is modest, and a full visit including the keep and museum takes about an hour to ninety minutes. Shiroishi sees far fewer tourists than Sendai proper, so the castle rarely feels crowded at any time. Cherry blossoms fill the castle park in mid-April, drawing local hanami crowds that are still much thinner than equivalent scenes in bigger cities.
