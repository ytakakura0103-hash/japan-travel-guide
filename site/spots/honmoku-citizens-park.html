Honmoku Citizens Park

Yokohama

For decades after WWII, much of the Honmoku coastline was occupied by US military housing and facilities, and it was only returned to Japanese control in stages starting in the 1980s, which is why this park feels relatively new compared to the older, Meiji-era green spaces elsewhere in the city. The land's history as a returned base area is part of what makes it worth knowing before a visit, a tangible marker of postwar Yokohama's slow reintegration of its own coastline.

The park itself is straightforward and pleasant rather than dramatic: open lawns, a rose garden section that blooms alongside the city's other spring and autumn rose displays, and walking paths with occasional glimpses toward the bay. It functions primarily as a neighborhood recreation space for nearby Honmoku and Negishi residents rather than a curated tourist attraction.

Because it sits well east of the main Minato Mirai and Chinatown sightseeing zone, and public transit access takes a bit more effort, it is rarely visited by travelers, which keeps its lawns and rose beds comfortably uncrowded even on pleasant weekends. It pairs naturally with a visit to the Honmoku area's coastline for a quieter, more residential side of Yokohama.

59 Honmoku-Minoya, Naka-ku, Yokohama

Open in Google Maps

About 15-20 minutes by bus from JR Negishi Station or JR Yamate Station.
