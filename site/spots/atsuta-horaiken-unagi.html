Atsuta Horaiken

Nagoya

Founded in the late nineteenth century near Atsuta Shrine, this restaurant is closely associated with the invention of hitsumabushi, a way of serving grilled eel over rice that's meant to be eaten in stages: first plain, then mixed with condiments like scallion and wasabi, and finally doused in a savory broth as a kind of eel ochazuke. The ritual of eating it in three distinct ways is as much a part of the appeal as the eel itself.

Expect prices on the higher side, reflecting the cost of quality eel, and be prepared for a wait, since the restaurant doesn't rush turnover and doesn't always take reservations for smaller parties; a meal including the wait can easily take over an hour.

Lunchtime, especially around visits to Atsuta Shrine next door, draws the longest queues; arriving right at opening or aiming for a later lunch slot after the initial rush cuts the wait meaningfully. Given the reputation, treating this as a planned meal rather than a spontaneous stop tends to work out better.

503 Kanbe-cho, Atsuta-ku, Nagoya, Aichi 456-0043
