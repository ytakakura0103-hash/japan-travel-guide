Duskin Museum

Osaka

Run by the company behind Mister Donut's operations in Japan, this small museum in Ikeda tells the story of the Duskin brand's founding and its various business lines through modest exhibits before arriving at its main attraction: a hands-on doughnut-making workshop where visitors fry their own dough and decorate it with toppings to eat or take home. The activity is simple enough for children to take part in comfortably alongside adults, and staff typically guide participants through each step, making it accessible even for visitors without Japanese.

A full visit including the workshop takes around an hour to ninety minutes, and unlike many hands-on museums in Japan, admission and the doughnut-making activity are both free, making it an easy, low-commitment addition to an Ikeda itinerary rather than requiring a dedicated half-day. Because it's free and popular with families, workshop slots can fill on weekends and school holidays, so arriving earlier in the day or checking availability on arrival is worth doing to avoid disappointment.

The museum is compact, so it's best paired with another stop in Ikeda rather than treated as a full outing on its own; the Cup Noodles Museum a short walk away covers similar hands-on territory with instant ramen instead of doughnuts, and the two work well combined into a single visit to the area. Reaching the museum takes about ten minutes on foot from Ikeda Station.
