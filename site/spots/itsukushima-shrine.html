Itsukushima Shrine

Hiroshima

Itsukushima Shrine's current layout dates largely to the 12th century, when powerful warlord Taira no Kiyomori rebuilt and expanded an earlier shrine on the site into the elaborate complex of pier-supported halls, corridors, and stages seen today. Because the entire island of Miyajima was traditionally considered sacred, the shrine's founders built out over the water rather than on the land itself, and the structure was engineered to allow tides to flow beneath the buildings without damaging them.

At high tide, the shrine's connected halls and the offshore torii gate appear to float on the sea, while at low tide visitors can walk out across the exposed sand to the base of the gate itself, giving the site two quite different visual characters depending on when it is visited. The complex includes a noh stage still used for performances, a treasure hall, and long connecting corridors, and it was jointly registered as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1996 along with its setting on Miyajima.

Because tide timing significantly changes the experience and photos, it is worth checking tide tables in advance; many visitors try to see the shrine at both high and low tide if their schedule allows, often by staying overnight on the island.
