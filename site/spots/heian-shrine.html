Heian Shrine

Kyoto

Heian Shrine was built in 1895 to mark the 1,100th anniversary of Kyoto's founding as the imperial capital, and its main buildings are a scaled-down reconstruction of the original 8th-century Imperial Palace, painted in vivid vermilion and green rather than the weathered wood tones typical of older Kyoto temples. The shrine fronts onto one of Japan's largest torii gates, set well back from the main complex on the approach avenue, and is dedicated to the first and last emperors to reign from Kyoto.

Behind the main halls lies a large strolling garden divided into several sections, added after the shrine's construction and considerably more atmospheric than the formal courtyard out front; it includes a covered bridge, a pond used for boating in the Meiji era, and a mix of plantings that means something is usually in bloom depending on the season, from cherry blossoms in spring to irises in early summer. Entering the shrine's main courtyard is free, but the garden requires a separate modest admission fee.

The courtyard and torii can be seen in a few minutes, while the garden alone takes closer to thirty to forty-five minutes to walk properly. The shrine sits in the Okazaki cultural district alongside several museums, and crowds are generally lighter here than at Kyoto's older, more famous temples, though cherry blossom season still draws a steady flow of visitors to the garden.

Nishitennocho, Okazaki, Sakyo Ward, Kyoto
