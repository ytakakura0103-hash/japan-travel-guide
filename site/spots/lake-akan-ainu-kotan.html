Lake Akan Ainu Kotan

Sapporo

Ainu Kotan on the shore of Lake Akan is home to one of the larger remaining concentrations of Ainu residents in Hokkaido, and unlike a museum reconstruction, it's a living community where families run their own wood-carving and craft shops along the main street. The wood-carved owl figures and other items sold here are made by local artisans rather than mass-imported souvenirs, though quality and pricing vary shop to shop, so it's worth browsing a few before buying.

A theater at the edge of the village hosts nightly performances of traditional Ainu dance and storytelling, some incorporating digital projection alongside live performers in newer productions, offering a rare accessible entry point into a culture that receives relatively little mainstream coverage even within Japan. Lake Akan itself, known for its rare spherical marimo algae, sits just beyond the village.

Because reaching eastern Hokkaido requires a real commitment, either a long train-and-bus combination or a flight into Kushiro, the village sees far fewer visitors than its cultural significance might suggest, and even the evening performances rarely sell out.

Winter brings ice-fishing and frozen-lake activities alongside the standard village visit, while summer keeps the lake accessible for boat tours to see the marimo up close.

Akancho Ainu Kotan, Kushiro, Hokkaido 085-0467

About 5 hours from Sapporo: fly into Kushiro Airport (about 55 minutes) then a bus to Lake Akan (about 75 minutes), or the full overland route via JR to Kushiro and onward bus, which takes considerably longer.
