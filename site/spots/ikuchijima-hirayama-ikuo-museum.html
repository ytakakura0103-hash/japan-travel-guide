Hirayama Ikuo Museum of Art

Hiroshima

Hirayama Ikuo was born on Ikuchijima and was exposed to radiation as a student mobilized for war work in Hiroshima in 1945; the experience shaped a decades-long career built around themes of peace, Buddhist pilgrimage routes, and the Silk Road, rendered in the traditional Japanese painting style known as Nihonga. This museum near his childhood home shows a rotating selection of his large-format landscape and pilgrimage paintings alongside sketches and personal items.\n\nThe building itself sits close to the water with views back toward the mountains of Ikuchijima, and the museum grounds include a reconstructed section of his family's old house, giving the visit a personal, biographical feel rather than a purely academic one.\n\nIt pairs naturally with a stop at nearby Kosanji Temple, and offers a quieter, more contemplative counterpoint to that site's dense ornamentation — useful if you want one art-focused stop and one architecture-focused stop on the same island.

200-2 Setodacho Sawa, Onomichi, Hiroshima

Open in Google Maps

About a 10-minute walk from Setoda Port on Ikuchijima; roughly 40–50 minutes by car from central Onomichi via the Shimanami Kaido expressway.
