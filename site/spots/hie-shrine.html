Local Pick

Hie Shrine

Tokyo

Hie Shrine sits on a small hill in the middle of Akasaka's office towers, and reaching its main hall means climbing (or riding an escalator alongside) a striking tunnel of stacked vermilion torii gates, visually similar to Kyoto's much larger Fushimi Inari Shrine but on a far more compact scale — a detail that makes it a popular alternative for visitors who want a similar photo without the trip to Kyoto. The shrine is dedicated to a deity associated with protection and business prosperity, and monkey statues rather than the more common fox or dog guardians flank parts of the grounds, tied to old beliefs about monkeys as messengers of the mountain god enshrined here.

A visit to the shrine, including a walk through the torii tunnel and a look at the main hall, takes about twenty to thirty minutes. There is no admission fee. Because the shrine sits within a dense business district, weekday visits during office hours can mean sharing the grounds with workers stopping by on breaks, while weekends are quieter overall in terms of foot traffic in the surrounding streets, even if the shrine itself sees a mix of visitors either way.

Hie Shrine hosts the Sanno Matsuri, one of Tokyo's major traditional festivals, in even-numbered years, drawing much larger crowds and a formal procession through the surrounding neighborhood during that period.

2-10-5 Nagatacho, Chiyoda City, Tokyo
