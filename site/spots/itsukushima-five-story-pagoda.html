Five-Story Pagoda, Miyajima

Hiroshima

The five-story pagoda standing above Itsukushima Shrine dates to 1407 and blends Japanese and Chinese-influenced architectural styles typical of the Muromachi period, with vermillion coloring that echoes the shrine complex below despite being administratively separate from it. It stands roughly 27 meters tall on a small rise near Toyokuni Shrine, and while its interior is not generally open to the public, the exterior and its surrounding grounds are freely accessible.

What makes the pagoda a particularly popular photo stop is its elevated position relative to the shrine and the Great Torii, allowing views that combine the pagoda's own tiered roofline with the shrine complex, the gate, and the sea beyond in a single frame, especially from certain angles along the path leading up to it. This makes it one of the more reliably photogenic spots on the island beyond the shrine and gate themselves.

The pagoda sits immediately next to Senjokaku, the large open pavilion also known as the Hall of a Thousand Mats, and the two are typically visited together as part of the same short uphill walk from the shrine's main approach.

Miyajima-cho, Hatsukaichi, Hiroshima Prefecture
