National Art Center, Tokyo

Tokyo

The National Art Center, Tokyo is unusual among major museums in that it holds no permanent collection of its own; instead, its large galleries are entirely given over to a rotating calendar of temporary exhibitions, ranging from major international loan shows to large-scale exhibitions organized by Japan's various art associations, meaning what's on view changes completely several times a year. This makes it worth checking the current exhibition schedule before visiting, since the experience depends entirely on what happens to be showing.

The building itself, designed by architect Kisho Kurokawa, is a destination in its own right, with a rippling, wave-like glass facade enclosing a soaring atrium anchored by two large inverted cone structures that house a restaurant and cafe. Even visitors not planning to see a specific exhibition sometimes stop in just to see the architecture and have a coffee in the atrium.

Because exhibition tickets are sold and priced individually rather than as a single general admission, cost and time needed vary considerably depending on what's showing; major touring exhibitions can draw significant crowds and are best visited on a weekday or shortly after opening. The museum sits within Tokyo's informal "Art Triangle" alongside the Mori Art Museum and Suntory Museum of Art, making it easy to combine with one or both in a single day focused on contemporary and fine art.
