Shibuya Scramble Crossing

Tokyo

Shibuya Scramble Crossing is the pedestrian intersection outside Shibuya Station's Hachiko Exit where traffic stops in all directions at once and pedestrians surge across from every angle, an image that has become shorthand for Tokyo itself in films and photographs. It is genuinely one of the busiest crossings in the world, and simply standing at street level and crossing with the crowd a few times is an experience in itself, especially after dark when the surrounding buildings are covered in video billboards.

For a wider view, the Shibuya Sky observatory near the station offers an open-air rooftop view down over the crossing and the whole city skyline, for a separate admission fee. A cheaper alternative is one of several cafes and building lobbies with windows directly overlooking the intersection, which let you watch the crossing from above without paying for an observation deck; the Starbucks inside the Tsutaya building is the best known of these, though it's often crowded and doesn't take reservations.

The crossing itself is busy at almost all hours, but the density peaks during evening rush hour and on weekend nights, when the energy is highest but photos are hardest to take cleanly. Weekday mornings and early afternoons are comparatively calmer if the goal is simply to see it without the thickest crowds. The nearby Hachiko statue, a popular meeting point, is worth a quick look on the way.
