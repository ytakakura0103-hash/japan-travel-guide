Aoba Shrine

Sendai

Former retainers of the Date clan petitioned to build this shrine in 1873, deifying Date Masamune under the name Takefuruhiko-no-mikoto in line with a Meiji-era trend of enshrining domain founders as protective kami. The current main hall dates from a 1927 rebuild, and the grounds sit close to the old Aoba Castle site, connected by paths that wind through the wooded hillside where Sendai's founding lord once ruled.

There is no admission fee, and most visitors spend twenty to thirty minutes exploring the shrine buildings and the smaller sub-shrines on the grounds. It pairs naturally with a castle-ruins visit since it sits only a short walk away, though the walk involves some uphill sections.

Almost everyone who comes to see Date Masamune's legacy heads straight for the castle observation deck and its famous statue, leaving this shrine largely to locals doing New Year's hatsumode visits or quiet weekday prayers. Autumn brings good foliage color along the approach path, and the shrine is peaceful enough for a genuine breather between busier stops.

7-1 Aobamachi, Aoba-ku, Sendai, Miyagi (Kitayama hill area, not the Kawauchi/castle-ruins area)

Open in Google Maps

About 7 minutes on foot from Kitasendai Station on the JR Senzan Line or the Sendai Subway Namboku Line. The shrine is not adjacent to the Aoba Castle ruins, which are in a separate part of Aoba-ku near Kawauchi.
