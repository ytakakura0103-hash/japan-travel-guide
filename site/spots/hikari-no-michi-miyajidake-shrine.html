Hikari no Michi (Miyajidake Shrine)

Fukuoka

Miyajidake Shrine sits atop a rise with a long approach road running straight down to the sea, and twice a year, in February and October, the setting sun aligns directly with that road, framed by the shrine's massive torii gates, in a phenomenon known as Hikari no Michi, the road of light. For the roughly two weeks around each alignment, the shrine holds a dedicated festival, drawing large crowds specifically for the chance to see and photograph the sun setting directly along the approach.

The shrine itself, known for one of Japan's largest shimenawa sacred ropes and a genuinely striking hilltop setting, is worth visiting outside the alignment window too, but the Hikari no Michi period is when it draws its heaviest crowds and its widest social media attention. Because the phenomenon depends on precise timing and clear weather, arriving well before sunset and checking the shrine's published festival dates for the current year is essential.

Given how popular the alignment period has become, visiting outside the two annual windows offers a calmer, less crowded experience of the shrine and its approach, worth considering for visitors who'd rather skip the crowds even if it means missing the specific light effect.

Miyajidake, Fukutsu, Fukuoka Prefecture
