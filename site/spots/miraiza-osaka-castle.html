MIRAIZA OSAKA-JO

Osaka

Built in 1931 in a style blending Western architectural elements with a traditional Japanese exterior, this building originally served as the headquarters of the Japanese army's Fourth Division and stood for decades as a somewhat overlooked structure at the base of Osaka Castle before being renovated and reopened as a dining and retail complex. The renovation kept much of the building's original character while adding restaurants, cafes and shops inside, and a rooftop terrace offers one of the closer, more direct views of the castle keep available anywhere in the park, without the walking required to reach some of the more distant viewpoints.

A visit for a meal or a coffee with a view takes around an hour, and the building's own architecture, along with occasional historical exhibits inside, is worth a short look even for visitors not planning to eat there. Prices at the restaurants vary by establishment, from casual cafes to sit-down dining.

Because of its position directly beside the castle keep, the building and its terrace get busiest at the same times as the castle itself, particularly midday and during cherry blossom season, so an early or late visit tends to be calmer. It sits within Osaka Castle Park, a ten-minute walk from Osakajokoen Station, and works naturally as a stop for a meal either before heading into the castle keep or afterward while still close to the main sightseeing area.

1-1 Osakajo, Chuo Ward, Osaka
