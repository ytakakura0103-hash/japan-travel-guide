Rokko Shidare Observatory

Kobe

Opened in 2010, this small observation structure was designed by architect Hiroshi Sambuichi using a lattice of local Japanese cypress wood arranged to evoke shidare, or weeping branches, cascading down over the viewing platform. The effect is unlike most standard glass-and-steel observation decks: the wooden ribs frame the sky and the view of Kobe below in a way that changes with the light and even fills with frost patterns on cold winter mornings, an effect the design specifically anticipated.

The deck sits just a short walk from Rokko Garden Terrace, so it is easy to combine both in the same mountaintop visit, and taking in the structure along with its view generally only adds 15 to 20 minutes to a trip already headed up the mountain. Photographers in particular tend to linger here longer than the average visitor, drawn by how the wooden lattice interacts with sunset light.

Because many visitors head straight to the larger Garden Terrace viewing decks and skip this smaller structure, it tends to be noticeably quieter even at peak sunset hours, making it one of the better spots on the mountain for an unhurried view.

Rokkosancho, Nada-ku, Kobe
