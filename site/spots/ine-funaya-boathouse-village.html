Ine (Funaya Boathouse Village)

Kyoto

Ine sits on a sheltered bay on Kyoto Prefecture's northern coast, a fishing village built almost entirely around funaya, traditional wooden boathouses with a boat garage on the ground floor and living space above, constructed directly at the water's edge so fishing boats can be stored right beneath the home. Roughly 230 of these structures line the bay in a continuous row, an arrangement distinctive enough in Japan that the village has earned repeated comparisons to Venice, despite looking nothing like it beyond the basic idea of buildings rising straight out of the water.

A number of the funaya have been converted into cafes and small guesthouses, several with seating built directly over the water, letting visitors experience the setting from inside one of the boathouses rather than only viewing them from a boat tour or the shoreline road that runs around the bay. Boat tours of the bay itself are also available for a closer look at the row of boathouses from the water, including a chance to see local wild kites and gulls that follow tour boats along the route.

Because Ine sits roughly two hours from central Kyoto, typically reached via a train to Nishi-Maizuru or Amanohashidate followed by a local bus, it works best as a full day trip rather than a stop squeezed into a Kyoto city itinerary, and pairs naturally with a visit to Amanohashidate given the overlapping travel route. Checking current bus schedules in advance is worthwhile, since services to Ine run less frequently than trains within the city.
