Sojiji Temple

Yokohama

Sojiji relocated to Tsurumi from the Noto Peninsula after a fire in the early twentieth century, but its lineage as one of Soto Zen Buddhism's two head temples, alongside Eiheiji in Fukui, stretches back roughly seven centuries. The current complex spreads across a genuinely large wooded site, several structures within it are registered cultural properties, and the scale alone sets it apart from the smaller neighborhood temples scattered around central Yokohama.

What makes it particularly worth the trip out to Tsurumi is that it is an active training monastery, and it runs regular zazen seated-meditation sessions that welcome outside visitors, including beginners, rather than restricting practice to resident monks. Sessions are usually held on a set schedule; checking ahead for the current calendar and any English guidance is worthwhile before showing up.

Because Tsurumi sits north of Yokohama Station and off most sightseeing routes through the city center, Sojiji sees a fraction of the visitors that Kamakura's temples pull in, despite its comparable historical rank. Go on a weekday morning for the quietest grounds, and allow extra time to walk the full complex, it is considerably larger than it first appears from the main gate.
