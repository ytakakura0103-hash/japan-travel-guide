Yokohama Foreign General Cemetery

Yokohama

Established not long after the port opened in 1859, this cemetery holds the graves of diplomats, missionaries, sailors, and merchants from dozens of countries who lived or died in Yokohama during the treaty-port era, making the headstones themselves a quiet record of the city's international history. The grounds are maintained by a foundation and general public entry is limited, typically to specific open days or with prior arrangement, so most visitors experience it from the public viewing area at its edge rather than walking among the graves themselves.

Even from outside, the sloped rows of Western-style headstones set against the greenery of the Yamate bluff make for a striking, contemplative view, quite different from the pace of Chinatown or the waterfront below. It is worth checking current visiting arrangements before making a special trip, since access rules have varied over time.

It sits right beside Harbor View Park, so it costs nothing extra in time or effort to include on a Yamate walking route, and the contrast between the cemetery's quiet and the park's more lively lookout makes for a natural pause partway through a longer walk around the bluff.

Yamate-cho, Naka-ku, Yokohama
