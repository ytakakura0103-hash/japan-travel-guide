Maruyama Zoo

Hokkaido

Maruyama Zoo occupies a modest footprint compared to Japan's biggest zoos, but its setting is unusual: the enclosures back directly onto Maruyama Primeval Forest, one of the few patches of old-growth forest left within a Japanese city. The zoo's polar bear and seal enclosures are its best-known draws, and cold-weather animals in general seem more at ease here than they might in warmer cities, given Sapporo's real winters.

It works well as a half-day stop for families traveling with children, and pairs naturally with a visit to neighboring Hokkaido Shrine or a walk into the adjacent forest reserve, which has its own hiking trail up Mt. Maruyama. The zoo is compact enough to see at an unhurried pace in two to three hours.

Weekday visits, especially outside school holiday periods, are noticeably quieter than weekends. Winter is a distinctive time to visit specifically because of how the cold-climate animals behave when it actually snows, which is not something every zoo can offer; keeper talks and feeding times are worth checking on arrival since they vary seasonally. Bring proper winter footwear if visiting between December and March, as outdoor paths can be icy.

3-1 Miyagaoka, Chuo-ku, Sapporo, Hokkaido
