Koko-en Garden

Kobe

Constructed in the 1990s on land that once held the residences of samurai retainers serving the Himeji domain, Koko-en is not a single garden but nine separate walled gardens connected by passageways, each designed in a different traditional style, from a pond garden with a teahouse to a bamboo garden and a hillside garden. Because the walls between sections were built to reference the old samurai residence layout, walking through feels like moving between distinct private properties rather than one continuous park.

A full walk through all nine gardens, including a stop at the teahouse for matcha if desired, takes roughly an hour to ninety minutes. The garden works well as a calmer follow-up to the climbing and crowds of Himeji Castle itself, which sits just across the moat.

Because nearly all visitors to Himeji make Himeji Castle the priority and treat Koko-en as an optional add-on, the garden consistently sees far lighter foot traffic than the castle, even though it sits right next door, making it a good place to slow down after the busier castle visit.

68 Honmachi, Himeji, Hyogo

About a 5-minute walk from Himeji Castle's west side, roughly a 15 to 20-minute walk from Himeji Station.
