Awaji Yumebutai

Kobe

The hillside where Yumebutai stands was quarried for landfill used to build Kansai International Airport, leaving a bare scar that Ando's design set out to rehabilitate rather than disguise, weaving conservatories, terraced gardens, a hotel, an outdoor theater and a memorial park into the reshaped slope. Concrete forms typical of Ando's style, sharp geometric planes, framed views, and controlled use of water, run throughout the complex, most strikingly in the Hyakudanen (hundred-terrace garden) and a large domed greenhouse at the site's center.

A section of the grounds also serves as a quiet memorial to the roughly 6,400 people who died in the 1995 Great Hanshin earthquake, with a walkway lined with candle-holders lit for anniversary events, giving the complex a more reflective undertone than a typical architecture-and-garden attraction.

Because it requires crossing onto Awaji Island, Yumebutai draws a mix of architecture enthusiasts making a deliberate pilgrimage and domestic day-trippers, rather than the general Kobe sightseeing crowd, and it rarely feels crowded even on weekends. The complex is large enough to occupy a couple of unhurried hours, and pairs naturally with the nearby Hyakudanen flower terraces.

From Sannomiya or Kobe Sannomiya Bus Terminal, take a highway bus across the Akashi Kaikyo Bridge to Awaji Island (roughly 40-60 minutes to the northern part of the island near Yumebutai).
