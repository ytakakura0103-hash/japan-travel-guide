Fushimi Inari Shrine

Kyoto

Fushimi Inari Taisha sits at the base of a low mountain and is the head shrine for the tens of thousands of Inari shrines across Japan, dedicated to the god of rice and prosperity. Its main draw is the tunnel of vermilion torii gates that climbs the slope behind the shrine buildings, each one donated by a business or individual whose name is painted on the back in black ink. The lower stretch near Senbon Torii is the most crowded part of the whole complex, since a large share of visitors photograph it and turn back without continuing further up.

Past the halfway point at Yotsutsuji intersection, where a small overlook offers a first view over the city, the trail thins out considerably and turns into a genuine forest hike, with smaller shrines, fox statues, and teahouses tucked among the trees. A full loop to the summit and back takes a few hours at a relaxed pace, though most visitors only go as far as Yotsutsuji, which is enough for a satisfying outing of an hour or two. Entry is free and the shrine never closes, which makes it one of the few major sights that can be visited well before sunrise or after dark. Arriving at dawn is the most reliable way to see the lower gates without a crowd; a weekday visit helps too, but weekends and holidays are busy at any hour.

68 Fukakusa Yabunouchicho, Fushimi Ward, Kyoto

5-minute walk from Inari Station (JR Nara Line)
