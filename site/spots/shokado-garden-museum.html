Shokado Garden & Museum

Kyoto

Shokado Garden and Museum in Yawata combines a traditional strolling garden, several tea houses used for tea ceremony, and a small museum, all connected to the 17th-century monk-calligrapher Shokado Shojo, who spent his later years at a hermitage on this site and is remembered today as much for a food container as for his religious or artistic work. The compartmentalized wooden box he is said to have used to organize seeds and small tools became the template for what is now called the shokado bento, a boxed meal format with distinct sections for different dishes that remains a standard style used in upscale Japanese restaurants and bento shops across the country. The garden itself, with bamboo groves, seasonal flowers, and several teahouses, makes for an unhurried walk of an hour or so, and visitors with an interest in tea ceremony can sometimes arrange to have matcha in one of the teahouses. The museum galleries display calligraphy, tea utensils, and rotating exhibitions related to the site's history. Because it sits outside the main sightseeing corridor of central Kyoto, in the town of Yawata near Iwashimizu Hachimangu, it draws a small, low-key crowd rather than tour groups, and weekday visits are close to guaranteed quiet. Admission is modest. The garden changes with the seasons, with plum and cherry blossoms in spring and maples in autumn, though it's a pleasant, unhurried stop at any time of year.
