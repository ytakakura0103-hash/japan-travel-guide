Momiji Manju Workshops, Miyajima

Hiroshima

Momiji manju are small, maple-leaf-shaped cakes with a sponge-cake-like exterior wrapped around a filling, most traditionally sweet red bean paste, though modern shops offer variations such as custard, chocolate, or cheese. The confection is generally traced to Miyajima in the early 20th century, and the maple leaf shape references the island's Momijidani, or "Maple Valley," known for its autumn foliage, though the cakes themselves are sold and eaten year-round.

Several bakeries along Omotesando and the surrounding streets make the cakes on-site using individual cast-iron molds, and it is common to see the process happening in the shop window: batter poured into open molds, filling added, then the molds closed and flipped to cook each cake individually on a rotating rig. Buying a cake straight off this line, still warm, is a noticeably different experience from the packaged versions sold as souvenirs elsewhere in Japan.

A related, more recent variation is fried momiji manju, dipped in batter and deep-fried to order, which has become its own minor specialty on the island and is often sold from separate stalls or counters near the main shops.

Miyajima-cho, Hatsukaichi, Hiroshima Prefecture
