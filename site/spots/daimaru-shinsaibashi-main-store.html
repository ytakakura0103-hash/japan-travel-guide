Daimaru Shinsaibashi Main Store

Osaka

Daimaru's roots at this exact site trace back to 1726, part of a company history stretching to 1717, making it one of the oldest continuously operating department store brands in Japan. The building that stands today dates to 1933, designed by William Merrell Vories, an American-born architect who became a major figure in early 20th-century Japanese architecture, and it blends Art Deco geometry with neo-Gothic detailing into a facade widely considered one of his masterworks; a ceramic peacock relief from 1925 still decorates the Shinsaibashi-suji entrance.

The store underwent its first full rebuild in 86 years, reopening in September 2019 after a renovation that deliberately preserved the original facade and key architectural details while modernizing the interior for contemporary retail. That makes it a rare case of a fully working department store that also functions as a piece of architectural heritage, worth a stop for the building alone even without shopping in mind. The basement depachika food hall is a highlight in its own right, with an extensive range of prepared foods, sweets, and gifts typical of Japan's department store food culture.

Because it sits directly on the Shinsaibashisuji arcade, it's easy to combine with a walk through Shinsaibashi's other shopping streets or a stop at nearby Amerikamura. The building's exterior is worth viewing from across the street as well as up close, since the full scale of the Vories design is easier to appreciate from a short distance back.

1-7-1 Shinsaibashisuji, Chuo Ward, Osaka

Directly on the Shinsaibashisuji shopping arcade, a few minutes on foot from Shinsaibashi Station (Osaka Metro Midosuji/Nagahori Tsurumi-ryokuchi Lines).
