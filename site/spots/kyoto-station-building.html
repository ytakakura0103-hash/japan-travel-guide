Kyoto Station Building

Kyoto

Kyoto Station's main building is a vast, mostly glass-and-steel structure completed at the end of the 1990s, deliberately modern and geometric in a way that stood out, and drew some local controversy, against the historic low-rise city it serves. Inside, the building's most distinctive feature is a wide, open-air staircase of well over a hundred steps that climbs through the atrium to a rooftop-level sky garden with a view back down over the platforms and out across the city, a free vantage point that's often overlooked by visitors passing straight through to catch a train.

The building also functions as a large shopping and dining complex, with a department store, a food hall, and a theater built into its floors, and each December it hosts a large illuminated Christmas tree display in the main atrium that draws crowds of its own for a few weeks. Simply walking the staircase and atrium takes twenty to thirty minutes; exploring the shops and food floors easily adds an hour or more.

Because it's a functioning train station as much as a sight, Kyoto Station is busy at all hours, though the upper staircase and rooftop areas thin out compared to the concourse below; the Christmas illumination period in December is by far the busiest stretch of the year for the atrium specifically.
