Okurayama Memorial Hall

Yokohama

Built in 1932 by businessman and scholar Okura Kunihiko as a private research institute for Japanese spiritual and cultural studies, this hall's severe, temple-like Greek Revival facade, with its heavy stone columns, makes it one of the most architecturally distinctive buildings in the entire city, closer in feel to a European civic monument than anything else in the surrounding residential hills of Kohoku Ward. It later passed into city ownership and now operates as a public hall for concerts, lectures, and exhibitions.

The approach up the hill from the station is lined with cherry trees that draw a modest, mostly local crowd during the spring bloom, when the pale stone building framed by pink blossoms becomes one of the better under-the-radar cherry-blossom photo spots in northern Yokohama. Outside of bloom season, the building and its quiet residential surroundings still make for a pleasant, unusual half-hour detour.

Interior access depends on whether the hall is being used for a scheduled event, so exterior viewing is the more reliable plan unless you time a visit around an open lecture or concert. It sits well off any typical Yokohama sightseeing route, appealing mainly to architecture enthusiasts and cherry-blossom chasers willing to go a bit further afield.
