Otaru Tenguyama Ropeway

Hokkaido

Mt. Tenguyama rises directly behind Otaru, and the ropeway to its summit gives a wide view over the harbor town and Ishikari Bay that some Hokkaido residents rate above the far more famous Mt. Hakodate night view, largely because it draws a fraction of the visitors. At the top, short walking trails pass a small shrine and a scattering of tanuki (raccoon dog) statues tied to local folklore, along with a ski area that operates in winter.

The summit also has an observation deck, a restaurant, and in winter a slope used by a small local ski resort, so the mountain serves double duty for sightseeing and skiing depending on the season. A short hiking trail exists as an alternative to the ropeway for those who want to walk up, though it takes considerably longer.

Because most Otaru day-trippers concentrate on the canal and Sakaimachi Street below, the ropeway line rarely gets as long as Hakodate's evening queues, even at sunset, which is the best time to catch both the daylight harbor view and the lights coming on.

Summer brings clearer long-distance views, while winter pairs the night view with snow-covered slopes, though ropeway operation can be affected by high winds in poor weather, so checking conditions before heading up is sensible.

2-16-15 Mogami, Otaru, Hokkaido 047-0023
