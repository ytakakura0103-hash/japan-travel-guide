JR Central Towers, Nagoya Station

Nagoya

Nagoya Station itself is a hub for JR, Meitetsu, Kintetsu, and subway lines, but the towers built above it turned the station into a destination in its own right: a JR-affiliated department store, restaurants stacked across multiple upper floors, and office space, all under one roof with the trains running beneath. The scale is disorienting at first — it's easy to spend twenty minutes just finding the right exit — but signage improves once you get oriented.

There's no cost to explore the shopping floors, and browsing or grabbing a meal can range from a quick half hour to a leisurely half day given the sheer number of restaurants stacked across the towers' dining floors. Department store sales floors close earlier than the station itself, so plan shopping before evening if that's the goal.

Rush hour, unsurprisingly, is the most chaotic time to pass through; visiting the upper shopping and dining floors mid-afternoon avoids both the commuter crush and the dinner rush. The towers' upper floors also offer a free or low-cost view back down over the station and city, a good fallback if the pricier observation decks elsewhere feel unnecessary.
