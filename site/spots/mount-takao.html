Mount Takao

Tokyo

Mount Takao rises to 599 meters at the western edge of the Tokyo area, and its combination of accessibility and genuine mountain scenery has made it one of the most visited peaks in the world by some counts, popular enough for a casual half-day hike that its main trails can feel closer to a busy sidewalk than a wilderness path on weekends. A cable car and chairlift cut a significant chunk of the climb, so visitors can choose anything from an easy walk from the mid-mountain station to a full hike from the base, with several trail options of varying difficulty looping toward the summit.

The summit itself offers a wide view that, on clear winter days, stretches all the way to Mount Fuji — a reward that draws a mix of casual tourists, day hikers, and serious trekkers testing gear before longer trips elsewhere. A round trip using the cable car for at least part of the climb typically takes two to three hours; a full hike up and down on foot can take four or more depending on route and pace.

Weekday visits, or an early start on weekends, are the best way to avoid the trail congestion that builds through the late morning. Autumn foliage season, generally mid-to-late November, is especially popular and worth planning around if crowds are a concern, alongside the mountain's own small temple complex partway up, Yakuoin.
