Hokkaido University Poplar and Ginkgo Avenues

Sapporo

Hokkaido University's main campus sits directly north of Sapporo Station, close enough to walk to from downtown, and its grounds have become an unofficial city park thanks to two features: a row of tall poplar trees and a separate ginkgo-lined avenue that both put on a strong show of autumn color, typically peaking in late October to early November. The university's origins as an agricultural college in the 1870s are still visible in a cluster of preserved wooden farm buildings and a small model barn on campus.

Because it's a real, functioning university rather than a tourist attraction, the atmosphere is calmer and more everyday than most sights on a typical Sapporo itinerary, with students cycling between buildings rather than crowds of visitors, except during the peak ginkgo weekends when the avenue draws a steady stream of photographers.

A slow walk through campus takes under an hour and pairs naturally with a morning arrival at Sapporo Station, either before checking into a hotel or before heading further into downtown. Spring brings its own quieter charm with fresh greenery along the same avenues, while winter turns the poplar-lined paths into a snow-hushed walk that's arguably underrated compared to the more famous autumn display.
