Maruyama Primeval Forest

Hokkaido

Most visitors to Maruyama stop at Hokkaido Shrine or the park's picnic lawns and never continue into the primeval forest that covers the rest of the hill, a protected stand of old-growth trees that has never been logged and is one of the few such forests left this close to a major Japanese city. A hiking trail climbs from behind the shrine to the summit of Mt. Maruyama, taking roughly an hour round trip at a moderate pace.

The forest is home to wild Ezo squirrels and a range of birdlife, and because logging has never touched it, the tree cover and undergrowth look noticeably different from the managed parkland below, denser and more tangled. It's a real hike rather than a paved stroll, with roots and uneven footing in places, so reasonable shoes matter.

Because the shrine and park draw the bulk of foot traffic and relatively few visitors continue onto the trail itself, the forest stays quiet even during Hokkaido Shrine's busiest festival days. It's a useful way to get genuine forest solitude without leaving the city.

The summit gives a partial view over Sapporo through the trees, best in late autumn and winter when the canopy thins, while spring and summer keep the trail shaded and cool.

Maruyama, Chuo-ku, Sapporo, Hokkaido 064-0959

Open in Google Maps

About 20-25 minutes from Sapporo Station: subway Namboku Line to Odori Station, transfer to the Tozai Line to Maruyama-koen Station, then a 15-20 minute walk through the park and shrine grounds to the trailhead.
