Motomachi District

Sapporo

As one of the first Japanese ports opened to foreign trade in the 1850s, Hakodate developed a genuine foreign community, and Motomachi, the hillside neighborhood just above the harbor, still carries the physical traces of that history: former British and Russian consulate buildings, an Orthodox church with a distinctive white exterior and green domes, and Catholic and Anglican churches, several of which sit within a few minutes' walk of one another.

Unlike a museum district, Motomachi is a lived-in neighborhood of sloped streets and staircases, and part of its appeal is simply wandering rather than following a fixed checklist of buildings; the mix of Japanese and Western architectural styles side by side is unusual even by Hokkaido standards, itself a relatively young, foreign-influenced region.

The steep streets offer some of Hakodate's best harbor views, particularly from Hachiman-zaka slope, and are correspondingly popular for photography around sunset. Weekend afternoons draw the most visitors; a weekday or early morning walk gives a quieter, more residential feel to the same streets. Snow on the sloped roads in winter makes for striking photos but also genuinely slippery walking, so sturdy footwear matters more here than in flatter parts of the city.
