Yushima Tenmangu

Tokyo

Yushima Tenmangu is dedicated to Sugawara no Michizane, the scholar-turned-deity worshipped across Japan as the god of learning, and it draws a steady stream of students and their families in the weeks before entrance exams, who come to write wishes on wooden ema plaques hung in clusters around the shrine grounds. Beyond its role as an exam-season pilgrimage site, the shrine is known for its plum orchard of roughly 300 trees, which blossoms into a fragrant, pale display each February — earlier and quieter than Tokyo's cherry blossom season, and celebrated with its own dedicated plum festival featuring food stalls and evening illumination.

A visit to the shrine itself is brief, typically twenty to thirty minutes, though the plum festival period extends that if you plan to browse the food stalls or read through the ema plaques left by visiting students. Outside of exam season and the February plum festival, the shrine is relatively quiet and can be visited comfortably at almost any time of day; during the festival, weekends draw noticeably larger crowds than weekday visits.

There is no admission fee to enter the shrine grounds. Yushima Tenmangu sits close to Ueno, making it easy to combine with a visit to Ueno Park or the museums nearby, particularly if timed around the February plum blossoms before Ueno's cherry blossoms take over in late March.
