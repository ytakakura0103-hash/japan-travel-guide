Nunobiki Falls

Kobe

Nunobiki Falls is traditionally counted among Japan's three most famous waterfalls alongside Nachi Falls and Kegon Falls, and it has been a subject for poets since at least the 10th century, appearing in classical texts like the Tales of Ise. The falls are actually four separate cascades, Ontaki, Mentaki, Tsuzumigataki, and Meotodaki, connected by a walking trail that climbs gently through forest just minutes from one of Kobe's main train stations.

What makes the falls unusual is how accessible they are: the lowest falls are only a short uphill walk from Shin-Kobe Station, and reaching all four on the way up to the Nunobiki Herb Garden ropeway station takes perhaps 30 to 45 minutes of walking at an easy pace. The largest of the four, Ontaki, drops from a notable height and is the most photographed.

Because the trailhead is so close to the station, the lower falls can see a steady trickle of visitors throughout the day, but the crowd thins quickly the higher you climb. Early morning is the quietest and coolest time to walk the trail, particularly in summer, and the surrounding maples turn color in November for anyone timing a visit around autumn foliage.
