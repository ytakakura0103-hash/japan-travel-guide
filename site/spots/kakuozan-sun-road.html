Kakuozan Sun Road

Nagoya

Kakuozan grew up as a monzen-machi, a town that exists to serve pilgrims walking to a temple, in this case Nittai-ji, which was founded in 1904. More than a century later the street that carried those pilgrims is still there, narrower and less polished than Nagoya's big shopping arcades, lined with sweet shops, a public bathhouse or two, and a growing number of small cafes and secondhand bookstores run by people who actually live in the neighborhood.

What makes Kakuozan worth a detour rather than a photo stop is the mix of eras sitting side by side: a decades-old tofu shop next to a natural wine bar, retro apartment blocks with hand-painted signage, and side streets that dead-end into someone's garden. A flea market selling antiques, kimono fabric and secondhand goods sets up along the street on a periodic basis, turning the whole strip into an open-air market for a day.

It rewards slow walking rather than a checklist, and pairs naturally with a stop at Nittai-ji itself or the university-town streets of nearby Yagoto.

Kakuozan-dori area, Chikusa-ku, Nagoya, Aichi

Directly above Kakuozan Station on the Higashiyama subway line, about 14 minutes from Nagoya Station (no transfer).
