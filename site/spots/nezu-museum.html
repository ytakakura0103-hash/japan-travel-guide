Nezu Museum

Tokyo

The Nezu Museum houses a private collection of pre-modern Japanese and East Asian art — paintings, calligraphy, ceramics, and a well-regarded set of Buddhist sculpture and metalwork — assembled originally by industrialist Nezu Kaichiro and later expanded by the family foundation that runs the museum today. The building itself, designed by architect Kengo Kuma, is worth as much attention as the collection: a long, sloped entrance corridor lined with bamboo leads visitors from busy Omotesando into a much quieter interior, and much of the museum's back wall opens onto a large traditional garden with ponds, stone paths, and several small teahouses tucked among the trees.

Exhibitions rotate on a regular schedule, focusing on different parts of the collection or specific themes rather than displaying everything at once, so the specific artworks on view will vary by visit. A full visit including the galleries and a walk through the garden takes about ninety minutes to two hours; visitors focused only on the garden can move through more quickly.

Admission is charged at the gate. Given its central Omotesando location, the museum is a natural stop to combine with shopping or a walk down Omotesando's main avenue, and its garden offers a genuinely quiet break from that same busy street just steps away. Weekday visits tend to be calmer than weekends.
