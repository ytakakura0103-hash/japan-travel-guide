Sakurai Futamigaura

Fukuoka

Along Itoshima's coastal Sunset Road, this pair of connected rocks rising from shallow water just offshore has become one of the peninsula's most photographed sights, especially as the sun drops directly behind them on clear evenings. A thick shimenawa rope, replaced periodically in a small local ceremony, links the two rocks and marks them as sacred, in the Shinto tradition of meoto iwa, or "wedded rocks," found at several sites around Japan.

A red torii gate stands in the shallows nearby, adding a second focal point for photographs, particularly around sunset when silhouettes form against the colored sky. The site is unstaffed and free to visit, with a small parking area and a short boardwalk, so there is no ticket line or opening hours to plan around beyond daylight itself.

The rocks are popular enough that sunset on a clear summer evening can draw a real crowd along the shoreline, especially around the summer solstice when the sun is said to set precisely between the two rocks. Visiting at a different time of day, or on a weekday outside peak season, trades a little of that famous alignment for a much quieter shoreline. The nearby coastal road has several small cafes worth combining with a visit.

Sakurai, Itoshima, Fukuoka
