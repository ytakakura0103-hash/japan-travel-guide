Shofukuji Temple

Fukuoka

Shofukuji was built with the backing of the shogun Minamoto no Yoritomo after Eisai returned from studying Zen Buddhism in China, and a plaque above its gate, said to bear calligraphy from a retired emperor, still proclaims it Japan's first Zen temple. Eisai is also credited with bringing tea cultivation to Japan and reportedly planted some of the country's first tea trees on these grounds, which makes this an easy-to-miss but genuinely significant stop for anyone interested in where Japanese tea culture actually began.

The temple buildings themselves are closed to the public, but the grounds are open to walk through, and the atmosphere of working monastery rather than tourist site is part of the appeal. Photography is fine outdoors, though tripods, drones, and photographing monks without permission are all off limits.

Because there isn't much to "do" here beyond walking and reading the information boards, it rarely gets crowded even on weekends, making it a calm contrast after the busier Kushida Shrine or Canal City area nearby. Fifteen or twenty minutes is enough for most visitors.

6-1 Gokushomachi, Hakata Ward, Fukuoka
