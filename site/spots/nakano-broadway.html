Nakano Broadway

Tokyo

Nakano Broadway is a shopping arcade built in the 1960s, and while its exterior and mall interior have aged with a slightly worn, retro charm, it has become one of Tokyo's most important destinations for collectors of vintage toys, manga, trading cards, and figures. The building's upper floors are largely taken up by branches of Mandarake, a major secondhand otaku goods chain, spread across multiple small specialized shops rather than one large store, alongside independent dealers selling everything from old video games to rare cel animation art.

Compared to Akihabara, Nakano Broadway feels distinctly local: prices can be more collector-oriented, stock tends to skew toward older and rarer items, and the crowd is more often longtime hobbyists than casual tourists. The building connects directly to Nakano Sun Mall, a covered shopping street running from Nakano Station, which has more everyday shops, food, and a market atmosphere worth walking through on the way in or out.

There's no real crowding issue here, since Nakano Broadway rarely gets as busy as central Tokyo shopping districts, though weekend afternoons are naturally livelier than weekday mornings. An hour is enough for a quick pass through the main floors, but collectors interested in specific genres can easily spend half a day working through the smaller specialty shops one by one.
