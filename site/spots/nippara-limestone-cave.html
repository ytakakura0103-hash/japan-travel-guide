Nippara Limestone Cave

Tokyo

Nippara Limestone Cave, deep in the mountainous Okutama area at Tokyo's far western edge, is the largest limestone cave in the Kanto region, formed over an extremely long span of time by water dissolving through the rock to create chambers, stalactites, and underground passages that visitors walk through on a lit, maintained path. The cave maintains a constant temperature of around 11 degrees Celsius year-round, which makes it a genuinely welcome escape from Tokyo's summer heat and, conversely, a reason to bring a jacket even in August.

A walk through the cave's illuminated route typically takes thirty to forty-five minutes, and the path includes some low ceilings, uneven footing, and narrow sections, so sturdy shoes are worth wearing even though the walkway itself is developed. Because Okutama sits well outside central Tokyo, reaching the cave involves a train ride followed by a bus into the mountains, making this a half-day or full-day trip rather than a quick stop, and it's often combined with a broader visit to Okutama's hiking trails or riverside scenery.

The cave is far less crowded than most attractions on a typical Tokyo itinerary, and weekday visits in particular are quiet enough that you may have long stretches of the passage to yourself. Admission is charged at the entrance and is generally considered modest for the length of the visit.

1052 Nippara, Okutama, Tokyo
