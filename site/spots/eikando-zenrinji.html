Eikando Zenrinji

Kyoto

Eikando, formally known as Zenrinji, has earned its popular nickname, "the temple of maples," through sheer density: the grounds hold several hundred maple trees planted across gardens, ponds, and hillside paths, and when they turn in November the whole complex becomes one of the most photographed autumn scenes in a city that takes its autumn foliage seriously. The temple's Amida hall houses a well-known statue of the Buddha looking back over his shoulder rather than facing forward, a distinctive pose tied to a temple legend, and the grounds also include a multi-story pagoda reached by a climb up a wooded hillside path, rewarding the effort with a wide view back over the maple canopy and, on clear days, toward the city beyond. During the peak autumn color period, usually mid to late November, the temple extends its hours into the evening for an illumination event, lighting the maples and their reflection in the central pond, an experience distinct enough from the daytime visit that some visitors choose to do both. Evening illumination requires a separate admission and draws substantial crowds, so arriving close to the opening of the evening session helps beat the worst of the lines. A daytime visit generally takes an hour to ninety minutes depending on how much of the hillside path you cover. Outside the autumn peak, the temple is considerably quieter and still worth visiting for its gardens and architecture even without the maple spectacle.

48 Eikandocho, Sakyo Ward, Kyoto
