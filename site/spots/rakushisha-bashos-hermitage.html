Rakushisha (Basho's Hermitage)

Kyoto

Rakushisha, meaning "house of fallen persimmons," takes its name from an incident involving Mukai Kyorai, a leading disciple of the haiku master Matsuo Basho, who reportedly sold the rights to the persimmon trees on this property only for a storm to knock the fruit down before the buyer could collect it, an anecdote that gives the small hut its distinctly modest, self-deprecating character. Basho himself stayed at the hut on visits to Kyoto and is said to have written some of his travel journal here, and the site today functions as a quiet literary pilgrimage spot for readers of Basho and Kyorai's haiku rather than a major sightseeing destination. The hut itself is small, thatched, and simply furnished, surrounded by a modest garden with the persimmon trees that gave the property its name, along with stone markers inscribed with haiku composed by visitors and poets over the centuries. A visit takes perhaps twenty to thirty minutes, and the site rarely sees more than a handful of visitors at a time, a real contrast to the crowds a short walk away at Arashiyama's bamboo grove and main temples. Persimmons ripen on the trees in autumn, aligning nicely with the temple's literary namesake and with the maple color across the wider Arashiyama area at the same time. A modest admission fee applies. Because it sits off the main Arashiyama path, reaching it requires a deliberate walk, which is exactly what keeps it peaceful.

20 Sagaogurayama Higashiharocho, Ukyo Ward, Kyoto

15-minute walk from Saga-Arashiyama Station (JR Sagano Line)
