Fukuurajima Island

Sendai

Fukuurajima sits a bit further out into Matsushima Bay than the more heavily visited islets near Godaido, reached by a long red pedestrian bridge, sometimes called a 'lovers' bridge,' that stretches roughly 250 meters over the water. Once across, the island itself is left largely in a natural, semi-wild state, home to a noted variety of native plant species and shaded walking paths that wind through the interior rather than a single straight sightseeing route.

Because reaching it takes a bit more effort than the waterfront sites clustered near the pier, and there's a small crossing fee involved, Fukuurajima gets meaningfully fewer visitors than Godaido or Zuiganji, even though the views back toward the mainland and across the bay are excellent. Benches scattered through the island make it an easy place to sit and take in the scenery rather than just pass through.

The crossing fee is modest, and a full loop of the island's paths takes roughly forty-five minutes to an hour. It's rarely crowded even in peak season; the bridge itself can sway slightly in wind, which is part of the appeal for some visitors and worth knowing about in advance for others. Spring brings a run of flowering plants across the island, adding some color beyond the pines.
