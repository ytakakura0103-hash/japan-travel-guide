Odori Park

Hokkaido

Odori Park is less a single park than a long civic stage that changes costume with the seasons. Locals cut through it on the way to work, sit on its benches with a convenience-store coffee at lunch, and pack it shoulder to shoulder for the Sapporo Snow Festival in February, when massive ice and snow sculptures line the walkways for about ten days. In summer the same lawns host a six-week beer garden where several breweries set up tents side by side, and in autumn the ginkgo and elm trees along the park turn a deep gold.

The park stretches between Odori Station, which several subway lines pass through, and is a short walk from JR Sapporo Station, so it works well as a orientation point for a first day in the city. The Sapporo TV Tower at its eastern end and the clock tower a block away are both easy add-ons to a stroll through.

During the Snow Festival the crowds are heaviest in the evening when the sculptures are lit up, so visiting in the mid-morning gives a calmer look at the same artwork with shorter lines for food stalls. Outside festival season, the park is at its quietest on weekday mornings before the lunch crowd arrives. Because Hokkaido's climate swings hard between seasons, check ahead if a specific event is the reason for the visit, since snow and beer-garden dates shift slightly from year to year.
