Aichi Prefectural Ceramic Museum

Nagoya

Located in the hills of Seto, roughly 20 kilometers northeast of central Nagoya, the Aichi Prefectural Ceramic Museum surveys the history of Japanese ceramics with a natural emphasis on the Seto and Mino kiln traditions that developed in this immediate area over the past thousand years. Its collection spans everyday utilitarian wares through to refined tea ceramics, giving visitors a broader technical and historical context than the smaller craft-focused stops elsewhere in Seto.

The museum's setting is part of its appeal: rather than a downtown building, it occupies a quiet, forested campus that changes with the seasons, making the walk between its buildings as pleasant as the exhibits themselves. Because it is a prefectural rather than municipal institution, it periodically hosts larger touring or thematic exhibitions in addition to its permanent collection.

Getting there requires a bit more effort than Seto's central pottery district, via the Linimo maglev line, which makes it a good option for visitors who want a quieter, more scholarly ceramics stop after the busier lanes of central Seto or Tokoname.

234 Minamiyamaguchi-cho, Seto, Aichi
