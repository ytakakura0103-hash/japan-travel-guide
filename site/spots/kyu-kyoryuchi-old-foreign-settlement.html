Kyu-Kyoryuchi (Old Foreign Settlement)

Kobe

When Kobe's port opened to foreign trade in 1868, this rectangular grid of blocks just south of Motomachi Station was laid out as the formal foreign settlement, distinct from the hillside residential district of Kitano. Several original and reconstructed trading-house buildings survive along its main avenues, now occupied by upscale fashion boutiques, jewelers and cafes rather than the shipping offices they once housed.

Unlike Kitano's residential ijinkan, built for living, these buildings were commercial from the start, and the district still carries an air of formality, wide sidewalks, planted trees and a noticeably calmer pace than the Chinatown and Motomachi shopping arcades just to the north. It's a pleasant place to walk even without a shopping agenda, simply to look at the architecture.

Because it functions as a working retail district rather than a preserved museum area, there's no admission fee and no fixed hours beyond individual shop schedules. Weekday mornings are quietest; the district picks up with shoppers in the afternoon and evening, especially around dinner time when several well-regarded restaurants in the old buildings fill up.

Kyoryuchi, Chuo Ward, Kobe

From Sannomiya Station or Motomachi Station, walk south about 5-10 minutes toward the harbor; the district sits between Motomachi's shopping arcade and Meriken Park.
