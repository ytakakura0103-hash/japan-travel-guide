Daigoji Temple

Kyoto

Daigoji is one of Kyoto's largest temple complexes, and unusually, it's really two temples in one: a lower precinct, Shimo Daigo, that most visitors see, and an upper precinct, Kami Daigo, reached by a genuinely strenuous hike up Mount Daigo that far fewer people attempt. The lower precinct alone is enough to fill a couple of hours, anchored by a five-story pagoda built in 951 that survived the fires and wars that destroyed so much of Kyoto's older architecture, making it the city's oldest surviving wooden structure. The temple's Sanboin subtemple holds a separately ticketed garden considered one of the finest in the city, designed under the direction of Toyotomi Hideyoshi for a lavish 1598 cherry blossom viewing party that remains one of the most famous events in Kyoto's garden history. That connection to Hideyoshi still shapes the temple's identity every spring, when its cherry trees draw large crowds trying to recreate a version of that original party atmosphere. Autumn maples across the grounds are similarly popular, if slightly less crowded than the spring peak. Admission includes several combination ticket options depending on how many areas you plan to see, and it's worth deciding in advance rather than at the gate. Visiting on a weekday morning, or venturing up to the quieter Kami Daigo precinct, are the more reliable ways to avoid the busiest crowds here.

22 Daigo Higashiojicho, Fushimi Ward, Kyoto
