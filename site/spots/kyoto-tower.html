Kyoto Tower

Kyoto

Kyoto Tower stands directly across the street from Kyoto Station, its white steel frame and lighthouse-like shape a deliberate departure from the wooden pagodas and temple roofs that otherwise define the city's skyline, a contrast that drew criticism when it was built in the 1960s but has since become a landmark in its own right. It's not a particularly tall structure by international standards, but Kyoto's strict height restrictions on surrounding buildings, kept low to preserve views of the historic city and its temples, mean the tower's observation deck offers close to the only genuine bird's-eye view over central Kyoto.

The circular observation deck near the top allows a full 360-degree look over the city and, on clear days, out toward the surrounding mountains, and a visit to the deck typically takes thirty to forty-five minutes including time to walk the full circle and locate landmarks below. Admission is charged for the observation deck; the tower's lower floors house a hotel, shops, and a public bath, separate from the deck ticket.

The tower is busiest around sunset, when the observation deck fills with visitors watching the city light up, and during December when illuminations are added to the tower's own lighting; visiting earlier in the day is a straightforward way to avoid the sunset crowd if a daytime view is enough.

721-1 Higashishiokojicho, Shimogyo Ward, Kyoto
