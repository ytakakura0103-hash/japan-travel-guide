National Diet Building

Tokyo

The National Diet Building has watched over Nagatacho since 1936, its central stepped pyramid roof and symmetrical stone wings making it one of the more distinctive government buildings in Asia — a deliberate blend of Japanese and Western architectural styles from an era when the country was consciously projecting a modern, unified image. The House of Representatives side offers free guided tours in Japanese (English-language options and audio guides are also available) that take visitors through the central hall, the emperor's reception room, and the chamber itself when the Diet is not in session, giving a rare look inside a working seat of government rather than a museum reconstruction.

Tours run on a fixed schedule several times a day and typically last under an hour, though a full visit including waiting time can take closer to ninety minutes. Photo identification is required, and bags go through a security check before entry, so arriving a little early is sensible. Because access depends on the Diet's session calendar, it is worth confirming tour availability before making a special trip, as chambers are closed to visitors while in active use.

The surrounding Nagatacho and Kasumigaseki area is otherwise fairly quiet outside of office hours, so mornings tend to be the least crowded time for a tour, and the walk from Kokkai-gijidomae Station passes several other government buildings worth a glance even if you skip the tour itself.
