Sorakuen Garden

Kobe

Sorakuen was once the private residence and garden of a wealthy Kobe merchant family, opened to the public in the early 20th century after the city acquired the land. Its pond-centered strolling garden layout, with stone paths circling the water and carefully placed trees, gives a sense of Japanese garden design without needing to leave the city center, and a preserved Meiji-era Western-style stable building and a moved 17th-century farmhouse both sit on the grounds as architectural counterpoints to the garden itself.

A relaxed walk around the full garden takes about 30 to 45 minutes. Azaleas and irises bloom in late spring, and the maple trees color in November, both of which are good times to visit if timing allows, though the garden is pleasant in any season thanks to its evergreen backbone.

Because it sits a few minutes off Kobe's busiest sightseeing routes and does not appear on every itinerary, Sorakuen rarely feels crowded even on weekends, making it a good option for anyone who wants a slower, quieter break from the Kitano hillside crowds nearby.
