Meguro River

Tokyo

The stretch of Meguro River running through Nakameguro is lined with roughly 800 cherry trees, and for about two weeks each spring — typically late March into early April — their branches meet overhead along the narrow riverside path to form a nearly continuous canopy of blossoms above the water. Paper lanterns strung along the walkway light the trees from below after dark during the bloom, and pop-up cafes and food stalls open specifically for the season, many of them only operating during those two weeks before disappearing again until the following year.

Because the path along the river is narrow, peak bloom weekends bring dense foot traffic, particularly in the evening when the lanterns are lit and the pop-up stalls draw the biggest crowds; a weekday visit or an early morning walk offers a noticeably calmer alternative to see the same blossoms with far more room to walk and take photos. A full walk along the blossom-lined stretch takes about thirty to forty-five minutes at a relaxed pace.

There is no admission fee to walk the riverside path, though the pop-up cafes and food stalls charge for their own offerings. Outside of cherry blossom season, Nakameguro is a pleasant but far less dramatic neighborhood of small boutiques and cafes along the same river, worth a visit in its own right if you're in the area outside of bloom.
