Ueno Park & Ueno Zoo

Tokyo

Ueno Park is one of Tokyo's largest and oldest public parks, and it does double duty as a green space, a cultural hub, and — for a few weeks each spring — one of the city's most popular cherry blossom viewing spots, when the central pathway is lined with blossoming trees and picnicking crowds. The park is home to Ueno Zoo, Japan's oldest zoological garden, with a wide range of animals across a fairly walkable layout suited to families. Note that the zoo's twin giant pandas, Xiao Xiao and Lei Lei, were returned to China in January 2026, leaving Japan without any pandas on public display for the first time since 1972 — check current exhibits before visiting.

Beyond the zoo, the park contains Shinobazu Pond, a large lotus-covered pond with a walking path and a small shrine on an island reached by causeway, plus a cluster of major museums including the Tokyo National Museum and the National Museum of Western Art. This concentration of things to see means Ueno Park works well as a full day out, or as a base to dip into one or two specific attractions.

Hanami (cherry blossom) season, typically late March to early April, is by far the busiest time, with reserved picnic spots claimed from early morning and a festive, crowded atmosphere in the evenings when the trees are lit up. Outside of that window, the park is comparatively calm, and weekday mornings are the easiest time to see the zoo and pond without heavy crowds. Admission to the park itself is free; the zoo charges a separate, modest entrance fee.
