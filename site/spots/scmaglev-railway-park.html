SCMAGLEV and Railway Park

Nagoya

Operated by JR Central rather than a generic transit authority, this museum lines up decommissioned Shinkansen trains nose to nose, including early models that look almost quaint next to today's sleek bullet trains, alongside a maglev test vehicle that once set a world speed record for rail travel. Visitors can walk beside and even board several of the retired trains, and detailed exhibits explain how Shinkansen operations, maintenance, and safety systems actually work behind the scenes.

Admission is inexpensive and the site easily fills half a day for train enthusiasts, less for a casual visit. Driving and conductor simulators are popular with kids and often require a separate reservation or lottery slip picked up on arrival, so grabbing one early in the visit is worthwhile.

School holidays and weekends bring long lines for the simulators specifically, though the main exhibition halls rarely feel overcrowded given their size. A large diorama with miniature trains running on a schedule is worth timing your visit around, since it operates at set intervals rather than continuously.

3-2-2 Kinjofuto, Minato-ku, Nagoya, Aichi
