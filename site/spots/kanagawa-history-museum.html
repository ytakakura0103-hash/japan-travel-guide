Kanagawa Prefectural Museum of Cultural History

Yokohama

Built as the head office of the Yokohama Specie Bank and completed in 1904, this Neo-Baroque building with its distinctive dome, nicknamed locally as one of Yokohama's landmark towers, lost its original dome to fire during the 1923 Great Kanto Earthquake before being restored and reopened decades later as a prefectural history museum. The Corinthian columns and ornate stone facade along Bashamichi make it one of the most photographed buildings in the district even by people who never step inside.

Inside, the exhibits move chronologically through Kanagawa Prefecture's history, from prehistoric archaeology through the samurai era and into the dramatic transformation triggered by Yokohama's 1859 port opening, giving useful context for anyone spending several days exploring the city's foreign-settlement sites. The building itself, with its grand stone stairwell and domed hall, is arguably as much the draw as the exhibits.

Bashamichi's wide, tree-lined street was Yokohama's original main shopping road for the foreign settlement, and this museum anchors its western end, making it an easy add-on to a walk that also takes in Nihon Odori and Zou no Hana Park. Weekday afternoons are noticeably quieter than weekends.

5-60 Minamigata-cho, Naka-ku, Yokohama

Open in Google Maps

About 3 minutes on foot from Minatomirai Line Bashamichi Station.
