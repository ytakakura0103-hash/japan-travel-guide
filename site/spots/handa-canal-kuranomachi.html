Handa Canal and Kuranomachi Storehouse District

Nagoya

The Handa Canal was dug during the Edo period specifically to move goods, chiefly sake, vinegar and cotton produced on the Chita Peninsula, out to the major markets of Edo and the Kamigata region around Kyoto and Osaka. That trade made Handa genuinely prosperous, and much of the wealth went into the tall, black-walled storehouses that still line the canal today, their weathered wooden siding and white-plaster trim largely unchanged since brewers used them to age and store barrels before shipment.

Walking the canal now means passing several still-operating breweries alongside the storehouses, including buildings connected to the Mizkan vinegar company and to sake producers whose names have been attached to this stretch of water for generations. In the evening, when the storehouses are lit and reflected in the still canal water, the district becomes one of the more photogenic streetscapes in the Nagoya day-trip radius, with almost none of the crowds that gather at more famous preserved townscapes elsewhere in Japan.

It sits close enough to the MIZKAN MUSEUM that the two can easily be combined into a single, walkable afternoon in Handa.
