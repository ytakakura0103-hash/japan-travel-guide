Zuiganji Temple

Sendai

Zuiganji traces its roots back centuries but owes its current form largely to Date Masamune, who had it rebuilt in the early 1600s as his family's dedicated Zen temple, sparing no expense on craftsmen and materials brought in specifically for the project. The approach path alone is worth the walk: a long avenue under towering old cedars, its rock walls pocked with small caves once used by monks for meditation and burial, giving the entrance a solemn, layered feel before the main halls even come into view.

Inside, the restored main hall shows off the same richly decorated Momoyama-style interiors found at Masamune's mausoleum, with gilded sliding doors and carved transoms, offering a sense of how the Date family expressed status through architecture across multiple sites in the region. A small museum on the grounds displays temple treasures and artifacts connected to the Date clan's patronage.

Admission is a modest, standard temple fee, and a full visit including the cedar approach takes around an hour. It's one of the more visited stops in Matsushima, so tour groups cluster here, especially around midday; going right at opening time gives a much quieter walk down the cedar avenue. The cedars overhead are cool and shaded even in mid-summer.

91 Matsushima, Aza-Chonai, Matsushima, Miyagi

A short walk from Matsushima-Kaigan Station, about 40 minutes by JR Senseki Line from Sendai Station.
