Hiroshima Peace Memorial Museum

Hiroshima

The museum sits at the southern end of Peace Memorial Park in a raised concrete building designed by Kenzo Tange, opened in 1955 and renovated over the following decades, with a major renewal of the main building completed in 2019. Its exhibits are split roughly between the East Building, which covers the historical background of the war and the decision to use the atomic bomb, and the Main Building, which focuses on the human toll through personal effects, photographs, and recorded testimony from survivors known in Japanese as hibakusha.\n\nObjects on display include items such as a child's tricycle, scorched clothing, and a stopped pocket watch, each paired with a short account of the person it belonged to. The museum deliberately keeps commentary factual and restrained, letting the objects and survivor accounts carry the weight of the material rather than framing it with strong editorial language.\n\nBecause the subject matter is heavy, a visit typically takes at least ninety minutes to two hours, and some exhibits may be difficult for young children. English-language audio guides and translated panels are available throughout, and the museum shop sells books and materials related to the bombing and postwar peace movement.

1-2 Nakajima-cho, Naka Ward, Hiroshima, Hiroshima Prefecture

Open in Google Maps

5-minute walk from Genbaku Dome-mae or Chuden-mae tram stops, at the southern end of Peace Memorial Park; about 25 minutes by tram from Hiroshima Station.
