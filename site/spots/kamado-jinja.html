Kamado Shrine

Fukuoka

Kamado Shrine sits partway up Mt. Homan, the forested peak that rises directly behind Dazaifu Tenmangu, and has long been known regionally as a shrine for love and good relationships, with a striking two-tiered approach of stone steps leading up through the trees. In recent years it has taken on a second identity among a younger, international fanbase: the shrine's name closely matches that of the lead family in the hit anime and manga series Demon Slayer, and its forested mountain setting bears enough resemblance to scenes from the story that it has become an informal pilgrimage stop for fans, alongside its long-standing role as a genuine place of worship.

Beyond the pop-culture connection, the shrine's elevated position gives sweeping views back over Dazaifu and the surrounding plain, especially rewarding after the climb up the stone steps from the lower approach. Autumn brings notable maple color along the mountain paths above the shrine, extending toward hiking trails up Mt. Homan itself for those with more time.

Because it requires either a bus transfer or a longer uphill walk from Dazaifu Tenmangu, Kamado Shrine sees far fewer visitors than the shrine below, despite its growing profile online. A local community bus runs partway up from central Dazaifu, though schedules are worth checking locally before relying on it.

883 Uchiyama, Dazaifu, Fukuoka

By local community bus from Nishitetsu Dazaifu Station or Dazaifu Tenmangu (roughly 10-15 minutes), or a longer uphill walk of 40-60 minutes.
