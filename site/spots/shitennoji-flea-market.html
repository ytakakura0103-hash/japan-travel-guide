Shitennoji Flea Market

Osaka

Held on the grounds of Shitennoji, one of Japan's oldest Buddhist temples, this flea market takes over the temple's courtyards and pathways on set days roughly twice each month, when vendors spread blankets and tarps across the ground and lay out an enormous, disorganized mix of secondhand goods: old kimono and obi, ceramics, vintage toys, coins, tools, and general curios of wildly varying age and condition. It's very much a browsing market rather than a curated antiques fair — bargains and junk sit side by side, and part of the appeal is digging through stalls rather than expecting everything to be neatly presented.

Bargaining is common and generally expected for larger purchases, though it's worth approaching it politely rather than aggressively. A thorough visit, working through the full spread of stalls across the temple grounds, can take two hours or more, especially if you stop to examine items closely or chat with vendors, many of whom have specific specialties and long histories with their stock.

The market draws a mix of serious collectors arriving early for the best selection and casual browsers who show up later in the morning; arriving close to opening is the more reliable strategy if you're after something specific. Because it operates on set market days rather than daily, it's essential to check the current schedule before planning a visit, and combining it with a look at Shitennoji's own temple buildings makes for a fuller morning.
