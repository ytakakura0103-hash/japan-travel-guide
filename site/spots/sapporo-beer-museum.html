Sapporo Beer Museum

Hokkaido

Sapporo Beer traces its roots to a government-run brewery opened in the 1870s as part of Hokkaido's broader development push, and this museum occupies one of the original brick brewery buildings. The exhibits move through the company's early years, its wartime and postwar history, and its role in shaping Sapporo's identity as a beer city, all housed inside handsome industrial-era brickwork that is worth seeing on its own terms.

The self-guided historical exhibit is free; a separate paid tasting course lets visitors sample several beers, including some brewed only for the museum, in the adjoining hall. Most visits run around an hour for the exhibit, longer if lingering over the tasting.

Tour groups tend to arrive in clusters, so visiting soon after opening or in the mid-afternoon lull tends to be calmer than late morning. The complex also houses restaurants serving Genghis Khan grilled lamb, a Hokkaido specialty, alongside beer, making it possible to turn the stop into a full lunch outing. It is a reasonably easy add-on to a day spent around the eastern edge of downtown, though it sits a bit further from the main Odori Park cluster than most central sights.
