Hiroshima National Peace Memorial Hall for the Atomic Bomb Victims

Hiroshima

Unlike the city-run Peace Memorial Museum nearby, this hall was built and is operated by the Japanese national government, opened in 2002 and designed by architect Kenzo Tange. Most of the building sits below ground level, entered from a plaza in Peace Memorial Park where a clock-shaped fountain marks 8:15, the exact time of the bombing on August 6, 1945.

The hall's central feature is the circular Hall of Remembrance, a quiet underground room whose curved wall is tiled with 140,000 small panels, the estimated number of deaths by the end of 1945, arranged to recreate a 360-degree view of the ruined city as seen from roughly the hypocenter. The effect is deliberately understated rather than dramatic, meant as a space for reflection.

Beyond the memorial hall, the facility maintains a registry of victims' names and photographs submitted by bereaved families, along with a video and testimony archive where visitors can watch recorded accounts from survivors. Admission is free, and because it draws fewer visitors than the main museum, it is often a calmer stop for those who want more time to sit with the material.

1-6 Nakajima-cho, Naka Ward, Hiroshima, Hiroshima Prefecture

Open in Google Maps

5-minute walk from Genbaku Dome-mae or Chuden-mae tram stops, within Peace Memorial Park.
