Tomonoura Port Town Streets

Hiroshima

Tomonoura sits at the tip of the Numakuma Peninsula, and for centuries it served as a waiting harbor where ships timed their departure to the tidal currents of the Inland Sea — a role that left it with an unusually complete set of Edo-period port infrastructure: stone quays, a still-standing lighthouse-style stone lantern (Joyato), boat repair docks, and a customs house, several of which are nationally designated historic sites.\n\nMiyazaki visited in 2005, stayed roughly two months, and used the town's coastline and rooftops as the visual basis for 'Ponyo on the Cliff by the Sea,' and fans now walk the same lanes looking for angles that match the film, though the town predates and outlasts that association by centuries.\n\nWalking the waterfront in the early morning, before day-trip buses arrive from Fukuyama, gives the clearest sense of Tomonoura as a living fishing port rather than a movie set — boats still head out from here, and the smell of the harbor is as much a part of the visit as the historic buildings.
