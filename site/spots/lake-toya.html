Lake Toya

Hokkaido

Lake Toya formed inside a volcanic caldera roughly 110,000 years ago, and its almost perfectly round shape, with a cluster of forested islands sitting near its center, makes it one of Hokkaido's more distinctive lake landscapes. Toyako Onsen, the hot spring town on its southern shore, has rebuilt itself more than once after eruptions from neighboring Mount Usu, most recently in 2000, and now runs regular summer fireworks displays over the water as one of its signature draws.

Beyond the lake itself, the surrounding Shikotsu-Toya National Park offers volcanic sightseeing, including the still-visibly scarred crater areas from the 2000 eruption, alongside more conventional lakeside walking paths and boat cruises to the central islands.

Because reaching the lake from Sapporo takes real commitment, a direct bus runs a few times a day and requires advance reservation, and driving takes a similar amount of time, it tends to draw overnight visitors staying at lakeside ryokan rather than the heaviest day-trip crowds; even so, summer weekends during fireworks season are noticeably busier than a quiet autumn weekday. Winter brings its own stark, still version of the lake, with steam occasionally rising off the unfrozen water against the surrounding snow.

Toyako-cho, Abuta-gun, Hokkaido
