Susukino Back Alleys and Ramen Alley

Hokkaido

Susukino's main boulevard, with its illuminated signage and larger restaurant chains, is what most visitors see first, but the actual character of the district lives in the narrow lanes branching off it, tightly packed with tiny bars seating six or eight people, single-counter ramen shops, and izakaya identifiable mostly by a hanging lantern. Ramen Yokocho, a short covered alley of competing ramen counters, is the best-known of these lanes, but similar smaller alleys run throughout the district with far less foot traffic.

Many of these bars operate as regulars-only in practice, if not in policy, so a bit of hesitation at the door is normal for first-time visitors; sticking to shops with visible English menus or picture displays generally signals a more visitor-comfortable spot. Prices in the smaller alleys tend to run lower than the main-street chains, since these businesses rely on repeat local customers rather than one-time tourist traffic.

Because most visitors stick to Susukino's lit-up main crossing for photos and then head to a known chain restaurant, wandering even one block off that main strip cuts the crowd dramatically while keeping the same neon atmosphere.

Late evening, after the dinner rush but before last call, tends to be when these alleys feel liveliest without being uncomfortably packed, and the experience holds up in any season since it's almost entirely indoor dining.
