Tenneiji Temple Three-Story Pagoda

Hiroshima

Tenneiji sits partway up the hill on the approach to Senkoji Temple, and its three-story pagoda — originally built in 1388 as a five-story structure before later rebuilding — has become the town's unofficial silhouette. It's a Nationally Designated Important Cultural Property, though the temple itself stays low-key, with no entrance fee and few staff in sight.

The pagoda's real value is as a viewpoint anchor: the gap between its roofline and the tiled houses below the hill frames the Onomichi Channel and Mukaishima's shipyard cranes in a way that photographers return to again and again, especially in the last hour before sunset when the wood tone deepens.

Because it sits directly on the standard route up to Senkoji Park and the ropeway, you'll pass it almost by accident on a temple walk rather than needing to seek it out — but it's worth pausing rather than walking straight through, since most people do exactly that.

17-29 Higashitsuchidocho, Onomichi, Hiroshima
