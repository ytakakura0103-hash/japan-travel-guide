Otaru Canal

Sapporo

Otaru grew wealthy as a herring-fishing and trading port in the early 20th century, and this canal, completed in 1923, was built to let cargo be transferred directly from ships into the stone warehouses that still line its banks. When the port's importance faded, part of the canal was slated for filling in during the 1980s, but a preservation campaign saved the section that remains today as the city's signature sightseeing stretch, now lined with cafes and shops rather than working warehouses.\n\nA walking path runs along both sides of the water, and the gas-style lamps that light it in the evening are a big part of why sunset and early evening are considered the best time to see it, when the warehouses' brick facades catch warm light and reflect in the canal surface. Rental boat rides and horse-drawn carriage tours both operate seasonally along or near the canal for visitors who want more than a walk.\n\nDaytime hours, especially midday in peak summer or during the February Snow Light Path festival, bring heavy crowds along the main photo spots near the canal's widest point; walking a bit further along the water away from that central section usually thins the crowd considerably. Winter turns the canal into the centerpiece of the Otaru Snow Light Path festival, when hand-lit snow lanterns line the water; summer evenings are calmer but still atmospheric with the lamps lit.
