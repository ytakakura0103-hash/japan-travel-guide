Nittai-ji Temple

Nagoya

Nittai-ji exists because of a diplomatic gift. In the early 1900s a Japanese delegation traveled to Bangkok and received a portion of the Buddha's ashes from King Chulalongkorn, along with an ancient gold-bronze Buddha statue that was itself a Thai national treasure. To house them, the temple's founders built something unusual for Japan: a temple that belongs to no single Buddhist sect. Every few years a different one of the country's nineteen major schools sends its own chief priest to serve here, an arrangement that has held since 1904.

The name itself records the deal: 'Nittai' combines the characters for Japan and Thailand, while 'Kakuo' means 'Enlightenment King,' a reference to the Buddha at the center of the arrangement. The main hall and grounds are modest rather than showy, which suits the neighborhood that grew up around its approach road, but the temple's split allegiance and Thai-gifted relics make it genuinely unlike anywhere else in the city.

Most visitors combine a stop here with a wander through the Kakuozan district that spread out from its front gate over the last century, since the temple itself takes only twenty or thirty minutes to see properly.

1-1 Hoo-cho, Chikusa-ku, Nagoya, Aichi 464-0057
