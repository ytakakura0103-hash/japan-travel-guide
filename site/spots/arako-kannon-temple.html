Arako Kannon Temple

Nagoya

Arako Kannon, formally Chorin-ji, was founded in the 8th century and grew over the centuries into one of the four major Kannon temples of the Owari region. Its most important structure, a two-story Tahoto pagoda built in 1536, survived the fires and wars that destroyed most of Nagoya's other early wooden buildings, and it now carries designation as a National Important Cultural Property as the oldest wooden structure remaining anywhere in the city. The warlord Maeda Toshiie personally funded repairs to the temple in 1576, a detail that hints at how significant the site once was in the region's power politics; the temple's own main hall was less fortunate, burning down in 1994 and being rebuilt a few years later.

The temple's other claim to fame is its collection of carvings by Enku, a 17th-century itinerant monk who traveled Japan making thousands of deliberately rough, expressive wooden Buddha images. Roughly 1,240 of them ended up here, left to the temple's tenth abbot after Enku stayed at Arako Kannon in 1676, making this one of the largest single collections of his work anywhere in Japan.

The temple sits a short walk from the Endoji shopping street, making the two an easy, low-key pairing on the west side of the station.

138 Miyamado, Arako-cho, Nakagawa-ku, Nagoya, Aichi 454-0861
