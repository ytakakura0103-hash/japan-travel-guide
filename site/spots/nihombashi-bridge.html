Nihombashi Bridge

Tokyo

Nihombashi has served as the symbolic starting point for Japan's road network since the Edo period, when distances to every province were measured from this spot; a bronze marker set into the roadway still marks the zero-kilometer point used for official highway distances today. The current bridge dates from 1911 and is decorated with bronze dragon and kirin (a mythical hybrid creature) statues at its corners, along with other detailed metalwork that reflects its status as a formal, ceremonial crossing rather than an ordinary city bridge.

For decades, the bridge's setting has been compromised by an elevated expressway built directly overhead in the 1960s ahead of the Tokyo Olympics, which blocks the sky above the bridge and makes it easy to walk past without noticing the historic structure below. A long-planned project to remove this section of expressway and route it underground has been underway for some years, aiming to restore an open view of the bridge, though the work is large in scale and is expected to continue for a considerable time yet, so visitors today should still expect the overhead structure to be present.

A visit to the bridge itself takes only a few minutes, and it's best treated as a short historical stop within a longer walk through the surrounding Nihonbashi district, which has its own mix of long-established department stores and newer redevelopment. There's no particular time of day or season that changes the experience much here.
