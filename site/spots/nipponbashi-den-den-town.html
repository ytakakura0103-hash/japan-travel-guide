Nipponbashi (Den Den Town)

Osaka

Nipponbashi built its reputation in the postwar decades as an electronics wholesale district, and that history is still visible in the older shopfronts along Sakaisuji, but from the 1990s onward the neighborhood gradually reinvented itself around anime, manga, and gaming, earning it the nickname Den Den Town (a play on denki, electronics, and the sound of a hand-drum). Today it functions as Osaka's most direct equivalent to Tokyo's Akihabara: multi-floor stores dedicated to figures, trading cards, retro consoles, and doujinshi sit alongside maid cafes and cosplay shops along the pedestrian street known as Otoroad.

What sets Nipponbashi apart from a simple shopping trip is what happens after the electronics stores close. The district has built up a small but distinct late-night scene of concept cafes and themed bars catering to the same otaku culture that drives its daytime business, everything from classic maid cafes to newer, more atmospheric spots built around specific themes, alongside a handful of clubs and bars that lean into the neighborhood's pop-culture identity rather than competing with the mainstream nightlife of Shinsaibashi or Namba.

Street cosplay gatherings are common on weekends, particularly around Otoroad, making it a good spot for photography as well as shopping, though as with any crowd of hobbyists, it's polite to ask before photographing anyone directly. Nipponbashi rewards slow browsing more than a single must-see stop; plan for at least an afternoon if collectible shops or retro games are of interest, and consider staying into the evening to see the district's after-dark side.
