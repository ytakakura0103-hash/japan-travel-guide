Kosanji Temple

Hiroshima

Kosanji was commissioned in 1936 by a businessman who, after becoming a Buddhist priest following his mother's death, spent over three decades building this temple as a personal memorial to her. Rather than a single unified design, the complex is a deliberate collage of replicas modeled on celebrated structures elsewhere in Japan, including gates and halls echoing Nikko's Toshogu Shrine and Kyoto temples, all packed onto one hillside site on Ikuchijima.\n\nBehind the temple buildings, a marble sculpture garden called Miraishin no Oka climbs the hillside in stark white stone, a striking contrast to the ornate wooden halls below and a favorite spot for photos looking back over the Inland Sea.\n\nBecause it's privately built rather than historically ancient, Kosanji sometimes gets dismissed by visitors expecting a centuries-old temple, but treated on its own terms — as one wealthy son's decades-long tribute — it's one of the more unusual religious sites in the region and worth the stop for anyone cycling or driving through Setoda.

553-2 Setodacho Setoda, Onomichi, Hiroshima

Open in Google Maps

About a 5-minute walk from Setoda Port on Ikuchijima; roughly 40–50 minutes by car from central Onomichi via the Shimanami Kaido expressway.
