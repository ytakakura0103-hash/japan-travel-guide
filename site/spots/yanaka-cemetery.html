Yanaka Cemetery

Tokyo

Yanaka sits within one of the few pockets of Tokyo that survived both the 1923 Great Kanto earthquake and the bombing of World War II largely intact, and the neighborhood's cemetery reflects that unusual continuity — narrow lanes, old stone lanterns, and family graves dating back generations sit beneath a canopy of trees that feels closer to a quiet park than a typical cemetery. A wide central avenue lined with cherry trees runs through the grounds and blossoms every spring, drawing local photographers and residents out for a walk beneath the branches, generally with far less crowding than Tokyo's more famous hanami spots.

Because it remains an active cemetery, visitors are expected to be respectful and keep to the paths rather than treating it purely as a sightseeing stop, but walking through is entirely permitted and a long-standing part of how locals experience the neighborhood. A slow walk through the grounds takes thirty to forty-five minutes, and it pairs naturally with the wider Yanaka area, a well-preserved old shopping street and residential district known for small crafts shops, cafes, and a noticeably slower pace than most of central Tokyo.

There is no admission fee. Weekday mornings are the quietest time to walk the cemetery, and cherry blossom season aside, Yanaka overall is rarely crowded even on weekends, making it a good choice for visitors seeking an unhurried, historic corner of the city.
