Mouriya

Kobe

Mouriya traces its beginnings to a butcher shop founded in the Meiji period, when Kobe was first becoming known internationally for high-quality local beef, and it has grown into one of the city's more established names for a formal Kobe beef teppanyaki meal. Unlike the faster-turnover, more casual steak counters found near the station, Mouriya leans into a slower, more refined dining experience, with the chef preparing each course table-side and explaining the cut and grade of the beef being served.

Course menus generally bundle the steak with a series of smaller dishes, from an appetizer through soup, salad, and dessert, and a full meal typically takes well over an hour given the pacing of a teppanyaki course rather than a quick set lunch. Pricing sits meaningfully higher than casual steak counters, reflecting both the beef grade typically used and the more elaborate service.

Reservations are strongly advisable, particularly for dinner and especially at the main locations in the city center, since walk-in availability is far less reliable than at more casual Kobe beef spots. Lunch courses, where offered, tend to be a somewhat more attainable way to experience the restaurant's quality without committing to the full dinner price point.
