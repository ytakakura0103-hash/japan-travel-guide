Local Pick

Tatsuno Old Town

Kobe

Tatsuno developed as a minor castle town and, more significantly, as the historical center of usukuchi shoyu, a lighter-colored, saltier soy sauce style widely used in Kansai-region cooking to season food without darkening its color, a defining characteristic of dishes like Kyoto-style kaiseki broths. Several breweries here have operated for generations, and a small museum in a converted brewery building explains the production process and the town's role in shaping Kansai cuisine's flavor profile.

The old town's streets retain a scattering of white-walled merchant houses and warehouse buildings from the Edo and Meiji periods, largely untouched by the kind of redevelopment that's reshaped busier historic districts elsewhere in the region. It's a genuinely quiet town to walk through, with more resident bicycles than tour groups, and a handful of small shops selling soy sauce, soy-sauce-flavored sweets and local sake.

Because it requires a further local train transfer beyond Himeji, most visitors to the region skip it entirely in favor of the castle, which keeps Tatsuno feeling unhurried and authentically local. It works best as an extension of a Himeji day trip rather than a stand-alone destination given the additional travel time involved.

Tatsuno, Hyogo Prefecture

From Himeji Station (reachable from Sannomiya or Kobe Station via JR Special Rapid, about 40-50 minutes), transfer to a local JR line or bus toward Tatsuno (a further 20-30 minutes).
