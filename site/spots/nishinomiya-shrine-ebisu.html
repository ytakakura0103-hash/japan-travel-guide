Nishinomiya Shrine

Kobe

Nishinomiya Shrine sits at the top of the nationwide network of shrines dedicated to Ebisu, the god of fishermen and prosperity in business, and its grounds include an Important Cultural Property-designated main gate along with a substantial wooded precinct that feels notably calm outside of festival periods. The shrine's origins connect to old maritime trade and fishing communities along this stretch of coast, tying it into the broader Hanshin region's port history.

Its most famous event, the Toka Ebisu festival each January, includes the Fukuotoko (Lucky Man) race, in which competitors sprint roughly 230 meters from the shrine's main gate to the inner shrine the moment it opens before dawn, with the first three finishers awarded the title of that year's lucky men and broadcast on national news. Outside of this single dramatic morning, the shrine is a peaceful, unhurried place to visit.

Because most visitors come specifically for the January festival, the rest of the year sees mostly local worshippers and the occasional wedding, making it an easy, quiet stop if your interest is architecture and atmosphere rather than the spectacle of the race itself.
