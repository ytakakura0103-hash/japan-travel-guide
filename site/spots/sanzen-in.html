Sanzen-in

Kyoto

Set in the rural Ohara valley well north of central Kyoto, Sanzen-in feels more like a countryside retreat than a city temple, and the slower pace of getting there is part of what makes it worth the trip. The Yusei-en garden of moss and pine, laid out in front of the Ojo Gokurakuin hall, is one of the most photographed moss gardens in the region, especially once maple leaves begin falling across it in November. Scattered through the moss beyond the main garden are small stone jizo statues, weathered and softened by lichen, each carved with a slightly different expression; locals often describe them as smiling, and finding them tucked among the greenery has become part of the appeal for repeat visitors. A visit to the temple grounds, including both main gardens, generally takes an hour or so at a relaxed pace. Because Ohara sits a bus ride outside the city center, Sanzen-in tends to draw fewer crowds than comparably famous temples closer to downtown, though the difference narrows considerably during peak autumn color, when tour buses make the trip specifically for the maples. Visiting on a weekday or arriving close to opening time helps avoid the worst of the seasonal crush. Spring brings fresh moss and early greenery rather than flowers, making Sanzen-in a year-round destination rather than one tied to a single narrow window.

540 Ohararaikoincho, Sakyo Ward, Kyoto
