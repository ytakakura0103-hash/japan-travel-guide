Tsuruma Park

Nagoya

Opened in the early 1900s, Tsuruma predates most of Nagoya's other major parks and retains some of that early-twentieth-century design sensibility, particularly in its formal rose garden and an ornate Western-style greenhouse that stands out from the more naturalistic landscaping found in the city's Japanese-style gardens. The park's pond and open lawns make it a popular but rarely overwhelming spot for a walk.

Entry is free, and the greenhouse charges a small admission; a full loop of the park with a greenhouse stop takes an hour or so.

The rose garden's bloom in late spring, and again in a smaller autumn flush, draws a genuinely large local crowd for a citywide rose festival, so visiting slightly before or after peak bloom offers a calmer experience with the roses still largely in flower. Cherry blossoms along the park's paths in early spring are a quieter, less-hyped alternative to the city's more famous hanami spots.
