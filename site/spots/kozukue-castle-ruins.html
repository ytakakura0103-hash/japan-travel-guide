Kozukue Castle Ruins Forest

Yokohama

Kozukue Castle was a strategic hilltop stronghold during the turbulent Sengoku period, associated with the Late Hojo clan's network of fortifications guarding the approaches to Odawara, though like most castles of its era it was built primarily of earth and timber rather than the stone walls associated with later castles, and no reconstructed keep exists here today. What remains are the earthworks themselves, ridges, ditches, and flattened terrace platforms cut into the hillside, now preserved as a municipal forest rather than restored into a tourist-facing historical park.

Walking trails loop through the wooded site, and with a little imagination, and the occasional signboard, you can trace the old moats and defensive terracing that protected the hilltop centuries ago. It is a genuinely quiet, shaded walk, more a nature outing with historical texture than a formal heritage attraction, and it lacks any ticket booth, gift shop, or paved visitor center.

Once a year, a local reenactment festival brings the site briefly to life with armor-clad participants recreating scenes from the castle's era, though outside that event the ruins see mostly dog walkers and history hobbyists rather than tourists. This is a spot for visitors who enjoy piecing together history from the landscape itself.

2957 Kozukue-cho, Kohoku-ku, Yokohama

Open in Google Maps

About 10 minutes on foot from JR Yokohama Line Kozukue Station.
