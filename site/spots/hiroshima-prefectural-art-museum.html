Hiroshima Prefectural Art Museum

Hiroshima

Opened in 1968 and rebuilt in its current form in 1988, the Hiroshima Prefectural Art Museum sits directly beside Shukkeien Garden and shares something of its calmer, less crowded pace compared with the museums closer to Peace Memorial Park. The permanent collection centers on modern and contemporary Japanese painting and crafts, including Nihonga works and ceramics connected to the region, alongside a notable holding of Salvador Dali bronzes and prints.

The museum also maintains works related to Hiroshima's postwar recovery and regional artists, and it regularly hosts touring exhibitions from other Japanese and international institutions, so the specific galleries on view can vary considerably between visits. Large windows on the upper floor look out over the garden, and some visitors treat the two sites as a single combined stop given their shared entrance area.

Because it draws a more local audience than the Peace Park museums, it tends to be a quieter option, and photography rules vary by exhibition, so it is worth checking current signage before assuming photos are permitted.

2-22 Kaminoborocho, Naka Ward, Hiroshima, Hiroshima Prefecture

Open in Google Maps

5-minute walk from Shukkeien-mae bus stop, adjacent to Shukkeien Garden, about 15 minutes on foot from Hiroshima Station.
