Nagoya Castle

Nagoya

Nagoya Castle was raised in the early 1600s as a strategic Tokugawa stronghold guarding the road between Kyoto and Edo. Allied bombing destroyed the wooden keep in 1945; what stands today is a concrete rebuild, though the surviving stone walls, turrets, and moats date to the original construction. Far more compelling for many visitors is the Honmaru Palace, reconstructed over about a decade (2009-2018) using traditional carpentry, joinery, and gold-leaf techniques, with sliding-door paintings recreated by hand.

A combined ticket covers the keep and palace, and a couple of hours is enough to see the highlights, though history enthusiasts can happily spend longer. The pair of golden shachihoko roof ornaments, symbols of the city, photograph best from the plaza in late-afternoon light.

Weekend mornings bring long queues at the palace entrance; arriving right at opening or visiting on a weekday afternoon eases the crush considerably. The quieter Ninomaru Garden nearby is a good spot to decompress, and in spring its plum and cherry trees draw a mostly local crowd without the intensity of Kyoto's famous hanami spots.
