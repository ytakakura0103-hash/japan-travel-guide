Miyoshi Mononoke Museum

Hiroshima

Formally the Yumoto Koichi Memorial Japan Yokai Museum, this collection opened in 2019 around the lifelong archive of Yumoto Koichi, a leading scholar of yokai — the ghosts, monsters, and spirits of Japanese folklore. The holdings run to more than 5,000 objects, spanning Edo-period woodblock prints, including Toriyama Sekien's influential 18th-century bestiary of supernatural creatures, through to modern illustrated and sculptural interpretations.

Miyoshi was chosen as the museum's home specifically because of its own local legend, the Inou Mononoke Roku, an Edo-period account of a young samurai's month-long haunting by a parade of yokai, which has been retold in scrolls, picture books, and manga for centuries and is treated in the museum as a foundational regional ghost story.

The museum sits well off the main Hiroshima tourist circuit in the inland city of Miyoshi, making it a deliberate detour rather than a casual add-on, but for anyone interested in Japanese folklore or the visual history behind modern yokai-influenced pop culture, it's a far deeper collection than the small city's size would suggest.

122-16 Kaminogo-cho, Miyoshi, Hiroshima
