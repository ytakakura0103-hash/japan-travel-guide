Gin-no-yu Public Bathhouse, Arima Onsen

Kobe

Where Kin-no-yu's water is iron-rich and famously amber, Gin-no-yu draws on a separate spring that's colorless and lightly carbonated, giving it a noticeably different feel on the skin, slightly effervescent rather than mineral-heavy. The two bathhouses are often visited as a pair, and a combination ticket covering both is typically available if you want to compare them in the same trip.\n\nGin-no-yu sits a little further up Arima's sloped streets, which seems to be enough to keep it quieter than its counterpart even during busy weekends. It's a modest, no-frills facility built for locals and day-trippers rather than a spa resort experience, in keeping with Arima's identity as a bathing town rather than a single luxury complex.\n\nBecause both baths are compact, they can get crowded during midday tour bus arrivals; an early morning or later afternoon visit generally means shorter waits. The surrounding lanes are worth exploring on foot regardless, lined with old wooden inns, small sweet shops selling Arima's traditional charcoal-black cider and hot spring manju buns, and carbonated-spring bottling shops selling the source water itself.

From Sannomiya, take the subway to Tanigami then the Shintetsu line to Arima-Onsen Station; Gin-no-yu is a short uphill walk from the station, past Kin-no-yu.
