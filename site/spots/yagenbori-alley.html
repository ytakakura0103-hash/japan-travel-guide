Local Pick

Yagenbori

Hiroshima

Yagenbori sits at the edge of the larger Nagarekawa entertainment district, but its character is noticeably more low-key and local. Rather than large izakaya chains, the area is defined by a dense cluster of tiny, independently owned bars, many seating only a handful of people, often run by a single owner-bartender who has been in the same spot for years.

The name comes from an old drainage canal, or hori, that once ran through the area, though the waterway itself is long gone, and the alleys today are lined with narrow buildings stacked with small bars across multiple floors, identified mostly by hand-lettered signs rather than flashy storefronts. Because many of these bars are effectively neighborhood living rooms for regular customers, some operate on a members-only or introduction basis, though a good number of standing bars and casual counters remain open to walk-in visitors.

For visitors comfortable navigating a slightly less English-friendly environment, Yagenbori offers a more intimate, unpolished counterpoint to the louder chain izakaya elsewhere in central Hiroshima, and it rewards wandering rather than heading in with a specific destination in mind.

Together with Nagarekawa, Yagenbori forms one continuous downtown entertainment zone made up of four named streets: Nagarekawa-dori, Yagenbori-dori, Butsudan-dori, and Ebisu-dori. Yagenbori's own stretch tends to run calmer and more subdued than Nagarekawa proper, with a concentration of stylish cafes, bars, and Japanese-style izakaya reflecting its history as an old pleasure quarter, a useful alternative for visitors who want a quieter evening without leaving the district entirely.

Yagenbori, Naka Ward, Hiroshima, Hiroshima Prefecture

5-minute walk from Ebisucho tram stop, just south of the main Nagarekawa strip.
