Kyoto Aquarium

Kyoto

Kyoto Aquarium, opened in 2012, was Japan's first large-scale aquarium built inland rather than on a coastline, using treated tap water for its saltwater tanks rather than direct seawater access, a technical detail that shaped much of its design. Its best-known exhibit centers on Japanese giant salamanders, an endangered species native to the rivers of the Kyoto region and among the largest amphibians in the world, displayed in a tank designed to recreate the rocky mountain stream habitat where they're found locally.

Beyond the salamanders, the aquarium covers a fairly typical range of exhibits for its size, including a dolphin show, penguins, and a large tank of local Kyoto river fish alongside more conventional marine displays, making it a comfortable stop for families with children as much as anyone with a specific interest in the giant salamanders. A full visit generally takes around two hours, including a dolphin show if timed to a showing. Admission is charged per adult and child.

The aquarium sits within Umekoji Park, a short walk from Kyoto Station, and pairs naturally with the nearby Kyoto Railway Museum for a family-oriented day away from the city's temple circuit. It's busiest on weekends and school holidays, particularly around show times; weekday visits, or arriving shortly after opening, are noticeably calmer.
