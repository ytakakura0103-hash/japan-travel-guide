Arimatsu-Narumi Shibori Kaikan

Nagoya

Opened in 1984 to help pass Arimatsu's shibori tie-dyeing tradition on to future generations, this museum splits its historic building into a straightforward ground-floor shop selling finished shibori textiles and a more hands-on second floor dedicated to the craft itself. There, working artisans demonstrate kukuri, the painstaking hand-tying process that creates shibori's distinctive patterns, in open view of visitors for most of the day, pausing only for a lunch break.

A short film gives an overview of the full production process, from carving the pattern and transferring it to cloth, through tying, dyeing and finally removing the stitches, a sequence that can take a single piece of cloth weeks to complete depending on its complexity. For visitors who want to go further than watching, the museum offers a reservation-based hands-on experience where guests tie their own piece of fabric, which is then dyed and shipped to them afterward.

Because it sits directly on the old Tokaido townscape, the museum is easy to combine with a walk through the rest of historic Arimatsu.

3008 Arimatsu, Midori-ku, Nagoya, Aichi
