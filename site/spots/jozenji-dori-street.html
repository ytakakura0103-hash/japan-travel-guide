Jozenji-dori Street

Sendai

Jozenji-dori cuts through the middle of downtown Sendai as a wide, tree-canopied median, planted with rows of mature zelkova trees that arch overhead and make the street feel more like a park than a thoroughfare. It anchors the city's self-image as a green, livable place in the middle of Tohoku's biggest urban center, and locals use it constantly for lunchtime walks, weekend markets, and simply sitting.

The street comes alive for events: the Sendai Aoba Festival in May fills it with parade floats and traditional dance, and the Jozenji Street Jazz Festival in September turns it into a sprawling, free outdoor concert with dozens of stages. Independent cafes and small galleries line the side streets running off it, making it a good base for wandering without a fixed itinerary.

There's no cost to simply walk the street, and it takes about fifteen minutes end to end at a stroll. Weekday mornings are calm and good for photos of the tree canopy with soft light; weekend afternoons, especially during festivals, are the busiest. The trees are green and shady in summer, but the pale gold of the leaves in November is arguably the best-looking season, just before the boulevard switches over to its famous winter light displays.
