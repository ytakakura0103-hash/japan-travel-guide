Hiroshima Flower Festival

Hiroshima

The Hiroshima Flower Festival began in 1977, launched partly as a way to give the city a positive, forward-looking public event during the Golden Week holiday period, following decades of postwar recovery. It is held annually across three days in early May along Heiwa Odori, or Peace Boulevard, the wide central avenue that runs near Peace Memorial Park, and regularly draws very large crowds, making it one of the biggest annual events in the city.

Despite the name, the festival is less a garden show and more a citywide street festival, featuring parades with floats and marching bands, stage performances of traditional and contemporary music and dance, food stalls lining the boulevard, and flower displays and decorations woven throughout the route. Local schools, community groups, and businesses all take part, giving it a strong grassroots, city-wide character rather than a purely tourist-facing event.

Because it falls during Golden Week, one of Japan's busiest domestic travel periods, accommodation in central Hiroshima can book up well in advance for festival dates, and the crowds along Peace Boulevard can be substantial, so visitors planning around it should expect a lively, dense atmosphere rather than a quiet stroll.

Heiwa Odori (Peace Boulevard), Naka Ward, Hiroshima, Hiroshima Prefecture
