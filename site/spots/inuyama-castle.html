Inuyama Castle

Nagoya

Most of Japan's famous castles, Nagoya's included, are postwar concrete reconstructions, which is what makes Inuyama notable: its keep is one of only a few in the country recognized as an original structure dating back centuries, built from real timber with steep, narrow stairs that make the climb to the top floor an experience in itself. The view from the top, overlooking the Kiso River and the town below, is the reward for the effort.

Admission is modest, and the keep itself takes under an hour to climb through, though the surrounding castle town, with its own older streets and snack shops, easily extends a visit to half a day. It works well as a day trip paired with nearby Meitetsu World or riverside scenery.

Weekday visits let you take the steep interior stairs at your own pace without a queue forming behind you, which matters more here than at most castles given how narrow the staircases are. Cherry blossoms along the riverside park below the castle draw a seasonal crowd each spring.

65-2 Kitakoken, Inuyama, Aichi
