Arimatsu Historic Townscape

Nagoya

Arimatsu was established in 1608 under an official decree from the Owari Tokugawa domain, positioned along the Tokaido, the great highway linking Edo and Kyoto, specifically to serve travelers passing through. According to local tradition, workers brought in from Bungo province to help build Nagoya Castle carried with them a tie-dyeing technique that a local merchant recognized as commercially promising, and by the 1630s Arimatsu's shibori cloth trade was firmly established, sold to travelers as a specialty souvenir of the road.

That trade made the town genuinely prosperous, and the merchant houses built with the proceeds, many dating from the late Edo period through the early Showa era, still line roughly 800 meters of the old highway today. A number of these buildings carry official Tangible Cultural Heritage designation, and the townscape as a whole gives one of the clearer pictures of what a Tokaido post town actually looked like, without the heavy restoration and souvenir-shop conversion seen at some more famous historic streets.

More than a hundred distinct shibori tying techniques are still practiced by craftspeople connected to the town, several of which are demonstrated at the nearby Shibori Kaikan museum.
