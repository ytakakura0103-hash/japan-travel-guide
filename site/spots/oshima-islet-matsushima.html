Local Pick

Oshima Islet

Sendai

Long before Matsushima became a sightseeing destination, Oshima served as a training ground for Buddhist ascetics, and the small islet is still pocked with rock-cut caves and worn stone inscriptions left behind from that earlier, more austere use. It sits close to Zuiganji and Godaido geographically, connected to the mainland by its own short bridge, yet somehow ends up skipped by the great majority of day-trippers who funnel straight from the temple to the main pier.

The result is one of the quietest, most atmospheric corners of Matsushima: uneven rock paths, weathered carvings, and views across the bay without another visitor in sight, even during periods when the main waterfront is packed with tour groups. It rewards visitors who enjoy poking around ruins and don't need a polished, signposted experience to feel like a stop was worthwhile.

There's no admission fee, and a visit takes twenty to thirty minutes, though the uneven, sometimes damp rock surfaces call for sturdy footwear rather than sandals. Because it's overlooked by design rather than actually hard to reach, simply knowing to detour here after Zuiganji is often all it takes to have the islet entirely to yourself.
