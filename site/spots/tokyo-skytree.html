Tokyo Skytree

Tokyo

At 634 meters, Tokyo Skytree is among the tallest towers in the world, built primarily as a broadcasting tower but designed from the outset with two public observation levels. The lower deck, Tembo Deck, sits around 350 meters up and has floor-to-ceiling windows and a small cafe; the upper level, Tembo Galleria, is a spiraling glass corridor near the very top that requires a separate ticket on top of the first. On clear days, especially in winter, the view stretches across the whole Kanto plain, with Mount Fuji visible on the horizon.

Tickets are sold for timed entry, and pre-booking online is generally worthwhile, particularly on weekends and holidays when same-day lines can be long. Weekday mornings tend to be the quietest time to go up, and sunset is popular for the transition from daylight to the city's night lights, though that popularity means it draws bigger crowds too. The tower's exterior lighting changes nightly and for special occasions, making it a recognizable part of the Tokyo skyline even without going up.

At the tower's base, the Tokyo Solamachi shopping and dining complex has several floors of shops, an aquarium, and a planetarium, enough to fill extra time before or after the observation decks. Budget an hour or two for the decks alone, more if the complex at the base is part of the plan.
