Kodomo no Kuni (Children's Land)

Yokohama

Opened in 1965 to commemorate the wedding of then-Crown Prince Akihito, this expansive park spans former military land repurposed into rolling pastures, cycling paths, adventure playgrounds, and a small working farm area where kids can interact with sheep and other animals. Its scale sets it apart from typical urban playgrounds, there is enough open space here that you genuinely need to plan how much ground to cover in a day.

Getting there is part of the experience: a dedicated short rail line, the Kodomo-no-Kuni Line, connects from Nagatsuta Station specifically to serve the park, a rare example in Japan of a train line built almost entirely for one destination. Seasonal flower displays, including a notable plum grove in late winter, add a changing backdrop across the year beyond just the playground facilities.

Because it sits toward the northern edge of the city near the Machida border, well outside the standard Minato Mirai and Chinatown circuit, it draws overwhelmingly local families rather than international visitors, giving it a genuinely low-key, unhurried atmosphere even on busy weekends. It suits travelers with children looking for a full outdoor day away from typical sightseeing rather than a quick photo stop.

700 Onodai, Midori-ku, Yokohama

Open in Google Maps

About 3 minutes on foot from the Kodomo-no-Kuni Line terminus, reached via Tokyu Denentoshi Line Nagatsuta Station.
