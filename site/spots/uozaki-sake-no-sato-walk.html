Uozaki Sake Brewery Walk

Kobe

The stretch of coastline between Sumiyoshi and Uozaki forms the historic heart of the Nada sake region, and walking it on foot reveals a texture that a single museum stop can't: black board-and-batten brewery walls running along narrow canals once used to float rice and finished sake to the port, small neighborhood shrines tucked between buildings, and retail shop-fronts selling bottles direct from breweries that don't otherwise see many foreign visitors.

There's no single official route, which is part of the appeal, wandering the backstreets between the JR line and the coast turns up brewery gates, retired kura buildings repurposed as shops or galleries, and the occasional vending machine selling sake by the cup. A few of the breweries along the way keep small tasting counters open to walk-ins.

This is a genuinely local pocket of Kobe with minimal English signage and no tourist infrastructure built around it, so it rewards visitors comfortable navigating with a map app rather than following posted directions. Spring brings cherry blossoms along some of the canal paths, and the smell of steaming rice occasionally drifts from active breweries during brewing season in the cooler months.

Uozaki, Higashinada Ward, Kobe

Open in Google Maps

From Sannomiya, take the JR Kobe Line to Sumiyoshi or Uozaki Station (about 10-15 minutes); the brewery streets run between the station and the coast.
