Local Pick

Daimon Yokocho

Hokkaido

Daimon Yokocho packs roughly thirty tiny bars and izakaya into a single lantern-lit alley a short walk from Hakodate Station, each shop typically holding no more than eight to ten seats around a counter. That intimate scale is the whole point: unlike the Bay Area's more spread-out sightseeing route, Daimon Yokocho concentrates a Showa-era, working-class drinking atmosphere into a space small enough to see in full over the course of a single evening, popping from one counter to the next.

Among its shops, Suginoko stands out as one of Hakodate's oldest surviving bars, open since 1958, a rare survivor in a category of small independent bars that typically doesn't last this long. The alley's mix of seafood-forward izakaya, yakitori stalls, and simple drinking counters reflects Hakodate's identity as a port city, and menus lean heavily on the fresh seafood the region is known for.

Its location right by the station makes it one of the most convenient nightlife stops in Hakodate, whether as a first evening activity after arriving by train or a last stop before an early departure. Given the small size of most shops here, arriving without a large group and being willing to squeeze into whatever counter has space makes for the smoothest experience.
