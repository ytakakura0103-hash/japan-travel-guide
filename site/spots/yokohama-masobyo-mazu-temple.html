Local Pick

Yokohama Mazu Miao (Masobyo)

Yokohama

Compared to Kanteibyo's centuries of rebuilding, this temple is a relatively recent addition, constructed in the mid-2000s to give Yokohama's Chinese community a dedicated shrine to Mazu, the goddess of the sea widely worshipped along the Chinese and Taiwanese coasts and a natural fit for a temple in a historic port city. The result is a building whose paintwork and carvings are notably crisper and less weathered than the older temples nearby, since it has not yet had to be rebuilt after disaster.

Inside, the ornate ceiling painted with dragons is a highlight, along with side altars to other deities associated with protection and good fortune. Because it sits a short walk off Chinatown's busiest restaurant streets, it draws a fraction of the visitors that pass through Kanteibyo, making it one of the easier places in the district to actually stand and look without a crowd behind you.

It is a natural add-on to a Kanteibyo visit rather than a separate destination, taking only a few extra minutes to reach, and its relative quiet makes it a good stop for anyone who wants a slower, less touristy pause in the middle of Chinatown's otherwise fast-paced main streets.
