Yamato Museum (Kure Maritime Museum)

Hiroshima

Kure has been one of Japan's major naval shipbuilding centers since the late 19th century, when it was chosen as the site of an Imperial Japanese Navy arsenal, and that industrial history is the museum's central subject. Opened in 2005, the Yamato Museum takes its popular name from its most prominent exhibit, a roughly 26.3-meter-long, one-tenth scale model of the battleship Yamato, which was built and launched from Kure in 1940 and remains one of the largest battleships ever constructed.

Beyond the Yamato model, the museum's galleries cover the broader history of Kure as a shipbuilding city, from its naval arsenal origins through wartime shipbuilding and the city's postwar shift toward commercial shipbuilding and industry, using ship models, salvaged artifacts, and technical displays. Exhibits also address the human and civilian side of the city's wartime experience, including Kure's own bombing during the war, alongside the more technical shipbuilding content.

The museum sits directly across from the JMSDF Kure Museum, home to the decommissioned submarine Akishio, and the two are commonly visited together given their shared focus on Kure's naval and maritime history and their close proximity to each other near the harbor.

5-20 Takaramachi, Kure, Hiroshima Prefecture
