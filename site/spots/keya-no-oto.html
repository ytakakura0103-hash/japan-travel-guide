Keya-no-Oto Sea Cave

Fukuoka

At the tip of the Itoshima peninsula, wave action has carved a large sea cave into a headland of hexagonal basalt columns, formed by the same kind of volcanic cooling process seen at sites like Northern Ireland's Giant's Causeway on a different scale. Designated a national natural monument, the cave and surrounding cliffs are the highlight of a short coastal trail that also passes small shrines and lookout points over the Genkai Sea.

Most visitors see the cave from a lookout point above rather than entering it directly, since access to the cave mouth itself depends on tide and sea conditions. During busier seasons, small sightseeing boats sometimes run from the nearby harbor, offering a closer view of the cave from the water, though schedules can be irregular and are worth confirming locally rather than assuming availability.

The walk from the parking area to the main viewpoint takes only fifteen to twenty minutes each way over a mostly clear path, making this an accessible stop even without serious hiking gear. It gets a fraction of the visitors that Sakurai Futamigaura sees down the coast, so even on a weekend the cliffs rarely feel busy. Sturdy shoes are worth it, since parts of the path are uneven rock.

Shima, Itoshima, Fukuoka
