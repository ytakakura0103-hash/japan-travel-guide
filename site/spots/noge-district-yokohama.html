Local Pick

Noge

Yokohama

Noge developed a reputation as a black-market and entertainment district in the years right after the Second World War, when land seizures reshaped much of the surrounding area but left this pocket largely alone, and something of that improvised, unpolished character has persisted even as the neighborhood has modernized around the edges. Narrow alleys hold hundreds of tiny bars and eateries, many seating only a handful of people at a counter, catering to regulars as much as newcomers.

It sits a short walk from Sakuragicho Station, close enough to Minato Mirai's glass towers that the contrast between the two areas is part of the appeal, a reminder that not all of central Yokohama has been rebuilt to a single polished standard. Evenings after work hours are when the district really comes alive, with many of the smallest bars only opening once the sun goes down.

Because many establishments are tiny, standing-room, or cash-only, it helps to have a rough plan or a guided bar-hopping route rather than wandering in cold, particularly for visitors without much Japanese. Weeknights tend to have a more local crowd than weekends, when younger visitors and a handful of newer, trendier bars draw a livelier scene.

Noge-cho, Nishi-ku, Yokohama
