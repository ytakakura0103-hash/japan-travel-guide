Uji Bridge & Murasaki Shikibu Statue

Kyoto

The bridge crossing the Uji River has been rebuilt many times since its first recorded construction in the 7th century, but it is still counted among Japan's three oldest bridges by tradition, and its location has held real significance for well over a thousand years. It appears in the final chapters of The Tale of Genji, the sprawling 11th-century novel often cited as one of the world's earliest, where the Uji setting gives its name to the closing chapters known as the Ten Quires of Uji. A bronze statue of Murasaki Shikibu, the noblewoman traditionally credited as the novel's author, sits near the riverbank not far from the bridge, offering a quiet spot to pause and take in the view of the river's fast-moving water. Visiting the bridge and statue takes only a few minutes on its own, but the location works well as a starting point for a longer walk through Uji, which also holds Byodo-in Temple and Ujigami Shrine within easy reach. The river view changes with the seasons, with cherry blossoms along the banks in spring and clear light in the cooler months; summer brings traditional cormorant fishing displays on the river in the evenings on certain nights. Because it sits directly on the route between the station and Uji's main attractions, it's easy to see without adding meaningfully to a day's itinerary.
