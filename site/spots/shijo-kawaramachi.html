Shijo Kawaramachi

Kyoto

Shijo Kawaramachi is Kyoto's busiest commercial crossroads, where the city's largest department stores and covered shopping arcades, including the long Teramachi and Shinkyogoku arcades, converge just a block from the Kamo River's restaurant-lined western bank. It functions less as a single attraction than as the connective hub for a whole cluster of things to do: shopping ranging from major department stores to small specialty shops, a dense concentration of restaurants and cafes covering every price range, and, after dark, one of the city's main nightlife areas spilling into the narrow lanes toward Pontocho and Kiyamachi. Nishiki Market, Kyoto's well-known food market street, sits just a few minutes' walk north, making the whole area easy to cover on foot without needing transport between stops. Because it's the commercial center of the city rather than a historic site, there's no real off-peak time in the way there is at a temple; weekday evenings and weekends are consistently busy, particularly around dinner time, while weekday mid-mornings before the shops open are comparatively calm. There's no admission or fixed time needed to see the area itself, since it's really about how long you want to shop, eat, or wander. Summer evenings bring diners out onto riverside terraces along the Kamo's west bank, and the area is lit for illumination events at various points through the year. It works well as a base for an evening after a day spent at temples further out.
