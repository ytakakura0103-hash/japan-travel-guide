Yanagawa River Punting

Fukuoka

Yanagawa's network of narrow canals, dug centuries ago for irrigation and moat defenses around the old castle town, is now best experienced from a long, narrow wooden boat poled along by a boatman standing at the stern, not unlike a Venetian gondola or an Oxford punt. The full route covers around four kilometers and takes roughly an hour, passing under low stone bridges and beneath overhanging willows, with boatmen traditionally singing folk songs along the way.

Several punting companies operate from docks near Nishitetsu Yanagawa Station, and no advance reservation is strictly required, though waiting ten to twenty minutes between registering and actually boarding is common, especially at busier times. Prices vary somewhat by season and operator.

Combination tickets pairing the train ride from Fukuoka with punting are sold at Nishitetsu stations and can work out cheaper than booking separately. The town itself, sometimes nicknamed the Venice of Kyushu, is worth a slow wander before or after the ride, and pairing the trip with a visit to Ohana, the old Tachibana clan villa nearby, makes for a fuller day out.
