Local Pick

Temiya Line Historical Railway Promenade

Hokkaido

The Temiya Line carried coal to Otaru's port as part of Hokkaido's first railway, the Horonai Railway, from 1880 until the line's closure in 1985. Rather than demolishing the corridor, Otaru converted it into a public promenade that keeps the original tracks, level-crossing gates, and signage in place, so walking it feels closer to tracing a piece of infrastructure history than visiting a manufactured attraction.

The trail runs from central Otaru out toward the Otaru City General Museum, where the former Temiya Station now displays a preserved railway turntable and old rolling stock, giving the walk a clear endpoint if you want a destination rather than just a stroll. It's free and open at all hours, though the museum itself keeps standard daytime hours and is closed Tuesdays.

Because it runs a short distance from the Otaru Canal but sees far less foot traffic, it's a reliable way to get a quieter, more local view of the city, especially in the early morning before the canal area fills up with tour groups.

Wildflowers and grasses grow up around the tracks in summer, softening the industrial feel, while a light dusting of snow on the old rails in winter is a favorite subject for local photographers.
