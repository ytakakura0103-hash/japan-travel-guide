Hatcho Miso Village

Nagoya

The name Hatcho Miso comes directly from this small district of Okazaki, home to two long-running miso breweries, Maruya and Kakukyu, that between them have shaped the identity of Aichi's distinctive dark, richly savory soybean miso. Maruya traces its history back to 1337, while Kakukyu began commercial production in 1645, though its founding family's involvement in miso-making reaches back further, to a samurai retainer who reportedly gave up his warrior life after the Battle of Okehazama and settled here to learn the trade.

Both breweries still flank the old Tokaido highway, Maruya on one side and Kakukyu on the other, and both offer factory tours that walk visitors past rows of massive wooden barrels, some of them generations old, each topped with a carefully stacked pyramid of river stones used to weigh down the fermenting miso over a two-year aging process. The traditional method has changed remarkably little since the Edo period, and tour guides are generally happy to explain the physics behind the stone-stacking technique in detail.

Both breweries run small shops selling their miso and miso-based snacks at the end of the tour, making this an easy, food-focused stop on the way to or from Okazaki Castle.

52 Okandori, Hatcho-cho, Okazaki, Aichi

About a 1-minute walk from Okazaki-koen-mae Station on the Meitetsu Nagoya Main Line, roughly 35 to 40 minutes from Nagoya (Higashi Okazaki Station, sometimes cited, is actually about a 20-25 minute walk away).
