Asahi Beer Oyamazaki Villa Museum

Kyoto

Built in the early 20th century as a countryside retreat in a British half-timbered style unusual for the area, the villa at Oyamazaki was later restored and converted into an art museum with backing from the Asahi Breweries group, whose name it now carries. The building itself, with its steep gables and dark wood framing set against the surrounding hillside, is worth seeing independent of the art inside, and a modern annex designed by architect Tadao Ando was added to house part of the permanent collection. That collection's standout piece is a genuine Water Lilies painting by Claude Monet, a notable inclusion for a museum of this size and one that draws visitors specifically interested in seeing an original Impressionist work outside a major city museum. Alongside it, the museum holds a substantial collection of Japanese folk-craft ceramics and other decorative arts, reflecting the taste of the villa's original owner. A visit through both the historic villa and the newer galleries generally takes one to two hours. Because the museum sits in Oyamazaki, a small town between Kyoto and Osaka rather than in the city center, it rarely feels crowded, even on weekends, and the walk from the station passes through a pleasant residential area near the base of Mount Tennozan. A modest admission fee applies. Seasonal exhibitions rotate through the year, so it's worth checking what's currently on display before visiting if a specific artist or period is the draw.

5-3 Oyamazaki Shimoshoden, Oyamazaki, Kyoto
