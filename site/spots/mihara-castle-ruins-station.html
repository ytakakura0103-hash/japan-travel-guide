Mihara Castle Ruins

Hiroshima

Mihara Castle was built in the mid-16th century by warlord Kobayakawa Takakage on a small tidal islet, engineered so that at high tide the walls appeared to rise straight out of the sea — earning it a reputation as one of Japan's earliest 'floating castles.' Much of the original site was built over as the city and its rail line developed, and JR Mihara Station now sits almost directly atop the former castle grounds.\n\nWhat survives — stone foundations, a section of moat, and the reconstructed Funairi turret — is accessible on a short walk from the station, and part of the old stonework is visible from the train platforms themselves, an odd but memorable detail for a castle site.\n\nMihara doesn't draw the crowds of Hiroshima's better-known castle towns, which makes this a low-key stop for anyone changing trains through the city on the way to or from Onomichi or Shikoku, worth ten minutes rather than a special trip.
