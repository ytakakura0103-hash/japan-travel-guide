Sendai Daikannon

Sendai

Built in 1991 on a hill in western Sendai, this enormous white statue of Kannon, the Buddhist goddess of mercy, is one of the tallest statues in Japan and visible from surprisingly far across the city skyline, often glimpsed unexpectedly from a train or highway before visitors realize what it is. Inside, an elevator and staircase climb through the hollow structure, passing floors of small Buddhist statues and shrines, up to an observation window positioned near the statue's shoulder.

It has a slightly kitsch, bubble-era grandeur that some visitors find charming and others find a bit much, but the view out over Sendai from inside the statue's body is genuinely worthwhile and unlike any other vantage point in the city. Because it sits off the main historical-sightseeing route around the castle and downtown, relatively few foreign tourists actually go inside, despite how visible the statue itself is.

Admission to enter and ride up is modest. A visit takes about thirty to forty-five minutes including the elevator ride and viewing platform. It's rarely crowded at any time of day, making it a low-effort way to get an elevated city view without the planning a taller structure downtown might require.

31-36 Sanezawa Nakayama-minami, Izumi-ku, Sendai, Miyagi

Open in Google Maps

About 15 minutes by taxi or bus from Sendai Station; the statue is also visible from the Tohoku Expressway.
