Moricoro Park (Expo 2005 Commemorative Park)

Nagoya

Long before Ghibli Park arrived, this site hosted the 2005 World Expo, and much of the infrastructure built for that event, wide lawns, wooded paths, ponds and a large Ferris wheel, was deliberately preserved afterward as a permanent public park rather than demolished. That history means Moricoro Park is considerably bigger than the ticketed Ghibli Park sections tucked inside it, and the large majority of its grounds remain completely free to enter and explore.

For visitors not interested in, or unable to book, Ghibli Park's timed tickets, Moricoro Park still offers a genuinely pleasant day out: seasonal flower fields that shift through the year, shaded cycling and walking paths, open lawns for picnics, and the original Expo-era Ferris wheel still turning for a modest fee. Families with young children in particular can spend a full day here without ever purchasing a Ghibli Park ticket, treating the anime-themed areas as an optional add-on rather than the whole point of the visit.

Because Ghibli Park's most Instagram-famous corners sit inside this larger free park, even a non-ticketed visit offers glimpses of the surrounding landscaping and atmosphere that made the site attractive to Studio Ghibli in the first place.

1533-1 Ibaragabasama, Nagakute, Aichi
