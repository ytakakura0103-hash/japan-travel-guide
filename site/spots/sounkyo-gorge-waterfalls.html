Sounkyo Gorge and Ryusei/Ginga Falls

Sapporo

Sounkyo Gorge cuts through Daisetsuzan National Park, Hokkaido's largest national park, with sheer columnar basalt cliffs formed by ancient volcanic activity lining both sides of the Ishikari River. A paved walkway runs along the gorge floor past the two best-known waterfalls, Ryusei-no-taki (Falling Star Falls) and Ginga-no-taki (Milky Way Falls), which drop close enough together that both are visible from a shared viewing area, an unusual pairing for a single roadside stop.\n\nSounkyo Onsen, a small hot spring town at the gorge's entrance, serves as the practical base for exploring both the gorge and the Kurodake Ropeway, which climbs into the surrounding peaks and is one of the more accessible ways into Daisetsuzan's alpine terrain without a full mountaineering trip.\n\nBeing well off the standard Sapporo-Otaru-Furano circuit and requiring a longer drive or bus transfer, Sounkyo draws a smaller, more specifically nature-focused crowd than Biei or Furano, and even the waterfall viewpoints rarely feel crowded outside the peak autumn foliage weeks.\n\nAutumn foliage across the gorge's cliffs, typically mid-to-late September, is considered one of Hokkaido's best displays, while winter brings an ice-sculpture festival that lights the frozen gorge after dark.

Sounkyo, Kamikawa-cho, Kamikawa District, Hokkaido 078-1701

About 3-3.5 hours from Sapporo: JR Limited Express to Kamikawa Station (about 2 hours), then a Dohoku Bus (about 30 minutes) to Sounkyo Onsen; direct highway buses also run from Sapporo on a more limited schedule.
