Kokubuncho

Sendai

Kokubuncho is the after-dark counterpart to Sendai's more genteel downtown image: a tight grid of narrow streets packed with izakaya, ramen counters, karaoke boxes, and bars, reputed to be the largest entertainment district in the Tohoku region. It's where salarymen, students, and visiting businesspeople alike end up after office hours, and the sheer density of options means you can wander a single block and find everything from a standing sake bar to a hole-in-the-wall gyutan grill.\n\nThe district has a slightly rougher edge than the polished shopping arcades nearby, with some hosts and touts working the main strip after dark, but the vast majority of it is ordinary izakaya and restaurant culture rather than anything to be wary of. Sticking to well-lit main streets and popular-looking shopfronts is the simplest way to navigate it comfortably.\n\nPrices vary enormously by venue, from cheap standing bars to higher-end restaurants, so it pays to glance at a menu before sitting down. Early evening, roughly 6 to 8pm, is the best window for a relaxed meal before the district gets loud and crowded later at night. Weekday evenings are calmer than Friday and Saturday nights, when the streets fill with groups moving between bars.

Kokubuncho, Aoba-ku, Sendai, Miyagi
