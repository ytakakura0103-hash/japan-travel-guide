Miyagi Zao Fox Village

Sendai

Unlike a conventional zoo, Zao Fox Village lets its resident foxes, around a hundred of them across several breeds including the classic red fox and rarer silver and platinum varieties, roam mostly freely across a forested hillside enclosure while visitors walk designated paths through the same space. The effect is closer to a nature reserve than an exhibit, and the foxes go about their own business, napping, playing, occasionally approaching visitors, rather than performing for anyone.

Because the animals are genuinely loose rather than caged, the park enforces some real rules: visitors generally can't touch the foxes in most areas, food is only given in designated feeding zones, and staff monitor interactions closely, all reasonable given that these are still wild animals rather than domesticated pets. Photographers tend to love it precisely because the foxes behave naturally rather than being staged.

Admission is inexpensive, and a visit typically takes about an hour. The park closes on some weekdays outside peak seasons, so checking the schedule before making the trip out is worth doing. Getting there without a car takes some effort, generally a train to Shiroishi Station followed by a taxi of about twenty minutes, or a seasonal bus on limited days. Winter brings out the foxes' thick coats and is a particularly striking season to visit, though snow can affect access.
