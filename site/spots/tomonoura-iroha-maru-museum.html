Iroha Maru Museum

Hiroshima

In 1867, the Iroha Maru, a steamship operated by the trading and paramilitary company of samurai reformer Sakamoto Ryoma, collided with a much larger vessel from the Kishu domain near Tomonoura and sank, triggering a tense negotiation over compensation that became one of the more dramatic episodes in the years leading up to the Meiji Restoration. Ryoma himself came to Tomonoura to negotiate the settlement, staying in the port town during the dispute.

This small museum, set inside an atmospheric old warehouse near the harbor, displays artifacts recovered from underwater surveys of the wreck alongside documents and models explaining the incident and its political stakes.

It's a minor footnote in most general histories of the Meiji Restoration but a significant local story in Tomonoura, and the museum's compact, unpolished setting — creaky floors, dim lighting, genuine salvaged objects — gives it more atmosphere than its small size would suggest. Easy to fit in alongside the port town walk and Taichoro.

Tomocho Tomo, Fukuyama, Hiroshima
