Fukuoka Castle Ruins (Maizuru Park)

Fukuoka

Fukuoka Castle was built in the early 1600s by the Kuroda clan and, unlike some reconstructed castles elsewhere in Japan, was never rebuilt after being dismantled in the Meiji era, leaving an extensive network of original stone walls, gates, and foundation platforms rather than a modern concrete keep. Climbing to the former main tower base rewards the walk with a wide view over Ohori Park and central Fukuoka.

Because there's no reconstructed building to charge admission for, the entire site is free to explore year-round, and information boards around the grounds help fill in what stood where. The scale of the stonework alone gives a decent sense of how large the original fortress complex was.

Maizuru Park, which surrounds the ruins, is also one of the city's best cherry blossom spots in early April, drawing large crowds for hanami picnics among the old walls; outside that season the site is considerably quieter. Visiting alongside Ohori Park next door makes for a natural half-day loop combining history and green space.

1 Jonai, Chuo Ward, Fukuoka

Open in Google Maps

5-minute walk from Ohori-Koen Station, or about 10 minutes from Akasaka Station (Kuko Line)
