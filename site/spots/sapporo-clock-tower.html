Sapporo Clock Tower

Hokkaido

This white wooden building was built in 1878 as a drill hall for what became Hokkaido University, back when American advisors were helping plan the island's agricultural development. It has become one of Sapporo's default sightseeing stops, largely because it is one of the few visibly pre-war structures left standing downtown. Inside, a small museum covers the building's history and the broader story of Sapporo's 19th-century founding as a planned city.

Many first-time visitors admit some disappointment at how modest the tower looks squeezed between office buildings, since photos tend to crop out the surrounding skyline. Seeing it as a quick five-minute photo stop on the way between Odori Park and Sapporo Station, rather than a dedicated destination, tends to leave visitors happier with the experience.

The clock mechanism itself is original and still keeps time, striking on the hour, which is a small detail worth pausing for if passing by near the top of the hour. The surrounding plaza is rarely crowded outside of festival periods, so there is no strong need to avoid particular times of day. In winter the building against fresh snow is the more photogenic version of the scene than in the flatter light of summer.
