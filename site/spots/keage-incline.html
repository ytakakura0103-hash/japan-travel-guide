Keage Incline

Kyoto

Keage Incline was built in the Meiji era as part of the Lake Biwa Canal system, a sloped railway track that once hauled boats between different water levels using cable-drawn cars, a piece of early Japanese industrial infrastructure that stopped operating decades ago and has since been left in place as a quiet historic relic. The abandoned tracks, still visible and walkable along much of their length, cut a long, gently sloped diagonal through the hillside, giving photographers a naturally symmetrical, converging-lines composition that's hard to find elsewhere in the city.

In spring, the incline is lined with cherry trees that arch over the tracks, drawing crowds specifically for the tunnel-like effect of blossoms framing the old rail line, but the site has a following year-round for portrait and kimono photography thanks to its quiet, uncluttered setting compared to Kyoto's more famous temple grounds. Unlike many of Kyoto's popular photo spots, it's free, unstaffed, and open at all hours, though visiting during daylight is recommended given the uneven surface underfoot.

It sits just a few minutes from Nanzenji Temple, making it an easy pairing for a single outing through this part of eastern Kyoto. Weekday mornings offer the best chance at an uncrowded shot, since the incline's popularity, especially during cherry blossom season, has grown substantially in recent years.

Nanzenji-Kusagawacho, Higashiyama Ward, Kyoto
