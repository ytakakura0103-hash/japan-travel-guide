Toji Temple

Kyoto

Toji is the last building still standing from the grid of temples that once framed the southern entrance to Heian-kyo, the city that would become Kyoto, and it has occupied the same plot since the capital was founded in 794. Its five-story pagoda, rebuilt in the 1600s after earlier fires, reaches close to 55 meters and remains the tallest wooden pagoda in Japan, visible from a considerable distance and often used as a visual anchor for the southern skyline. The Kondo and Kodo halls hold an important collection of Heian-period Buddhist statues arranged in a three-dimensional mandala, worth the modest additional admission for anyone interested in Buddhist sculpture. On the 21st of every month, the grounds fill with a long-running flea market known as Kobo-san, selling antiques, kimono, and street food, drawing much larger crowds than a typical visit. Visitors who prefer a quiet temple experience should avoid that date, while those chasing a lively, local shopping atmosphere should aim for it directly. Outside market days, a walk through the grounds and pagoda area can be done in under an hour. Cherry blossoms frame the pagoda in spring, and the reflecting pond beside it makes for one of the more photographed views in the city at that time. Toji is an easy add-on for anyone arriving by train, being a short walk south of Kyoto Station.

1 Kujocho, Minami Ward, Kyoto
