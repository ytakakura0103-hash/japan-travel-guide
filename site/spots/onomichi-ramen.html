Onomichi Ramen

Hiroshima

Onomichi ramen is generally traced back to noodle stalls that began operating in the town in the years following the Second World War, and it has since become recognized alongside a handful of other regional styles, such as Hakata and Kitakata ramen, as one of Japan's distinct local ramen traditions. Its defining feature is a clear soy-sauce-based broth typically made from chicken or pork bones and dried small fish, into which small chunks of rendered pork back fat are mixed, giving the soup a lightly rich, slightly sweet character despite its relatively clear appearance.

The noodles used are typically flat and slightly wide rather than the thin curly noodles associated with some other regional styles, and toppings tend to be kept fairly simple, commonly sliced chashu pork, bamboo shoots, green onion, and a piece of narutomaki fish cake. A number of long-running, family-run shops around Hondori and the harbor area are considered among the originators of the style, though as with most regional food traditions, opinions on the single best shop vary widely among locals.

Because Onomichi is a compact town, most of its well-regarded ramen shops sit within easy walking distance of JR Onomichi Station and the main Hondori shopping street, making it simple to combine a bowl with sightseeing in the temple district.
