Local Pick

Cat Street

Tokyo

Cat Street traces the old course of a covered stream that once ran between Shibuya and Harajuku, and the gentle curve of the road today still follows that original waterway rather than the grid pattern of the streets around it. The result is a walkable lane lined with a mix of international streetwear flagships, small independent boutiques, vintage shops, and casual cafes — generally a calmer and more browsable experience than the crowded, teen-focused Takeshita Street a short walk north, with wider sidewalks and noticeably less foot traffic even on weekends.

There is no single must-see stop along Cat Street; the appeal is in wandering and ducking into whichever storefronts catch your eye, so visit length varies widely depending on how much shopping you plan to do — anywhere from twenty minutes to a couple of hours. Weekday afternoons are the quietest time to browse without crowds, while weekend afternoons bring out a younger, fashion-focused crowd along with occasional street style photographers near the Harajuku end.

Cat Street sits conveniently between Shibuya and Omotesando, so it works well as a walking route rather than a round trip — start at one end near Shibuya and let it lead naturally toward Omotesando's tree-lined avenue and Omotesando Hills, rather than treating it as an isolated destination.

Jingumae area, Shibuya City, Tokyo
