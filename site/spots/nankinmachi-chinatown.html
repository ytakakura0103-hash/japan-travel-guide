Nankinmachi (Kobe Chinatown)

Kobe

Nankinmachi grew out of a Chinese trading community that settled near Kobe's port after it opened to foreign commerce in the late 1800s, and today it packs dozens of food stalls and restaurants into a few compact, lantern-strung blocks anchored by an ornamental gate at each end. Steamed buns, soup dumplings, and skewered street snacks are the main draw, sold from walk-up counters that make it easy to graze your way through the district rather than sit down for a full meal.

A slow walk-and-eat visit takes roughly an hour, though it is easy to linger longer around the central plaza, where a stone dragon fountain marks the middle of the district. Prices per item are generally modest, making it a good option for a light lunch or an afternoon snack between sightseeing stops in central Kobe.

Weekends and lunchtime on any day bring dense crowds along the main street, so visiting on a weekday morning or early evening gives more breathing room at the popular stalls. The Chinese New Year period is by far the busiest and most festive time to visit, with lion dance performances filling the plaza, but it is also when queues for food are at their longest.

Sakaemachidori/Motomachidori area, Chuo-ku, Kobe
