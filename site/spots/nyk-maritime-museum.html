NYK Maritime Museum

Yokohama

Nippon Yusen Kaisha, or NYK, grew into one of Japan's dominant shipping companies from the Meiji era onward, and this museum occupies its former Yokohama branch building, a solid stone-faced structure from the 1930s that survives as a good example of the port district's prewar commercial architecture in its own right. Inside, detailed ship models trace the evolution from early steamships to postwar ocean liners, alongside route maps, uniforms, and printed ephemera like menus and passenger tickets from the golden age of transpacific travel.\n\nIt appeals most to visitors with some interest in maritime or transport history, and the scale is modest enough to see thoroughly in under an hour, making it an easy secondary stop rather than a full outing on its own. Signage includes English in most sections, which is not guaranteed at every smaller Yokohama museum.\n\nSitting just a short walk from the Red Brick Warehouse and Osanbashi Pier, it gets bypassed by most visitors heading straight for those bigger attractions, keeping the galleries pleasantly uncrowded. It rewards anyone curious about how central shipping was to Yokohama's identity as Japan's premier international port.

3-9 Kaigan-dori, Naka-ku, Yokohama

Open in Google Maps

About 3 minutes on foot from Minatomirai Line Nihon-odori Station, near the Red Brick Warehouse.
