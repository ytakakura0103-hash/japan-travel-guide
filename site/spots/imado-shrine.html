Imado Shrine

Tokyo

Imado Shrine is a small, unassuming Shinto shrine northeast of Asakusa's main temple district, best known for its association with the maneki-neko, the beckoning cat figurine seen in shop windows across Japan. Local legend ties the shrine to the origin of the figure, and the grounds lean into that connection with pairs of larger cat statues near the altar and a steady supply of cat-themed charms and ema (wooden prayer plaques) for sale.

The shrine is particularly popular with visitors hoping for good relationships, since it's dedicated in part to a pair of deities associated with marriage and romantic connection, and the paired cat statues are meant to symbolize a couple. This makes it a common stop for people praying for love or a good match, distinct from the more general-purpose blessings sought at larger shrines nearby.

Because it sits a bit further from the train station than Senso-ji and the other major Asakusa sights, Imado Shrine sees a fraction of the crowds, and even a weekend visit tends to feel calm and unhurried. It's a quick stop, well under an hour, and works well tacked onto a longer walk through Asakusa for visitors who don't mind the extra distance. There's no strong seasonal draw here; it's a pleasant, low-key visit any time of year, and free to enter.
