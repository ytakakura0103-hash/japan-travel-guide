Tsuruhashi Korea Town

Osaka

Clustered tightly around Tsuruhashi Station, this market grew from a Korean community that settled in Osaka in the early 20th century and has remained one of the largest concentrations of Korean businesses in Japan ever since, with narrow covered lanes packed with grocers, barbecue restaurants, street food stalls and shops selling Korean cosmetics and hallyu merchandise. The market rewards slow browsing more than a fixed itinerary: expect to spend an hour or two working through the alleys, sampling items like tteokbokki or Korean-style fried chicken from stalls, and stopping for a full yakiniku meal at one of the many barbecue restaurants if time allows.

Prices are generally accessible, and the food here tends to run more authentic and less tourist-oriented than Korean food found elsewhere in the city. The lanes are narrow and can feel crowded at peak times, particularly weekend evenings when barbecue restaurants fill up; visiting on a weekday afternoon offers more room to move and shorter waits for a table. Some shops and stalls operate limited hours or close one day a week, so it's worth having a backup option if a particular restaurant isn't open.

There's no cost to walk through the market itself. Just outside the main food streets, quieter residential lanes reflect the everyday side of the neighbourhood rather than its tourist-facing edge. For visitors interested in the history of Japan's Korean-Japanese community as well as the food, this is one of the more genuine places in Osaka to explore it.

Tsuruhashi area, Ikuno Ward, Osaka
