Sounkyo Gorge

Hokkaido

Sounkyo sits inside a gorge carved through volcanic basalt columns in the heart of Daisetsuzan National Park, Japan's largest national park and one of the least developed by mainland standards, with cliff walls rising around 100 meters on either side of the narrow valley. Two named waterfalls along the gorge, Ginga and Ryusei, are the main roadside sights for visitors without time for a longer hike, both easily viewed from pull-offs along the gorge road.

The small onsen town of Sounkyo serves as the practical gateway into the park's alpine zone, with a ropeway and chairlift combination climbing partway up Mount Kurodake, from where hiking trails continue into genuinely high alpine terrain, some of the earliest snow-capped scenery in Japan each autumn given how far north and how exposed this part of the park sits.

Because the trip from Sapporo runs through Asahikawa and takes a real chunk of a day, Sounkyo draws far fewer casual day-trippers than Hokkaido's more central sights, and even in peak autumn foliage season, generally late September into early October here, it feels distinctly less crowded than comparable mainland Japan foliage spots. Winter brings an ice festival with illuminated ice sculptures built over the frozen river, a colder and quieter counterpart to Sapporo's own snow festival.

Sounkyo, Kamikawa-cho, Hokkaido
