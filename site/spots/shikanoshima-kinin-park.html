Shikanoshima Island and the Gold Seal Site

Fukuoka

Shikanoshima sits at the tip of the sandy Umi-no-Nakamichi spit and is connected to the mainland by land, unlike Nokonoshima's ferry-only access, making it an easy stop by car or bicycle. Its main historical claim is the 1784 discovery of the gold seal inscribed "King of Na, vassal of Han," unearthed by a local farmer and now a National Treasure displayed at Fukuoka City Museum; a small park near the reputed discovery site marks the spot, though the seal itself is not kept on the island.

Beyond the history, the island has a scattering of small shrines, beaches, and hiking paths up Mt. Shika, with views back across Hakata Bay toward the city skyline. It is a working fishing community as much as a sightseeing stop, so much of its charm is in the unhurried coastal scenery rather than a single headline attraction.

Because most visitors head straight for the more famous Umi-no-Nakamichi Seaside Park nearby, Shikanoshima itself stays relatively quiet, especially on weekdays. Renting a bicycle at Uminonakamichi Station and riding out to the island is a popular way to combine both in a single day, particularly in the cooler months when the coastal ride is most comfortable.
