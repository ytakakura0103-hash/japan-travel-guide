Akizuki Castle Town

Fukuoka

Akizuki was the seat of a minor domain established in 1624 for a son of the powerful Kuroda clan, and the resulting castle town, laid out along a stream at the base of forested hills, still holds enough of its Edo-period street plan and stone walls to have earned its "little Kyoto" nickname. The Suginobaba approach leading toward the old castle grounds is lined with roughly two hundred cherry trees along a few hundred meters, forming a tunnel of blossoms each spring during a small local festival, without anything like the crowds of Kyoto's famous hanami spots.

The old castle's Kuromon gate and a long-standing gate structure survive as protected cultural properties, and the surrounding streets hold traditional shops selling the town's noted amber-colored kuzu starch sweets alongside a handful of small museums, including one with an eclectic art collection donated to the town. Much of Akizuki functions as a quiet residential town rather than a preserved theme district, which keeps the pace unhurried outside of the spring cherry season.

Getting here without a car takes real effort, via a local railway and a further bus connection, and that friction is precisely what keeps Akizuki free of the tour-bus crowds found at more accessible historic towns. Autumn foliage along the same stream and hillside offers a second, quieter seasonal draw.
