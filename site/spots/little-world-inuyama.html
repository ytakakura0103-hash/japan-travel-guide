Little World Museum of Man

Nagoya

Founded in 1983 as an anthropological open-air park (the concept originated around the 1970 Osaka Expo), Little World takes a different approach from Japan's more common single-country heritage museums by recreating traditional dwellings and village life from more than twenty countries across a single site. A roughly 2.5-kilometer walking route connects 33 structures representing everyday architecture from places as varied as Peru, India, Germany and Thailand, with some buildings faithfully reconstructed to local specifications and others relocated using genuine original materials.

An indoor exhibition hall supplements the outdoor village walk with around 6,000 cultural artifacts drawn from roughly 70 countries, covering clothing, tools and everyday objects that don't fit neatly into a single reconstructed building. Visitors can rent traditional outfits from several of the represented cultures for photos, and restaurants scattered along the route serve dishes loosely tied to the regions they sit near, from Indian curry to Peruvian folk-craft-adjacent snacks.

It is a substantial site best paired with a full day rather than a quick stop, and works well as a joint outing with nearby Meiji-mura for visitors focused on architecture and world cultures rather than castles and gardens.

90 Iwasakizarayama, Inuyama, Aichi
