Great Torii of Miyajima

Hiroshima

The torii marking the sea approach to Itsukushima Shrine has stood in some form since at least the 12th century, though the current gate dates to 1875 and is built mainly of camphor wood, chosen partly for its resistance to rot and marine pests. Unlike many torii gates, it is not fixed into the seabed with deep foundations; instead its considerable weight (roughly 60 tons), reinforced with stones packed into the upper crossbeam, holds it upright against tides and typhoons.

The gate's appearance changes dramatically with the tide: at high tide it appears to rise directly from the sea, reinforcing the impression of the shrine complex floating nearby, while at low tide the surrounding sand flats are exposed and visitors can walk out across the seabed to stand at its base. The gate underwent a major restoration completed in December 2022 after three and a half years of scaffolding, its first large-scale renovation in about 70 years.

Evening illumination after sunset is another distinct viewing window, when the gate and shrine are lit against the dark water, drawing a different crowd than the daytime low-tide walk. Because of the gate's fame, the immediate viewing area along the shore can be crowded, especially around sunset and low tide windows in peak season.

Offshore from Itsukushima Shrine, Miyajima-cho, Hatsukaichi, Hiroshima Prefecture
