Abeno Harukas Observatory

Osaka

At 300 metres, Abeno Harukas is the tallest building in the Kansai region, and its top three floors form Harukas 300, an observation area split across an enclosed main deck with floor-to-ceiling glass and, weather permitting, an open-air edge terrace above it where visitors can feel the wind directly. The building itself, completed in 2014, also houses a department store, hotel and offices below the observatory, so the elevator ride up passes through several very different uses of the same tower before reaching the panoramic floors.

A visit to the observatory alone takes roughly an hour, though the view rewards lingering, stretching from the city centre out toward Osaka Bay and, on especially clear days, as far as the mountains beyond. Sunset is the most requested time slot, when the deck gradually shifts from daytime views to the city's lights coming on, and it's correspondingly the busiest period; visiting earlier in the afternoon or well after dark tends to mean shorter queues for tickets and elevators.

Admission is charged separately from the rest of the building and is broadly comparable to Osaka's other major observatories. The tower sits directly above Tennoji Station, one of Osaka's major transit hubs, making it an easy stop to combine with nearby Tennoji Park, Shitennoji Temple, or the retro streets of Shinsekai, all within a short walk or train ride.
