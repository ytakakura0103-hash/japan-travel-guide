Nogeyama Park Hillside Gardens

Yokohama

Nogeyama Park wraps around the zoo but deserves attention on its own, since it was originally landscaped on the ruins of a wealthy silk merchant's Meiji-period villa. The terraced hillside layout means you get shifting views over Nishi-ku's rooftops as you climb, and benches are tucked into quiet corners away from the zoo crowds next door.

The rose garden blooms twice a year, in late spring and again in autumn, and is modest in scale compared to Yokohama Park downtown, which keeps it relatively peaceful even in bloom season. Cherry trees line several of the upper paths and draw a small but devoted crowd of neighborhood hanami groups in late March and early April, well before the bigger crowds descend on Sankeien or Minato Mirai.

Because the park sits between Sakuragicho and Hinodecho stations, it works well as a shortcut walking route rather than a dedicated destination, letting you stumble onto the gardens without planning a special trip. There are no ticket booths or facilities beyond basic restrooms, so bring your own drinks. Early morning is the best time for photography, when the light angles through the trees and before dog walkers and joggers fill the paths.
