Miraikan (National Museum of Emerging Science)

Tokyo

Miraikan, officially the National Museum of Emerging Science and Innovation, is built around big, hands-on demonstrations of current and near-future science and technology rather than static displays. Its best-known feature is Geo-Cosmos, a large spherical globe suspended in the main atrium and covered in LED panels that display real-time data about weather, climate, and other global patterns, making the whole planet visible at once from multiple viewing levels around it. Regular demonstrations featuring humanoid robots are held on the main floor, showing off current robotics research in a format aimed at a general audience rather than specialists.

Exhibits cover a wide range of subjects beyond robotics, including space exploration, with material connected to Japan's own space program, as well as sections on biology, information technology, and the technology behind disaster preparedness, an area the museum treats with particular seriousness given Japan's earthquake risk. Many displays are interactive, making it a strong choice for families with school-age children, though the explanatory panels are detailed enough to hold adult interest as well.

The museum also has a dome theater showing science-themed films on a separate ticket. A visit through the main galleries takes a couple of hours at a comfortable pace. Miraikan is closed on Tuesdays, and it's located within walking distance of several other Odaiba attractions, making it easy to combine with a broader day in the area.
