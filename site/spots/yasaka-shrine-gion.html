Yasaka Shrine

Kyoto

Yasaka Shrine sits at the top of Shijo-dori where it meets Gion, its brightly painted gate marking one of the clearest transitions from downtown Kyoto into the old geisha district. The shrine has stood in some form for well over a thousand years and remains closely tied to the neighborhood around it, functioning as a local gathering spot as much as a sightseeing stop, with rows of stone lanterns and a stage used for dance and music performances.

Its biggest claim to fame is the Gion Matsuri, held throughout July and considered one of Japan's three great festivals; the climax comes with processions of enormous wooden floats, some multiple stories tall and pulled by hand through the streets, accompanied by weeks of smaller neighborhood events, food stalls, and evening gatherings in the streets around the shrine. Outside of July, the shrine grounds can be walked in twenty to thirty minutes, and entry is free at any time, day or night, since the grounds don't close.

Because of its location, Yasaka Shrine gets a steady flow of visitors throughout the day, but rarely feels as packed as Kiyomizu-dera; early morning or evening visits are quietest. It connects directly into Gion to the west and Maruyama Park, a popular cherry blossom spot, just behind it.

625 Gionmachi Kitagawa, Higashiyama Ward, Kyoto
