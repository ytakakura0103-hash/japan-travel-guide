Akashi Park and Castle Ruins

Kobe

Akashi Castle was built in the early 17th century by order of the Tokugawa shogunate to help control the strategically important Akashi Strait, but unlike Himeji Castle nearby, its main keep was never constructed; instead, two corner turrets and long stretches of stone walling survive as the tangible remains of the fortification. The park built over the former castle grounds has become one of the area's most popular cherry blossom viewing spots, with hundreds of trees planted along the old ramparts.\n\nBecause it lacks a dramatic central keep to draw crowds the way Himeji does, Akashi Park functions more as a genuine local park than a paid historical attraction, entry to the grounds is free, and the atmosphere is closer to a city park than a museum site. Locals use it for picnics, sports and dog walking as much as for the history.\n\nThe elevated turret platforms offer decent views over the city and out toward the strait, and the stonework itself, some of the largest surviving Edo-period castle walls in the region, is worth a slow walk. Spring bloom season is by far the busiest time; outside of that, it stays quiet.

Akashi, Hyogo Prefecture

From Sannomiya, take the JR Kobe Line or San'yo Electric Railway to Akashi Station (about 20-25 minutes); the park is directly north of the station.
