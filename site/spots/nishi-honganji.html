Nishi Honganji

Kyoto

Nishi Honganji, the head temple of the Hongwan-ji branch of Jodo Shinshu Buddhism, is one of the few Kyoto UNESCO sites where entry to the main grounds costs nothing, though the interiors of its most celebrated structures are more restricted. The Karamon Gate, with its dense, gilded carving, and the Hiunkaku pavilion are both registered National Treasures and stand as two of the best-preserved examples of Momoyama-period design in the country. Hiunkaku, a three-story pavilion once linked to Toyotomi Hideyoshi's Jurakudai palace, sits within a pond garden and is normally viewed from a distance rather than entered, since public access is limited to occasional special openings. Inside the main hall complex, visitors can walk across wide tatami-matted halls connected by covered corridors, an experience that gives a strong sense of the temple's working religious life rather than a purely museum-like presentation. A visit to the accessible areas can be done in under an hour, though those with a strong interest in architecture may want longer to take in the details on the gates and corridor ceilings. The temple is a working place of worship, so mornings sometimes include services open to observe respectfully from the back of the hall. Weekday mornings tend to be calmer than weekend afternoons. Its walking distance from Kyoto Station makes it easy to combine with Higashi Honganji, its unrelated but similarly named neighbor to the east.

1 Monzencho, Shimogyo Ward, Kyoto
