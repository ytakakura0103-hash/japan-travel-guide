Arima Onsen (hot spring town)

Kobe

Arima Onsen's hot springs are mentioned in some of Japan's earliest historical chronicles, and the town has drawn bathers, including feudal lords and warlords in earlier centuries, for well over a thousand years, which makes it one of the country's most storied onsen destinations despite sitting less than an hour from central Kobe. The town itself is small and steeply built, with narrow stone-paved lanes, traditional wooden facades, and small shops selling local sweets and hot-spring-themed souvenirs like carbonated candy made with the mineral water.

Arima is known specifically for two distinct spring types found close together: an iron-rich reddish-brown 'gold' spring and a clear, carbonated 'silver' spring, each believed to have different effects and each available at public bathhouses as well as many of the town's ryokan. Staying overnight at a ryokan is the classic way to experience Arima properly, though a day trip focused on a public bathhouse and a walk through the lanes is entirely workable for visitors based in Kobe or Osaka.

Weekends bring a steady flow of day-trippers from the Kansai cities, filling the main street's shops and cafes, while a weekday visit, or simply arriving early before the day-trip buses do, gives a much more low-key feel to the same streets.

Kobe Dentetsu's Arima Onsen Station underwent a renewal in February 2025, adding onsen-themed touches throughout, including benches styled after traditional bathing stools and washing-area seating, a playful nod to the hot spring town it serves right from the moment visitors step off the train.
