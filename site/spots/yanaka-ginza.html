Yanaka Ginza

Tokyo

Yanaka Ginza is a modest, sloped shopping street in one of the few parts of central Tokyo that escaped major damage in the 1923 earthquake and World War II bombing, which is part of why the neighborhood still has the feel of an old-fashioned shitamachi (downtown) district rather than a rebuilt one. The street itself is short, lined with small family-run shops selling menchi-katsu (fried minced meat cutlets), senbei rice crackers, croquettes, and other snacks meant to be eaten while walking, plus a scattering of cafes and general stores.

The neighborhood is known locally as a cat-friendly area, with several resident strays and cat-themed goods in the shops, and the stairway at the top of the street, Yuyake Dandan, is a popular spot to look back down over the rooftops at sunset. Yanaka Ginza connects easily to Yanaka Cemetery, a large, quiet burial ground shaded by cherry trees that doubles as a pleasant walking route and a good cherry blossom spot in spring without the crowds of Ueno Park nearby.

An hour or so is enough to wander the street and sample a few snacks, though it is easy to lose more time browsing the smaller shops. Weekday afternoons are noticeably calmer than weekends, when the narrow street can get crowded with both tourists and local shoppers. Because most vendors close in the early evening, this is better suited to a daytime visit than a nighttime one.
