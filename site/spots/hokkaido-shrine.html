Hokkaido Shrine

Hokkaido

Hokkaido Shrine was established in the 1870s to enshrine deities associated with the settlement and development of the island, and it remains the region's most important Shinto site, drawing large crowds for New Year's first visit (hatsumode) and for weddings throughout the year. Unlike shrines that sit alone on a city block, this one is embedded in Maruyama Park's forest, so the walk in through tall trees is as much a part of the experience as the shrine buildings themselves.

The surrounding grove includes several hundred cherry trees, making the shrine one of Sapporo's most popular hanami spots in late April to early May, when the city's blossom season runs later than most of mainland Japan. Outside that window and New Year, the shrine grounds are usually calm, and a slow lap of the forest paths makes for a pleasant half-hour away from downtown traffic.

Weekday mornings are the quietest time to visit; weekend afternoons, especially during blossom season, draw the heaviest crowds. Maruyama Zoo sits just across the park, so the two can reasonably be combined into one outing. Because Sapporo's cherry blossoms bloom noticeably later than Tokyo's, roughly late April into early May, it is worth checking a current-year forecast before timing a trip specifically around them.

474 Miyagaoka, Chuo-ku, Sapporo, Hokkaido
