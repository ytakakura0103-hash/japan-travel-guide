Local Pick

Otokoyama Sake Brewery Museum

Hokkaido

Otokoyama traces its sake-brewing roots back to the Edo period in the Kansai region before its brand was revived in Asahikawa in the 1960s, a location chosen deliberately for its cold winters and clean groundwater filtered through the surrounding Daisetsuzan mountains, conditions long considered favorable for brewing. The on-site museum displays historical brewing equipment, old sake bottles and awards, and generally covers the craft of sake-making in more depth than most casual visitors expect from what looks like a modest company museum.

A tasting counter at the end lets visitors sample several grades of the brewery's sake for free, from everyday table sake to premium daiginjo, before deciding what, if anything, to buy at the attached shop; non-drinkers can usually sample a soft, sake-adjacent drink instead, though it's worth asking on-site since offerings can change.

Because Asahikawa itself sees a fraction of the tourist traffic Sapporo does, and this brewery is a further bus ride from the station, it draws mostly a local and sake-enthusiast crowd rather than tour groups, making almost any time a fairly calm visit. Winter is arguably the most fitting season to visit, given that snow and cold water are central to the brewery's own story about why it settled in Asahikawa in the first place.

1-33 Nagayama 2-jo 7-chome, Asahikawa, Hokkaido

Open in Google Maps

About 25-30 minutes by bus from JR Asahikawa Station.
