Kurama-dera

Kyoto

Kurama-dera occupies a steep, densely forested mountainside north of central Kyoto and has long been associated with tengu, the long-nosed, red-faced goblin figures of Japanese folklore. According to legend, the young Minamoto no Yoshitsune, later one of the most celebrated warriors in Japanese history, trained here as a boy under a tengu master, learning the swordsmanship and mountain-craft that would define his later career. The approach alone is part of the experience: a steep, winding trail through cedar forest climbs from the temple town below, though a short cable car covers part of the ascent for visitors who'd rather save their energy for the upper sections. Even with the cable car, reaching the main hall involves a genuine uphill walk, and comfortable shoes are worth prioritizing over convenience. The full round trip, including time at the main hall and its viewing terrace over the surrounding mountains, generally takes a couple of hours or more. Beyond the temple itself, the trail continues toward Kibune on the other side of the mountain, a popular but more strenuous extension for visitors with extra time. Weekday mornings are far quieter than weekend visits, when the narrow trail can bottleneck near the cable car station. Maple foliage in autumn and fresh greenery in early summer are the two most visually rewarding times to come, though the forest setting keeps the site atmospheric in any season.
