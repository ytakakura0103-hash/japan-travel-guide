Neko no Hosomichi (Cat Alley)

Hiroshima

Neko no Hosomichi, literally "Cat's Narrow Path," runs along part of the hillside route between the town center and the temple district above, threading between old wooden houses, retaining walls, and stairways in one of Onomichi's older residential neighborhoods. Like much of the town's hillside, it developed as housing squeezed onto steep terrain overlooking the harbor, and the resulting maze of narrow lanes has become part of the area's appeal for visitors exploring on foot.

The path takes its name from the number of cats that have long lived in the neighborhood, and over time local artists and residents added small hand-painted stones with cat designs along the route, alongside a scattering of cat-themed folk art, murals, and a small private gallery dedicated to local artist Shodo Kubota's cat-and-stone artwork. The actual number of cats encountered on any given walk varies and is not something the area guarantees, since they are free-roaming rather than kept as a formal attraction.

Because the lane connects into the wider network of hillside alleys and stairways near Senkoji, it works well as part of a longer walk through Onomichi's temple district rather than a single isolated stop, and the slower pace of wandering its turns is arguably more the point than reaching any specific endpoint.

Higashitsuchido-cho / Nagaecho area, Onomichi, Hiroshima Prefecture
