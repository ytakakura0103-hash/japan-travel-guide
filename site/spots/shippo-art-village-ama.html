Shippo Art Village

Nagoya

Shippo-yaki, the Japanese term for cloisonne enamelware, uses a colorful glass glaze fired onto a metal base and takes its name from a Buddhist term for the 'seven treasures,' a nod to the technique's jewel-like finish. Though cloisonne existed in Japan earlier, the craft's modern revival is generally traced to 1833, when a goldsmith named Tsunekichi Kaji, working in the Owari domain, became fascinated by imported Dutch cloisonne plates and reverse-engineered the technique through careful study, establishing this stretch of Owari as the country's production base for the craft.

The town where this happened became known simply as Shippo-cho, and it eventually merged into the modern city of Ama in 2010, though the name and craft tradition remain closely tied to the area. The Shippo Art Village opened in 2004 to promote the craft, which received formal designation as a traditional national craft in 1995, offering exhibition space alongside shops where visitors can buy contemporary cloisonne jewelry, dishware and decorative pieces made using techniques that trace directly back to Kaji's nineteenth-century experiments.

The village is a modest, single-purpose stop best suited to visitors with a specific interest in traditional crafts rather than a broader sightseeing itinerary.

2000 Toshima Jusanwari, Shippo-cho, Ama, Aichi
