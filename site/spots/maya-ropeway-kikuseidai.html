Mt. Maya's Kikuseidai Night View

Kobe

Mt. Maya's Kikuseidai observation deck looks out over Kobe, Osaka Bay and, on clear nights, the lights stretching toward Kansai International Airport and beyond. It's regularly grouped with the night views from Mt. Rokko and Mt. Inasa in Nagasaki as one of Japan's three great nightscapes, but it draws noticeably smaller crowds than its more famous counterparts.

Getting there is part of the experience: a cable car climbs from the base to Tenjo-ji temple, followed by a ropeway called the Maya View Line that continues to the summit area, offering its own views over forested slopes on the way up. The temple partway up is worth a brief stop, with a striking modern glass-and-concrete rebuild of its 500 Rakan hall.

Because the whole route requires two separate cable systems and a bus or drive to reach the base, it takes more planning than Rokko's more direct access from Kobe, which is exactly why it stays quieter. Bring a layer even in summer, as it's noticeably cooler and windier at the top than in the city below, and check that the ropeway is running, since it sometimes closes for maintenance or high winds.
