Gioji Temple

Kyoto

Gioji is a small, thatched-roof temple tucked into a bamboo-and-maple grove west of central Arashiyama, so thoroughly covered in moss that the ground beneath the trees looks almost carpeted, a texture that has made the temple a quiet favorite among visitors seeking something calmer than the area's busier attractions. Its name comes from Gio, a dancer at the court of the powerful 12th-century warlord Taira no Kiyomori, who, according to the tale recorded in the Heike Monogatari, was cast aside when Kiyomori's affections shifted to a younger performer; Gio is said to have retreated here with her mother and sister to become a Buddhist nun, and the temple preserves that story of quiet retirement from a glamorous but precarious life at court. The main hall is modest in size, and a visit typically takes well under an hour, most of it spent walking slowly through the moss garden rather than touring interior rooms. Because it sits further from the station than Arashiyama's bamboo grove or its central temples, and requires a genuine walk to reach, Gioji tends to stay noticeably quieter even during busy periods, rewarding visitors willing to go a little further. Moss looks richest after rain or during the humid rainy season, while maples color the grounds in November alongside the temple's bamboo. A modest admission fee applies, and the small scale of the site makes it an easy addition to a longer Arashiyama walking route.

32 Sagatoriimoto Kozakacho, Ukyo Ward, Kyoto
