Sumida Edo Kiriko Glass

Tokyo

Edo kiriko is a style of cut glassware that developed in Tokyo (then Edo) in the 19th century, recognizable by intricate geometric patterns hand-carved into colored glass so that light catches the cuts differently depending on the angle. This studio and shop in Sumida is one of a number of small workshops in the ward continuing the craft, and it's set up to let visitors watch the actual carving process up close, with craftspeople working at grinding wheels to cut patterns freehand into glassware destined for the shop floor.

Beyond watching, many visitors come specifically for the short cutting lessons offered on site, where a staff member guides beginners through carving a simple pattern into a glass or small dish to take home; these usually need to be booked in advance, and availability can be limited since only a few people can be guided at once. The finished glassware sold in the shop ranges from small sake cups to larger vases, priced according to the complexity of the pattern and the time it took to cut.

A visit focused just on browsing the shop takes well under an hour, while the cutting lesson adds a meaningful chunk of time, often the better part of an hour by itself. This is a quieter, craft-focused alternative to the more tourist-heavy workshops elsewhere in the city, and it rarely feels crowded.

Taihei area, Sumida City, Tokyo
