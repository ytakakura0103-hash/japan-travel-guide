Makomanai Takino Cemetery (Hill of Buddha & Moai Avenue)

Hokkaido

Makomanai Takino Cemetery is, first and foremost, a functioning cemetery, but two large-scale artworks on its grounds have turned it into one of Sapporo's more unusual sightseeing stops, drawing visitors who'd otherwise have no reason to visit a burial ground. The centerpiece is the Hill of Buddha, designed by internationally recognized architect Tadao Ando, where a 13.5-meter seated Buddha statue emerges from within an artificial hill, visible only as a hint of its head from a distance until visitors walk through a tunnel entrance that reveals the full statue from below, ringed by roughly 150,000 lavender plants that bloom in summer.

A short distance away, an entirely different kind of spectacle runs along a promenade: life-sized replicas of the Moai statues from Easter Island, installed decades ago as a memorial garden feature and since become a recurring subject of social media curiosity for how genuinely out of place they look in a Hokkaido cemetery. The combination of serious contemporary architecture and slightly surreal statuary makes for an unusual pairing that's hard to find described accurately without visiting.

Because it functions as an active cemetery, visitors should keep a respectful tone throughout, particularly around any ongoing services or family visits encountered on the grounds. Getting there requires a bus connection from the nearest subway station, making it more of a deliberate half-day outing than a casual stop, but the combination of Ando's architecture and the Moai avenue makes it one of the more distinctive sights in the greater Sapporo area.

Makomanai, Minami Ward, Sapporo
