MIZKAN MUSEUM (MIM)

Nagoya

The Mizkan Group's history begins in 1804, when a sake brewer named Matazaemon Nakano succeeded in producing vinegar from leftover sake lees, a byproduct that had previously gone to waste, launching what would eventually become one of Japan's largest vinegar producers. The company's own museum, known as MIM, sits directly on the Handa Canal that once carried its barrels of vinegar, along with sake and cotton from across the Chita Peninsula, out to Edo and the Kamigata region during the Edo period.

The museum is organized into five themed zones tracing that journey from the early nineteenth century to the present, walking visitors through traditional brewing techniques inside a recreated 'Room of Earth' before letting them climb aboard a full-scale, 20-meter reconstruction of a bezai-style sailing ship, the type of vessel that once carried Mizkan's vinegar barrels on the multi-week voyage to Edo. The setting itself, with the canal's aged black-walled warehouses still standing outside, does as much to convey the history as the exhibits inside.

A combined ticket covering MIM and the nearby Kunizakari Sake Museum is a reasonable way to see two of Handa's brewing traditions in a single afternoon.

2-6 Nakamachi, Handa, Aichi

Open in Google Maps

About a 15-minute walk from Handa Station on the JR Taketoyo Line, roughly 40 minutes from Nagoya.
