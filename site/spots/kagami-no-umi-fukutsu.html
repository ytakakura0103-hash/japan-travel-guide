Kagami no Umi (Mirror Sea)

Fukuoka

Kagami no Umi refers to a roughly three-kilometer stretch of shallow coastline near Fukutsu, spanning the Fukuma, Miyajihama, and Tsuyazaki areas, where a thin, still layer of water left behind at low tide turns the sand into a near-perfect mirror. Under the right conditions, calm water and clear skies, the reflection is sharp enough to have earned the beach a nickname referencing Bolivia's famous Uyuni Salt Flat, a comparison that's driven a steady stream of social media attention as photographers chase the reflective effect at sunset.

Unlike a fixed attraction, the mirror effect depends entirely on tide timing, weather, and how much water remains on the sand, meaning the same stretch of coast can look completely ordinary on one visit and produce a striking reflection on another. Checking tide tables and weather forecasts before heading out is essential, and even then, results vary; this is a spot for visitors willing to accept some unpredictability in exchange for the chance at a genuinely striking photo.

Because the effect spans a fairly long stretch of coastline rather than one fixed viewpoint, there's some flexibility in where to set up, and arriving with enough time to walk along the shore and find the calmest water improves the odds of a good shot. It's a straightforward drive from central Fukuoka, making it a feasible half-day trip for anyone specifically chasing this kind of photography.
