Yamashita Park

Yokohama

This park was created using debris cleared after the catastrophic 1923 Great Kanto Earthquake, turning wreckage into what is now one of the city's most popular open spaces, a long green strip running along the harbor between Chinatown and the Marine Tower. The rose gardens near the Hikawa Maru end are a particular draw in season, and the park's flat, wide promenade makes it comfortable for a slow, unstructured walk with harbor views the whole way.

Because it connects several major sights, including Chinatown, the Silk Museum, Marine Tower, and the Hikawa Maru museum ship, it tends to be busy throughout the day, especially on weekends when it also hosts food trucks and occasional events. Early morning is noticeably quieter and gives the clearest light for photos of the harbor and passing ships.

Roses bloom here twice a year, in spring and again in autumn, drawing crowds specifically for that display, while summer evenings bring people out for the sea breeze once the heat of the day passes. It remains free and open at all hours, making it an easy anchor point for planning a day around the waterfront district.

279 Yamashita-cho, Naka-ku, Yokohama
