Kobe Port Tower

Kobe

Kobe Port Tower is instantly recognizable by its pinched, drum-shaped lattice frame, an engineering choice meant to evoke a Japanese hand drum (tsuzumi) while resisting wind and earthquakes. It has stood at the edge of Meriken Park since 1963 and underwent a multi-year renovation that reopened its observation floors with updated lounges and mirrored ceiling effects that play with reflections of the harbor lights.

Riding to the top takes only a couple of minutes, and the view stretches across the working port, the Rokko mountain range behind the city, and on clear days toward Osaka Bay. A ticket generally costs a modest sum for adults with discounts for children, and most visitors spend well under an hour inside unless they linger for the sunset-to-night light transition, which is when the tower and its neighbors along the bay reflect most dramatically on the water.

To avoid the thickest crowds, aim for a weekday morning right after opening or come in the last hour before closing, when tour groups have thinned out and the lighting turns golden. The tower is lit up nightly and is one of the best free photo backdrops in the city even without going inside, so it is worth a stop regardless of the weather.

5-5 Hatobacho, Chuo-ku, Kobe

About a 15-minute walk from the south exit of JR Kobe Station through Meriken Park (roughly 12-15 minutes from Motomachi Station), or take the City Loop sightseeing bus from Sannomiya Station to the Port Tower-mae stop. The Port Liner does not serve this area.
