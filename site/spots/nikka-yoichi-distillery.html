Nikka Whisky Yoichi Distillery

Sapporo

Yoichi is where Masataka Taketsuru, who trained in Scotland and is often credited as the father of Japanese whisky, chose to build his distillery in 1934 because the town's cool, humid coastal climate reminded him of Speyside. The distillery still uses coal-fired direct-flame stills, a rare and labor-intensive method that most other distilleries abandoned, and the stone buildings from the founding era give the site a distinct look compared to more modern whisky facilities.\n\nSelf-guided walking routes take visitors past the kiln, warehouses, and a small museum on Taketsuru and his Scottish wife Rita, whose story became well known in Japan through a popular television drama. Tastings are typically included or available for a fee depending on current policy, so it's worth checking current details before visiting, especially for anyone planning to drive afterward.\n\nBecause reaching Yoichi requires a slow transfer through Otaru with limited onward train frequency, roughly hourly, it takes real effort compared to central Sapporo attractions, and that friction keeps it from feeling overrun even though it's a well-known name internationally.\n\nThe distillery grounds are attractive across seasons, but the ivy-covered stone buildings in autumn color are a particular highlight, and winter brings a quieter, snow-covered version of the same walk.

7-6 Kurokawa-cho, Yoichi, Yoichi District, Hokkaido 046-0003

About 75-90 minutes from Sapporo Station: JR Rapid Airport train to Otaru (about 35 minutes), transfer to the JR Hakodate Line bound for Kutchan/Yoichi (about 25 minutes); the distillery is a 3-minute walk from Yoichi Station. Trains on this stretch run only about once an hour.
