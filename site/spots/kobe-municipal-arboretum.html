Kobe Municipal Arboretum

Kobe

This arboretum spreads across the slopes of Mt. Suwayama, organized into sections by tree family so it works equally well as a research collection and a scenic walking course. At its center sits the Nunobiki Reservoir, a brick dam completed in the Meiji era that still supplies part of Kobe's water and is itself a recognized piece of industrial heritage, with old pump buildings dotted along the paths.

Because it continues on from the Nunobiki Falls trail, many visitors combine the two into a single half-day walk, climbing from the falls up into the tree collection before looping back down or continuing to the herb garden and ropeway. The gradient is steady rather than steep, and benches are placed at regular intervals for anyone who wants to slow down and take in the views back over the city and harbor.

Maple and cherry sections make this a worthwhile stop in both spring and autumn, though it's pleasant in any season simply for the quiet. Entry is inexpensive, and because it requires a bit more legwork than the more famous Kitano and Chinatown stops, it tends to stay uncrowded even on weekends.
