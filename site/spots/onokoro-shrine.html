Onokoro Shrine

Kobe

According to the creation narrative recorded in Japan's oldest chronicles, the gods Izanagi and Izanami stirred the primordial ocean with a jeweled spear, and the brine that dripped off its tip formed the first island, Onokoro. Several sites on Awaji Island claim to be that mythical spot, and this shrine is one of the most visited among them, its main approach marked by one of the largest torii gates in the country, visible from a good distance along the surrounding roads.

The shrine grounds themselves are relatively small and unhurried compared to the scale of the gate, with a main hall dedicated to the two creator deities and a scattering of smaller subsidiary shrines. It functions as a genuine place of worship for the local community, hosting weddings and seasonal festivals, rather than existing purely as a mythology-themed tourist stop.

Because it sits well off Kobe's usual sightseeing radius, most visitors are Awaji Island day-trippers combining it with Yumebutai or the Naruto whirlpools rather than dedicated pilgrims, and it rarely feels crowded outside of festival dates. The oversized torii gate alone is worth the detour for photographs.

Awaji Island, Hyogo Prefecture

Open in Google Maps

From Sannomiya, take a highway bus (Kobe Sannomiya-Fukura line, Awaji Kotsu/Shinki Bus) to the Enami (Enokiichi) bus stop, about 60 minutes; the shrine is roughly a 10-11 minute walk south from there, so it is easily reached on foot without needing a car or taxi.
