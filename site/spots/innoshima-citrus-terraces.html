Innoshima Citrus Terraces

Hiroshima

Hassaku, a citrus variety that's tart and slightly bitter compared to sweeter mandarins, was first cultivated on Innoshima in the 19th century, and the island still grows it on terraced slopes that climb steeply from the coastline. There's no single ticketed attraction here — the appeal is driving or cycling the back roads above the ferry ports, where the hillsides are laid out in these terraces and the Inland Sea appears between the rows.

The most striking time to visit is late autumn into early winter when the fruit ripens to a pale yellow-green against dark green leaves, though the terraces are quietly photogenic year-round for their sheer geometry. A few farm stands along the coastal road sell hassaku products, from raw fruit in season to marmalade and citrus sweets.

This is a spot for slow exploring rather than a checklist stop, and it rewards anyone taking the Shimanami Kaido at a relaxed pace rather than racing straight through Innoshima toward Ikuchijima.

Innoshima area, Onomichi, Hiroshima
