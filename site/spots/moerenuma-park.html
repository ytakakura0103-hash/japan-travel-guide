Local Pick

Moerenuma Park

Hokkaido

Isamu Noguchi designed this entire park as one unified work of land art shortly before his death in 1988, and it was completed according to his plan years later. The result treats the whole site, roughly the size of a small neighborhood, as sculpture: a grass pyramid mound called Mount Moere, a glass pyramid building housing exhibits and a cafe, and a large fountain that performs on a set schedule are arranged with a precision that rewards slow walking rather than a quick photo stop.

Because it sits outside the usual downtown sightseeing loop, on land that used to be a garbage dump reclaimed for public use, it draws a mostly local crowd of families cycling or picnicking rather than tour groups, which keeps it noticeably calmer than the city's more famous parks even on a nice weekend.

Bicycle rental is available on-site and is worth using given how spread out the park is. Summer brings the wading pool and fountain shows to life; winter turns the mounds into a cross-country ski and sledding area, which is an unusual and worthwhile way to see Noguchi's design under snow. Either season, allow at least half a day if the goal is to see the park properly rather than skim it.

1-1 Moerenuma Koen, Higashi-ku, Sapporo, Hokkaido

Open in Google Maps

Take a bus from Kanjodori-Higashi subway station (Toho Line), about 20-25 minutes; renting a bicycle at the park entrance is recommended given its size.
