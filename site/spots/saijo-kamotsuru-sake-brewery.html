Kamotsuru Sake Brewery

Hiroshima

Kamotsuru's roots trace to the Kimura family, who had been involved in brewing in the Kamo district for generations, but the brewery in its modern form dates to 1873, when the fourth-generation owner studied brewing techniques in Nada and began producing sake under the Kamotsuru name — making it one of the defining names on Saijo's brewery street since the Meiji era. It later became a pioneer of daiginjo — sake milled down to a small fraction of the original rice grain for an exceptionally refined flavor — with its Tokusei Gold label released in 1958. The brewery's profile jumped internationally in 2014 when Prime Minister Shinzo Abe was photographed holding a bottle of Kamotsuru Gold during then-President Barack Obama's visit to Tokyo's Sukiyabashi Jiro restaurant.

The brewery's shop and tasting counter sit within the historic brick-and-timber building on Saijo's brewery street, and visitors can sample a range from everyday table sake up to premium daiginjo, with staff generally willing to explain the differences in production and taste rather than just pouring and moving on.

It's one stop among several on the same street, so most visitors treat it as an anchor point for a broader brewery walk rather than a single destination, but its history and international profile make it a reasonable starting point if you only have time for one.
