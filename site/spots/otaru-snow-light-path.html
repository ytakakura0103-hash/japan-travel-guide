Otaru Snow Light Path

Hokkaido

For roughly ten days each February, Otaru fills its canal-side path and the route of a former Japan National Railways line, the old Temiya Line, with small hand-made snow lanterns and floating glass candles, lit by volunteers each evening. Unlike Sapporo's Snow Festival, which is built around large sculpted structures, the appeal here is intimacy and scale: thousands of small, warm lights rather than a handful of massive centerpieces.

Because the event runs at the same time as the Sapporo Snow Festival, it's common for visitors to combine both in a single Hokkaido winter trip, seeing Sapporo's spectacle by day and Otaru's quieter glow by evening, or vice versa. Illuminations typically run from around 5pm to 9pm each night of the festival.

The canal-side venue draws the largest crowds, especially on weekend evenings, while the former railway line section tends to be noticeably quieter and arguably more atmospheric for a slower walk among the lanterns. Dress for serious cold: Otaru in February is a coastal town with damp, biting wind on top of typical Hokkaido winter temperatures, so this is not an event to underdress for even for a short outdoor stroll.
