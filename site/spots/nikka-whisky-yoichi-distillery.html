Nikka Whisky Yoichi Distillery

Sapporo

After training in Scotland and helping establish Suntory's first distillery, Masataka Taketsuru eventually struck out on his own and founded Nikka in Yoichi, a small coastal town he picked specifically because its cool, humid, sea-facing climate reminded him of the whisky regions of Scotland. The distillery's original stone buildings and coal-fired pot stills, unusual even among Japanese distilleries for still using direct coal firing, are part of what visitors come to see alongside the whisky itself.

Guided and self-guided tour options typically cover the malting, distilling and aging buildings, along with a small museum on Taketsuru and his Scottish wife Rita, whose relationship became well known in Japan after a popular television drama. A tasting session at the end, usually included with the tour, lets visitors sample a few of Nikka's whiskies, though it's worth checking current rules and hedging on details, since drink limits and tasting formats have changed over time.

Because it requires a somewhat involved train journey, the crowds here are notably lighter than more central Hokkaido attractions, and weekday visits in particular tend to be quiet. Whisky fans should check ahead if a specific bottle or tasting flight is the goal, since availability shifts. The distillery grounds themselves, with their stone walls and slate roofs, look distinctly different from typical Hokkaido architecture and are worth photographing regardless of interest in the whisky.

7-6 Kurokawacho, Yoichi, Hokkaido

From Sapporo Station, take the Rapid Airport train to Otaru (about 35 minutes), then transfer to the JR Hakodate Line to Yoichi Station (about 25 minutes); the distillery is a short walk from the station.
