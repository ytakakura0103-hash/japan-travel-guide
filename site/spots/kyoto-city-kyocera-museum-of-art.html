Kyoto City KYOCERA Museum of Art

Kyoto

Opened in 1933, this is generally described as Japan's oldest public art museum still in operation, built in an "Imperial Crown" style that combines a Western-style stone base with Japanese-style roofing, a design common to a handful of major public buildings from that era. A major renovation completed in 2020, funded partly by the Kyocera corporation, added a new sunken glass-walled entrance in the plaza in front of the original building, along with expanded gallery space, while preserving the historic facade largely as it was.

The museum's collection focuses on modern and contemporary Japanese art, particularly work connected to Kyoto's own painting and craft traditions, shown alongside a regular program of special exhibitions that can range from major international loan shows to more local retrospectives, usually ticketed separately from the permanent collection. A visit to the permanent galleries alone takes around an hour; a major special exhibition can extend that considerably.

The museum sits in the Okazaki cultural district alongside Heian Shrine and several other museums, an area generally less crowded than Kyoto's older temple neighborhoods, though a popular touring exhibition can draw significant weekend crowds of its own regardless of the surrounding area's usual pace. Weekday visits or the first hour after opening are the most reliable way to see busy special exhibitions comfortably.

124 Okazaki Enshojicho, Sakyo Ward, Kyoto

Open in Google Maps

8-minute walk from Higashiyama Station (Kyoto Municipal Subway Tozai Line)
