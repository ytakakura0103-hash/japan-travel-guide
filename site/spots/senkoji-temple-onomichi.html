Senkoji Temple

Hiroshima

Senkoji is said to have been founded in 806, and its bright vermillion halls sit partway up the hillside that rises directly behind Onomichi's harbor-front streets, making the temple visible from much of the lower town and, in turn, giving temple visitors sweeping views back down over the city's tiled roofs to the strait and islands beyond. The temple complex includes several halls and a scattering of large, naturally weathered boulders integrated into the grounds, some inscribed or associated with local legend.

Onomichi is sometimes described as a city of temples, with more than two dozen Buddhist temples spread across its hillside neighborhoods, and Senkoji functions as the anchor point of the popular Onomichi Temple Walk, a signposted walking route that links many of them along narrow stone stairways and alleys. Senkoji itself, along with the adjoining Senkoji Park, tends to be the most visited stop on that route given its striking hilltop position and easy access via the Onomichi Ropeway.

The surrounding hillside is also known for cherry blossoms in spring, and the combination of temple, boulders, and layered city-and-sea views makes it one of the more consistently photographed spots in Onomichi.

15-1 Higashitsuchido-cho, Onomichi, Hiroshima Prefecture

Open in Google Maps

Reachable by the Onomichi Ropeway from Nagaeguchi Station near JR Onomichi Station, or via a signposted walking path up the hillside; about 90 minutes to 2 hours from central Hiroshima by JR train.
