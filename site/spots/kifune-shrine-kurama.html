Kifune Shrine & Kurama

Kyoto

Kifune Shrine sits in a narrow mountain valley north of central Kyoto, dedicated to a Shinto deity associated with water, rain, and, by extension, matchmaking; its main approach is a stone stairway lined with vermilion lanterns that's one of the more photographed shrine settings outside the city center. A wooded hiking trail over Mount Kurama connects Kifune to the village of Kurama on the other side, passing Kurama-dera, a mountain temple with its own legends tied to the tengu, mythical mountain spirits.

The full hike between the two villages, done in either direction, generally takes a couple of hours at a moderate pace, including stops at the temple buildings along the way; visitors short on time can instead take the train to one village and a short bus or taxi to the other, skipping the trail itself. Admission to Kurama-dera involves a modest fee, while Kifune Shrine's outer grounds are free to enter.

Because the valley sits higher and shadier than central Kyoto, it's a noticeably cooler place to spend a summer day, and Kifune's riverside restaurants set up platform seating directly over the water in the warmer months for that reason. The area is quiet by Kyoto standards year-round, with the only real bottleneck being weekend hikers on the mountain trail in autumn foliage season.
