Miyajima Omotesando Shopping Street

Hiroshima

Omotesando runs for a few hundred meters between the ferry pier and the approach to Itsukushima Shrine, and it has served as the island's commercial main street for generations, adapting over time from a fishing and pilgrim town's shopping row into today's mix of souvenir shops, food stalls, and craft workshops. It remains largely pedestrian and is one of the few parts of the island with a dense concentration of shops, since most of Miyajima outside this strip is residential, forested, or shrine grounds.

Among its specialties, the street is closely associated with the shamoji, a wooden rice paddle said to have originated on Miyajima in the late 18th century, now sold in every size from kitchen tool to oversized decorative pieces. Food stalls along the street sell grilled and fried oysters, another Hiroshima specialty, as well as the maple-leaf-shaped cakes called momiji manju, often available freshly made and warm.

Because the street funnels essentially all foot traffic between the ferry and the shrine, it can get crowded during midday hours in peak season, and wild deer, which roam freely across the island, are a regular presence looking for food scraps or occasionally nosing at paper bags.

Miyajima-cho, Hatsukaichi, Hiroshima Prefecture
