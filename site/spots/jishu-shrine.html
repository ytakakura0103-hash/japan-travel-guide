Jishu Shrine

Kyoto

Jishu Shrine is a small Shinto shrine within the Kiyomizu-dera temple complex, dedicated to a deity associated with love and good relationships, which makes it a popular stop for visitors hoping for romantic luck, particularly younger travelers and couples. Its best-known feature is a pair of stones set some distance apart in front of the main hall; tradition holds that successfully walking from one to the other with your eyes closed means your wish for love will be granted, while needing guidance along the way suggests the path to it won't be entirely straightforward.

The shrine itself is compact and can be seen in ten to fifteen minutes, making it an easy addition to a Kiyomizu-dera visit rather than a separate trip. There's no separate admission fee beyond what's required to enter the Kiyomizu-dera grounds the shrine sits within.

Because it's folded into the much larger crowds visiting Kiyomizu-dera, Jishu Shrine tends to be busiest at the same times as the main temple, especially around the love stones, where a short queue can form as people wait their turn to attempt the walk. Visiting earlier in the day, before the temple's peak late-morning crowds build up, gives a better chance of trying it without an audience.
