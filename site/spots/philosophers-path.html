Philosopher's Path

Kyoto

The Philosopher's Path follows a narrow canal for close to two kilometers between Ginkaku-ji in the north and the Nanzenji area in the south, lined for most of its length by cherry trees planted decades ago that form a canopy over the water when in bloom. It takes its name from Nishida Kitaro, a Kyoto University philosopher known for walking the route on his way to campus, and the path retains a contemplative, unhurried feel even now, especially away from its two crowded ends.

Walking the full length at a relaxed pace takes somewhere around thirty to forty-five minutes, not counting stops, and there's no fee to walk it. Small cafes, craft shops, and a scattering of temples sit just off the canal, making it easy to stretch the walk into a longer outing.

Cherry blossom season, usually in early April, is by far the busiest and most photographed time, when the path can get quite congested near Ginkaku-ji; visiting on a weekday morning, or walking the path in the opposite direction from the crowds arriving at Ginkaku-ji, tends to leave more breathing room. Outside of cherry season the path is considerably quieter and still pleasant, with fresh green in early summer and some autumn color later in the year. The path connects naturally with Nanzenji, Eikan-do, and Ginkaku-ji at either end.
