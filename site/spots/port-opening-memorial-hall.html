Yokohama Port Opening Memorial Hall (Jack Tower)

Yokohama

Completed in 1917 to commemorate the fiftieth anniversary of Yokohama's port opening (the anniversary fell in 1909, but construction dragged on until 1917), this red-brick hall with its square clock tower is one of three landmark towers that locals nicknamed after playing cards decades ago, King for the Kanagawa prefectural government building, Queen for the Customs House, and Jack for this one. Inside, stained-glass windows depicting the port's history and a grand assembly hall are still used for civic ceremonies, weddings, and public events, which is part of why it feels less like a museum and more like a functioning piece of the city.

Public access varies depending on whether the hall is booked for an event on a given day, so it is worth checking ahead if you want to see the interior stained glass rather than just the exterior. The building survived the 1945 air raids largely intact, giving it a rarer continuity with prewar Yokohama than many of the city's other historic structures.

It sits within easy walking distance of Yokohama Park and the Kanagawa Prefectural Museum, making it simple to fold into a downtown architecture walk. Photographing the three towers together in one outing, spread across a short walk through Kannai, has become a small tradition among Yokohama history buffs.

1-6 Honcho, Naka-ku, Yokohama

Open in Google Maps

About 10 minutes on foot from JR Kannai Station, or about 1 minute from Minatomirai Line Nihon-odori Station (exit 1).
