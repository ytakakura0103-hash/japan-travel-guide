Osaki Hachiman Shrine

Sendai

Date Masamune had this shrine built at the very start of his rule over Sendai, and its main hall is one of the oldest surviving examples of the ornate Momoyama architectural style in the entire Tohoku region, designated a National Treasure. The dark lacquer exterior, punctuated with gold leaf and colorful carved details, was meant to project the wealth and legitimacy of a new domain, and up close the woodwork is dense with painted flowers, waves, and mythical animals.\n\nThe shrine is also Sendai's most important site for Shinto ritual life. It hosts the city's largest hatsumode (New Year's) crowds and, on January 14th, the Dontosai fire festival, in which men run through the cold in loincloths carrying sacred fires to be burned, a striking and very local scene if your dates line up.\n\nOutside of those events, the shrine grounds are calm, and admission to walk the precinct is free. A weekday midday visit lets you actually study the carvings without a crowd; avoid January 1st through 3rd and the Dontosai evening unless the festival atmosphere is what you're after. The approach path is shaded by old trees and pleasant in any season, with a subtle seasonal lift from fresh green leaves in May.

4-6-1 Hachiman, Aoba-ku, Sendai, Miyagi

About 15 minutes by bus from Sendai Station, or a 15-minute walk from Kitayobancho Station on the Tozai subway line.
