Antarctic Research Ship Fuji

Nagoya

The Fuji spent eighteen years breaking ice for Japan's Antarctic research program after its first voyage south in 1965, a bright orange-and-white vessel about 100 meters long capable of pushing through ice up to 80 centimeters thick. When it was finally retired, the ship was towed to Nagoya Port in 1985 rather than scrapped, and it has sat in the Garden Wharf area, opposite the Nagoya Port Aquarium, as a floating museum ever since, complete with a Sikorsky Sea King helicopter still parked on its rear deck.

What sets the Fuji apart from a typical maritime museum is how much of the working ship was left intact rather than converted into display cases. Visitors can walk through crew bunks, the ship's barber shop, the mess hall and other functional spaces exactly as they were used, which gives a much more visceral sense of what eighteen years of Antarctic service actually looked like day to day than a set of photographs would.

It is an easy, inexpensive add-on to a visit to the aquarium and port area, and a good option for families traveling with kids who want something more hands-on than a standard museum.
