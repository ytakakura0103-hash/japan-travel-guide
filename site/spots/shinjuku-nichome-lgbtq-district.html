Shinjuku Ni-chome

Tokyo

Ni-chome holds the world's densest concentration of LGBTQ+ bars, several hundred venues packed into roughly a 300 by 300 meter grid centered on Naka-dori. Most bars are tiny, some seating fewer than ten people, and lean toward a regulars-and-conversation atmosphere rather than large dance clubs, though bigger venues with dance floors exist too. It's widely described as Asia's largest gay district, and today it's genuinely mixed: straight allies and curious first-time visitors are common, especially at the larger, more foreigner-friendly bars, and the area doesn't require insider knowledge to enjoy.

A handful of well-established venues are known for being especially welcoming to newcomers and non-Japanese speakers, including Advocates Bar, an open-air corner spot with no cover charge that functions as an informal community hub, and Arty Farty, a larger club with a dance floor. Smaller, quieter options like Campy!, known for its drag-hosted nights, and GB, a compact and friendly standing bar, are good entry points for a calmer first visit.

A few etiquette notes are worth knowing before a visit: ask before photographing people, since privacy matters more here than in most nightlife districts, and keep in mind that some of the smallest bars are community or identity-specific spaces rather than general tourist venues, so it's worth reading the room, or simply asking, before walking in. There's no particular dress code, and cover charges where they exist are modest, typically a few hundred to around a thousand yen, often bundled with a first drink.
