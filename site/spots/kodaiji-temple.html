Kodaiji Temple

Kyoto

Kodaiji was founded in the early 17th century by Nene, the widow of the warlord Toyotomi Hideyoshi, as a place to pray for his memory after his death, and several of its structures and gardens were built with support from the ruling Tokugawa shogunate despite the political tension between the two families. The temple grounds include a bamboo grove path considerably smaller and quieter than the famous one in Arashiyama, along with karesansui rock gardens and a pond garden originally designed by a noted landscape architect of the era.

Kodaiji is particularly known for its seasonal evening illuminations, held during limited windows in spring cherry blossom season and again in autumn for the maples, when the gardens and reflecting ponds are lit after dark and stay open later than usual. A daytime visit to the main grounds takes about forty-five minutes to an hour; the evening illuminations draw larger crowds and are worth timing carefully, arriving either right at opening or checking whether a quieter window exists on a given evening.

Admission is a modest fee, sometimes with a separate charge during illumination periods. The temple sits a short walk from Kiyomizu-dera and the Ninenzaka-Sannenzaka lanes, making it an easy, less crowded detour on the same afternoon.

526 Shimogawaracho, Higashiyama Ward, Kyoto
