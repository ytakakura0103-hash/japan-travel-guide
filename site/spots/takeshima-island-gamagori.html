Takeshima Island

Nagoya

Takeshima is a small, densely wooded island just off the coast of Gamagori, connected to the mainland by a roughly 387-meter causeway that crosses shallow, calm water. Its rocky outcrops, formed thousands of years ago and rising over 20 meters at their highest points, support an unusually dense and varied plant community for such a small area, over two hundred species by some counts, a diversity significant enough to earn the island National Natural Monument status in 1930.

The island's most distinctive feature is architectural rather than natural: the causeway passes directly beneath a large stone torii gate, one of the only places in Japan where a bridge runs straight through a shrine gateway rather than approaching it from the side. Local tradition holds that couples who cross the causeway together, passing under the torii, will be blessed with good fortune, and the walk is popular for that reason alone. At the island's center sits Yaotomi Shrine, said to date back to 1181 and dedicated to Benzaiten, one of the seven deities of good fortune associated with easy childbirth and marriage.

The short causeway walk and modest island loop make Takeshima an easy, low-effort stop for visitors continuing on to other parts of the Mikawa Bay coastline.
