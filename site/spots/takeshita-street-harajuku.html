Takeshita Street

Tokyo

Takeshita Street is a short, narrow pedestrian lane running from Harajuku Station into the surrounding neighborhood, and for decades it has been the birthplace and testing ground for Tokyo's youth fashion trends. The street is dense with crepe stands, cotton candy vendors, pastel and gothic-lolita fashion boutiques, character goods shops tied to popular anime and games, and photo-sticker booths, all crammed into a stretch that can be walked end to end in a few minutes if it weren't so packed with things to stop for.

The crepe stands near the station entrance are something of a local institution, with rotating seasonal flavors and elaborately decorated versions piled with fruit and cream. Beyond food, the street is a good place to see current Japanese youth style up close, whether that means head-to-toe kawaii outfits or more alternative subcultures, and several shops sell affordable, playful accessories that make easy souvenirs.

Takeshita Street is almost always crowded, but weekend afternoons push it to a genuine crawl, with the narrow width of the street doing the crowd no favors. Visiting on a weekday morning, shortly after shops open, is by far the easiest way to actually move through it comfortably. The street connects at its far end to the quieter, more upscale Omotesando and Cat Street areas, making it easy to continue on to a calmer stretch of Harajuku afterward.
