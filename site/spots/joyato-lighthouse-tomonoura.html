Joyato Lighthouse

Hiroshima

Built in 1859 near the end of the Edo period, the Joyato is a solid stone tower standing at the tip of Tomonoura's harbor, historically lit at night to guide ships safely into port. It stands roughly 11 meters from its underwater stone foundation to its top, and it is generally cited as the tallest (largest) stone lighthouse surviving from the Edo period anywhere in Japan, having continued to function as a navigational aid for many decades after its construction.

The lighthouse sits at the end of a stone breakwater and jetty area that forms one of the most photographed spots in Tomonoura, with the tower, the harbor's fishing boats, and the hills across the water often composed together in a single shot, particularly around sunset. Its position at the harbor mouth also places it directly beside several of the town's other surviving Edo-period port structures, including stepped stone landings and a boat repair dock, which together make up a rare, largely intact set of historic harbor infrastructure.

Because the whole harbor area is small and walkable, the lighthouse is usually seen as part of a short loop around Tomonoura's waterfront rather than a standalone stop, and it remains a working part of the harbor rather than a purely decorative monument.

Tomo-cho, Fukuyama, Hiroshima Prefecture
