Meriken Park

Kobe

Meriken Park occupies a spit of reclaimed land jutting into Kobe Harbor, and it functions as the city's front porch: Port Tower and the Maritime Museum anchor one end, cafes and a hotel line the middle, and a quieter memorial zone sits toward the water. The Port of Kobe Earthquake Memorial Park preserves a short, tilted, cracked section of the original 1995 quay wall exactly as the Great Hanshin earthquake left it, a sobering and rarely crowded corner of an otherwise cheerful park.

The white BE KOBE letters near the park's edge have become one of the most-photographed spots in the city, especially at sunset when the letters catch the light against the bay. Walking the full loop of the park, taking photos, and reading the memorial plaques takes roughly 30 to 45 minutes, longer if you stop for coffee at one of the waterside cafes.

Evenings are noticeably calmer than midday, when tour buses cluster near the tower; the memorial section in particular rarely draws a crowd even during busy periods. If visiting in winter, check whether the Kobe Luminarie light festival route passes nearby, since the park becomes a natural extension of that event's evening foot traffic.
