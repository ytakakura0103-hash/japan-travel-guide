Kure Warship Curry

Hiroshima

Curry became a regular meal aboard Imperial Japanese Navy ships in part because it was practical to prepare in bulk and helped sailors keep track of days at sea, since it was traditionally served on a fixed day of the week, a practice the Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force has continued into the present with each vessel often having its own house recipe (curry is served every Friday). Because Kure has been a major naval base since the Meiji era and remains home to JMSDF facilities today, the city has leaned into this connection, branding a local style of curry rice as "Kaiji Curry" (Kure Kaiji Curry), or JMSDF curry, sold at restaurants across town.

Menus and exact recipes vary by restaurant, since many are modeled on the specific recipe of a particular current vessel, but the format is generally a thick, mild curry roux served over rice with a side salad and glass of milk, echoing standard shipboard navy meal presentation rather than the spicier or more elaborate curries found elsewhere in Japan. Some restaurants display the naval unit or ship their recipe is associated with, and a few operate inside or near the JMSDF Kure Museum and Yamato Museum area.

Because the dish is tied specifically to Kure's identity as a naval city, trying it here carries more context than eating a similar curry elsewhere, and it pairs naturally with a visit to either of the two nearby maritime museums.
