Mitarai Historic Port Town

Hiroshima

Mitarai, on Osakikamijima at the far end of the Tobishima Kaido island chain southeast of Kure, grew wealthy in the Edo period as a waiting harbor where ships timed their passage through the Inland Sea's currents, developing a lively pleasure district of teahouses and merchant houses to serve sailors passing through. When steamship routes and modern shipping bypassed the island in the 20th century, the town's growth essentially stopped, leaving its Edo and Meiji-era streetscape unusually intact and now protected as a national Important Preservation District.

Walking the narrow lanes today, past latticed wooden facades, a preserved public bathhouse, and a small red bridge over the old canal, there's little sense of having to imagine the historic scene — it's simply still there, largely because nothing came along to replace it.

Mitarai sees very few tourists even by the standards of Hiroshima Prefecture's back roads, since reaching it means driving or busing the full length of the Tobishima Kaido chain, but for visitors willing to make the trip, it's about as close as the region gets to an untouched Edo-period port town.
