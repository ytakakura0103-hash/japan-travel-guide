Takehara Historic Townscape

Hiroshima

Takehara grew wealthy during the Edo period through salt production and sake brewing, and the profits show in the merchant district that survives largely intact today — a designated Important Preservation District for Groups of Historic Buildings since 1982. Wooden facades with fine lattice screens, clay-tiled roofs, and narrow lanes barely wide enough for two people to pass give the district a nickname it's earned honestly: the 'Little Kyoto of Aki,' referring to the historical name for this part of Hiroshima Prefecture.\n\nUnlike Kyoto's equivalent districts, Takehara sees a fraction of the foot traffic, so you can walk the main street and its side alleys without constantly stepping around other visitors, and several of the old houses are open to enter, including former merchant residences now run as small museums.\n\nThe district is compact enough to see in two or three hours, making it a workable half-day trip from Hiroshima city or an easy add-on to a visit to Takehara's sake breweries, several of which sit within or just beside the historic streets.

Honmachi, Takehara, Hiroshima
