Tezuka Osamu Manga Museum

Kobe

Osamu Tezuka, often called the godfather of modern manga and anime for works including Astro Boy, Black Jack and Kimba the White Lion, spent his formative years in Takarazuka, and this museum, built with his family's cooperation, sits close to where he lived as a child. Exhibits trace his artistic development from schoolboy notebooks through his professional career, alongside original manuscript pages, character design displays and a small theater showing animation.

A hands-on studio area lets visitors, especially kids, try basic animation and drawing techniques, keeping the museum engaging for a broader age range than a straightforward archival exhibit would be. Because Tezuka's influence touches nearly every major anime and manga creator who followed him, the museum works as a genuine pilgrimage stop for fans, even though it's smaller in scale than some of the anime-focused attractions in Tokyo.

Takarazuka itself, better known for its all-female musical theater troupe, gives the museum an unexpected local context, and it's easy to combine both attractions in a single day trip. The building and grounds reference Tezuka's own character designs in playful architectural details worth looking for as you walk around.

Takarazuka, Hyogo Prefecture

Open in Google Maps

From Sannomiya, take the JR Kobe Line to Amagasaki, then transfer to the JR Fukuchiyama Line (JR Takarazuka Line) to Takarazuka (roughly 50 minutes total, one transfer), or take the Hankyu Kobe and Imazu Lines directly to Takarazuka (about 35 minutes via Nishinomiya-Kitaguchi); the museum is a short walk from Takarazuka Station.
