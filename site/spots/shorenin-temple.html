Shorenin Temple

Kyoto

Shorenin's garden, laid out around ponds and shaded by old camphor trees said to have been planted centuries ago, has the kind of understated, moss-covered elegance typical of many Kyoto temple gardens during the day, but the temple has built a distinct reputation around a very different evening event: a seasonal illumination that lights the garden in blue LED, an unconventional choice compared to the warmer lantern light used at most other temple illuminations, giving Shorenin's spring and autumn night openings a noticeably different, almost otherworldly atmosphere. The daytime visit is calmer and more traditional, with covered verandas overlooking the garden making for a comfortable, unhurried place to sit, and a visit at that time of day generally takes around an hour. The illumination events, held for limited stretches in spring and autumn to coincide with cherry blossoms and maple color respectively, require a separate evening admission and draw a different, often younger crowd specifically for the photography opportunity, so expect a livelier, more crowded atmosphere than the daytime visit. Arriving near the start of the evening session tends to be less crowded than the later hours. The temple's camphor trees, some of the largest and oldest in the city, are worth noting even outside the illumination events, standing well above the surrounding rooftops and giving the entrance a distinct, shaded character year-round. It sits close to Chionin and its other Higashiyama neighbors, making it easy to combine into a single walking route through the area's temples.

69-1 Awataguchi Sanjobocho, Higashiyama Ward, Kyoto
