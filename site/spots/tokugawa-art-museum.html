Tokugawa Art Museum

Nagoya

This museum grew directly out of the private collection of the Owari branch of the Tokugawa family, one of the most powerful samurai lines of the Edo period, and the objects on display reflect that pedigree: ceremonial armor, swords, Noh masks and costumes, tea ceremony utensils, and lacquerware made for daimyo households rather than temples or commoners. Its most celebrated holding is a portion of the Genji Monogatari Emaki, an illustrated handscroll of The Tale of Genji considered a national treasure, though it is fragile enough that it's only exhibited publicly for a brief window each year, usually in autumn.

Admission is moderate and a visit runs one to two hours; the adjoining Tokugawaen garden can be combined with a joint ticket for a fuller half-day.

The museum rarely feels crowded except during the short Genji scroll display period, when fans of classical literature travel specifically to see it — worth checking the schedule if that interests you, and worth avoiding if you'd simply prefer a quiet gallery visit.

1017 Tokugawa-cho, Higashi-ku, Nagoya, Aichi
