Etajima Museum of Naval History

Hiroshima

The Imperial Japanese Navy moved its officer academy from Tokyo to Etajima Island in 1888, and the resulting campus — including an 1893 Officer Candidate School and a grand 1917 Ceremonial Hall, both built with brick imported from Britain, the nation that helped modernize Japan's navy — trained generations of officers through the Second World War. The campus now operates as the JMSDF's First Service School, an active officer training institution, and the historic buildings remain in daily use rather than being preserved as a static museum piece.

Public access is limited to guided tours, run several times daily and more frequently on weekends, which take visitors through the parade grounds, the ceremonial hall's ornate interior, and an Education Reference Museum holding roughly 16,000 naval artifacts, of which about a thousand are on rotating display.

Because it's a working military school rather than a heritage site catering to tourists, the visit has a distinctly formal, unpolished character — no gift shop sheen, just genuine institutional history — reached by a short ferry hop from Kure that most Hiroshima visitors never think to take.
