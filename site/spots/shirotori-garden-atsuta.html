Shirotori Garden

Nagoya

Opened in 1991 next to Atsuta Shrine, Shirotori Garden is a chisen-kaiyu-shiki, or pond-and-stroll style, garden designed around a single idea: the journey of water from mountain to sea. A hill near the entrance stands in for Mount Ontake, a stream represents the Kiso River, and the central pond plays the part of Ise Bay, so a full circuit of the garden retraces, in miniature, the landscape that surrounds Nagoya itself.

Within that framework are some genuinely well-crafted details: a sukiya-style teahouse called Seiutei where visitors can have matcha overlooking the pond, and a smaller enclosed section called the Shioiri Garden where the water level rises and falls on an artificial one-hour tidal cycle, mimicking the bay it represents. Around 1,500 Japanese maples and enkianthus shrubs turn the garden into one of the city's better autumn-color spots, usually with far fewer visitors than the more famous gardens near Nagoya Castle.

Because it sits just a few minutes from Atsuta Shrine, Shirotori Garden works well as a quieter second stop after visiting the shrine's busier grounds, offering a very different, more contemplative pace.
