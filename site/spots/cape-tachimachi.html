Local Pick

Cape Tachimachi

Hokkaido

Cape Tachimachi sits on the southern side of Mt. Hakodate, where a roughly 30-meter cliff drops to the Tsugaru Strait, and the observation area gives a wide, mostly unobstructed panorama that on clear days extends across the strait to the Shimokita and Tsugaru peninsulas of Aomori, Honshu. It's a very different vantage point from the famous Mt. Hakodate night view, which faces the city; this one faces open water.

Rugosa roses (hamanasu), Hokkaido's prefectural flower, bloom along the cape in summer, and a small rest area nearby sells simple snacks like grilled corn, oden, and soft ice cream, making it easy to linger rather than just snap a photo and leave. There's free parking for those driving, though it can close early during heavy winter snow.

Getting there by public transit involves a tram ride to Yachigashira followed by a roughly 15-20 minute walk, part of which passes through a cemetery, which keeps casual foot traffic lower than at Hakodate's more central viewpoints; it's a spot locals with cars visit more than tour groups.

Winter can bring dramatic, wind-whipped views of the strait, while summer's clearer, calmer air generally gives the best chance of seeing all the way to Honshu.
