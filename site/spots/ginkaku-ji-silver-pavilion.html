Ginkaku-ji (Silver Pavilion)

Kyoto

Despite its name, Ginkaku-ji was never covered in silver; the shogun Ashikaga Yoshimasa who commissioned it in the late 15th century may have intended a silver finish, but it was never applied, whether for lack of funds or by design remains debated. What survives instead is a restrained two-story wooden pavilion set among meticulously maintained gardens, including a raked sand mound known as the "moon-viewing platform" and a sea of raked gravel meant to evoke the surface of water. The pared-back aesthetic here, favoring texture and suggestion over ornament, is often cited as a founding reference point for wabi-sabi.

The temple grounds also rise up a wooded hillside behind the main buildings, with a path leading to an overlook with a view back down over the pavilion and across the city, an area many first-time visitors miss if they only walk the flat garden loop. A full visit, including the hillside path, takes roughly forty-five minutes to an hour. Admission involves a modest fee.

Ginkaku-ji draws heavy crowds, particularly since it anchors one end of the Philosopher's Path; visiting right at opening is the clearest way to see the garden without a dense crowd on the narrow paths. It connects naturally with a walk south along the Philosopher's Path toward Nanzenji.

2 Ginkakujicho, Sakyo Ward, Kyoto
