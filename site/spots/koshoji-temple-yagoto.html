Kosho-ji Temple, Yagoto

Nagoya

Yagoto is better known to most visitors as a university district than a temple town, which is exactly why Kosho-ji tends to get skipped. The temple sits on a low rise above the neighborhood's shopping streets, its five-story pagoda visible over the rooftops long before you reach the gate. Multi-story pagodas of this type are rare survivors in a city that lost most of its older wooden architecture to wartime bombing and postwar redevelopment, which makes this one worth the climb even for visitors who have already seen Nagoya's bigger, more famous shrines.

The grounds are quiet on an ordinary weekday: a Kannon hall, stone lanterns, and paths shaded by trees that turn color in late autumn. Because Kosho-ji functions as a working neighborhood temple rather than a tourist attraction, there is no admission fee to walk the grounds and no crowd management to navigate, just the ordinary rhythm of a temple that locals actually use.

Afterward, the surrounding Yagoto area has a dense cluster of cafes and bakeries catering to Nanzan University students, making it easy to turn the visit into a longer, more relaxed afternoon than the temple alone would suggest.
