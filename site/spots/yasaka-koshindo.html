Yasaka Koshindo

Kyoto

Yasaka Koshindo is a small temple tucked into the Higashiyama backstreets near Kiyomizu-dera and Yasaka Shrine, easy to walk past if not for the mass of colorful cloth balls, called kukurizaru, hanging from its eaves and surrounding structures. Each ball, shaped like a bound monkey, represents a wish that a visitor has symbolically "tied down" by giving up one desire or bad habit, following the temple's Koshin faith tradition around self-restraint. Visitors can buy and write their own kukurizaru to add to the display, which grows more visually dense as more are added.

Because the temple is compact and built around a single striking visual, most visits are short, ten to fifteen minutes, largely spent taking photos of the colorful balls against the temple's dark wood and stone. There's no significant fee to enter, though buying a kukurizaru or omamori involves a small cost.

Its photogenic quality has made it one of the more Instagrammed spots in Higashiyama, so despite its size it can get genuinely crowded at peak times, particularly midday on weekends, with a short wait sometimes needed just to photograph the main wall of kukurizaru without other visitors in frame. An early morning or later afternoon visit, slightly off the main Kiyomizu-dera rush, tends to be considerably calmer.

390 Kanenzonocho, Higashiyama Ward, Kyoto

Open in Google Maps

10-minute walk from Kiyomizu-Gojo Station (Keihan Main Line)
