Tokyo Tower

Tokyo

Tokyo Tower, completed in 1958, was explicitly modeled on the Eiffel Tower but built taller, standing 333 meters and painted in the white-and-international-orange pattern required at the time for tall structures under aviation safety rules, a color scheme that has since become part of its identity. It served for decades as Tokyo's primary broadcasting tower before Tokyo Skytree took over that role, and it remains a well-loved, slightly retro landmark rather than a purely functional structure today.

The tower has two observation levels: a main deck around 150 meters up, and a smaller top deck near 250 meters that requires a separate, appointment-based ticket. The main deck includes a small glass floor panel, letting visitors look straight down at the streets and buildings below, a popular if slightly nerve-testing photo spot. At the tower's base, a multi-floor complex called FootTown has shops, restaurants, and a small aquarium, enough to fill time before or after going up.

Tokyo Tower's exterior lighting changes nightly and for special occasions and holidays, making it a recognizable presence across much of central Tokyo after dark. Weekday mornings tend to be the calmest time to go up, while weekends and evenings draw larger crowds, especially around sunset. An hour or two covers the main deck comfortably; the top deck experience adds more time given its separate ticketing process.
