Yokohama Customs Museum (Queen's Tower)

Yokohama

Housed inside the Yokohama Customs building, whose green-domed tower is the Queen of the local trio of nicknamed landmark towers, this small museum is free to enter and covers a topic most visitors never think to ask about: how customs enforcement actually works at one of Japan's busiest ports. Displays include real seized items, from disguised drug smuggling containers to counterfeit goods, along with the history of Yokohama Customs since the port's 1859 opening.

It is a quick visit, generally well under an hour, and skews toward a slightly quirky, procedural kind of interesting rather than grand historical narrative, more true-crime museum than art gallery. An observation area near the top of the building offers a decent harbor view as a bonus, since the tower itself is one of the taller structures in this older part of the port district.

Because it operates inside an active government building, opening hours are more limited than at typical museums and it closes on weekends and public holidays, so plan a weekday visit. It pairs naturally with a walk past the Port Opening Memorial Hall and Zou no Hana Park, both a short distance away, for a loop through Yokohama's historic customs and harbor district.

1-1 Kaigan-dori, Naka-ku, Yokohama
