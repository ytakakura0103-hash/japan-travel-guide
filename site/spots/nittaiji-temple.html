Nittaiji Temple

Nagoya

Nittaiji's name literally combines the characters for Japan and Thailand, reflecting its origin: the temple was purpose-built in the early twentieth century to enshrine relics of the Buddha presented as a gift from the Thai royal family, an arrangement that makes it unaffiliated with any single Buddhist sect in Japan, unusually. That history shows up in small architectural details around the grounds, including a five-story pagoda and structures that nod toward Thai design rather than following a purely Japanese temple template.

The grounds are free to enter and can be seen in twenty to thirty minutes, though the calm, spacious layout makes it easy to slow down and just sit for a while under the trees.

It draws far fewer visitors than Osu Kannon or Atsuta Shrine, so even weekends feel unhurried; pairing it with a walk down the nearby Kakuozan shopping street makes for a relaxed half day. Cherry blossoms and autumn foliage on the grounds are both worth checking the season for, since neither draws the crowds you'd expect at a more famous cherry-blossom spot.
