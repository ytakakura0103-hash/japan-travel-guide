Matsuzakaya Nagoya

Nagoya

Matsuzakaya's lineage traces back to 1611, when a samurai named Ito Yudo opened a kimono and dry-goods shop in Nagoya's castle town, making it one of the oldest continuously operating retail brands in Japan. The Nagoya store became a modern department store in 1910, the city's first, and by 1937 had expanded to a scale that at the time surpassed even its own flagship store in Tokyo's Ueno district. Today the complex spans three interconnected buildings, Main, North, and South, with a combined sales floor area that multiple Japanese retail sources confirm as the largest of any single department store in the country.

A particular highlight for visitors is the store's rooftop amusement park, a genuinely rare feature among Japanese department stores today, which closed for roughly nine months and reopened in March 2025 after its first full renovation in 47 years. The refresh kept several of the original retro coin-operated rides intact alongside new attractions, deliberately preserving the slightly nostalgic, small-scale charm that made the original rooftop park a local fixture rather than replacing it with something generic.

The South Building is currently undergoing a separate, larger redevelopment into a joint Matsuzakaya-PARCO facility, expected to reopen in spring 2027, so parts of that building may be under construction or operating with a reduced tenant lineup depending on when a visit falls; the Main and North Buildings continue operating normally throughout. Checking current South Building access before planning a visit around it is worth doing during this transition period.
