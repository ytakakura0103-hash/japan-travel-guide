Naruto Whirlpools via Uzu-no-Michi

Kobe

The Naruto Strait, between Awaji Island and Shikoku, produces some of the largest tidal whirlpools in the world, driven by the dramatic difference in sea level between the Seto Inland Sea and the Pacific as tides shift. Uzu-no-Michi is a walkway built onto the underside of the Onaruto Bridge's road deck, with sections of reinforced glass floor letting visitors look straight down at the swirling water some 45 meters below.

Whirlpool size and intensity depend heavily on the tidal cycle, with the biggest swirls forming around peak spring tides a few days each month; checking a tide chart before visiting makes a real difference to the experience, since a mistimed visit can mean calm water and no whirlpools at all. Sightseeing boats depart from both the Awaji and Naruto sides for a closer, on-the-water view as an alternative or complement to the walkway.

Because this is a day trip requiring a bridge crossing onto Awaji Island, it sees far fewer visitors than Kobe's central sights, mostly domestic tourists and a smaller number of independent international travelers willing to rent a car or take a direct bus.

From Sannomiya or Kobe Sannomiya Bus Terminal, take a highway bus across the Akashi Kaikyo Bridge and onward across the Onaruto Bridge to the Naruto Koen-guchi bus stop in Naruto, Tokushima Prefecture (roughly 1.5 hours total); a car is otherwise the most flexible option.
