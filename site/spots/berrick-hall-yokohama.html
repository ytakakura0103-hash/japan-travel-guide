Berrick Hall

Yokohama

Designed in 1930 by an American architect for a British trading company employee, Berrick Hall is generally described as the largest of the pre-war foreign residences still standing in Yamate, and its Spanish-influenced style, with arched windows and a stucco exterior, sets it apart from the more austere Western houses elsewhere on the bluff. The interior has been restored to something close to its original appearance, including furnishings and fixtures that give a real sense of upper-class foreign residential life in prewar Yokohama.

Entry is free, and the house is compact enough to see thoroughly in twenty to thirty minutes, making it easy to combine with several other Yamate houses in a single afternoon without any one stop taking too long. Because it is one of the better-known houses on the standard Yamate walking route, weekend afternoons can bring small tour groups through, so a weekday or early visit tends to be calmer.

Its garden, though smaller than the nearby Italian Garden, is also worth a slow look, and the house's elevated position gives partial views out over the surrounding neighborhood, adding a bit of scenery to what is primarily an architectural stop.

Yamate-cho, Naka-ku, Yokohama
