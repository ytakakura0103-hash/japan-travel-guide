Hitsujigaoka Observation Hill

Hokkaido

Hitsujigaoka is a sheep-pasture hill on Sapporo's southeastern edge, best known for the statue of William S. Clark, the American educator whose parting words to Hokkaido's early agricultural students, sometimes translated as "Boys, be ambitious," became a local motto. The statue itself is a popular photo stop, but the wider draw is the open view across the Sapporo plain toward the city skyline and, on clear days, distant mountains.

The grounds include a small visitor center with local snacks and souvenirs, and actual sheep graze on the hillside in warmer months, which gives the spot a working-farm feel rather than a purely manufactured viewpoint. It's a fairly quick visit, generally under an hour unless you linger for the view.

Because it sits outside the subway network and requires a bus transfer, it gets a fraction of the visitors that the city-center attractions do, and even the Clark statue rarely has a real line for photos. Late afternoon light works well for the skyline view.

Winter view is often the clearest of the year on cold, dry days, while snow-covered pastures and occasional illuminations add a different character than the green summer hillside.

1 Hitsujigaoka, Toyohira-ku, Sapporo, Hokkaido 062-0045

Open in Google Maps

About 30-40 minutes from Sapporo Station: subway to Fukuzumi Station on the Toho Line, then a local bus (about 10 minutes) to Hitsujigaoka Tenboudai.
