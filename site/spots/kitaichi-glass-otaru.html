Kitaichi Glass Otaru

Hokkaido

Otaru's glassmaking history began with practical objects, floats and lamps for the herring fishing industry, before evolving into the decorative glassware the city is now known for. Kitaichi Glass, and particularly its No. 3 hall (Sangokan), occupies a converted Meiji-era warehouse originally used to store herring-processing oil, and the building itself, with its heavy timber beams and stone walls, is as much a reason to visit as the glassware inside.

The main hall's ceiling is strung with hundreds of oil-lamp-style glass chandeliers, all lit rather than displayed under standard lighting, which gives the interior a warm, amber glow that photographs distinctly differently from a typical retail space. Beyond the main shop, a cafe and smaller specialty rooms sell everything from affordable souvenir pieces to higher-end handblown work.

Because it sits on Sakaimachi Street, one of Otaru's most visited blocks, it does draw real crowds, particularly around midday tour bus arrivals; visiting soon after opening or later in the afternoon noticeably thins the lines.

The glass itself is a year-round product, but the warehouse's dim, lamp-lit interior is an especially welcome stop on Otaru's colder or wetter days, when the golden lighting feels more like a deliberate escape than a shopping errand.

7-26 Sakaimachi, Otaru, Hokkaido 047-0027
