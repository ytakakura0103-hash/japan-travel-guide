BLUE DOG Monument

Hiroshima

The BLUE DOG Monument sits on Etajima's coastline, a sculpture by a Hiroshima-born artist positioned to frame the open sky and sea behind it, a simple but effective setup that Etajima's city tourism office has actively promoted as one of the area's best photo spots. Its appeal is straightforward: a striking piece of public art set against an uncluttered horizon, the kind of composition that photographs well regardless of the season or time of day.

Etajima itself, already covered elsewhere in this guide for its naval history and coastal cycling route, offers a quieter, more relaxed alternative to central Hiroshima, and the monument gives visitors a specific, easy-to-find destination within that broader island visit. At roughly thirty minutes from central Hiroshima, it's close enough to add on without derailing a day built around the city's main sights.

Combining a stop at the monument with Etajima's coastal cycling route or its naval history museum makes for a fuller day on the island, rounding out a visit that might otherwise be built around just one of the island's several distinct attractions.
