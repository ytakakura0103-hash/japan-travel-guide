Naruko Onsen

Sendai

Naruko is one of Miyagi's classic onsen towns, notable among Japanese hot spring enthusiasts for the unusual range of water types found within a small area, locally said to include several of the eleven officially recognized categories of hot spring mineral content, giving different baths in town noticeably different colors, smells, and textures. It's a smaller, more traditional-feeling town than Akiu, with older wooden ryokan and a main street that still moves at an unhurried pace.

Beyond the baths, Naruko is considered the birthplace of kokeshi, the simple, cylindrical wooden dolls with painted faces that have become a recognizable symbol of Tohoku folk craft, and the town's identity is wrapped up in that history as much as its water. Free or low-cost footbaths are scattered through the town center, a good way to sample the onsen character without booking a full soak.

Day-use baths at several ryokan are available for a modest fee and a set time window. Because the JR Rikuu-to Line connecting Naruko to areas further west has faced service disruptions in recent years, it's worth confirming current train schedules before planning transit through the town. Autumn, when the nearby gorge turns brilliant red and gold, is by far the busiest season for the whole area; a summer or early spring visit trades some of that color for a much quieter town.
