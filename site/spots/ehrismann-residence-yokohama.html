Ehrismann Residence

Yokohama

Built for a Swiss trading company representative in the 1920s, this house was designed by Antonin Raymond, an architect better known for later, more modernist work in Tokyo, which makes the residence an interesting early example of his style before his aesthetic shifted. Its relatively simple, functional lines stand out next to the more ornate mansions elsewhere on the Yamate bluff, and the building has been preserved with much of its original layout intact.\n\nA small cafe operates inside part of the house, occupying what was once a dining or living space, giving visitors an unusual chance to actually sit inside a preserved foreign residence rather than only walk through it as a museum. It is a low-key, easy-to-miss detail among Yamate's more famous houses, and it draws noticeably fewer visitors than Berrick Hall or the Italian Garden despite sitting close by.\n\nBecause of that relative quiet, it is a good place to pause partway through a longer Yamate walking route, and pairing a coffee here with a visit to nearby Motomachi Park or Berrick Hall makes for an easy, unhurried stretch of the itinerary.

Yamate-cho, Naka-ku, Yokohama

Open in Google Maps

8-minute walk from Ishikawacho Station, near Motomachi Park
