Furaibo

Nagoya

Tebasaki, double-fried chicken wings seasoned with a sweet-spicy glaze and a dusting of sesame and pepper, is one of Nagoya's best-known bar food exports, and Furaibo is generally regarded as the restaurant that originated the style, growing from a single izakaya into a small regional chain. The wings are eaten by hand, ideally with cold beer, and the appeal lies in the contrast between the crisp skin and the sticky, spicy glaze underneath.

An order of wings is inexpensive by restaurant standards, and the atmosphere is casual izakaya rather than formal dining, so a meal can be as quick as thirty minutes or stretch into a longer evening of drinks and shared plates.

Evenings, particularly weekends, bring a lively after-work crowd and some wait for a table at the busier locations; an early dinner or a weekday visit avoids most of that. Ordering extra napkins upfront is a practical tip, since the wings are eaten with fingers and the glaze is genuinely sticky.
