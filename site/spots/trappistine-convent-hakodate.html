Trappistine Convent

Sapporo

Established by French nuns in the 1890s, this convent belongs to the Trappist order, known for a contemplative life built around silence, prayer and manual labor, and it remains an active, enclosed community rather than a historical relic. Because the sisters live a cloistered life, visitors cannot enter the convent itself; the appeal for outside visitors is really the grounds, a small chapel area, and, most of all, the gate shop selling butter cookies, candies and dairy products made using techniques the community has kept largely unchanged for generations.\n\nIt sits well outside Hakodate's main sightseeing loop, on the edge of the city where the built-up area gives way to farmland, which gives the whole site a noticeably calmer, more contemplative feel than downtown Motomachi or the waterfront district, in keeping with its purpose.\n\nBecause the actual visitable area is small, a stop here typically takes well under an hour, making it a reasonable add-on to a wider loop through Hakodate's outskirts rather than a dedicated half-day trip on its own. There's no strong seasonal draw beyond general Hokkaido weather considerations, though the surrounding rural scenery does shift with the seasons like anywhere else in the region.

3 Chome Nakanocho, Hakodate, Hokkaido

About a 30-minute bus ride from Hakodate Station; the cloister is closed to visitors, with only the grounds and gate shop open to the public.
