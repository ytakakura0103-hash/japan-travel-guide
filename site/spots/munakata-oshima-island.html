Oshima Island, Munakata

Fukuoka

Oshima sits offshore from the Munakata coast and is reached by a short ferry crossing, making it a natural extension for anyone who has already visited the mainland Munakata Taisha shrine, since Oshima holds the Nakatsu-miya shrine, the second of the three linked shrines that together form the UNESCO-listed Munakata Taisha complex. The island itself is small enough to explore substantially on foot or rented bicycle in a few hours, with a scattering of fishing houses, a lighthouse, and coastal lookout points.

The island's most distinctive spot is a clifftop lookout facing Okinoshima, the remote, still tightly restricted third shrine island roughly 50 kilometers further out to sea, where ancient offerings gave rise to the treasures now displayed at the Shinpokan hall on the mainland. Standing at this lookout, with nothing but open water toward the horizon, is as close as most visitors will ever get to Okinoshima itself, since the island bars general public access.

Because reaching Oshima requires a deliberate ferry trip on top of an already out-of-the-way visit to Munakata, it remains a genuinely quiet destination, visited mostly by pilgrims completing the full three-shrine circuit and by a handful of hikers and cyclists. Ferry schedules are limited, so checking current departure times before planning the day is essential.
