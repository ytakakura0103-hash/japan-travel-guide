Former Imperial Japanese Naval Academy, Etajima

Hiroshima

The Imperial Japanese Naval Academy was established on Etajima, an island in Hiroshima Bay, in 1888, and it trained the country's naval officers through to the end of the Second World War, including figures such as Admiral Isoroku Yamamoto and Admiral Heihachiro Togo, both covered in the on-site museum's exhibits. After the war the campus continued in a related role as the First Service School of the Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force, which still trains personnel there today, meaning visitors are touring an active military education facility rather than a purely historic site.

The centerpiece building is a large red-brick structure completed in 1893 to a design by a British architect, built using bricks imported from England, and it remains in use for classrooms and living quarters. The grounds also include the wooden residence built in 1920 for Prince Takamatsu during his own naval service, along with the Museum of Naval History housing ship models, photographs, and personal items connected to the academy's graduates.

Visits are only possible via a guided tour, roughly 90 minutes long and led by a former JMSDF member, run on a set daily schedule with individual visitors generally not needing a reservation, though groups do. Photography inside the museum is not permitted, and admission is free; an on-site restaurant also serves navy-style curry.

1-1 Enokibuchi, Etajima, Hiroshima Prefecture
