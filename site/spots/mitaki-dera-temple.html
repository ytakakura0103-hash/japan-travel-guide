Local Pick

Mitaki-dera Temple

Hiroshima

Mitaki-dera was founded in the early 9th century and takes its name, meaning "three waterfalls temple," from a trio of falls that run through its wooded grounds on the slopes of Mount Mitaki, just west of central Hiroshima. Stone-carved Buddhist statues line the paths between the falls and the temple's halls, and the overall setting, thick with trees and running water, feels distinctly different from the flat, open memorial sites downtown.

The temple's most distinctive structure is a two-story pagoda originally built in 1526 at a shrine in Wakayama Prefecture and moved to Mitaki-dera in 1951 specifically to help console the spirits of those killed in the atomic bombing. Water from the temple's three waterfalls has also been used as a symbolic offering during Hiroshima's official Peace Memorial Ceremony, tying this quiet mountainside site directly back to the city's central historical event even though it sits several kilometers away.

Mitaki-dera is well known locally as one of the better spots in Hiroshima for autumn foliage, with maple color typically peaking around mid-November, and for spring cherry blossoms, drawing weekend crowds during those windows while remaining fairly calm the rest of the year.

411 Mitakiyama, Nishi Ward, Hiroshima, Hiroshima Prefecture

Open in Google Maps

About 15 to 20 minutes on foot uphill from JR Mitaki Station (JR Kabe Line), which is roughly 10 minutes by train from Hiroshima Station.
