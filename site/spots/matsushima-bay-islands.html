Matsushima Bay

Sendai

Matsushima has been ranked among Japan's Three Great Views for centuries, alongside Miyajima near Hiroshima and Amanohashidate in Kyoto Prefecture, and the reputation holds up: the bay is dotted with something like 260 small islands and islets, most topped with wind-sculpted pines, creating a layered, almost painted-looking seascape from nearly any shoreline vantage point. The 17th-century haiku master Matsuo Basho is said to have been so struck by the view that he skipped writing a poem about it entirely, an anecdote locals bring up often and with obvious pride.

The bay works well as a loose base for a half or full day, combining a boat cruise among the islands with temple visits at Zuiganji and Entsuin, the small red-bridged islet of Godaido, and the longer walk out to Fukuurajima. Shoreline restaurants specialize in the bay's oysters and other seafood, giving the visit a food dimension beyond scenery.

There's no cost to view the bay itself from the shore, though boats, temples, and the Fukuurajima bridge each charge modest separate fees. The waterfront gets busy with tour groups around midday, especially near the main pier; walking the shoreline early morning or heading first to Entsuin or Oshima, which draw fewer visitors than the main pier area, gives a calmer first impression. Cherry blossoms in April and snow-dusted pines in January are both well-regarded, less crowded alternatives to the busier autumn foliage season.
