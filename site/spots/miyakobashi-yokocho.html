Local Pick

Miyakobashi Shotengai (Noge's Harmonica Alley)

Yokohama

Built in the 1960s to formally house vendors displaced ahead of the Tokyo Olympics, this crescent-shaped building follows the bend of the Ooka River for about ninety meters, its narrow bays lined up so tightly the whole strip earned the nickname harmonica alley. It survives today as one of the few registered historic postwar buildings in the city, and its curved concrete form is genuinely unlike anything else in central Yokohama.

Inside, dozens of counter-only bars and tiny izakaya seat maybe six to ten people each, so a night out here means squeezing in shoulder to shoulder with whoever else is drinking. Menus and prices vary wildly by shop, and English is not a given, so this suits travelers comfortable with a bit of pointing and improvising rather than those wanting a curated experience.

The surrounding Noge district has been Yokohama's blue-collar drinking quarter since the postwar black-market days, and the energy after dark, lanterns, grilled skewer smoke, salarymen spilling onto the narrow lanes, feels a world away from the polished waterfront a few train stops east. Early evening on a weekday is easiest for a first-timer; weekend nights get lively and some shops fill up fast.

Miyagawa-cho, Naka-ku, Yokohama

Open in Google Maps

About 5 minutes on foot from JR/Yokohama Municipal Subway Sakuragicho Station, or 8 minutes from Keikyu Hinodecho Station.
