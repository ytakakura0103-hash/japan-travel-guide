Shikanoshima Island

Fukuoka

Shikanoshima sits at the mouth of Hakata Bay, linked to the mainland by a sand-and-pebble sandbar known as a tombolo, a geographic feature that lets visitors walk or drive straight across without a boat if approaching from that side. Its main claim to fame is the 1784 discovery of an ancient gold seal, thought to have been a diplomatic gift from a Chinese emperor to an early Japanese ruler; the actual seal is now kept at Fukuoka City Museum, but a small free museum on the island covers the story and the island's maritime history in more depth.

Beyond the history, the island offers coastal scenery and cycling routes along the water, a pleasant contrast to the denser city center, and a handful of small seafood restaurants near the harbor.

Getting there involves a genuine trip, whether by ferry from Hakata Port, a combination of JR train and bus, or driving across the causeway, so it works best as a half-day or full-day excursion rather than a quick add-on. Weekday visits tend to be quiet outside of local fishing and beach activity; the small museum keeps limited hours and is closed on Mondays.

Shikanoshima, Higashi Ward, Fukuoka
