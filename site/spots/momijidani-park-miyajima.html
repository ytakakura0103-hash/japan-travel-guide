Momijidani Park

Hiroshima

Momijidani Park sits at the foot of Mount Misen, a short walk inland from Itsukushima Shrine, and takes its name directly from the maple trees planted throughout the valley, many of which are estimated to be well over a century old. A stream runs through the park crossed by several small bridges, and wild deer wander freely across its lawns and paths, generally undisturbed by visitors and reasonably comfortable being photographed at a respectful distance.\n\nThe park is most crowded, and arguably most striking, during autumn foliage season, typically peaking in mid-to-late November, when the maple canopy turns deep red and orange and draws large numbers of both domestic and international visitors. Outside that window it remains a pleasant, shaded rest stop, particularly useful in summer given the relative lack of shade around the shrine and torii areas closer to the water.\n\nThe lower station of the Mount Misen ropeway sits at the edge of the park, so many visitors pass through Momijidani anyway on their way up the mountain, making it easy to combine with a ropeway trip rather than requiring a separate detour.

Miyajima-cho, Hatsukaichi, Hiroshima Prefecture

About a 15-minute walk from Miyajima pier, or a short bus ride toward the ropeway station.
