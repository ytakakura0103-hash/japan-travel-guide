Shomyoji Temple

Yokohama

Founded in the thirteenth century by Hojo Sanetoki, a regional lord and dedicated scholar who also established the adjacent Kanazawa Bunko library, Shomyoji has anchored the Kanazawa-Hakkei district for over seven centuries, its Jodo-sect halls set behind a pond crossed by a distinctive curved vermillion bridge that has become one of the more photographed quiet spots in eastern Yokohama. The garden layout, with the pond and bridge as its centerpiece, follows a classical Pure Land Buddhist design meant to evoke a paradise garden.

Seasonal plantings give the grounds a changing character across the year, irises and lotus flowers through the summer months, plum and cherry blossoms in late winter and spring, each drawing a modest but devoted following of local photographers rather than tour groups. The temple remains an active place of worship, so visits should stay respectful of that ongoing use rather than treating it purely as a photo backdrop.

Kanazawa-Hakkei, the surrounding neighborhood, takes its name from a historical set of eight scenic views once celebrated in poetry, of which the temple and its pond were traditionally counted among the finest, a reminder that this quiet corner of the city was considered scenic long before Minato Mirai existed.

212 Kanazawa-cho, Kanazawa-ku, Yokohama
