Tokugawaen Garden

Nagoya

Once part of the Owari Tokugawa family's estate, this garden was largely destroyed in the Second World War and later reconstructed to evoke the style of a traditional daimyo strolling garden, with a central pond, a man-made waterfall, and paths that reveal new framed views at each turn rather than one open vista. It sits directly beside the Tokugawa Art Museum, and the two are often visited together on a joint ticket.

Admission is modest and the garden itself can be walked in under an hour, though benches along the pond make it easy to slow down and just sit for a while. A traditional building on the grounds occasionally hosts tea ceremony experiences or seasonal events.

Autumn foliage around the pond and waterfall is the garden's standout season, drawing noticeably more visitors than a typical weekday; spring brings a quieter but still attractive show of blossoms. Late afternoon light on the water tends to be the most photogenic and coincides with a natural lull in visitor numbers before closing.

1001 Tokugawa-cho, Higashi-ku, Nagoya, Aichi
