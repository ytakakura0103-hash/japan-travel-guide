Local Pick

Kinkasan (Koganeyama Shrine)

Sendai

Kinkasan is a small mountainous island considered one of the great power spots of the Tohoku region, home to Koganeyama Shrine, dedicated to a deity of wealth and prosperity. Local tradition holds that visiting the island three years in a row secures financial good fortune for life, a belief still taken seriously enough to draw dedicated pilgrims alongside more casual visitors. The entire island is treated as sacred ground, and roughly wild populations of sika deer and Japanese macaques roam freely across it, adding a genuinely untamed feel rare this close to a shrine complex.

Beyond the shrine itself, hiking trails climb toward the island's summit, offering sweeping ocean views back toward the Oshika Peninsula and out into the Pacific, though the terrain is steep enough that a full summit hike takes real time and reasonable fitness.

Reaching Kinkasan takes real commitment: a ferry runs from Ayukawa port, itself a couple of hours from Sendai, and sailings are limited and weather-dependent, so this works best as a planned full-day trip rather than a casual add-on. There's a modest fee both for the ferry and for shrine grounds access. Because of how much effort it takes to get here, the island stays genuinely uncrowded even in good weather, one of the least touristed sacred sites of its stature in Japan. Early summer and autumn tend to have the most reliable ferry conditions.
