Kozanji Temple

Kyoto

Kozanji sits in a remote, forested part of northwestern Kyoto known as Toganoo, distant enough from the city center that it draws a fraction of the visitors seen at more central temples, despite holding two claims that would headline almost any other site: Japan's oldest tea garden, planted with seeds said to have been brought back from China by the monk Myoe in the 13th century, and the original Choju-giga scrolls, a set of playful ink paintings depicting frogs, rabbits, and monkeys behaving like humans, often cited as a spiritual ancestor of modern manga and animation. The original scrolls are now kept in a museum rather than displayed at the temple itself, but high-quality reproductions are on view in the temple's Sekisui-in hall, giving visitors a real sense of the work's humor and looseness even without the originals present. The tea garden, a small fenced plot rather than a sprawling estate, is a modest but genuinely significant historical site for anyone interested in the history of Japanese tea culture. A visit to the grounds, including the hall and garden, typically takes about an hour. Getting there requires a long bus ride from Kyoto Station, making this a half-day commitment at minimum. That distance is exactly what keeps the temple quiet even during Kyoto's busiest seasons, and autumn maples here, considered among the finest in the region, can be enjoyed with noticeably less crowding than closer, more famous foliage spots.

8 Umegahata Toganoocho, Ukyo Ward, Kyoto
