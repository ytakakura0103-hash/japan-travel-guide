Toyokawa Inari

Nagoya

Despite its name and its thousand-plus fox statues, a symbol most visitors associate with Shinto Inari shrines, Toyokawa Inari is technically a Soto Zen Buddhist temple, formally named Myogon-ji, whose guardian deity happens to be a fox-riding form of the bodhisattva Dakiniten. That overlap between Buddhist and folk-Shinto imagery makes it genuinely distinctive among Japan's major Inari worship sites, and it is counted, alongside Kyoto's Fushimi Inari and Saga's Yutoku Inari, among the country's three great Inari pilgrimage destinations.

The temple's extensive grounds are lined with row after row of stone fox statues of every size, many donated by worshippers over generations seeking success in business, a common association with Inari devotion. A long approach lined with shops selling inari-zushi, the fried tofu pocket sushi named for the same fox connection, leads up to the main hall, giving the visit a genuine pilgrimage-town atmosphere rather than a single isolated building.

Being further from Nagoya than most of the region's other day-trip destinations, Toyokawa Inari sees fewer casual tourists and more dedicated worshippers, which gives it a noticeably different, more devotional atmosphere than the city's central shrines.
