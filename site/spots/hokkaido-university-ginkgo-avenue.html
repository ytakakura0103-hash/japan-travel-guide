Hokkaido University Ginkgo Avenue

Hokkaido

Hokkaido University's main campus is open to the public and doubles as one of the easiest green escapes near Sapporo Station. The Ginkgo Avenue, a row of about 70 trees planted around 1939, forms a dense yellow tunnel for a couple of weeks in late October to early November, and it is genuinely one of the better foliage displays in the city despite being free and unticketed.

A separate row, the Poplar Avenue, runs through fields on the northern part of campus and has its own quieter, less-photographed charm in summer when the fields around it turn green. Between the two avenues, the campus has ponds, old brick buildings from the school's agricultural-college origins, and a small museum on the university's history.

Because it is a functioning university with students and researchers moving between buildings, it never feels staged the way a dedicated tourist garden does, and even during peak ginkgo season the crowd is a fraction of what gathers at more famous foliage spots like Jozankei. Early morning on a weekday is close to empty.

Outside of the ginkgo and poplar seasons the avenues are simply pleasant tree-lined walks, and the campus is worth a short detour even for visitors just passing through Sapporo Station, since the north gate is roughly a ten-minute walk away.

Kita 8, Nishi 5, Kita-ku, Sapporo, Hokkaido 060-0808
