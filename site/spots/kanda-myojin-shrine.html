Kanda Myojin Shrine

Tokyo

Kanda Myojin traces its founding back more than 1,270 years, making it one of the older shrines in the Tokyo area, and its buildings are finished in a striking vermilion and gold that stands out even among Japan's typically colorful shrine architecture. Its location just south of Akihabara led to an unusual modern development: the shrine has been informally embraced by Akihabara's anime, gaming, and IT industries as a place to pray for business success, and it sells charms specifically aimed at protecting computers and data — a modern twist that sits alongside its more traditional role as a guardian shrine for the wider Kanda, Nihonbashi, and Akihabara districts.

The shrine also hosts the Kanda Matsuri, one of Tokyo's three major traditional festivals, held in odd-numbered years with a large procession through the surrounding neighborhoods; visiting during the festival offers a dramatically different, much busier atmosphere than a typical day. A casual visit to the shrine grounds takes twenty to thirty minutes.

There is no admission fee. Weekday mornings are the quietest time to visit outside of festival periods, while weekends and any anime or gaming-related events in nearby Akihabara can bring a noticeably younger, pop-culture-oriented crowd through the shrine grounds. It's an easy stop to combine with a walk through Akihabara itself, a short distance to the south.
