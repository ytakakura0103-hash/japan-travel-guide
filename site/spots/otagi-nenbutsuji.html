Otagi Nenbutsuji

Kyoto

Otagi Nenbutsuji's roughly 1,200 stone rakan statues, representing the historical disciples of the Buddha, look nothing like the uniform figures found at most temples: each one was carved in the 1980s by an amateur volunteer under the guidance of a sculptor-priest, and the results range from serene to openly comic, with statues shown laughing, drinking, playing instruments, or pulling exaggerated faces. That variety is the main draw, and visitors often spend their time here simply wandering the hillside grounds looking for a favorite, or a statue that seems to capture a mood or personality. Because the statues are a relatively recent addition rather than an ancient relic, the temple carries a more playful, informal atmosphere than most of Kyoto's historic sites, though the grounds themselves, with worn stone steps climbing a wooded hillside, still feel appropriately old and quiet. A visit generally takes half an hour to an hour. The temple sits well beyond the main Arashiyama sightseeing area, past Adashino Nenbutsuji, which keeps visitor numbers modest even during busier seasons, though the uphill walk to get there discourages some visitors and helps keep the grounds calm. Moss grows thickly over many of the statues, giving them a greener, softer look in the rainy season, and maples add color in autumn. A modest admission fee applies, and photography of the statues is generally welcomed, since they were made specifically to be individual and expressive.

2-5 Sagatoriimoto Fukatanicho, Ukyo Ward, Kyoto
