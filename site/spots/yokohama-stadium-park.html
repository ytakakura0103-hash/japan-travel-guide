Yokohama Stadium and Yokohama Park

Yokohama

Yokohama Park was laid out in the late 19th century on land cleared after a major fire, making it one of the older planned green spaces in the city, and the stadium was later built within its grounds, hosting professional baseball as well as some international matches over the decades. On game nights the area around the stadium fills with fans in team colors heading in for a match, giving the otherwise quiet park a burst of local energy.

Outside of game days, the park itself is a pleasant, unhurried green space in the middle of the Kannai district, known particularly for a large tulip display each spring that draws a modest but appreciative local crowd, distinct from the more heavily touristed cherry blossom spots elsewhere in the city. Its central location, close to the Three Towers, Isezakicho, and Kannai Station, makes it easy to fold into a walking day even without attending a game.

For visitors interested in seeing a Japanese baseball game, tickets are generally easier to get for weekday games than weekend ones, and the atmosphere, with organized cheering sections and stadium food specific to the team, is worth experiencing even for those without a strong baseball interest.
