Local Pick

Shinkaichi

Kobe

Shinkaichi was once one of Japan's major entertainment districts, earning the nickname "the Asakusa of the West" during the Taisho and early Showa eras, when it packed roughly two dozen theaters into a compact area west of Motomachi. That golden age has long since faded, and today Shinkaichi carries a distinctly Showa-retro, slightly worn character, a marked contrast to the polished shopping streets of central Sannomiya just a short train ride away.

Rather than a nostalgic dead end, the district has found some renewed relevance in recent years, including a rakugo comedy hall that opened in 2018 and a steady trickle of local media coverage framing it as newly interesting again for visitors curious about a less filtered, more historically layered side of Kobe. Its bars and eateries tend toward the unpretentious, drawing a local crowd more than a tourist one.

Because it sits somewhat off the standard Kobe sightseeing route, Shinkaichi suits visitors specifically curious about the city's entertainment history and willing to explore a less polished district than Sannomiya or Kitano. It pairs naturally with a stop at Motoko, the atmospheric shopping arcade beneath the elevated tracks between Motomachi and Kobe Station, for a fuller sense of this less-visited side of the city.
