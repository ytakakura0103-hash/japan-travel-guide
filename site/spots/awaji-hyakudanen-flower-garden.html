Awaji Hyakudanen Flower Terraces

Kobe

Built as part of the same Tadao Ando-designed complex as Yumebutai, the Hyakudanen consists of exactly what the name suggests, one hundred small square planting beds arranged in a stepped grid climbing the hillside, each filled with seasonal flowers that rotate through the year. Rather than a single showcase bloom, the terraces are planted to keep something in flower more or less continuously, from spring bulbs through summer annuals to autumn color, so the specific look of a visit depends heavily on timing.

The strict geometric grid of the terraces, softened by the informality of the flowers themselves, makes for a striking contrast that photographs particularly well from the elevated walkways that run alongside and above the beds. It's a quieter, more contemplative stop than a typical flower park, without souvenir stands or crowds pushing through.

Because it sits directly adjacent to Yumebutai's other facilities, most visitors combine the two into a single stop, and admission arrangements sometimes bundle access to both. Checking a seasonal bloom calendar before visiting, where available, helps set expectations, since the terraces can look sparse between major planting rotations.
