Daimaru Kobe

Kobe

Daimaru has maintained a continuous presence in Kobe since the early twentieth century, and its current flagship occupies the former National City Bank building, constructed in 1929 in the Old Foreign Settlement district. The building's arched, gaslit European-style facade has become the architectural signature of the settlement, standing out even among the district's dense concentration of historic Western-style buildings.

Spread across ten floors above ground and two basement levels, the store carries a full range of fashion, cosmetics, furniture and interiors, and art and crafts, alongside a well-regarded food hall. Its performance has been notably strong in recent years, with annual sales surpassing one hundred billion yen for the first time in nineteen years, driven by both wealthy domestic shoppers and a steady flow of inbound tourists, reflecting the Old Foreign Settlement's continued draw as a shopping and sightseeing destination.

Its location within the Old Foreign Settlement, already covered elsewhere in this guide for its historic architecture, makes it easy to combine a Daimaru visit with a broader walk through the district's colonial-era streetscape. The building itself is worth viewing from outside even for visitors not planning to shop, given its architectural significance to the area.
