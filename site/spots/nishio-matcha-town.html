Nishio, the Matcha Town

Nagoya

Tea cultivation in Nishio dates back to 1271, when the founder of a local temple is said to have first planted tea seeds here, but the town's specific focus on matcha developed much later, expanding significantly from the late Taisho era onward as growers shifted toward the shade-cultivation techniques that produce tencha, the specially grown leaf ground into matcha powder. Today Nishio accounts for roughly a fifth of all tencha produced in Japan, standing alongside Kyoto's Uji as one of the country's two historically defining matcha regions, even though it remains far less known to overseas visitors.\n\nThe surrounding countryside, where the Yahagi River meets Mikawa Bay, is dotted with tea fields still shaded using traditional straw and cloth racks during the weeks before harvest, a technique that concentrates chlorophyll and amino acids in the leaf and produces matcha's characteristic vivid green color and umami-forward flavor. Several tea companies and small workshops in town offer hands-on matcha experiences, letting visitors grind leaf by hand with a traditional stone mortar and prepare their own bowl of tea in a simple tearoom setting.\n\nThe grounds of the former Nishio Castle, now a public park in the town center, make a pleasant, unhurried base for a day built around tea rather than monuments.
