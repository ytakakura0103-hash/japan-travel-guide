Kurodani (Konkaikomyoji)

Kyoto

Kurodani, formally Konkaikomyoji, is a large temple complex founded by Honen, the monk who established the Jodo school of Buddhism, on the site where he is said to have first settled after leaving Mount Hiei to begin teaching independently. The grounds include a three-story pagoda, a substantial main hall, and an extensive cemetery climbing the hillside behind the temple, giving the whole complex a scale that rivals better-known Higashiyama temples nearby, without anywhere near the same visitor numbers. That gap between scale and popularity is Kurodani's main appeal for visitors chasing Kyoto's famous autumn maple displays without the crowds that come with them: the temple's grounds turn a comparably vivid red and orange each November, and photographs from here can look remarkably similar to shots taken at far more crowded sites just a short walk away, without the wait or the crush of other visitors. A walk through the accessible grounds, including the approach to the pagoda, generally takes about an hour. Admission to the main grounds is free or covers only a token fee, with any interior hall visits requiring a small additional charge. Because Kurodani sits slightly uphill and off the most obvious walking routes between Higashiyama's headline temples, it stays comparatively quiet even during the November peak, and weekday visits make it quieter still. Its cemetery, home to graves connected to a Kyoto guard unit from the late Edo period, adds a layer of history for visitors interested in the Bakumatsu period.

121 Kurodanicho, Sakyo Ward, Kyoto
