Ichibansan Yokocho

Sendai

Ichibansan Yokocho, also known as Iroha Yokocho, traces its roots to a postwar black market that took shape here in the late 1940s, one of the informal street-stall districts that sprang up across Japan's cities in the immediate aftermath of the war. What began as improvised trading stalls gradually formalized into the narrow, densely packed lane that stands today, lined with more than a hundred tiny shops, cafes, and food stalls squeezed into a space barely wide enough for two people to pass.

Within Kokubuncho, one of Japan's three major regional entertainment districts alongside Susukino in Sapporo and Nakasu in Fukuoka, Ichibansan Yokocho has carved out a specific identity as a standing-bar cluster, a good fit for solo visitors who want a drink and some local atmosphere without needing to commit to a full sit-down izakaya. The postwar market character still shows in the alley's improvised, patched-together feel, a contrast to the more polished bars and clubs elsewhere in Kokubuncho.

Because most of its bars are tiny, single-counter operations, it rewards wandering and popping into whatever looks inviting rather than following a fixed plan. It's an easy first or last stop on a night out in Kokubuncho, worth combining with a walk through neighboring Bunka Yokocho for a fuller sense of the district's older, more retro side.

Kokubuncho, Aoba Ward, Sendai

Within Kokubuncho, a short walk from Sendai Station or Kotodai-koen Station (Sendai Subway Namboku Line).
