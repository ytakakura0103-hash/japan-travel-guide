Kosho-ji Temple, Yagoto

Nagoya

Kosho-ji is one of five temples making up the Yagoto Reijo pilgrimage route established in the Edo period, each dedicated to a different guardian direction around the city, and its five-story pagoda remains one of the more elegant pieces of religious architecture in Nagoya despite being almost entirely absent from international guidebooks. The temple grounds slope gently up a wooded hillside typical of the Yagoto area, giving it a slightly more rural feel than temples closer to downtown.

The grounds are free to enter, and a visit takes twenty to thirty minutes, though the surrounding hillside paths invite a longer, slower walk if time allows.

Autumn foliage around the pagoda is genuinely striking and draws a modest local crowd of photographers without ever approaching the density of Kyoto's famous foliage spots, making it a good option for travelers who want a quiet, uncrowded autumn temple visit within city limits. Weekday visits, even during peak foliage weeks, tend to be remarkably calm.

2-56 Yagotohonmachi, Showa-ku, Nagoya, Aichi
