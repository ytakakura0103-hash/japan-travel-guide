Local Pick

Akashi City Science Museum

Kobe

Note: the museum is closed for renovation from October 2025, with a reopening planned for July 30, 2026 — check current status before visiting if traveling around that date.

Akashi sits on the 135th line of east longitude, the meridian Japan uses to set its standard time nationwide, and the city has leaned into that geographic fact as a point of local pride, with several markers and monuments around town noting the line's exact path, including a striped line built into the pavement outside this museum. Inside, a planetarium and hands-on science exhibits cover astronomy and timekeeping alongside more general science displays aimed at families.

It's a distinctly local attraction, more likely to be full of Akashi schoolchildren on a field trip than international visitors, and its low profile among outside tourists means it rarely feels crowded even during planetarium showtimes. The building itself, along with the meridian tower nearby, is a recognizable piece of the Akashi skyline for anyone arriving by train.

Planetarium programs run on a set schedule through the day rather than continuously, so it's worth checking showtimes on arrival if that's the main draw. The museum works well paired with a walk to the nearby Uonotana fish market or Akashi Park, both within easy walking distance.

Akashi, Hyogo Prefecture

From Sannomiya, take the JR Kobe Line or San'yo Electric Railway to Akashi Station (about 20-25 minutes); the museum is a short walk from the station.
