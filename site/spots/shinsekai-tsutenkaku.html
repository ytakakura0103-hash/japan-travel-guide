Shinsekai & Tsutenkaku Tower

Osaka

Built in the early 20th century around a boulevard modelled loosely on Paris, with a tower inspired partly by the Eiffel Tower, Shinsekai was Osaka's vision of a modern entertainment district before the war, and although the current Tsutenkaku Tower is a 1950s rebuild after the original burned, the district has kept much of its old-fashioned character rather than modernising alongside the rest of the city. Narrow streets lined with kushikatsu restaurants, cheap game arcades and old pachinko parlours give it a rougher, more lived-in feel than Dotonbori or Namba, and prices and atmosphere here still lean distinctly working-class.

The tower has an observation deck reachable by elevator, with a modest admission fee, and the climb takes perhaps thirty to forty minutes including the view; the streets around it can be explored in an hour or extended into a full kushikatsu dinner. Evenings bring the most atmosphere, when neon signage lights the narrow lanes, though the area is safe and walkable at most hours. Weekday early evenings tend to be calmer than weekend nights, when queues form outside the most famous kushikatsu shops.

The unofficial house rule at kushikatsu restaurants here is no double-dipping skewers into the shared sauce, worth knowing before sitting down. Shinsekai pairs naturally with a visit to nearby Tennoji Park or Abeno Harukas, both within easy walking distance.

After dark, Shinsekai doubles as one of Osaka's more atmospheric standing-bar districts: the same narrow lanes that fill with kushikatsu smoke by day fill with the low hum of tachinomi counters by night, drawing an older, more local drinking crowd than the youth-oriented bars of Amerikamura or the club scene in Shinsaibashi. It's a good option for a first, cheap drink before moving on to somewhere livelier, or for anyone who wants a slower, more old-fashioned night out than Minami's main nightlife strips offer.
