Kanazawa Bunko Museum

Yokohama

The original Kanazawa Bunko was founded around 1275 by Hojo Sanetoki as a private library for the regional Hojo clan, gathering Buddhist scriptures, Chinese classics, and historical documents at a time when such a concentrated collection was rare anywhere in Japan, making it one of the country's earliest dedicated libraries. The current building is a twentieth-century successor institution, now run as a prefectural museum, that preserves and displays medieval manuscripts, Buddhist statuary, and artifacts connected to the original collection and to neighboring Shomyoji Temple.

Exhibits rotate through different themes drawn from the museum's medieval holdings, and because much of the material is genuinely rare, some pieces hold national cultural property status, the museum periodically closes galleries between rotations, so checking current exhibition status ahead of a visit is worth the extra step. English information is limited, which suits visitors with a specific interest in medieval Japanese history and Buddhist art more than casual sightseers.

Sitting directly beside Shomyoji's pond garden, the museum is best visited as a single combined trip to Kanazawa-Hakkei rather than a separate outing, letting you pair the temple's scenery with a deeper look at the scholarly history that gave the whole district its name.

142 Kanazawa-cho, Kanazawa-ku, Yokohama

Open in Google Maps

About 12 minutes on foot from Keikyu Line Kanazawa-Bunko Station, adjacent to Shomyoji Temple.
