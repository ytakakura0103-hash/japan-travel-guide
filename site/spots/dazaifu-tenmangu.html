Dazaifu Tenmangu Shrine

Fukuoka

Dazaifu Tenmangu was established over the grave of Sugawara no Michizane, a scholar and politician who was exiled here after losing a power struggle with a rival court clan and died in Dazaifu in 903. He was later deified as Tenjin, the god of learning, and this shrine grew into the head shrine of thousands of Tenjin shrines across Japan, making it a major pilgrimage site for students hoping for exam success.

The approach to the shrine, lined with shops selling the local umegae-mochi rice cake, leads to a vermillion main hall set among some 6,000 plum trees, planted in reference to a poem Michizane is said to have written about a beloved plum tree before his exile. Late February into March, when the plum blossoms bloom, is one of the most popular times to visit, alongside the equally busy New Year period.

The main hall recently underwent a multi-year renovation, so current photos and descriptions elsewhere may not fully reflect its present appearance. Arriving early on a weekday morning is the most reliable way to see the shrine grounds before tour groups and school trips arrive later in the day.
