Tarumi Fish Market

Kobe

Tarumi's small fishing port sits between Suma and Maiko along Kobe's western coastline, and its adjoining market operates as a genuine trade market for local restaurants and households rather than a designed tourist attraction. Stalls sell whatever the day's boats have brought in from the Akashi Strait, one of the richest fishing grounds in the region thanks to its strong tidal currents, alongside dried goods, pickled vegetables and prepared local specialties.

Because it caters primarily to Tarumi residents doing their regular shopping, the atmosphere is unpolished in a way that markets built for visitors rarely manage, vendors call out to regulars by name, prices are in line with what locals actually pay, and there's minimal English signage or tourist infrastructure. Some stalls grill or fry fish to order for an inexpensive on-the-spot meal.

The market is liveliest on Saturday afternoons, when the weekly public sale (doyoichi) runs from around 1:30pm until the day's catch sells out; the attached direct-sales shop otherwise keeps regular hours from 9am to 4:30pm. It's a worthwhile detour if you're already heading toward Maiko or the Akashi Kaikyo Bridge, offering a much more everyday counterpoint to those engineered tourist sights just down the coast.

From Sannomiya, take the JR Kobe Line or Sanyo Dentetsu Line to Tarumi Station (about 20-25 minutes); the market is a short walk toward the harbor.
