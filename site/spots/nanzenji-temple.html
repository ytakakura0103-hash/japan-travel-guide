Nanzenji Temple

Kyoto

Nanzenji is one of the most important Zen temple complexes in Japan, its grounds spread across a wide area at the base of the Higashiyama mountains and made up of a main temple along with several subtemples, each with their own gardens and, in some cases, separate admission. Its most unexpected feature is Suirokaku, a red-brick aqueduct built in the Meiji era to carry water and power generation infrastructure from Lake Biwa into the city; the arched, Roman-style structure still carries water today and cuts directly through the temple grounds, an odd but photogenic contrast to the wooden halls and gravel gardens around it.

The main temple's Hojo hall has a well-regarded rock garden, and one of the subtemples, Tenju-an, is known particularly for its maple trees in autumn. Seeing the aqueduct and main grounds without entering any paid buildings takes about thirty minutes; adding the Hojo garden or a subtemple extends a visit to an hour or more, each carrying its own modest admission fee.

Nanzenji is popular but spread out enough that it rarely feels as congested as more compact sights like Kiyomizu-dera, and the aqueduct area in particular tends to stay reasonably calm even at midday. Autumn foliage weeks are the busiest stretch of the year here. The temple sits at the southern end of the Philosopher's Path, making the two an easy combined walk.

Nanzenji Fukuchicho, Sakyo Ward, Kyoto
