Kagurazaka

Tokyo

Kagurazaka was one of Tokyo's prominent geisha districts through the early 20th century, and although that world has largely faded, the neighborhood's tangle of narrow, stone-paved alleys (ishidatami) behind the main shopping street still carries some of that atmosphere, especially after dark when lanterns light the way and the noise of the city recedes. Unlike much of central Tokyo, the street layout here is irregular and hilly, a legacy of its Edo-period origins, and getting slightly lost while wandering is part of the experience.

The area has a notably French-influenced dining scene, a legacy of a wave of French residents and businesses that settled here decades ago, and small bistros and patisseries are tucked in among more traditional Japanese restaurants and tea houses, many of them unmarked or easy to miss from the street. Bishamonten Zenkokuji, a small but well-known temple, anchors the main street and hosts a lively festival with food stalls several times a year.

Kagurazaka rewards an evening visit more than a daytime one, since many of its best restaurants and bars only open for dinner, though the backstreets are pleasant to explore at any hour. It is less crowded than more famous old neighborhoods like Asakusa, making it a good choice for visitors who want an old-Tokyo feeling without the crowds. An hour or two of wandering, plus a meal, makes for a satisfying evening.
