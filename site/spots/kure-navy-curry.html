Kure Navy Curry Restaurants

Hiroshima

The Imperial Japanese Navy adopted curry rice in the Meiji era, from the 1880s, as an easy way to feed large crews a balanced meal and combat beriberi. Kure, as one of Japan's principal naval bases, leaned into this history — but the now-famous 'Friday curry' custom is not a wartime tradition: it dates only to around 1985, when the postwar Maritime Self-Defense Force adopted a five-day work week and curry shifted from a Saturday staple to a Friday one, later popularized nationwide as a way to help sailors on long deployments keep track of the days. Today dozens of restaurants across Kure serve curry based on recipes tied to specific JMSDF vessels or units, each registered and stamped as an official 'Kure Navy Curry' by a local certification project.

Compared to typical Japanese household curry, navy curry tends to be milder and more vegetable-forward, closer to the classic Japanese-style recipe that spread through the military, often served with a side of milk, a tradition tied to efforts to prevent beriberi among sailors.

Restaurants around JR Kure Station and near the Yamato Museum post the certification mark clearly, so you can spot an 'official' bowl versus a generic curry shop — a fun scavenger-hunt element if you're visiting the museums nearby.
