Shintenchi

Hiroshima

Before the atomic bombing, Shintenchi was Hiroshima's most prosperous prewar entertainment quarter, packing more than 120 theaters and halls into a compact area that made it the cultural heart of the city. That history was almost entirely erased in 1945, and the district that stands today was rebuilt from the ground up, carrying only an echo of its former scale but retaining its identity as one of central Hiroshima's key dining and drinking pockets.

Its best-known modern claim is as the birthplace of Hiroshima-style okonomiyaki, and Okonomimura, the multi-floor building of okonomiyaki stalls covered elsewhere in this guide, sits within Shintenchi itself, making the district a natural anchor for anyone visiting specifically for that dish. Beyond okonomiyaki, the area holds a broad mix of restaurants and bars that give it a livelier, more food-focused character than the bar-and-club density of neighboring Nagarekawa.

Its position right beside Nagarekawa and Yagenbori makes it easy to treat Shintenchi as one stop on a broader evening through central Hiroshima's nightlife cluster, moving from dinner here into drinks in the surrounding streets. Given its history, it's also worth a moment's thought for visitors interested in how the city rebuilt its everyday cultural life after 1945, not just its memorial sites.

Shintenchi, Naka Ward, Hiroshima
