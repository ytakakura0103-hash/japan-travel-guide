Ohana, the Former Tachibana Estate

Fukuoka

Ohana began as a secondary residence for the Tachibana clan, who governed the Yanagawa domain through the Edo period, and it was substantially expanded in the Meiji era once the family took on the rank of count, adding a Japanese-style great hall, a Western-style building, and the Matsu-Tei-en pond garden that remains the estate's centerpiece today. The garden centers on a pond ringed by roughly 280 pine trees, fed by water circulated from the old castle moat, and wild ducks reliably arrive to winter on the pond, adding a small seasonal spectacle each year.\n\nThe entire roughly 7,000-tsubo estate is designated a National Place of Scenic Beauty, and the grounds now operate partly as a traditional ryokan and restaurant alongside the areas open for general visitors to walk through. Viewing the garden from the veranda of the main hall, tatami mats underfoot and the pond just beyond the eaves, gives a strong sense of how the estate would have looked to its original residents.\n\nOhana pairs naturally with a Yanagawa river cruise, since many boat routes pass near or dock close to the estate, and a full visit combining both takes a comfortable half-day. Autumn maple color around the pond and winter's visiting ducks are the two strongest seasonal draws.
