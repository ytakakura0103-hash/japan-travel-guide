Expo '70 Commemorative Park

Osaka

Once the site of the 1970 World Exposition, Japan's first, this expansive park in Suita has kept the Expo's most recognisable landmark, Taro Okamoto's Tower of the Sun, a strange, multi-faced sculpture originally built as the fair's centrepiece and now the park's defining feature, visible from a distance across the open lawns that replaced most of the original pavilions. Beyond the tower, the park is broken into distinct zones, including a large Japanese garden, a natural history museum, and open fields planted with seasonal flowers that shift through the year, from tulips in spring to cosmos in autumn.

A relaxed visit covering the main areas takes at least half a day, and the park is large enough that a full exploration of every section, including the Japanese garden and museum, can fill an entire day. Cherry blossoms bloom here in spring alongside the tulip displays, making late March through April one of the most popular times to visit, while the park is also known for its autumn foliage.

Weekday visits are noticeably quieter than weekends, when local families fill the lawns for picnics. Entry to the park grounds carries a modest fee, with the Japanese garden and tower interior charging separately. The park sits a short walk from Bampaku-kinen-koen Station on the Osaka Monorail and is close to NIFREL, making it straightforward to combine both into a single day trip north of central Osaka.

Senribanpakukoen, Suita, Osaka
