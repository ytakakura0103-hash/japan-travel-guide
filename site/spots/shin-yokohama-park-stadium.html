Shin-Yokohama Park & Nissan Stadium

Yokohama

Nissan Stadium is the largest stadium in Japan by capacity, built for the 2002 FIFA World Cup, whose final it hosted, and it later hosted the final of the 2019 Rugby World Cup as well, giving it a genuine claim to being one of the country's most significant sporting venues even for visitors with no ticket to a match. The stadium's sweeping, modern roofline is visible from well across the surrounding park, making it a striking piece of architecture even when empty.

The park around it follows the Tsurumi River, with a paved jogging and cycling course, open lawns, and a cherry-blossom-lined stretch that draws local hanami crowds each spring who treat it as a genuine neighborhood park rather than just stadium grounds. On non-event days, the area is quiet enough for a relaxed riverside walk, a sharp contrast to the roar of a sold-out match day.

Because Shin-Yokohama is better known internationally for its shinkansen station and the nearby ramen museum, most tourists never realize the park and stadium sit just a short walk further, leaving this side of the neighborhood comfortably uncrowded outside major events. Checking the event calendar ahead of a visit helps you decide whether you want match-day energy or a quiet riverside stroll.

3300 Kozukue-cho, Kohoku-ku, Yokohama

Open in Google Maps

About 15-20 minutes on foot from JR/Shinkansen/Yokohama Municipal Subway Shin-Yokohama Station (roughly 1.1-1.2 km); alternatively, about 7 minutes on foot from JR Yokohama Line Kozukue Station to the stadium's west gate.
