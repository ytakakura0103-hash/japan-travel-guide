Shibamata Taishakuten

Tokyo

Shibamata is a small, old-fashioned neighborhood in eastern Tokyo built around Taishakuten, a temple whose approach street became famous nationwide as the setting for Otoko wa Tsurai yo (It's Tough Being a Man), a Japanese film series that ran for decades starting in the late 1960s and centered on a lovable, wandering peddler character from this area. Fans of the films still make pilgrimages here, but the street holds up on its own merits as one of the more genuinely old-feeling shopping streets left in central Tokyo.

The approach to the temple is lined with old-fashioned shops selling senbei rice crackers, dango dumplings, and other traditional snacks, many run by families that have operated the same storefront for generations, alongside a scattering of small restaurants serving eel and other regional specialties. The temple itself has an inner garden and a set of finely detailed wood carvings on its main hall that are worth the modest separate admission fee to see up close.

Because Shibamata sits well outside the usual central Tokyo sightseeing loop, it rarely feels crowded even on weekends, and the whole area, including a stroll along the nearby Edogawa riverbank, can be covered in a couple of hours. There's a small museum dedicated to the film series near the temple for anyone curious about its local cultural significance.
