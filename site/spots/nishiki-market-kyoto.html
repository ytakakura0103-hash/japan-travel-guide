Nishiki Market

Kyoto

Nishiki Market runs about 390 meters through central Kyoto, a narrow covered arcade of roughly 130 small shops that has supplied the city's restaurants and households with fresh and prepared food for centuries, earning it the nickname "Kyoto's Kitchen." Stalls sell pickled vegetables, skewered sweets, fresh seafood, tofu products, and kitchen tools like knives and lacquerware, many made by shops that have been run by the same families for generations.

Walking the full length takes only about fifteen to twenty minutes, but most visitors move slowly, stopping to sample street food from stalls as they go; a reasonable visit runs closer to an hour once you factor in browsing and snacking. Some shops sell items meant to be eaten on the spot rather than carried around, and eating while walking is generally discouraged, so most people stand near the stall to finish what they buy. There's no entry fee to the market itself.

Nishiki gets busy from late morning through the afternoon, particularly on weekends, and the narrow width of the arcade means crowds feel denser here than at open-air markets of a similar size; arriving close to opening in the morning gives the best chance of a comfortable walk. The market sits just north of Shijo-dori in the middle of Kyoto's main shopping district, close to Pontocho and the Kamo River, and connects conveniently into a broader walk through downtown.
