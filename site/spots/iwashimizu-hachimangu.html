Iwashimizu Hachimangu

Kyoto

Iwashimizu Hachimangu is ranked among Japan's three most important shrines dedicated to Hachiman, the deity of archery and war who came to be seen as a protector of the imperial court and, later, of samurai clans including the Minamoto. Founded in the 9th century atop Mount Otokoyama, the shrine's main hall complex is a National Treasure, notable for its ornate, tightly layered roofline built in a style distinct from the more austere Ise-style shrines found elsewhere in Japan. Reaching the hilltop shrine on foot means a fairly steep climb through forest, but a short cable car from the town of Yawata below covers most of the elevation gain for visitors who'd rather skip the hike, making the shrine accessible without much physical effort. The forested approach and hilltop setting keep the site notably cooler and quieter than shrines closer to central Kyoto, and views from the grounds stretch out over the confluence of the Kizu, Uji, and Katsura rivers on clear days. A full visit, including the cable car ride, typically takes a couple of hours round trip from the base. The shrine draws large crowds for New Year's visits and its September festival, one of Kyoto's three major shrine festivals, but is otherwise fairly calm, especially on weekday mornings. Its location south of central Kyoto, near Yawata, makes it a reasonable stop when combined with sights in nearby Uji or Osaka.

30 Yawata Takabo, Yawata, Kyoto
