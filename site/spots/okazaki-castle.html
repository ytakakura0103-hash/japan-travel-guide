Okazaki Castle

Nagoya

The site of Okazaki Castle dates back to a modest fortification built in 1455, later taken over and relocated a short distance by the Matsudaira clan in 1526. It was here, in 1543, that Tokugawa Ieyasu was born as Matsudaira Takechiyo, decades before he would go on to unify Japan and found the Tokugawa shogunate that ruled the country for the next two and a half centuries. Ieyasu's own time at the castle was brief and interrupted; he was sent away as a child hostage at age six and, though he returned at nineteen, relocated his base to Hamamatsu Castle roughly a decade later.

Following Ieyasu's victory at the Battle of Sekigahara in 1600, Okazaki Castle passed into the hands of Tokugawa vassals, who held it until the Meiji Restoration. The new Meiji government ordered the castle demolished in 1873, leaving only foundation stones, moats and stone walls, a fate shared by many Japanese castles during that period of rapid modernization. The current keep is a 1959 reconstruction built in the original style and scale, along with an annex and well house.

The surrounding park is one of the region's popular cherry-blossom spots each spring, and a small museum inside the keep covers Ieyasu's life and the broader Sengoku-era history connected to the site.
