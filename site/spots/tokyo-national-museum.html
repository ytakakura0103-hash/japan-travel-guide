Tokyo National Museum

Tokyo

The Tokyo National Museum, founded in the 1870s, is Japan's oldest and largest museum, and its main building alone (Honkan) houses a chronological survey of Japanese art and history that runs from ancient pottery through samurai armor and swords to Edo-period painting. Beyond the main hall, separate galleries hold one of the world's major collections of ukiyo-e woodblock prints, and a dedicated building displays Buddhist sculpture and ritual objects. The Gallery of Horyuji Treasures, a quiet, minimalist wing with its own separate entrance, holds a smaller but exceptional collection of 7th-century artifacts and rewards visitors who make the short walk to find it.

Because the museum spans several buildings across a fairly large campus inside Ueno Park, a thorough visit can easily take half a day; visitors short on time are usually better off picking two or three galleries rather than trying to see everything. English-language signage is generally good for major pieces, and special exhibitions, held in a separate building, often require a separate ticket from general admission.

The museum tends to be calmest on weekday mornings shortly after opening, and busiest on weekend afternoons, especially during a popular special exhibition. It is closed on Mondays, as is typical for museums in Japan, so it's worth checking the schedule before visiting, particularly around public holidays when closing days can shift. Combine it with a walk through the rest of Ueno Park's museum cluster for a full cultural day out.

13-9 Uenokoen, Taito City, Tokyo
