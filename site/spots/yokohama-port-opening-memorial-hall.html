Yokohama Port Opening Memorial Hall (Jack's Tower)

Yokohama

Funded largely through citizen donations to commemorate the 50th anniversary of Yokohama's 1859 port opening, this building combines a red-brick and granite exterior with a roughly 36-meter clock tower that sailors coming into port long ago nicknamed Jack, rounding out the King, Queen, and Jack trio of towers found within a short walk of each other in central Yokohama. Unlike the still-functioning King's and Queen's towers, this hall has a more public-facing role and occasionally hosts civic events, so it is sometimes possible to see more of the interior than at the other two.\n\nThe building survived both the 1923 earthquake and wartime bombing with damage but was restored rather than replaced, giving it a slightly different character from its two rebuilt neighbors, more patched-together history than clean reconstruction. Its clock tower remains a working timepiece and a recognizable silhouette against the Kannai skyline.\n\nBecause it sits closest to Kannai Station of the three towers, it works well as either the starting or ending point of a Three Towers walk, and checking whether any public event is scheduled inside before visiting can occasionally turn a quick photo stop into a chance to see the hall's interior as well.

Honcho-dori area, Naka-ku, Yokohama
