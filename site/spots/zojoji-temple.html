Zojoji Temple

Tokyo

Zojoji was the funerary temple of the Tokugawa shogunate, the family that ruled Japan for over two and a half centuries from Edo, and several shoguns are buried in a mausoleum on the temple grounds. The temple's main gate, San-gedatsumon, dates from the early 17th century and survived the wartime destruction that leveled much of the rest of the complex, making it one of the oldest wooden structures in central Tokyo; walking through it toward the main hall, with Tokyo Tower rising directly behind, has become one of the more recognizable juxtapositions of old and new Tokyo, and a popular photo composition.

Beyond the main hall, the grounds include a quieter garden area lined with rows of small stone jizo statues dressed in red knitted caps and bibs, placed in memory of children, which visitors often find more affecting than the main temple buildings. The temple is free to enter, with a modest fee for access to the treasure gallery displaying items connected to the Tokugawa family.

A visit to the grounds takes well under an hour, and it pairs naturally with a trip up Tokyo Tower given their close proximity and shared sightline. Early morning light is particularly good for photographing the gate-and-tower view, and the temple is noticeably quieter than Tokyo Tower itself at any time of day.

4-7-35 Shibakoen, Minato City, Tokyo
