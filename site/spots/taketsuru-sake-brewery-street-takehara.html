Taketsuru Sake Brewery Street

Hiroshima

The Taketsuru family operated a sake brewery in Takehara dating back to at least 1733, one of several brewing families whose wealth built the preserved merchant townscape that survives in the Honmachi district today. Masataka Taketsuru was born into this family in 1894, the third son, and grew up expected to eventually take over the family sake business before instead pursuing a very different path in alcohol production.

In 1918 Taketsuru traveled to Scotland to formally study organic chemistry and distilling, working briefly at Scottish distilleries and absorbing techniques that were, at the time, essentially unknown in Japan. He returned home, worked for other Japanese companies applying what he had learned, and eventually founded his own company in 1934, which grew into Nikka Whisky, one of Japan's two major whisky producers alongside Suntory; the story became widely known in Japan through an NHK morning drama based loosely on his life and his marriage to a Scottish wife.

The family's original brewery building still stands along Takehara's preserved main street, its exterior a recognizable stop for visitors tracing Taketsuru's story, even though the building functions today mainly as a historic landmark within the townscape rather than an operating public brewery tour.

Honmachi, Takehara, Hiroshima Prefecture
