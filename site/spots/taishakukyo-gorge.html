Taishakukyo Gorge

Hiroshima

Carved by the Taishaku River through limestone terrain in Shobara, Taishakukyo stretches roughly 18 kilometers and is often described as one of the largest limestone gorges in Japan, with sheer rock walls, caves, and a large natural stone bridge, Onbashi, formed by the collapse of an underground cavern roof.\n\nUnlike most Japanese gorges that are experienced primarily on foot, a central stretch of Taishakukyo is explored by small sightseeing boat across Shinryuko lake, giving a low-effort way to take in the cliff faces and forest color, particularly striking during autumn foliage from late October into November.\n\nBecause it sits well north of Hiroshima city in a sparsely populated part of the prefecture, Taishakukyo requires a car or a fairly involved bus connection to reach, which keeps visitor numbers modest even in peak foliage season compared to more accessible nature spots — a genuine advantage for anyone trying to avoid crowds while still seeing dramatic scenery.

Toyomatsucho, Shobara, Hiroshima

Open in Google Maps

Best reached by car, roughly 1.5–2 hours from Hiroshima city; limited bus connections run from JR Shobara or Tojo stations.
