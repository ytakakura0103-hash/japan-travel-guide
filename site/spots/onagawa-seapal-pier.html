Local Pick

Onagawa Seapal Pier

Sendai

Onagawa was devastated by the 2011 tsunami, and its rebuilt town center is one of the more thoughtfully designed post-disaster reconstructions on the Sanriku coast. The new JR Onagawa Station, designed by architect Shigeru Ban and completed in 2015, functions as a genuine community hub rather than just a railway stop, with a public bathhouse, shops, and an upper-floor lookout over the bay, and its curved roofline has been widely praised as a piece of civic architecture in its own right.

From the station, a wooden pedestrian promenade called Seapal Pier runs down toward the water, lined with small independent shops, cafes, and restaurants housed in low, uniform wooden buildings, ending near the waterfront at Hama Terrace, a small market of stalls selling fresh and processed local seafood. Walking the full stretch from station to sea gives a clear physical sense of how the town has been rebuilt around openness to the water rather than away from it.

Most food and shopping here is inexpensive to moderate. Because Onagawa sees far fewer visitors than Ishinomaki or Matsushima despite the architectural and human-interest draw, it stays comfortably uncrowded even on weekends, and a relaxed visit, walking the pier, browsing the market, and stopping for fresh seafood, takes about two hours.
