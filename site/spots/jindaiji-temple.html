Jindaiji Temple

Tokyo

Jindaiji is considered Tokyo's second-oldest temple, with roots said to trace back over 1,300 years, and its wooded grounds retain a more rural, unhurried atmosphere than temples closer to central Tokyo, helped by its location in the western suburb of Chofu rather than the city center. The temple's main hall and bell tower are worth a slow look, but much of Jindaiji's local reputation actually comes from what surrounds it: a cluster of soba noodle restaurants that have operated in the area for generations, drawing on naturally pure spring water once used for both temple rituals and noodle-making, a tradition that continues at several long-established shops nearby.

A visit to the temple grounds alone takes about thirty minutes, but most visitors extend that into a longer stop for a soba lunch, easily bringing the total to an hour or more. The temple and surrounding streets are busiest around midday on weekends, when the popular soba restaurants can have a short wait; arriving before noon or visiting on a weekday makes for an easier lunch.

There is no charge to enter the temple grounds. Jindaiji's more rustic, small-town feel makes it a worthwhile change of pace from Tokyo's larger, more heavily visited temples, and it combines well with a walk through the adjacent Jindai Botanical Gardens for visitors with extra time.
