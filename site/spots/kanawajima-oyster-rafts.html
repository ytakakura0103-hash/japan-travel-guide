Local Pick

Kanawa Island Oyster Rafts

Hiroshima

While Miyajima gets the tourist attention for Hiroshima's oyster culture, much of the actual farming happens on the calmer, more sheltered waters around smaller islands like Kanawajima, near Kure, where rows of floating rafts suspend oyster ropes just offshore. The island itself is modest and residential, with no major sightseeing beyond the working waterfront, but a handful of simple restaurants and direct-sale outlets serve oysters harvested essentially that morning.

Season runs roughly from autumn through spring, when the water is coolest, and prices tend to run noticeably lower than at more touristed spots, since there's little markup built in for foot traffic that mostly isn't there. Some operators also run short boat trips out to the rafts themselves for visitors who want to see the farming process rather than just eat the result.

It's a spot aimed at people who already know they like oysters and want to see where a large share of Japan's supply actually comes from, rather than a general sightseeing stop — best combined with a Kure museum day, given the short distance between them.
