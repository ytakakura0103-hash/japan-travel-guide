Yasui Konpiragu

Kyoto

Yasui Konpiragu is a shrine most visitors come to specifically for one ritual object: a large stone, well over a person's height, completely covered in white paper slips and set with a crawl-through hole at its base. The shrine is dedicated to cutting off bad relationships, habits, or luck, and connecting with good ones like a new job or health, and the ritual involves writing a wish on a paper slip, crawling through the hole from front to back to sever the bad, then crawling back through from back to front to connect with the good, before pasting the paper onto the stone's surface to seal it.

The whole process, including buying the paper slip and writing a wish, takes most visitors around fifteen minutes, though there can be a short queue at busier times since only one person moves through the hole at a time. The shrine itself is small and free to enter, with only a nominal cost for the ritual paper.

Because the stone-crawling ritual and the sea of white paper covering it make for a striking, slightly eerie photograph, the shrine draws a steady trickle of visitors throughout the day; weekday mornings are quieter than weekend afternoons. It sits a short walk from Gion and Yasaka Shrine, making it easy to fold into a Higashiyama walking route.

70 Shimobenten-cho, Higashiyama Ward, Kyoto

10-minute walk from Gion-Shijo Station (Keihan Main Line)
