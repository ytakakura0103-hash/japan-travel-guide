Takehara Historic Townscape

Hiroshima

Takehara grew wealthy from the late 17th century onward through salt production using tidal flats along the coast, and later through sake brewing that drew on the surrounding region's rice and water, and the merchant families who profited from both industries built the substantial wooden townhouses that still line the old town's central streets today. The Honmachi district, roughly a 5-minute walk from Takehara Station, is designated as a nationally recognized Important Preservation District for Groups of Traditional Buildings, protecting its lattice-fronted houses, tiled roofs, and narrow lanes largely as they were in the Edo and Meiji periods.

The town is also known as the birthplace of Masataka Taketsuru, founder of Nikka Whisky and the real-life basis for a well-known NHK television drama, and the family's historic sake brewery building still stands along the main preserved street, drawing visitors interested in that connection even though it is not generally open for interior tours. Several other sake breweries in town remain active or partially open to visitors, some offering tastings.

Compared with Kyoto's more famous preserved districts, Takehara draws a fraction of the foot traffic, giving its streets a noticeably quieter, more lived-in atmosphere, with local shops, cafes, and residences still operating within the historic buildings rather than the area functioning purely as an open-air museum.
