Children's Peace Monument

Hiroshima

The monument was funded by donations from Japanese schoolchildren and built in 1958 in memory of Sadako Sasaki, a girl who was two years old when the bomb fell and later developed leukemia, dying in 1955 at age twelve. According to the story that spread after her death, Sadako folded paper cranes while hospitalized in the belief that a thousand cranes could grant a wish, and her classmates continued folding cranes in her memory after she died, which led to a nationwide fundraising campaign for the monument.

The statue itself depicts a girl standing on a domed pedestal with a crane held above her head, and the base is inscribed with the words "This is our cry, this is our prayer: for building peace in the world." Surrounding the monument are glass display cases that hold paper cranes sent continuously by school groups, individuals, and organizations worldwide; older cranes are periodically removed and either archived or recycled into paper products, since the volume received is far more than the cases can hold at once.

The monument is one of the more visited spots in the park, particularly with school groups, and it is common to see students reciting a short peace pledge in front of it.

Peace Memorial Park, Nakajima-cho, Naka Ward, Hiroshima, Hiroshima Prefecture
