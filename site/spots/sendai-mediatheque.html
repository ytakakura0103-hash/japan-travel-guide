Sendai Mediatheque

Sendai

Completed in 2001, the Sendai Mediatheque is one of the more influential pieces of contemporary Japanese architecture, designed by Toyo Ito around a radical structural idea: instead of conventional walls and columns, the building is held up by thirteen irregular, lattice-like steel tubes that run through the transparent facade like structural seaweed. Architecture students and design-minded travelers make a point of visiting specifically for the building itself.

But it's not a museum piece behind glass, it's a genuinely functioning public facility, combining a city library, a media arts gallery with rotating exhibitions, a media lab, and quiet reading areas with big windows onto Jozenji-dori below. Dropping in to browse the gallery floors costs nothing and gives a good sense of how the open, column-light interior actually feels to move through.

Most galleries are free; special exhibitions occasionally charge a modest fee. The building tends to be busiest with students after school hours and on weekend afternoons; a weekday morning visit gives the calmest look at the architecture. It pairs naturally with a walk down Jozenji-dori right outside, especially in autumn when the trees outside frame the glass facade nicely.

2-1 Kasuga-machi, Aoba-ku, Sendai, Miyagi

A 15-minute walk from Sendai Station, or a short walk from Kotodai-Koen Station on the Tozai subway line.
