Tsushima Shrine

Nagoya

Tsushima Shrine's own history stretches back to the year 540, making it one of the older continuously operating shrines in the region, and it grew over the centuries into the head shrine of a network of roughly three thousand branch shrines spread across Japan, all connected to the worship of the deity Gozu Tenno, historically understood as a protector against plague and epidemic disease. That rank places Tsushima Shrine on a level with Kyoto's famous Yasaka Shrine, its counterpart in the same religious tradition, even though it remains far less visited by international travelers.

The shrine's biggest event, the Tsushima Tenno Festival, unfolds over an evening and the following morning in late July and counts among Japan's three great river festivals. On the first night, five boats laden with lanterns and playing traditional Tsushima flute music drift down the Tenno River from a nearby pond, their lights reflected on the water in a scene that has drawn visitors for centuries. The following morning brings six more elaborately decorated boats, this time carrying men who leap into the river and swim ashore carrying banner-adorned pikes as an offering at the shrine.

Outside festival dates, the shrine grounds themselves are calm and unhurried, making a good stop for visitors interested in Shinto history without the crowds of Nagoya's larger shrines.
