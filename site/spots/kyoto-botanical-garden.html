Kyoto Botanical Garden

Kyoto

Opened in 1924, the Kyoto Botanical Garden holds the distinction of being Japan's oldest public botanical garden, and its scale reflects that long history, with wide lawns, themed plant collections, and a large greenhouse conservatory housing tropical and subtropical species that would otherwise be out of place in Kyoto's climate. Unlike many of the city's more famous cherry blossom spots, which can become uncomfortably crowded during peak bloom, the garden's cherry trees are spread across enough open ground that even a busy day rarely feels as packed as the grounds around Kyoto's temples in the same season. The garden also plants a long-running succession of flowers through the rest of the year, including irises in early summer and chrysanthemums in autumn, giving it a reason to visit outside the spring rush. General admission is modest, with a separate, similarly modest fee to enter the conservatory. A relaxed visit covering the outdoor grounds takes an hour or two, longer with the conservatory included. Families tend to appreciate the open lawns and relatively gentle pathways, which are easier for young children and strollers than the stone steps common at hillside temples. Weekday mornings are the quietest time to visit, even during cherry blossom season, and the garden's location right by a subway station makes it an easy stop to combine with other sights in northern Kyoto.

Shimogamo Hangicho, Sakyo Ward, Kyoto
