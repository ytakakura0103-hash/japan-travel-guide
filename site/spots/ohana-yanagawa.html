Ohana

Fukuoka

Ohana was the residence of the Tachibana clan, who governed the Yanagawa domain through the Edo period, and the property combines a formal Japanese garden centered on a large pond with a separate Western-style building added in the early 20th century, a reminder of how quickly Japan's elite absorbed Western architectural tastes after the Meiji Restoration. Walking the garden paths and viewing the traditional buildings from the veranda gives a sense of how a feudal-era ruling family actually lived, distinct from the more purely religious focus of a shrine or temple visit.

Part of the complex operates as a ryokan and restaurant today, so depending on timing it's possible to eat a meal overlooking the garden, though this generally requires advance booking. The garden itself, along with at least some of the historic buildings, is open to day visitors for a separate admission.

It sits close enough to the river punting docks that the two are easily combined into one Yanagawa outing, and because many visitors go straight from the train station to the boats, Ohana tends to see a bit less foot traffic than the punting route itself.

Shimoyanagawa area, Yanagawa, Fukuoka Prefecture
