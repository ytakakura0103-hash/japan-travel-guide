Yokohama Customs Headquarters (Queen's Tower)

Yokohama

Completed in the early 1930s, the Yokohama Customs building stands out from its neighbors thanks to a dome styled after Islamic architecture rather than the more European or Japanese references used elsewhere in the district, an unusual design choice that has made it the most visually distinct of the Three Towers. At roughly 51 meters, its tower is the tallest of the trio, which is how it earned the nickname Queen among the King, Queen, and Jack towers scattered nearby.

As an active customs facility, general public access to the interior is limited, so like its counterpart the King's Tower, it is best appreciated from outside as part of an architecture-focused walk through the Kannai and harbor district. Its position close to the waterfront also means it is visible from Osanbashi Pier and parts of Yamashita Park, making it a recognizable reference point even from a distance.

Pairing a look at this building with the nearby King's and Jack's towers, ideally seeking out a spot where all three line up, follows a small local tradition said to grant good luck to anyone who manages the sightline, giving an otherwise simple architecture walk a bit of a game to it.
