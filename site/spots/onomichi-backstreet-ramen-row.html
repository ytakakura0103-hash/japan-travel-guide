Onomichi Ramen Backstreets

Hiroshima

Onomichi ramen is built on a soy sauce base enriched with rendered pork or chicken back fat, which floats on top and keeps the broth hot down to the last sip — a practical trait for a town where cold sea winds cut through the streets. The flat, slightly chewy noodles are locally made rather than mass-produced, and most shops have stuck to a short menu of ramen and gyoza for decades.

The best way to experience it isn't at a single famous shop but by wandering the lanes just off the covered Onomichi shopping arcade, where several small counters, most seating fewer than ten people, have been run by the same families for two or three generations. Lines form at lunch on weekends, so an early or late visit avoids the wait.

This is a good stop to break up a day of hill-climbing between temples — cheap, fast, and filling — and it gives a much better sense of daily Onomichi life than any single sightseeing spot does.

Tsuchidocho area, Onomichi, Hiroshima
