Himeji Castle

Kobe

Himeji Castle is widely regarded as the finest surviving example of Japanese castle architecture, having escaped the wartime bombing, fires, and demolitions that destroyed most of Japan's other feudal-era castles, and its complex of connecting keeps, gates, and defensive corridors remains largely as built in the early 1600s. The brilliant white plaster covering its walls, restored to a particularly bright finish after a major renovation completed in the 2010s, gave rise to its nickname, the White Heron Castle, for the way its silhouette is said to resemble a bird taking flight.

A visit involves climbing through the maze-like approach to the main keep, up steep wooden staircases inside, and typically takes a couple of hours to see thoroughly, longer if paired with the adjacent Koko-en Garden. As a day trip from Kobe, it generally makes sense to allocate most of a day once travel time is included.

Himeji Castle draws large numbers of both domestic and international visitors year-round, and it is genuinely crowded during cherry blossom season in spring and on weekends generally, so arriving right at opening is the most reliable way to beat the queues for the inner keep's staircases.

68 Honmachi, Himeji, Hyogo

Open in Google Maps

About a 44-minute direct train ride on the JR Special Rapid from Sannomiya or Kobe Station to Himeji Station, or roughly 15 to 20 minutes by San'yo Shinkansen from Shin-Kobe Station, then about a 15-minute walk from Himeji Station.
