Marunouchi Naka-dori

Tokyo

Naka-dori is a wide, tree-lined boulevard running through Marunouchi between Tokyo Station and the Imperial Palace moat, developed over the past few decades into one of Tokyo's most polished shopping streets, with flagship stores for international luxury brands occupying ground floors of otherwise fairly restrained office towers. Public art installations are placed along the street at intervals, and the planting is dense enough that the street has a genuinely different, calmer character from the busy station area just a block or two away.

On weekday lunch hours, the street closes to vehicle traffic and tables and chairs are set out along the pavement, turning it into an outdoor dining area for the office workers who fill the surrounding buildings; this is one of the more pleasant times to walk through, with a lively but unhurried atmosphere quite different from a typical Tokyo street. In winter, the trees along Naka-dori are strung with illuminations, drawing evening visitors for a display that runs for several weeks.

Because Marunouchi is primarily a business district, Naka-dori is at its emptiest on weekend mornings and liveliest on weekday afternoons; anyone hoping to see the pedestrian lunchtime closure specifically needs to visit on a weekday. A walk down the full length of the street takes well under an hour, and it combines naturally with a stop at Tokyo Station or KITTE at one end and the Imperial Palace grounds at the other.
