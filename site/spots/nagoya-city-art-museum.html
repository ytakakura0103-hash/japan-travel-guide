Nagoya City Art Museum

Nagoya

Set within the same Shirakawa Park complex as the Science Museum, this gallery's building itself, designed by the noted architect Kisho Kurokawa, is worth a look before you even get to the art: geometric brick-and-stone forms that stood out sharply from more conventional museum architecture when it opened. Inside, the permanent collection leans toward twentieth-century modern art with a notable concentration of Mexican muralist-era works alongside European and Japanese pieces, an unusual focus for a municipal museum in Japan.

Admission to the permanent collection is inexpensive, with special exhibitions priced separately; a visit to the permanent galleries alone takes about an hour.

It tends to be considerably quieter than the neighboring Science Museum, making it a good counterpoint if that venue feels overcrowded with school groups. Special exhibitions occasionally draw bigger crowds, so checking what's currently on display helps set expectations for how busy it might be.

17-25 Sakae 2-chome, Naka-ku, Nagoya, Aichi

8-minute walk from Fushimi Station (Higashiyama or Tsurumai subway line), inside Shirakawa Park
