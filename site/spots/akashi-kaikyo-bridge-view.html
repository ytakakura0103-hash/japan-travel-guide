Akashi Kaikyo Bridge (viewed from Kobe)

Kobe

Completed in 1998 to link Kobe's Maiko district with Awaji Island across the Akashi Strait, this suspension bridge ranks among the longest of its kind in the world and is a genuine feat of engineering, built to withstand both the strait's strong currents and Japan's earthquake activity, tested only a year into construction by the 1995 Great Hanshin earthquake, which the bridge structure survived with only a minor design adjustment needed. Its twin towers and long main span are visible from much of the surrounding coastline, but the closest and most dramatic views come from directly underneath, on the Kobe side.

Most visitors take in the bridge as part of a visit to Maiko Park and the Maiko Marine Promenade, which sits directly beneath the structure and offers walkways with views straight up into the bridge's underside engineering. Simply viewing the bridge from the park and coastline, without paying to enter the promenade facility, is free and takes as little as 20 to 30 minutes.

Sunset is a particularly popular time to view the bridge from this side, when the structure and sky both catch color, which means the park can get busier in the evening hours during good weather; a midday visit trades some atmosphere for a quieter, less crowded look at the structure.

Higashi Maiko-cho, Tarumi-ku, Kobe
