Tomonoura

Hiroshima

Tomonoura sits at the eastern edge of Hiroshima Prefecture, in the city of Fukuyama, at a point where tidal currents from either side of the Seto Inland Sea meet, which historically made it a natural waiting point for ships timing their passage with favorable tides. That role made Tomonoura an important stop for merchant ships, diplomatic missions, and travelers for centuries, and the town's layout of narrow lanes, wooden merchant houses, and sake breweries still reflects its Edo-period prosperity.

What distinguishes Tomonoura today is the survival of its full set of historic port facilities in close to original form: the stone Joyato lighthouse, tiered stone landings called gangi, a wharf, a boat repair dock, and a guardhouse station, together considered a uniquely complete surviving set of Edo-period harbor infrastructure in Japan. Walking the harbor front takes visitors past all five within a compact area.

The town's preserved streetscape and slower pace have also made it a recurring filming location for Japanese cinema and, in one well-known case, an inspiration for a Studio Ghibli film's setting, though the town's appeal predates and extends well beyond that association. A local specialty, a medicinal herbal liqueur called Homeishu, has been produced in Tomonoura for centuries and remains sold at breweries in town.

Tomo-cho, Fukuyama, Hiroshima Prefecture
