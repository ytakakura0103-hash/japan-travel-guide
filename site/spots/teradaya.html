Teradaya

Kyoto

Teradaya is a working inn turned historical site, remembered chiefly for a violent night in 1866 when shogunate agents raided the building hoping to capture Sakamoto Ryoma, one of the key figures pushing for the modernization that would end the Tokugawa shogunate. Ryoma escaped with help from his companion Narasaki Ryo, who is often credited with alerting him to the danger while bathing, an episode retold by nearly every guide who leads visitors through the building today. Though the current structure is a later reconstruction, since the original burned down not long after the incident, it is furnished to evoke the same period, and displays include swords, letters, and other artifacts connected to Ryoma and the broader Bakumatsu-era turmoil before the Meiji Restoration. Visitors can walk through several rooms including the space said to be linked directly to the attack, giving the site a more intimate feel than a typical museum. A visit takes well under an hour for most people, less if you skip the more detailed Japanese-language panels. Because it's a smaller attraction off the main tourist circuit in central Kyoto, it rarely feels crowded even on weekends. Combining it with the nearby Gekkeikan Sake Museum and a walk along the Fushimi canal makes for an easy, low-key half-day trip focused on this historic district south of the city center.

263 Minamihamacho, Fushimi Ward, Kyoto
