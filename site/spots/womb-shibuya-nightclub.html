WOMB

Tokyo

WOMB opened in 2000 in Maruyama-cho, the hillside district behind Shibuya's love hotels that has long doubled as the neighborhood's club district, and it has stayed open long enough to celebrate its 25th anniversary in 2025, a genuine rarity in a scene where venues routinely close within a few years. It was built with input from people involved in New York's influential Twilo club, and that lineage shows in its booking policy, house, techno, and trance events with a consistently more serious, dance-floor-focused crowd than Tokyo's more mainstream hip-hop and EDM clubs.

The main floor is built around what's often billed as Asia's largest mirror ball, paired with a sound system and lighting rig that regularly draws comparisons to Europe's bigger techno clubs rather than a typical Tokyo nightspot. Multiple floors and bar areas give it more room to spread out than most Shibuya clubs, useful on busy nights when the main floor gets packed.

Because WOMB leans toward serious electronic music programming rather than a general night-out crowd, it suits visitors specifically interested in club culture more than casual bar-hoppers; check the event calendar before going, since the lineup and door price vary significantly by night. It's tucked a few minutes' walk past Dogenzaka into Maruyama-cho, worth knowing since the walk there passes through a fairly quiet, love-hotel-lined stretch that can feel like the wrong direction if you don't know the area.

2-16 Maruyamacho, Shibuya City, Tokyo, 1F

Open in Google Maps

About 10 minutes on foot from Shibuya Station's Hachiko exit, or 5 minutes from Shingen Station (Keio Inokashira Line).
