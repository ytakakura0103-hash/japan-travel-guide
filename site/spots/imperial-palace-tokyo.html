Imperial Palace

Tokyo

The Imperial Palace occupies the site of the old Edo Castle, the seat of the shogunate that ruled Japan for over two and a half centuries before the site was converted into the residence of the Imperial family after the Meiji Restoration. The palace itself, where the current Emperor and his family live, is closed to the public for most of the year, but the East Gardens, built on part of the former castle grounds and retaining sections of its stone walls and a surviving guard structure, are open to visitors free of charge on most days of the week.

The Nijubashi double bridge, visible from the plaza in front of the main palace grounds, is the most photographed spot here, its stone and iron double-arched structure a familiar image even to people who have never visited Tokyo. Around the outer moat, a paved path of a few kilometers has become one of the city's most popular running and walking loops, used by office workers and residents at most hours of the day.

Spring brings cherry blossoms along Chidorigafuchi, a section of the moat, drawing large crowds for both daytime and evening illuminated viewing, while autumn brings a quieter show of colored leaves around the East Gardens. A walk through the East Gardens takes about an hour; the full outer moat loop takes considerably longer. The East Gardens are closed on Mondays and Fridays.
