Ameyoko Market

Tokyo

Ameyoko runs along and beneath the elevated JR tracks between Ueno and Okachimachi stations, and it has kept much of the loud, unpolished energy it had as a black market in the years right after World War II. Today it's a dense strip of stalls and small shops selling fresh fish and seafood, dried snacks, cheap clothing and sneakers, cosmetics, and imported spices and foodstuffs, with vendors calling out prices and vegetables piled high on tables that spill into the walkway.

Unlike more manicured shopping streets, Ameyoko rewards a bit of bargaining, particularly at the seafood and dried-goods stalls, and prices are often written on hand-painted signs rather than fixed. Food stalls scattered throughout sell everything from fried snacks to bowls of fresh seafood, making it a reasonable lunch stop as well as a place to shop. It connects at one end to the newer, indoor Ameyoko Center Building, which has a more low-key, local market feel on its upper floors.

The street is busy most afternoons, but it becomes especially packed in the final days of December, when Tokyo households traditionally come here to buy fresh seafood and ingredients for New Year's osechi meals; that period is worth experiencing for the atmosphere but not ideal for anyone who dislikes crowds. A calmer visit is easiest on a weekday morning. An hour is enough for a walk-through, longer if you plan to eat or shop seriously.

Ueno area, Taito City, Tokyo
