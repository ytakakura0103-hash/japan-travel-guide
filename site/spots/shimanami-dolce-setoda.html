Shimanami Dolce Setoda

Hiroshima

Setoda produces a large share of Japan's domestically grown lemons, and this gelato shop, a short walk from Kosanji Temple, is built almost entirely around using that harvest and other regional ingredients rather than importing standard flavor bases. The menu changes monthly, but citrus flavors — lemon, orange, and blends with local herbs — are a constant, alongside dairy-forward options like salted milk made with salt from nearby Hakatajima.

It's become something of an informal checkpoint for Shimanami Kaido cyclists, who stop here as a reward roughly halfway through the route, and outdoor seating faces the street so you can watch the steady stream of touring bikes pass by while you eat.

There's nothing elaborate about the shop itself — a simple counter and a short bench — but the quality of the gelato and the direct link to what's actually grown on the island make it worth seeking out over a generic convenience-store ice cream stop.
