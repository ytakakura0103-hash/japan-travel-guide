Noritake Garden

Nagoya

Noritake, the tableware brand recognizable from fine china cabinets worldwide, was founded in Nagoya, and this site preserves part of its original early-twentieth-century factory grounds as a landscaped public garden. A tall red-brick chimney, one of the few remnants of the original kiln buildings, anchors the site visually, while an on-site museum walks through the company's design history with galleries of historic and contemporary pieces. A working craft center lets visitors watch, and sometimes try, hand-painting techniques.

The outdoor garden area is free to walk through; the museum and craft center charge modest separate admission. A relaxed visit covering the garden and museum runs one to two hours, longer if you take part in a painting session.

The garden stays fairly calm even on weekends, since it draws more of a design-and-craft crowd than a mass-tourism one. An on-site shop sells discounted or discontinued Noritake tableware, worth checking if souvenir shopping for tableware is on your list, particularly on weekday afternoons when it's least crowded.

Inside the neighboring TSUTAYA Bookstore, a mirrored ceiling reflects a roughly four-meter-tall bookshelf below it, creating an illusion of a towering, endless wall of books that's become one of the area's most consistently photographed spots on social media. It's a quick, free detour from the main garden grounds worth a short stop for anyone visiting primarily for the ceramics and architecture.

3-1-36 Noritake Shinmachi, Nishi-ku, Nagoya, Aichi
