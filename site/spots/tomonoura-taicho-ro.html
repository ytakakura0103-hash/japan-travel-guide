Taichoro Guesthouse Viewpoint

Hiroshima

Taichoro was built in 1690 as part of Fukuzenji Temple specifically to receive Korean embassy delegations traveling to Edo, and the hall's main tatami room is positioned so that its window opening frames the view of Benten-jima and Sensui-jima islands in the bay almost like a hung scroll painting — a design choice contemporary visitors still comment on today. One of the Korean envoys reportedly described the view as the most beautiful in Japan east of Korea, a line still quoted locally.

The hall itself is simple and uncrowded, a rare chance to sit in a genuine Edo-period diplomatic reception space rather than viewing one from behind a rope barrier, and the low admission fee keeps it from feeling like a major tourist draw despite the significance of the site.

It's a short uphill walk from Tomonoura's main harbor streets, making it easy to combine with a walk through the port town itself, and the elevated position gives a different, calmer vantage point over the bay than the harborside views below.

Tomocho Tomo, Fukuyama, Hiroshima
