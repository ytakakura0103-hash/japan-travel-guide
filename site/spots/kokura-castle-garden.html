Kokura Castle and Castle Garden

Fukuoka

Kokura Castle's distinctive tower, known for its unusual "karazukuri" construction in which the top floor overhangs the one below without connecting eaves, was originally completed in the early 1600s under Hosokawa Tadaoki, destroyed by fire in 1837, and rebuilt in its current concrete form in 1959. The reconstructed keep now houses exhibits on the castle's history and the wider Kokura domain, with an observation floor offering views over the surrounding city.

Just beside the castle, a separate castle garden recreates a pond-and-stream strolling garden on the former site of a lower villa belonging to the Ogasawara clan, the castle's later lords, complete with a reconstructed samurai residence where visitors can often join an informal tea ceremony without advance reservation. Viewed from the garden's veranda, the castle tower makes for one of the more elegant photo compositions in the wider Fukuoka area.

Kitakyushu sits further from central Fukuoka than Dazaifu or Itoshima, making this a longer day trip best paired with the Kokura area's covered shopping arcades and Tanga Market rather than treated as a quick stopover. The castle and garden are comparatively uncrowded outside cherry blossom season, when the grounds draw larger local crowds for hanami.

2-1 Jonai, Kokurakita-ku, Kitakyushu, Fukuoka
