Local Pick

Kiyosumi Garden

Tokyo

Kiyosumi Garden's defining feature is its collection of unusually large ornamental rocks, positioned along the paths circling its central pond, many transported from across Japan by the wealthy merchant family that developed the garden in the Meiji era as a company retreat before it was later donated to the city. The rocks aren't just decorative — stepping stones made from similarly substantial slabs cross parts of the pond, letting visitors walk out over the water itself for a closer view of turtles, koi, and water birds.

Set east of central Tokyo in a quieter, more residential part of the city, Kiyosumi draws a noticeably smaller crowd than Tokyo's more famous gardens, giving it a peaceful, unhurried feel even on weekends. A full walk through the grounds takes about forty-five minutes to an hour, and admission is charged at the gate, generally at a modest rate.

The garden is pleasant across all seasons, without a single dominant highlight the way cherry blossoms or maple foliage define some of Tokyo's other gardens, which makes it a comfortable stop nearly any time of year. Weekday mornings are the quietest, though even weekend visits rarely feel crowded compared to the city's larger, more tourist-heavy gardens. Its location near Kiyosumi-Shirakawa Station also puts it close to a small cluster of well-regarded specialty coffee shops worth combining with a visit.
