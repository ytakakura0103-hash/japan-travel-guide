Kyoto Imperial Palace Park

Kyoto

Kyoto Imperial Palace Park, known as Kyoto Gyoen, is a large public park that surrounds the former Imperial Palace grounds, the residence of Japan's emperors until the capital moved to Tokyo in 1869. The park itself, a long stretch of open land in central Kyoto, is free to the public year-round, planted with roughly 50,000 trees and crossed by wide gravel paths that are popular with local joggers, cyclists, and families as much as sightseers, giving it a noticeably more everyday, local feel than most of Kyoto's other historic sites.

The former palace buildings within the grounds can be viewed from outside the walls at any time, and the inner palace grounds themselves are open for a free self-guided walk during regular visiting hours, with occasional closures for events or ceremonies. A walk covering the main palace exterior and surrounding park takes anywhere from thirty minutes to well over an hour depending on how much of the wider park you explore.

The park is large enough to comfortably absorb crowds, so it rarely feels busy even on weekends, aside from cherry blossom season in spring when the tree-lined paths draw larger numbers. It sits in central Kyoto near Karasuma-Oike, making it an easy, calm stop for a walk between other sightseeing plans.
