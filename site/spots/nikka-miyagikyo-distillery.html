Nikka Whisky Miyagikyo Distillery

Sendai

Founded in 1969 by whisky pioneer Masataka Taketsuru, Miyagikyo was built at the confluence of the Hirose and Nikkawa rivers, a site chosen deliberately for its cool temperatures and heavy mist, conditions Taketsuru believed suited slow, gentle whisky maturation. It's the quieter, less internationally famous sibling to Nikka's original Yoichi distillery in Hokkaido, but produces a distinctly different, softer style of malt that whisky fans consider well worth the detour.

Self-guided and guided tours walk through the distillation and aging process, ending at a tasting counter where visitors of legal drinking age can sample several whiskies, including expressions not always easy to find outside Japan. The valley setting itself, surrounded by forested hills, makes the walk between buildings pleasant even for non-drinkers or a driver in the group, and there's a shop for bottles that don't always turn up elsewhere.

Basic tours and tastings are free, though advance reservation is recommended, especially for guided sessions. Getting there takes some planning: a train to Sakunami Station followed by a shuttle bus that runs weekends and holidays only, or roughly a 40-minute walk, or a direct city bus from Sendai Station that takes about an hour. Weekday visits are quieter, and a designated driver is essential if anyone in the group is tasting.
