Nagoya City Science Museum

Nagoya

The museum's giant spherical planetarium, embedded in the building like a metal moon, was among the largest domes of its kind when it opened and remains the building's signature feature, visible well before you reach the entrance. Inside, exhibits push well past passive displays: a chamber can generate an actual small-scale tornado on demand, another simulates an aurora, and multiple floors cover physics, biology, and space in ways built for hands-on experimentation rather than reading placards.

General admission is separate from the planetarium show, and combining both easily fills half a day; the planetarium in particular tends to sell specific time-slot tickets that are worth booking as soon as you arrive, or in advance online during busy periods.

Weekends and school holidays draw families in large numbers, especially around the tornado and aurora demonstrations, so a weekday visit or an early arrival makes a real difference. The surrounding Shirakawa Park, with its fountains and the neighboring art museum, is a pleasant spot to eat a packed lunch between exhibits.
