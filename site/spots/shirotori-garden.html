Shirotori Garden

Nagoya

Shirotori Garden's design concept is unusually literal for a Japanese strolling garden: it recreates the course of the Kiso River in miniature, starting with a narrow rocky stream meant to evoke the mountain headwaters and widening gradually toward a pond representing the river's mouth at the sea. Walking its paths in order gives a sense of that journey, with a traditional teahouse positioned partway through where visitors can stop for matcha and a sweet.

Admission is modest, and the compact layout means a full walk-through takes under an hour, making it an easy add-on to a visit to nearby Atsuta Shrine.

Because it sits slightly off the main tourist path near the shrine, it rarely feels crowded even on weekends. Its plum and cherry blossoms in spring, and maple foliage in autumn, are genuinely well-regarded among locals despite the garden's relatively low profile with overseas visitors.

2-5 Furuwatari-cho, Atsuta-ku, Nagoya, Aichi
