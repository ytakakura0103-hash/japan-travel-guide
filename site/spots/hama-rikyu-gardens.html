Hama-rikyu Gardens

Tokyo

Hama-rikyu Gardens began as a shogunal falconry and duck-hunting retreat, and its layout still carries traces of that purpose: tidal ponds once used to lure ducks into hunting blinds, and canals connected directly to Tokyo Bay so the garden's water levels rise and fall with the tide, an unusual feature among Tokyo's traditional gardens. A roughly 300-year-old black pine, trained and supported over centuries into a wide, low, sculptural shape, stands as one of the garden's most recognizable landmarks, while a teahouse on a small island in the central pond, reached by a wooden footbridge, offers a spot to sit with matcha and a traditional sweet while looking back over the water toward the garden and the office towers beyond it.

A relaxed walk through the grounds takes about an hour, longer if you stop at the teahouse or linger near the pine and the seaside stone wall. Admission is charged at the gate and is generally modest. Weekday mornings are the quietest time to visit, and because the garden borders Tokyo Bay directly, it also offers boat access — a water bus service connects Hama-rikyu to Asakusa, making it possible to combine a garden visit with a scenic river approach to or from that neighborhood rather than only arriving on foot or by train.
