Yasukuni Shrine

Tokyo

Yasukuni Shrine holds a complicated place in Japan's modern history and remains a politically sensitive site tied to the country's wartime past, but for casual visitors it's also notable for a more everyday reason: the shrine's grounds include the cherry tree that the Japan Meteorological Agency officially monitors each year to declare the start of Tokyo's cherry blossom season, making it a genuine barometer for bloom timing across the city. The long approach path lined with cherry trees becomes one of central Tokyo's more crowded hanami spots during peak bloom, typically late March.

Alongside the shrine buildings, the grounds house the Yushukan, a war history museum presenting the perspective of the shrine and its associated organizations on Japan's modern military history — a viewpoint that differs notably from mainstream historical accounts taught elsewhere and is worth approaching with that context in mind. A walk through the shrine grounds alone takes thirty minutes or so, while a thorough visit to the museum can extend that to two hours or more.

The shrine is free to enter, though the Yushukan charges a modest admission fee. Weekday mornings are the quietest time to visit outside of cherry blossom season; during peak bloom, the approach path draws large crowds throughout the day, with early morning offering the best chance of a comparatively quiet walk beneath the trees.
