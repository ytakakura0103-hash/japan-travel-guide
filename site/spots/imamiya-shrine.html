Imamiya Shrine

Kyoto

Imamiya Shrine, founded in the 10th century, is known locally as a shrine for social and career advancement, and while it's less visited by international tourists than Kyoto's headline sights, it draws a steady stream of local visitors for that reason. Just outside its east gate stand two restaurants, said to be among the oldest continuously operating restaurants in Japan, both serving a single dish: aburi mochi, small grilled rice cakes coated in a sweet miso sauce, a recipe and format essentially unchanged for over a thousand years according to the shops' own histories.

A visit to the shrine grounds alone takes about twenty to thirty minutes, and there's no fee to enter. Most visitors pair the shrine with a stop at one of the two aburi mochi shops, whether for a quick snack or a proper seated tea break, which adds another twenty to thirty minutes depending on how busy the shops are.

Imamiya Shrine sits north of central Kyoto near Daitokuji, a large Zen temple complex worth combining with the same trip, and the area sees noticeably fewer visitors than central Higashiyama. The two aburi mochi shops can occasionally have short queues around lunchtime and on weekends, but a visit earlier or later in the day usually avoids any wait at all.

21 Murasakinocho, Kita Ward, Kyoto
