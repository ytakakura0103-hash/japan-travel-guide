Hirayama Ikuo Museum of Art

Hiroshima

Ikuo Hirayama was born in 1930 in Setoda, the small port town on Ikuchijima where this museum now stands, and as a teenager he was exposed to radiation while working near Hiroshima at the time of the atomic bombing, an experience that shaped much of his later artistic and humanitarian work. He went on to become one of the most prominent Nihonga, or traditional Japanese-style painters, of the postwar era, known especially for large-scale works depicting Silk Road landscapes across Central Asia, the Middle East, and China, produced during decades of travel along that route.

Opened in 1997 in his hometown, the museum holds the largest collection of Hirayama's work, spanning childhood drawings that hint at early talent through to his mature Silk Road paintings, alongside sketches, travel diaries, and personal items documenting his career. Hirayama was also known for advocacy around cultural heritage preservation internationally, and some exhibits touch on that side of his work as well as his purely painted output.

Because the museum sits in Setoda alongside Kosanji Temple and the Hill of Hope, the two make a natural pairing for visitors passing through this stretch of the Shimanami Kaido on Ikuchijima.

200-2 Setoda-cho Sawa, Onomichi, Hiroshima Prefecture
