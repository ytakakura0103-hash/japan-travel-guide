Gion & Hanamikoji Street

Kyoto

Gion is Kyoto's most famous geisha (geiko, in the local dialect) district, and Hanamikoji is its central street, a stretch of low wooden teahouses and restaurants running between Shijo-dori and Kenninji Temple. The buildings, many of them still working ochaya where geiko and maiko entertain guests by appointment, give the street a consistent, well-preserved look that's rare even elsewhere in Kyoto.

Visitors don't enter these teahouses without an introduction, so the appeal of a walk here is mostly atmospheric, though there's a chance, particularly around dusk, of seeing a geiko or maiko in full dress moving briskly between appointments. Sightings aren't guaranteed and shouldn't be forced; the district has posted rules and fines against photographing or following geiko and maiko on side streets, both to protect their privacy and to cut down on the crush of visitors that had become a problem in recent years. Sticking to the main street and being unobtrusive avoids any issue.

A slow walk through the area takes well under an hour, and there's no fee involved in simply walking it. Early evening is the liveliest and most atmospheric time, though also the busiest with visitors; a daytime walk is quieter and still worthwhile for the architecture alone. Gion sits close to Yasaka Shrine, Kenninji, and Pontocho, and is easily combined with any of them.
