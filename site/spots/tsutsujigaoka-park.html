Tsutsujigaoka Park

Sendai

Tsutsujigaoka takes its name from the tsutsuji, or azalea, bushes planted throughout the park, which put on a dense, colorful display for a few weeks each spring alongside the last of the season's cherry blossoms. Outside of bloom season it functions as a fairly ordinary neighborhood green space with walking paths and open lawns, which is exactly why it stays low-key even at its most photogenic.

There is no entrance fee, and it's an easy stop of thirty minutes to an hour, often combined with a walk through the nearby Nagamachi area.

Because it's a local park rather than a marketed attraction, it rarely appears on tourist maps, so even peak azalea season tends to mean local families and photographers rather than crowds. The best color usually falls in late April into May, shortly after the cherry blossoms have finished.

Tsutsujigaoka, Miyagino-ku, Sendai, Miyagi (adjacent to Sendai Station's east side)

Open in Google Maps

About a 5-minute walk from Tsutsujigaoka Station on the Sendai Subway Tozai Line, or roughly 10-15 minutes on foot from Sendai Station's east exit.
