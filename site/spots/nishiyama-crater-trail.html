Nishiyama Crater Walking Trail

Hokkaido

When Mt. Usu erupted in 2000, new craters opened up in what had been an ordinary residential area of Toyako, and rather than fully rebuilding, the town preserved a section of the damage as the Nishiyama Crater Walking Trail. A boardwalk winds past crumpled roads, a partially swallowed bridge, and buildings still tilted or buried from the ground shift, offering a direct, unfiltered look at volcanic disaster damage that's rare to see preserved this deliberately.

Information panels along the route explain the eruption's timeline and the successful evacuation that meant the event caused no deaths despite the scale of the destruction, a point local guides tend to emphasize. The trail is flat and fairly short, generally manageable in under an hour, making it accessible for most visitors.

Because it competes for attention with Lake Toya's more scenic waterfront and the Usuzan Ropeway's mountain views, the crater trail draws a smaller, more specifically disaster-history-interested crowd, and it rarely feels busy even during Toyako's summer high season.

The steam and volcanic activity are still occasionally visible depending on conditions, but the trail is not open year-round: it closes for winter (roughly early November through late April) and can only be visited during that period as part of a paid guided tour, so a spring-through-autumn visit is the reliable option for seeing it independently.

Nishiyama, Toyako-cho, Abuta District, Hokkaido

About 2.5-3 hours from Sapporo: JR Limited Express to Toya Station (about 2 hours), then a Donan Bus toward Toyako Onsen and a transfer, or a taxi/bus from Toyako Onsen (about a 5-minute ride) to the Nishiyama trailhead.
