Dotonbori Glico Sign

Osaka

The Glico running-man sign above Ebisubashi Bridge has become the closest thing Osaka has to a single, universally recognized symbol, and the bridge below it functions almost like an informal stage — there's a small stretch of railing where a steady stream of visitors pause to strike the runner's pose for photos, day and night. The sign itself has been updated and redesigned several times since it first appeared decades ago, but the basic image of a runner crossing a finish line, arms raised, has stayed consistent throughout.

There's no admission or formal viewing area; it's simply part of the streetscape, best appreciated by walking the bridge itself and the canal-side path just below it, from which the illuminated sign reflects nicely on the water at night. The immediate surroundings, particularly after dark, are some of the most densely packed and brightly lit streets in Osaka, so the sign is best understood as one landmark within a much larger sensory experience of Dotonbori's restaurants, giant 3D signage, and crowds.

Given how central it is to Dotonbori's main pedestrian flow, the bridge is busy at almost any hour, but it's genuinely packed in the evening, especially on weekends. An early morning visit, before the crowds and most shops have opened, offers a rare and much calmer view of the sign, along with a very different, quieter atmosphere for the surrounding canal.

Dotonbori area, Chuo Ward, Osaka
