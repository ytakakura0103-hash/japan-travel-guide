Naruko Kokeshi Workshops

Sendai

Kokeshi dolls, simple wooden figures with cylindrical bodies, round painted heads, and no arms or legs, originated in this exact region as toys carved by woodworkers in the local onsen towns, and Naruko remains one of the recognized traditional centers of the craft, with its own distinct painted style passed down through specific artisan families. Small workshops around town still turn kokeshi on foot-powered lathes much as they have for generations.\n\nSeveral studios let visitors go beyond browsing and actually paint a blank wooden kokeshi themselves, choosing colors and facial expressions under light guidance from the artisan, a genuinely satisfying souvenir-making activity that produces something unlike the mass-produced versions sold in gift shops elsewhere in Japan. A small kokeshi museum in town rounds things out with historical examples from different regional styles.\n\nA paint-your-own session costs a modest fee and takes around thirty to forty-five minutes depending on how much detail you add. Because most visitors to Naruko come primarily for the onsen or the gorge, the workshops themselves rarely feel busy, and dropping in without a reservation is usually fine outside of peak autumn weekends, when booking ahead is safer.
