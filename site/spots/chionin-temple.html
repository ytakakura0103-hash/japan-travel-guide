Chionin Temple

Kyoto

Chionin serves as the head temple of the Jodo school of Buddhism, founded by the monk Honen, and its scale reflects that status: visitors enter through the Sanmon gate, the largest wooden temple gate in Japan, a two-story structure imposing enough that it's sometimes mistaken for the main hall itself before you reach the actual temple buildings further up a long flight of stone steps. Beyond the gate, the grounds include several large halls connected by covered corridors, one of which, the Uguisubari or "nightingale floor," is built to creak in a way said to warn of intruders approaching, a defensive feature common to temples built during politically unstable periods. The temple's bell, one of the largest in Japan, requires a coordinated team of monks to ring it, an event most visibly performed at New Year and worth timing a visit around for those in Kyoto at that time. A general walk through the accessible halls and grounds takes roughly an hour, more if the separately admissioned garden is included. The steep entrance steps mean the walk in is more physically demanding than most city temples, something to plan for with mobility concerns. Weekday mornings tend to be quieter than weekend and holiday visits, when the temple's popularity and central Higashiyama location bring larger crowds. It combines naturally with a walk through nearby Maruyama Park and on to Yasaka Shrine and Gion.

400 Rinkacho, Higashiyama Ward, Kyoto

8-minute walk from Higashiyama Station (Kyoto Municipal Subway Tozai Line)
