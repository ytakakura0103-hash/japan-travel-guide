Innoshima Suigun Castle

Hiroshima

The Murakami Suigun were a maritime clan active in the Seto Inland Sea from roughly the 14th through 16th centuries, often described in English as pirates but functioning more as toll-collecting naval escorts and shipping guides who used their deep knowledge of the region's dangerous tidal currents to pilot vessels safely through the narrow straits between islands. Innoshima, one of the Shimanami Kaido's chain of islands, was one of the clan's main bases of operation, and some form of fortification is believed to have stood at this hilltop site historically.

The present castle-form building was constructed in 1983, not as a reconstruction of a specific historical structure but as a museum designed in castle style to display weapons, armor, documents, and other artifacts connected to the Murakami Suigun and their role in regional maritime history. Its turret houses a small exhibition space, and the surrounding hilltop grounds offer views over Innoshima's coastline and the bridges of the Shimanami Kaido nearby.

Because it is a modest, purpose-built museum rather than an original castle, it tends to appeal most to visitors specifically interested in the Murakami Suigun story or in Seto Inland Sea maritime history, and it makes a manageable stop for those cycling or driving the Shimanami Kaido through Innoshima.

3228-2 Innoshima Nakasujicho, Onomichi, Hiroshima Prefecture

Open in Google Maps

About 15 minutes by taxi or local bus from Innoshima's ferry terminals; reachable via the Shimanami Kaido cycling and driving route.
