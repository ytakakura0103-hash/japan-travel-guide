Mazda Museum

Hiroshima

Mazda was founded in Hiroshima in 1920, originally producing cork products before moving into three-wheeled trucks and eventually passenger cars, and the company has remained headquartered in the city ever since, making it one of Hiroshima's most visible postwar industrial success stories. The museum sits within the active Hiroshima Plant complex in the neighboring town of Fuchu, and covers the company's history, its development of the rotary engine, and its motorsport record, including Le Mans-winning race cars on display.

Unlike a standard museum, visits are only possible via a scheduled guided tour, conducted mostly in Japanese with an English audio guide or handout available, and reservations are generally required in advance since spots are limited. The roughly 90-minute tour typically includes a walk along part of the actual vehicle assembly line, giving a rare look at real production rather than a static display.

Because it sits outside central Hiroshima in Fuchu-cho and requires advance booking, it suits visitors with a specific interest in cars or industrial history more than a casual drop-in stop, and comes with practical restrictions such as no open-toed shoes and limits on photography in certain areas.

3-1 Shinchi, Fuchu-cho, Aki District, Hiroshima Prefecture

Open in Google Maps

About 12 minutes by train from Hiroshima Station (JR Sanyo Line) to Mukainada Station, then a 5-minute walk to the Mazda Head Office, where visitors gather in the lobby before boarding a free shuttle bus to the museum entrance.
