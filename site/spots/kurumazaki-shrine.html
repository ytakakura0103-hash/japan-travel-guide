Kurumazaki Shrine

Kyoto

Kurumazaki Shrine has become known over the past several decades as a shrine for entertainers, performers, and anyone hoping for success in a creative or public-facing career, a reputation that grew from its dedication to a deity associated with academic and artistic accomplishment. Its approach path is lined with thousands of small wooden plaques, each inscribed with the name of an actor, musician, comedian, or other public figure who has visited to pray for continued success, making a walk up to the shrine feel a bit like scanning a long, informal registry of Japanese entertainment figures.

The shrine grounds are compact, and a visit, including a slow read through some of the name plaques, takes about fifteen to twenty minutes. There's no fee to enter.

Because it sits at the western edge of Arashiyama, a short walk or tram ride past the main bamboo grove and river area, Kurumazaki Shrine sees considerably fewer visitors than the district's headline sights, and it's rarely crowded at any time of day. It's an easy, low-effort addition for visitors who've already covered central Arashiyama and have some extra time, or for anyone with a specific interest in Japanese entertainment and pop culture looking for the name of a favorite performer among the plaques.
