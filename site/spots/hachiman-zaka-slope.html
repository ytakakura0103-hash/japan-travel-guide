Local Pick

Hachiman-zaka Slope

Sapporo

Among the several sloped streets that cut down through Motomachi toward the harbor, Hachiman-zaka is the one most often photographed, thanks to its straight, symmetrical run down to the water and the way it frames a clean view of the bay, and sometimes the red brick warehouses beyond, at its base. It takes its name from a shrine, Hachimangu, that once stood near its top before relocating elsewhere in the city.

Compared to the Red Brick Warehouses or the morning market, this is a much smaller, purely visual stop, essentially a five-minute pause on foot rather than a destination requiring real time, which makes it easy to underrate on a rushed itinerary despite being one of the more atmospheric single spots in the district.

Because it's a step removed from Motomachi's more heavily trafficked church cluster, it tends to draw a noticeably thinner crowd even during Hakodate's busier tourist periods, especially outside the golden hour around sunset when photographers concentrate here. A light dusting of snow on the slope in winter, framed against the dark water below, is a genuinely striking and comparatively underused version of the same view most visitors only see in summer photos.

Motomachi, Hakodate, Hokkaido
