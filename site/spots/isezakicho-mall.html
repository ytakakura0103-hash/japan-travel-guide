Isezakicho Shopping Street

Yokohama

Long before the glass towers of Minato Mirai went up, Isezakicho was simply where Yokohama went to shop, and the covered mall running through its middle chome still carries that older commercial character: independent clothing shops, arcades, discount stores, and local eateries rather than the flagship department stores and chain boutiques found near the station. Rebuilt after wartime destruction, it became a symbol of the city's postwar recovery and retained its role as the primary shopping district for decades.

A small piano-shaped monument along the street plays a short clip of Isezakicho Blues, Mina Aoe's 1968 hit about the district's nightlife, at the push of a button, and older residents sometimes hum a few bars when they pass it. The street's energy shifts noticeably by block, busier and more retail-focused near Kannai, quieter and more local further along toward Yoshidamachi.

Minato Mirai has since pulled much of the trendier retail and tourist foot traffic away from Isezakicho, which paradoxically makes it a more interesting stop now for visitors wanting a sense of how Yokohama shopped for generations before the waterfront redevelopment. Weekday afternoons show the street at its most everyday and unhurried.

Isezakicho, Naka-ku, Yokohama
