Yokohama Chinatown's Gates

Yokohama

Chinatown is ringed by a number of decorative gates, each built in the traditional Chinese paifang style and color-coded and carved according to the cardinal or intercardinal direction it faces, following feng shui principles. Zenrin-mon, meaning gate of good neighbors, is the most prominent, standing at the district's main approach and often used as the meeting point or starting photo for a Chinatown visit.

Walking the perimeter to see several gates in one loop is a low-effort way to appreciate details easily missed while focused on food, from the mythical guardian figures carved into the beams to the specific colors assigned to each direction. Early morning, before the lunch crowd fills the main streets, is the easiest time to photograph the gates without people constantly passing through the frame.

The gates are free to view at any time and do not require a special detour, since most sit directly on routes visitors are already walking between restaurants and temples. They are especially striking after dark when illuminated, though the streets immediately behind them tend to be brightest and busiest, so a quieter photo is easier caught from a slight distance.
