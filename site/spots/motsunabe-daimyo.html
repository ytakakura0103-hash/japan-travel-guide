Motsunabe in Daimyo

Fukuoka

Motsunabe, a hot pot built around beef or pork offal simmered with cabbage, garlic chives, and often tofu, is considered one of Fukuoka's defining local dishes, and the Daimyo neighborhood has several long-running specialty restaurants dedicated to it. Broths typically come in soy sauce or miso versions, sometimes with a spicier or vinegar-forward variation, and the offal itself, when done well, is tender rather than chewy or gamey, a common surprise for first-time visitors unsure about eating offal at all.

The usual order is for two or more people, since the pot is meant to be shared and simmers at the table over a burner, finished off at the end by adding noodles or rice to the remaining broth so nothing goes to waste. Reservations are worth making for popular spots on weekend evenings, since seating tends to be limited counters and small tables rather than large dining rooms.

Because motsunabe restaurants cluster fairly densely around Daimyo and Nakasu, it's easy to walk between a few and check posted menus and photos before committing to one, particularly useful for travelers without a specific reservation in hand.
