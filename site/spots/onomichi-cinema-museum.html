Onomichi Cinema Museum

Hiroshima

Onomichi's steep lanes, temple rooftops, and channel views have attracted filmmakers for nearly a century, starting with Ozu's 'Tokyo Story' in 1953 and continuing through Nobuhiko Obayashi's 'Onomichi trilogy' of teen films in the 1980s and 90s. This small museum, housed in a renovated 19th-century warehouse near the harbor, lays out that history through posters, props, and photographs rather than trying to be a general film museum.

It's a compact stop — most visitors are through in under an hour — but it gives context that changes how you see the rest of town, since several of the alleys and staircases you'll walk through later in the day are recognizable from the films on display.

Cinema Onomichi, the town's single surviving movie theater a few minutes away, still runs a program of independent and classic films in a similarly retro building funded partly by local donations, so pairing the museum with a screening (if the schedule allows) rounds out the picture of Onomichi as a genuine 'movie town' rather than just a filming backdrop.

17-19 Tsuchidocho, Onomichi, Hiroshima
