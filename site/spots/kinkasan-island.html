Kinkasan Island

Sendai

Kinkasan is considered one of the holiest sites in the Tohoku region, centered on Koganeyama Shrine, said to have been established around 1,250 years ago following Japan's first recorded gold mining, which gave the island its lasting association with wealth and good fortune. The island's deer, numbering in the hundreds, roam freely in and out of the shrine grounds and are regarded by Shinto tradition as messengers of the gods, while wild monkeys add an extra layer of untamed atmosphere to the forested trails.

The island is only reachable by boat, generally departing from Ayukawa Port or Onagawa, and because sailings are limited, a day trip is possible but requires careful timing around the ferry schedule; an overnight stay is often recommended for a more relaxed visit. Costs cover the round-trip boat fare plus any lodging if staying overnight.

Because reaching the island takes real planning compared to a straightforward train ride, Kinkasan sees a fraction of the visitors that Matsushima or Sendai attract, keeping its trails and shrine grounds genuinely peaceful. An antler-cutting ritual held on the first Sunday of October is a distinctive seasonal event worth timing a visit around if the dates align.
