Kiyosu Castle

Nagoya

Kiyosu Castle was originally built in the early 1400s to guard the junction of two major highways, and it grew into a genuinely important stronghold once Oda Nobunaga retook it in 1555 and used it as his base during the years he consolidated power over Owari Province. It was at Kiyosu that Nobunaga formalized his alliance with Tokugawa Ieyasu in 1562, a partnership that would shape the following century of Japanese history, before Nobunaga himself relocated to Iwakura and later Gifu.

The castle's original buildings did not survive Nobunaga's own successors: between 1609 and 1613, much of Kiyosu Castle was deliberately dismantled so its materials could be reused in the construction of the new Nagoya Castle a short distance away, part of a broader relocation of the region's political center. By the Meiji era almost nothing remained beyond earthworks, and the Tokaido rail line was eventually built directly across part of the old castle grounds.

The reinforced-concrete keep visitors see today dates only from 1989, built to mark the modern town's centennial and modeled loosely on Inuyama Castle's donjon rather than reconstructed from Kiyosu's own original plans, so it should be understood as a commemorative structure rather than a historically accurate rebuild.
