Demachiyanagi River Delta

Kyoto

The point where the Kamo and Takano Rivers meet forms a distinctive triangular delta in northern Kyoto, and its defining feature is a line of large, flat stepping stones shaped like turtles and birds that cross the shallow water, connecting the two riverbanks in a route used daily by university students, cyclists, and commuters as a genuine shortcut rather than a designed tourist attraction. That everyday function is part of the appeal: unlike a manicured garden or temple approach, the delta feels like an authentic piece of local life, and visitors are welcome to walk across the stones themselves, though care is needed since they can be slippery, especially after rain, and the river's water level rises noticeably during and after storms. There's no fee and no fixed visiting hours, since it's simply an open public space. The surrounding delta area, with grassy banks and mature trees, is popular for sitting, reading, or picnicking, and it connects directly to the Kamo River's walking paths heading south toward downtown and toward Shimogamo Shrine's forested approach just to the north. Sunset is a particularly popular time, when the shallow water catches the evening light and the stepping stones fill with a mix of locals and visitors crossing back and forth. Spring brings cherry blossoms along both riverbanks, while the open grassy areas nearby become popular hanami picnic spots. It's a free, flexible stop that works well combined with Shimogamo Shrine or a longer riverside walk.
