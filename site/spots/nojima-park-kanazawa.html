Local Pick

Nojima Park

Yokohama

Nojima holds the distinction of being the only natural, non-reclaimed island within Yokohama city limits, connected to the mainland by a few short bridges that make it easy to overlook as an island at all. In the Meiji era, the site held a seaside villa built in 1898 by Ito Hirobumi, Japan's first prime minister, now preserved as the Former Ito Hirobumi Kanazawa Villa, and traces of that history, along with Jomon-era shell mounds, are still scattered through the park's wooded paths.

Today the island is largely left as informal parkland, walking trails through modest woods, small clearings with benches, and a shoreline that offers unobstructed sunset views across the water toward Hakkeijima and the wider bay. It lacks any major facilities or paid attractions, which keeps it a low-key nature stop rather than a destination in its own right.

Because most visitors to Kanazawa-Hakkei head straight for Umi no Koen beach or Hakkeijima Sea Paradise, Nojima stays quiet even on busy weekends, making it a good choice for travelers who want a genuinely peaceful coastal walk without hunting for a remote hiking trail. Sunset is the best time to visit, when the light over the water is at its most dramatic.

24 Nojima, Kanazawa-ku, Yokohama

Open in Google Maps

About 10 minutes on foot from Seaside Line Umi-no-koen-minami Station.
