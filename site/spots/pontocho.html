Pontocho

Kyoto

Pontocho is a single narrow alley running parallel to the Kamo River between Shijo and Sanjo, one of Kyoto's five traditional hanamachi, or geisha districts, and one of its most atmospheric streets after dark, when strings of lanterns light up a corridor barely wide enough for two people to pass comfortably. Restaurants and bars line both sides, ranging from long-established, exclusive establishments to far more casual spots open to walk-in visitors, covering everything from kaiseki to yakitori to modern bars.

From late spring through summer, many restaurants along the river-facing side set up kawadoko, raised outdoor platforms built directly over the Kamo River, giving diners a rare open-air river view that's specific to this stretch of Kyoto and only available for those few months. Walking the alley itself takes only about ten minutes end to end, though most visitors come specifically to eat or drink rather than just pass through.

Pontocho is busiest in the evening, its natural time of day, and reservations are worth making at any restaurant with a river platform, especially in peak summer. During the day the alley is far quieter and some businesses are closed, so an evening visit is really the point here. It sits a short walk from Gion, across the river, making the two districts an easy combined evening out.

Pontocho's side alleys have their own quiet logic worth knowing: the narrow lanes branching off Kiyamachi-dori, the street running parallel to Pontocho along the Takase River, cut all the way through to Pontocho-dori itself, while the alleys branching off Pontocho-dori are dead ends, each holding just two or three hidden restaurants tucked out of sight from the main street. Wandering a few steps down one of these dead-end lanes is often the easiest way to stumble onto a smaller, quieter restaurant than the ones directly fronting the main alley.
