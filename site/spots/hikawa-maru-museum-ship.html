Hikawa Maru

Yokohama

Launched in 1930 for the Yokohama to Seattle and Vancouver route, the Hikawa Maru carried passengers across the Pacific for decades, including a young Charlie Chaplin on one crossing, before later serving as a hospital ship during the war and eventually settling into permanent retirement at Yamashita Park. Its art deco interiors, particularly the first-class lounge and cabins, have been kept largely as they were, giving a tangible sense of what transpacific travel looked like in the 1930s rather than a purely modern museum reconstruction.

Visitors can walk through several decks including the bridge, engine room, and passenger cabins, with information panels in Japanese and English explaining the ship's service history. It typically closes one weekday, commonly Monday, so it is worth checking the current closing day before planning around it.

Because it sits directly in Yamashita Park, it is easy to combine with a harbor walk, and its moored silhouette is itself a landmark visible from much of the park and from the nearby Marine Tower observation deck. Crowds are generally manageable except around midday on weekends, when tour groups tend to arrive in clusters.

Yamashita Park, Yamashita-cho, Naka-ku, Yokohama
