Atsuta Shrine

Nagoya

Atsuta Shrine ranks alongside Ise Grand Shrine in importance within Shinto tradition, believed to house Kusanagi-no-Tsurugi, one of the three sacred treasures of the Japanese imperial regalia. The sword itself is never displayed, but the sense of reverence is palpable as soon as you pass beneath the towering torii gates into the wooded precinct. Some of the camphor trees lining the gravel paths are said to be well over a thousand years old, and the whole complex feels like a pocket of deep countryside dropped into central Nagoya.

Entry to the grounds is free, and a visit can be as short as thirty minutes or stretched over an hour if you also stop at the on-site treasure hall, which rotates displays of swords, mirrors, and other ritual objects donated over the centuries; a modest admission applies there.

Early morning is the best time to feel the forest's stillness before tour groups and shrine visitors build up around midday. The shrine hosts frequent festivals throughout the year, most notably a large summer festival with fireworks, so checking the calendar before visiting can turn a quiet stroll into something more lively.
