Shinjuku Gyoen National Garden

Tokyo

Shinjuku Gyoen was originally a feudal lord's residence before becoming an imperial garden and later a public park, and that layered history shows in its design: distinct sections laid out in French formal, English landscape, and traditional Japanese garden styles sit within the same grounds, connected by wide paths rather than blended together. The variety means the garden rewards a slower visit — the geometric French rose garden, the open lawns of the English-style section, and the ponds and teahouses of the Japanese garden each have a different rhythm, and most visitors find an hour or two comfortable for seeing all three without rushing.

Shinjuku Gyoen is consistently ranked among Tokyo's best cherry blossom spots, partly because it holds a wide range of cherry varieties that bloom at slightly different times, extending the viewing window beyond the single peak week seen elsewhere in the city; it is equally well regarded for autumn foliage in November. Admission is modest and paid at the gate, and because the garden enforces rules against alcohol and large group parties that are common in other hanami spots, it tends to feel calmer even during peak bloom.

Weekday mornings shortly after opening are the quietest time to visit regardless of season, while weekend afternoons during cherry blossom or autumn leaf season can draw large crowds, particularly near the main entrance close to Shinjuku Station.
