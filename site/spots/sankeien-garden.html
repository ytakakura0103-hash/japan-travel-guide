Sankeien Garden

Yokohama

This garden was the private project of Hara Sankei, a Yokohama silk merchant who used his fortune to relocate more than a dozen historic buildings, including farmhouses, tea houses, and a three-story pagoda originally from a Kyoto temple, into a landscaped garden he then opened partly to the public in the early 1900s. The result feels less like a single unified attraction and more like a curated collection of historic Japanese architecture spread across ponds, paths, and wooded hillside.

The garden is split into an inner and outer section, with the outer garden generally free-flowing and the inner garden holding most of the relocated historic buildings, some of which are nationally designated cultural properties. Because it sits outside the central waterfront district, it draws a more local, less rushed crowd than Yamashita Park or Chinatown, and its size means even weekend crowds spread out rather than clustering.

Autumn foliage and spring plum and cherry blossoms are the garden's best-known seasonal draws, with photography groups turning out heavily during peak color weeks, so a weekday visit during those windows helps avoid the largest crowds. Given the distance from the main waterfront district, it works best as a half-day trip rather than a quick add-on to a Minato Mirai itinerary.

58-1 Honmoku Sannotani, Naka-ku, Yokohama 231-0824
