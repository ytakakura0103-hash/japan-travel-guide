Joten-ji Temple

Fukuoka

Joten-ji was founded in 1242 by the monk Enni, who is said to have brought back milling and confectionery techniques from Song-dynasty China that eventually gave rise to udon noodles, soba noodles, and manju buns as Japan knows them today. A stone monument near the entrance marks this claim, and it is a point of genuine local pride rather than a marketing invention, even if the precise historical details are debated by scholars.

The temple itself is understated: a modest Zen compound with a small garden, tucked between office buildings just steps from Hakata Station's Hakata-guchi exit. It rarely draws crowds, which makes it a pleasant five-minute detour rather than a destination in its own right. Visitors passing through on the way to or from the Shinkansen platforms can duck in for a quiet moment among cypress and stone before diving back into station traffic.

Because it sits so close to Hakata Station, it is easy to combine with a meal at one of the udon or soba shops nearby, closing the loop on the temple's own culinary legend. There is no strong seasonal draw, though the small maple trees color modestly in autumn.

1-29-9 Hakataekimae, Hakata-ku, Fukuoka

About 3 minutes on foot from the Hakata-guchi (west) exit of JR Hakata Station.
