Maruyama Park

Kyoto

Maruyama Park, Kyoto's oldest public park, centers on a single enormous weeping cherry tree, technically a shidarezakura, that has become one of the most recognizable individual trees in the city; when it blooms each spring, the park installs lantern lighting that keeps it illuminated well into the evening, and the area beneath it fills with people picnicking on blue tarps late into the night, a livelier, more communal version of hanami than the quieter viewing found at most temple gardens. The current tree is actually a later-generation planting rather than the very first specimen the park was known for, since the original tree eventually died and was replaced, though the tradition around it has continued largely unbroken for decades. Outside cherry blossom season, the park itself is a fairly simple green space with paths, a pond, and benches, connecting directly to Yasaka Shrine on one side and the approach to Chionin and Kodaiji on the other, making it more of a convenient central hub for exploring Higashiyama than a standalone attraction most of the year. There's no admission fee. During peak bloom, typically early April, the park gets extremely crowded in the evening, and finding a spot to sit near the main tree can take real effort; arriving in the late afternoon before the evening rush, or visiting during the day instead, is far more comfortable if crowds aren't the goal. The rest of the year, it's a pleasant, quiet walk-through park.
