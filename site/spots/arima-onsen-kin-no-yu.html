Kin-no-yu Public Bathhouse, Arima Onsen

Kobe

Arima Onsen is one of Japan's oldest hot spring towns, with a history stretching back over a thousand years and mentions in some of the earliest Japanese literary records. Its distinctive "kinsen" (gold water) gets its deep amber-brown color from high concentrations of iron and salt, which oxidize on contact with air, and Kin-no-yu is the municipal bathhouse built specifically to let day visitors experience it without a ryokan reservation.

The facility is simple and functional rather than luxurious, more community bathhouse than resort spa, which keeps the entry fee low compared to staying at one of the surrounding inns. There's also a free public foot bath just outside for anyone who doesn't want to commit to a full soak.

Arima's narrow streets get busy with day-trippers and coach tours, especially on weekends, so visiting Kin-no-yu on a weekday morning or combining it with an early trip up before the crowds arrive makes for a more relaxed soak. Bring your own small towel or buy one on-site, as is standard at Japanese public baths, and note the water's iron content can discolor light-colored fabrics if you're not careful.

From Sannomiya, take the Kobe Municipal Subway to Tanigami, then the Shintetsu line to Arima-Onsen Station (roughly 30-40 minutes total); the bathhouse is a short walk from the station through the town's main street.
