Festival Museum Dondenkan

Nagoya

The Inuyama Festival, held on the first Saturday of April, is one of the region's major float festivals, with elaborately decorated Yama floats pulled through the streets of the castle town in a display of lacquerwork, gold foil and fine woodcarving. For the rest of the year, when the floats are in storage, the Dondenkan museum offers the only way to see them up close, displaying four of the festival's Yama floats inside a compact, narrow-fronted building styled after the traditional castle-town merchant houses that once lined the street outside.

A short light-and-sound presentation inside recreates the atmosphere of festival day in a few minutes, giving a reasonable sense of the event's scale and energy without the crowds. A separate display case holds the ornate gold-embroidered garments, known as kinjuban, worn by children who take part in the festival procession, offering a closer look at details that are easy to miss amid the noise of the real event.

Admission is inexpensive, and the museum takes well under an hour to see properly, making it a natural stop on the walk between Inuyama Castle and Urakuen Garden.

62 Higashikoken, Inuyama, Aichi

Open in Google Maps

About an 8 to 10-minute walk from Inuyama Station on the Meitetsu line, near the base of Inuyama Castle.
