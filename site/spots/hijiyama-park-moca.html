Hijiyama Park and Hiroshima City Museum of Contemporary Art

Hiroshima

Hijiyama Park, opened in 1903 as Hiroshima's first city park, sits on a modest 71-meter hill in Minami-ku with clear views across the urban grid toward the bay, and its roughly 1,300 cherry trees make it one of the city's major hanami spots each spring — busy with local families and office parties, but noticeably less crowded than Peace Memorial Park during the same season.

At the top of the hill, the Hiroshima City Museum of Contemporary Art, designed by metabolist architect Kisho Kurokawa and opened in 1989, holds the distinction of being the first Japanese public museum built entirely around contemporary art rather than adding a contemporary wing to an existing collection. Its permanent holdings run to more than 1,400 works addressing art movements since 1945, several engaging directly with Hiroshima's own postwar history.

The park itself has no gate or entrance fee and no closing time, making it a flexible stop whether you're there for a quick walk, a full cherry-blossom picnic, or a dedicated museum visit — a useful counterweight to Hiroshima's downtown sights for visitors who want green space without leaving the city center.
