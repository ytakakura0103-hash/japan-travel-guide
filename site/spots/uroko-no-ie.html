Local Pick

Uroko no Ie (House of Scales)

Kobe

Uroko no Ie takes its name from the grey scale-patterned slate tiles covering its exterior, giving the whole building a textured, almost reptilian look that stands out even among Kitano's varied architecture. It was originally built as a foreign residence for rent near the port before later being relocated to its current hillside spot, and it is generally considered the first of Kobe's ijinkan to open to the public as a paid house museum, a role it has held for decades.

Inside, the furnished rooms give a sense of an affluent foreign household from the early 1900s, and the attached Uroko Museum of Fine Arts, included with the same ticket, displays European paintings and decorative art that many visitors do not realize is part of the same admission. A full visit, house plus gallery, runs close to 45 minutes for those who read the exhibits, less for a quick walkthrough.

It sits slightly apart from the main Kitano cluster around the Weathercock House, which means it gets meaningfully less foot traffic even at peak times. The small terrace out front has one of the better rooftop views over Kobe's harbor of any building in the district, and it is rarely crowded enough to fight for the spot.

2-20-4 Kitanocho, Chuo-ku, Kobe

About a 15-minute walk uphill from Sannomiya Station, a short walk beyond the main Kitano cluster.
