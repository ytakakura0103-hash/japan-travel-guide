Ramen Yokocho

Hokkaido

Tucked into Susukino, Ramen Yokocho is only about two blocks long, but it packs in a dense row of small ramen shops, most seating well under twenty people, each with its own take on the rich, miso-based style Sapporo is known for nationally. The alley's postwar-era roots as a cluster of street stalls give it a scrappier, more local feel than the polished ramen halls found in shopping malls elsewhere in the city.

Because the individual shops are so small, queues form quickly once one or two fill up, especially for the handful of shops with strong reputations; being willing to try a less-famous counter often means a shorter wait and a meal just as good. Butter and corn as ramen toppings are a genuinely Hokkaido touch worth trying here if not encountered elsewhere.

Late evening, after the main dinner rush around 7-8pm, tends to be calmer than the 6-7pm window when both locals and visitors converge. Some shops keep unusual or late hours, running into the early morning, which fits the surrounding nightlife district's rhythm. Winter is arguably the best season for a bowl here if only for the contrast between the cold streets outside and the small, steamy interior of each shop.

Minami 5 Jo Nishi 3-chome, Chuo-ku, Sapporo, Hokkaido
