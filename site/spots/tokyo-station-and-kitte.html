Tokyo Station & KITTE Marunouchi

Tokyo

Tokyo Station's red-brick Marunouchi building, completed in 1914 and carefully restored to its original three-domed design after wartime damage and decades of postwar alterations, is one of the city's most recognizable pieces of early 20th-century architecture, standing in deliberate contrast to the glass towers surrounding it. The domed ceilings inside the station's north and south entrance halls are decorated with detailed relief sculptures and are worth a look even for visitors just passing through on a train.

Facing the station across the plaza, the KITTE Marunouchi complex occupies a former post office building and combines shops and restaurants with a small postal museum and an open rooftop garden. The rooftop is the real draw for many visitors: it looks directly down onto the platforms and tracks of Tokyo Station, giving an unusually close view of shinkansen and commuter trains arriving and departing, especially striking after dark when the station building is lit up.

Both the station exterior and the KITTE rooftop are free to visit, and neither requires much time on their own, perhaps thirty minutes to an hour combined, making this an easy add-on to a day spent in the Marunouchi or Ginza area rather than a standalone destination. Weekday lunchtime brings out office workers eating in the plaza, while the rooftop is generally calm throughout the day, with early evening offering the best light for photos of the trains below.
