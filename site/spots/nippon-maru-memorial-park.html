Nippon Maru Memorial Park and Yokohama Port Museum

Yokohama

Nicknamed the swan of the Pacific for its grace under sail, the Nippon Maru trained generations of Japanese sailors from the late 1930s onward before retiring to this dry dock in the 1980s. Visitors can walk the deck and go below into the crew quarters and engine room, and on certain scheduled days volunteer crews unfurl the sails for a few hours, which is worth timing a visit around if you can find the schedule in advance.

The attached Yokohama Port Museum is compact but goes deeper into the story of how a fishing village became one of Japan's first treaty ports after 1859, with models and artifacts tracing the port's growth. It is a useful stop for context before wandering the Red Brick Warehouse or Osanbashi pier nearby, both built during the same era of port development.

The park itself is a pleasant, uncrowded green space even when the ship and museum are busy, since many passersby only look at the ship from outside without paying to board. It sits right next to Sakuragicho Station, making it an easy first or last stop on a Minato Mirai day, and the dry dock building is illuminated in the evening for a quieter alternative photo spot to the busier waterfront areas.
