Komaki Castle

Nagoya

Oda Nobunaga built Komaki Castle in 1563, his third castle after Nagoya and Kiyosu, choosing a solitary 86-meter hill north of the Nagoya area for its commanding view over the flat Nobi Plain during his campaign against the Saito clan of neighboring Mino Province. What made Komaki historically significant was not its size but its design: it was among the first Japanese castles to make extensive use of stacked-stone defensive walls, arranged in concentric rings around the summit, a technique that would later be refined into the more famous stonework of Nobunaga's later castle at Azuchi.

Komaki also broke new ground by pairing the hilltop fortress with a purpose-built castle town laid out below it and partially protected by moats and earthen walls, an early example of the administrative-residential castle-town model that later became standard across Japan. Nobunaga abandoned Komaki as his main base in 1567 after defeating the Saito and moving on to Gifu, but the site saw renewed military significance in 1584 during the Komaki-Nagakute campaign between Toyotomi Hideyoshi and Tokugawa Ieyasu.

Today the hill is a public park topped by a small history museum and observation hall, with walking paths through the old stone-wall remains for visitors willing to climb.
