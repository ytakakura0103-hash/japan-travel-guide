Togetsukyo Bridge

Kyoto

Togetsukyo, meaning "moon crossing bridge," spans the Katsura River (called the Hozugawa upstream) at the entrance to central Arashiyama, and its current version, rebuilt multiple times over the centuries in a style using wood for the railings over a modern support structure, has become the visual symbol of the district. The mountains rising directly behind it, Arashiyama itself, turn a soft pink with cherry blossoms in spring and a dense red and orange with maples in autumn, and the bridge is positioned as the natural foreground for photographing both.

Walking across the bridge itself takes only a few minutes, and there's no fee involved; most of the time spent here is really spent along the riverbank on either side, where boat rentals, walking paths, and river-facing cafes give reasons to linger. A relaxed stop at the bridge and immediate riverside area runs anywhere from twenty minutes to an hour depending on how much time is spent by the water.

Because it sits at the entrance to Arashiyama's main sightseeing area, the bridge and surrounding riverbank get busy from mid-morning through the afternoon, especially on weekends and during the two peak foliage seasons; an early morning visit, before the bamboo grove and temple crowds spill down to the river, is the clearest way to see it with some open space.
