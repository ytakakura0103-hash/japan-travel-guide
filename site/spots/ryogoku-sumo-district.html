Ryogoku Sumo District

Tokyo

Ryogoku is the historic home of sumo in Tokyo, centered on the Ryogoku Kokugikan, the arena that hosts three of the sport's six annual grand tournaments (in January, May, and September). During a tournament, the neighborhood comes alive with fans, wrestlers in yukata walking between the station and the arena, and a roar from inside the hall that can be heard from the street; tickets for the cheaper upper-level seats are the most realistic option for casual visitors, though they can still be hard to get for popular days.

Outside of tournament periods, Ryogoku is quieter but still worth a visit for anyone interested in sumo culture. The neighborhood has one of the highest concentrations of sumo stables (heya) in Japan, where wrestlers live and train; some stables allow public viewing of morning practice, though rules and availability vary and typically require advance arrangement. Chanko-nabe, the protein-heavy hot pot dish associated with a sumo wrestler's diet, is served at numerous restaurants throughout the area year-round, many run by retired wrestlers.

The nearby Sumida Hokusai Museum and Edo-Tokyo Museum make Ryogoku easy to combine with a broader half-day of sightseeing. A tournament-day visit can easily take several hours if you plan to watch matches; a non-tournament visit focused on walking the district and eating chanko-nabe fits comfortably into an hour or two.
