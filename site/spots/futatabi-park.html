Futatabi Park

Kobe

Named for a temple that legend says the monk Kukai visited twice, Futatabi Park occupies a valley of pine and maple forest just north of central Kobe, centered on a reservoir lake ringed by walking paths. It's the kind of park that fills with dog walkers, trail runners and retirees doing morning exercise routines rather than visitors with cameras, which is part of its charm.

Several hiking routes fan out from the park toward Mt. Maya and the wider Rokko mountain range, so it also works as a starting point for a longer day hike if you're equipped for one. Closer to the entrance, flatter paved paths loop around the reservoir and are manageable for a relaxed hour-long walk.

The park is one of Kobe's better-regarded spots for autumn foliage among people who actually live here, with maples coloring up from mid-November in a typical year, though timing shifts year to year so it's worth checking recent reports before a dedicated trip. Because it sits slightly further from the station than Nunobiki, it sees a fraction of the foot traffic despite arguably prettier forest scenery.

Futatabisan-cho, Chuo Ward, Kobe

From Shin-Kobe Station or Sannomiya Station, take a bus toward Futatabi-koen, or continue on foot/by trail from the Nunobiki Falls and Arboretum area (roughly 30-45 minutes further on foot).
