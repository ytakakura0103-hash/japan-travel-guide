Kobe Maritime Museum & Kawasaki World

Kobe

The museum's exterior, a soaring white steel-frame roof shaped like a ship's sail or ocean waves, has become as photographed as the tower it sits beside in Meriken Park. Inside, exhibits walk through the history of Kobe's harbor from the 1868 opening of the port to foreign trade, including ship models, old cargo equipment, and displays on the devastation and rebuilding after the 1995 Great Hanshin earthquake.

Attached under the same roof is Kawasaki World, run by the heavy-machinery maker Kawasaki, where visitors can climb into a replica Shinkansen driver's cab, sit on a full-scale motorcycle model, and see submarine and aircraft engine displays aimed at both kids and engineering-curious adults. Combined, the two halls make for a fairly compact visit, generally an hour or so, and are easy to pair with a stroll around Meriken Park.

Because it sits right next to the more famous Port Tower, foot traffic is heaviest in the early afternoon; arriving soon after opening tends to mean quieter galleries. The museum is indoors and air-conditioned, making it a reasonable rainy-day or midsummer option compared to the open-air park around it.

2-2 Hatobacho, Chuo-ku, Kobe

Open in Google Maps

About a 10-minute walk from JR Kobe Station or Kobe Sannomiya Station, next to Kobe Port Tower in Meriken Park.
