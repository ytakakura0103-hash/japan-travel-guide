Mount Yotei

Sapporo

Mount Yotei's strikingly regular cone shape, close enough to Mount Fuji's silhouette to have earned it the nickname Ezo Fuji (Ezo being an old name for Hokkaido), makes it a near-constant backdrop to the Niseko and Lake Toya area rather than a single destination visited on its own. Skiers at Niseko in particular spend entire winter days with the mountain framing the view from the lift lines, even if relatively few actually climb it.

In summer, roughly June through October depending on snow conditions, several hiking trails lead to the crater rim at the summit, a genuinely full-day hike given the mountain's isolated, freestanding height; it's a serious undertaking rather than a casual walk, and weather on an exposed volcanic peak can turn quickly even in summer.

Because public transport barely reaches the trailheads, most visitors experience Mount Yotei as scenery from Niseko, Lake Toya or the surrounding farmland rather than as a summit to reach, and a rental car genuinely opens up far more viewpoints than relying on buses. Dawn and the golden hour before sunset tend to give the clearest, least hazy views of the peak, and winter, when the cone is fully snow-capped, is when it looks most like its Fuji-esque nickname.
