Roppongi Hills & Mori Art Museum

Tokyo

Roppongi Hills is a large mixed-use complex of office towers, apartments, shops, and restaurants built around Tokyo's Mori Tower, and its main draw for visitors is the Mori Art Museum, a contemporary art space occupying the 53rd floor with a rotating program of major exhibitions rather than a fixed permanent collection. A ticket to the museum typically includes access to the adjoining Tokyo City View indoor observation floor, and for an additional fee, the open-air Sky Deck one floor above offers an unobstructed rooftop view across the city.

At ground level, the complex has a large outdoor plaza dominated by a giant bronze spider sculpture, one of Roppongi Hills' best-known photo spots, along with seasonal events and illuminations, particularly around winter. The surrounding Roppongi neighborhood is one of Tokyo's busiest nightlife districts, with a dense concentration of bars and clubs that pick up well after the shops and museum have closed, so the same area has a noticeably different character by day and by night.

A visit to the museum and observation deck together takes roughly two hours, longer if a major exhibition is showing. Weekday evenings, when the museum stays open later than most, are a good time to combine art with a sunset or night view without the daytime crowds. Roppongi Hills is part of Tokyo's informal "Art Triangle" along with the nearby National Art Center and Suntory Museum of Art.
