Shin-Okubo Korea Town

Tokyo

Shin-Okubo Korea Town grew from a small enclave of Korean residents and businesses into one of Tokyo's most concentrated single-cuisine, single-culture shopping streets, packed into just a few blocks immediately north of Shinjuku. The main strip is lined with Korean barbecue and fried chicken restaurants, tteokbokki and hotteok street stalls, cosmetics shops stocked with the latest Korean skincare trends, and multiple floors of K-pop merchandise stores selling photocards, albums, and light sticks tied to current idol groups.

The area is genuinely busiest on weekends, when fans of Korean pop culture — a mix of Japanese teenagers and international visitors — pack the narrow main street shoulder to shoulder, and some of the more popular cosmetics and merchandise shops can have short lines at the register. Visiting on a weekday, or arriving in the late morning before the lunch crowd builds, makes for a noticeably easier walk through the strip. A couple of hours covers a casual visit that includes browsing shops and a meal, though dedicated K-pop merchandise hunting can easily take longer.

Cash is still commonly needed at smaller stalls even though card payment has become more widespread. Shin-Okubo sits an extremely short walk from Shin-Okubo Station and is close enough to Shinjuku itself to combine easily with a day spent in that district.
