Osu Kannon Temple

Nagoya

Osu Kannon's bright red-and-white main hall feels almost theatrical compared with the understated wooden shrines elsewhere in the city, and it sits right at the entrance to the buzzing Osu shopping district, so temple and street life blend into one visit. The temple traces its origins back centuries and relocated to its current site in the early Edo period; its library once held an extremely rare manuscript of the Kojiki, Japan's oldest chronicle, along with other classical texts of national importance.

The grounds are free to enter and can be seen in ten or fifteen minutes, though pigeons, incense smoke, and the steady flow of visitors buying charms make it worth lingering a little. A market selling antiques, curios, and secondhand goods sets up on the temple grounds on certain days of the month, drawing collectors from across the region.

Because it sits at the mouth of a major shopping street, the immediate plaza gets crowded on weekend afternoons; visiting on a weekday morning gives a calmer view of the hall itself before diving into the surrounding arcades. Illuminations and events around New Year draw especially large crowds.
