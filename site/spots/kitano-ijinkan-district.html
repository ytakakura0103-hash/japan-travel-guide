Kitano Ijinkan District

Kobe

When Kobe opened to foreign trade in 1868, merchants and diplomats from Europe, the US, and elsewhere settled on the hillside above the harbor and built houses in their home styles rather than Japanese ones. Dozens of these ijinkan (foreigner's houses) survive today, scattered along sloping lanes, and about fifteen or so are open to the public as small house museums, each with its own admission and character, from half-timbered European cottages to a colonial-style veranda house.

Most visitors pick a handful of houses to actually enter, such as the Weathercock House and Moegi House near the top of the hill, and simply walk past the rest, since the streetscape itself, with its stone walls, slate roofs, and mixed architectural styles, is much of the appeal. A loop through the core of the district, popping into two or three houses, typically takes a couple of hours including photo stops.

The area gets busy on weekends and around cherry blossom or autumn foliage season, so a weekday morning visit makes a real difference in how much of the neighborhood you get to yourself. The uphill walk from Kitano-zaka is fairly steep, so comfortable shoes help more here than almost anywhere else in central Kobe.
