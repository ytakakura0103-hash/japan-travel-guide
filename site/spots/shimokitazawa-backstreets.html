Shimokitazawa Backstreets

Tokyo

Shimokitazawa is a warren of narrow, largely car-free lanes just a few train stops from Shibuya, and it has long been one of Tokyo's centers for secondhand fashion, live music, and small independent theaters. Racks of vintage clothing spill out of shopfronts, standing bars and tiny izakaya are tucked into alleys barely wide enough for two people to pass, and the overall pace feels closer to a university town than a tourist district, which is largely because it is one: the area is popular with students and young creatives rather than tour groups.

There's no single must-see landmark here; the appeal is in wandering without much of a plan, ducking into vintage and record shops, and stopping for coffee or a drink when something looks interesting. The area also has a strong live music and theater scene, with small venues hosting bands and plays most nights of the week.

Shimokitazawa is at its liveliest in the evening, when the bars and standing counters fill up, and quieter (and easier to browse shops) earlier in the afternoon. Weekends bring bigger crowds, particularly in the evening. Because the neighborhood is spread across several small blocks rather than one main street, allowing two to three hours gives enough time to explore a good portion of it without feeling rushed.
