Jozankei Onsen

Hokkaido

Jozankei sits inside a narrow gorge cut by the Toyohira River, and although it's still administratively part of Sapporo, the drive or bus ride in feels like leaving the city entirely, with steep forested slopes closing in around the road. The hot spring here was discovered and developed in the 19th century and has since grown into a proper onsen resort town with ryokan of varying sizes lining the riverbanks.

Because it doesn't require an overnight stay to enjoy, many visitors treat it as a half-day escape from downtown Sapporo, either booking a day-use bath at one of the larger hotels or simply walking along the river and browsing the small handful of shops in the town center. Autumn foliage along the gorge, typically peaking in mid-to-late October, is a particular local draw and adds real color to what is otherwise a fairly quiet, low-key town.

Weekday visits are noticeably calmer than weekends, when day-trippers from Sapporo fill up the more popular day-use baths. Winter brings snow-viewing baths with a genuinely dramatic contrast between hot water and falling snow, which is arguably the town's best-known selling point, though it does mean icy walking paths need care. A free shuttle or footbath area near the town center is a reasonable way to sample the atmosphere without committing to a full bath if time is short.
