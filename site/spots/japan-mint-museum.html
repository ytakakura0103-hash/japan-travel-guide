Japan Mint Museum

Osaka

Inside the headquarters of Japan's official mint, this small museum traces the history of Japanese currency and medal-making through coin collections, minting equipment and historical documents, offering a fairly specialist but genuinely unique look at a side of Japanese history rarely covered at larger museums. A visit through the exhibition floors takes around thirty to forty-five minutes for most visitors, longer for anyone with a particular interest in numismatics.

The museum's real claim to fame, though, is the back lane running through the mint grounds, lined with roughly 350 cherry trees of many different varieties, which is opened to the public for only about one week each April, timed to the blossoms' peak and drawing large crowds during that narrow window. Outside that week, the lane is closed to the public and the museum itself receives a much smaller, steadier stream of visitors.

If visiting during the cherry blossom opening, expect queues and crowds, especially on weekends within that week, and consider a weekday or early morning visit for a calmer walk beneath the trees. Admission to the museum itself is free, and the cherry blossom lane opening is also free to enter during its public days. The mint sits a fair walk from the nearest station, closer to Sakuranomiya or Temmabashi depending on route, so it's worth checking current transit options before visiting, particularly during the crowded blossom week.

1-1-79 Temma, Kita Ward, Osaka
