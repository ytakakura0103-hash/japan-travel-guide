Club Metro

Kyoto

Club Metro has run out of a basement space beneath Jingu-Marutamachi Station since 1990, making it the oldest surviving nightclub in Kyoto by a wide margin in a city where nightlife venues tend to have a shorter lifespan than in Tokyo or Osaka. Its booking policy has always ranged wider than most single-genre clubs, covering house, techno, hip-hop, reggae, and rock across different nights, alongside an associated program called Metro Daigaku that brings arts and culture events into the same space.

Its location near Kyoto University and the Kamo River's left bank puts it close to Hyakumanben's student nightlife cluster, and the club has long drawn a crowd that overlaps with the university's student population as well as Kyoto's broader independent music scene. That longevity hasn't come without difficulty; the club has publicly discussed financial pressures at points over the decades, including community fundraising support, which underscores how precarious small independent venues can be even when they've operated for over thirty years.

Given its underground, community-rooted character, Club Metro suits visitors specifically interested in independent electronic and alternative music culture more than a mainstream night out. As with any long-running but resource-constrained venue, checking the current event calendar before visiting is worth doing, both to see what's on and to confirm operating hours.
