Chidorigafuchi

Tokyo

Chidorigafuchi runs along the northwest edge of the Imperial Palace grounds, where a wide moat is lined on one bank by a path shaded with cherry trees whose branches lean far out over the water, creating one of Tokyo's most photographed cherry blossom scenes each spring. Rowboats are available to rent during the bloom period, letting visitors drift beneath the overhanging branches at water level rather than only viewing them from the path above — a popular enough activity that boat rental lines can form well before the boats open in the morning during peak bloom.

After dark during the height of cherry blossom season, the trees and moat are illuminated by lanterns, extending the viewing period into the evening and drawing some of the largest crowds of the entire season along the walking path. Outside peak bloom, the moat and path are far quieter and make for a pleasant, unhurried walk along the edge of the Imperial Palace grounds at almost any time of year.

A walk along the full length of the path takes twenty to thirty minutes; renting a boat adds roughly another thirty minutes including the wait. There is no fee to walk the path itself, though boat rental is paid separately. Weekday mornings offer the calmest cherry blossom viewing, while weekend evenings during peak bloom are, by a wide margin, the busiest time to visit.
