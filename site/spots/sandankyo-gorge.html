Sandankyo Gorge

Hiroshima

Sandankyo Gorge cuts through the mountains of what is now Akiota-cho, northwest of central Hiroshima, following the Shibaki River for roughly 16 kilometers, and it is officially designated a Special Place of Scenic Beauty, one of the highest such classifications in Japan and the only one held by a gorge in western Japan. The name Sandankyo, meaning roughly "three-step gorge," refers to its signature feature, Sandandaki waterfall, which drops in three distinct stages over a total height of around 130 meters.

Trail options range considerably in length: a walk as far as a spot called Kurobuchi takes roughly 50 minutes each way and covers some of the gorge's scenic highlights without requiring a full-day commitment, while reaching Sandandaki itself is a longer hike of several hours round trip through forest and along the riverbank. Small boat rides are also available on certain calmer stretches of the river within the gorge during the operating season, offering a lower-effort way to see part of the valley.

The gorge is only open seasonally, roughly from mid-April to late November, and closes over winter. Autumn is generally considered the peak season for visitors, when the forest along the trails turns to red and gold, though this also brings the largest crowds of the year.

Togouchi, Akiota-cho, Yamagata District, Hiroshima Prefecture
