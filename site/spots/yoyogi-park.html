Yoyogi Park

Tokyo

Yoyogi Park is one of Tokyo's largest green spaces, and unlike the more manicured gardens nearby it has a loose, unstructured feel — wide lawns, a network of paths under tall trees, and enough open ground that street performers, drum circles, dance groups, and picnicking families can all share the space without crowding each other out. Weekends in particular tend to bring pop-up food festivals, flea markets, or themed events to the areas near the park's main entrances, so the specific atmosphere on any given visit can vary a lot depending on the day and season.

In cherry blossom season, generally late March into early April, Yoyogi Park becomes one of the liveliest hanami spots in the city, with picnic blankets covering much of the open lawn from late morning onward on warm weekends; arriving early or visiting on a weekday morning is the more comfortable way to see the blossoms without competing for grass space. Outside of peak season, the park is far more relaxed, and a slow walk plus some time on a bench can easily fill an hour or two.

There is no admission fee, and the park connects directly to Meiji Jingu's forested approach on one side and Harajuku's shopping streets on the other, making it a natural midpoint for a day that mixes nature, shrine visits, and people-watching.
