Kobe Muslim Mosque

Kobe

Completed in 1935 with donations from Turkish, Tatar, and Indian Muslim traders who had settled in Kobe's port economy, this mosque holds the distinction of being the first purpose-built mosque in Japan. Designed by a Czech architect working in a style that blends Turkish and broader Islamic architectural elements with a European sensibility, the building has survived being requisitioned during the war, the air raids of 1945, and the Great Hanshin earthquake of 1995 with relatively little structural damage, which locals sometimes point to as remarkable given how much of the surrounding neighborhood was rebuilt.

The mosque remains an active place of worship for Kobe's Muslim community, so visitors should dress modestly and be mindful during prayer times, but respectful visits outside those hours are generally welcomed and the exterior can always be photographed from the street.

It sits just a short detour from the main Kitano Ijinkan walking route, yet very few tourists make the small diversion, so it tends to be one of the quietest architectural stops in the whole hillside district. Even a brief pause outside to take in the minaret and dome against Kobe's cityscape offers a different angle on the city's long history as a meeting point of cultures.

2-25-14 Nakayamatedori, Chuo-ku, Kobe
