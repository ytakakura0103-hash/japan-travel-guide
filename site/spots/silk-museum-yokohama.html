Local Pick

Silk Museum

Yokohama

Silk was, for decades after the port opened in 1859, Yokohama's most important export, and this modest museum lays out that history through exhibits on sericulture, weaving, and the trade networks that once made the city the country's main gateway for raw silk heading abroad. Live or preserved silkworm displays give a hands-on sense of the material's origins that is easy to overlook when only seeing finished silk products in shops.

The museum is small enough to see thoroughly in under an hour, which makes it an easy addition rather than a dedicated half-day stop, and its modest size also means it almost never feels crowded, even when Yamashita Park outside is busy. An attached shop sells scarves and other silk goods, offering a souvenir option with a more specific local connection than typical Chinatown gift shops.

Because exhibit content and hours can change, and the museum has a lower profile than the area's bigger attractions, it is worth confirming it is open before a special trip. It sits close enough to Yamashita Park and the Marine Tower to fit naturally into a slower-paced harbor walk for visitors interested in Yokohama's trading history beyond the usual port and ship narratives.

1 Yamashita-cho, Naka-ku, Yokohama
