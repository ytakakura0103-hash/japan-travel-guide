Chigusa Jazz Kissa

Yokohama

Chigusa traces its roots back to the 1930s, making it one of the pioneering jazz kissa, a distinctly Japanese institution where the point is not conversation but silent, devoted listening to vinyl through a carefully tuned speaker setup. The current shop is a later incarnation of the original after wartime disruption and later closures, run by devotees determined to keep the format alive rather than let it fade into nostalgia pieces.

Unlike a bar, the etiquette here leans quiet: you sip your coffee or a drink, and you listen, rather than chat over background music. It rewards visitors with some patience and curiosity about Japan's postwar jazz-cafe culture, which once numbered in the hundreds across the country and has now dwindled to a small, dedicated circle of holdouts.

It sits in the Noge district, so it pairs naturally with an evening covering the Miyakobashi bar alley nearby, letting you contrast the hushed reverence of the jazz kissa with Noge's louder drinking scene just a few minutes' walk away. Seating is limited, so this is not a place to bring a large group, and mobile phone calls or loud talk will draw quiet disapproval from regulars.
