Ehrismann Residence & Motomachi Park

Yokohama

This house was built for Fritz Ehrismann, a manager at a Swiss silk-trading firm, and designed by Antonin Raymond, the Czech-American architect often credited as a father figure of modern architecture in Japan through his decades of later work across the country. Compared to the more ornate Berrick Hall nearby, the Ehrismann Residence feels restrained and functional, an early hint of the modernist sensibility Raymond would go on to develop.

It now sits inside Motomachi Park, a small green space built over part of Yokohama's old water reservoir system, and remnants of that infrastructure, brick arches and stonework, are still visible near the park's edges if you look for them. The park itself is a quiet break from the shopping crowds on Motomachi's main street just below the hill.

Because the residence and park sit at the boundary between Motomachi's shopping strip and the quieter Yamate bluff, it works well as a transition point on a walking route, letting you step from souvenir shops into a genuinely peaceful architectural and green space in a couple of minutes. Weekday mornings are the quietest time to explore both the house and the park grounds.

89-6 Motomachi, Naka-ku, Yokohama

Open in Google Maps

About 5 minutes on foot from Minatomirai Line Motomachi-Chukagai Station (exit 5), via the Motomachi shopping street.
