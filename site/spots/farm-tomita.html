Farm Tomita

Hokkaido

Farm Tomita has grown lavender commercially since the 1950s, originally for the perfume industry before that business collapsed under competition from synthetic fragrances; the farm survived by pivoting toward tourism, and its striped rows of lavender and other flowers became one of the most recognizable images of Hokkaido summer. Beyond lavender, the farm grows several other flower varieties in bands of color across its slopes, so there's more to see than a single purple field even outside peak lavender season.

Lavender typically peaks from roughly early to mid-July through early August, though exact timing shifts year to year with the weather, so checking a current bloom report before planning a trip specifically around it is worth the effort. Lavender-flavored soft serve and other lavender products are sold on-site and have become something of a tradition for visitors.

July weekends during peak bloom are extremely crowded, with tour buses arriving in waves through the late morning and early afternoon; visiting on a weekday, or arriving right at opening, noticeably thins the crowd. Outside lavender season, roughly September through June, the farm is far quieter but also far less colorful, so timing genuinely matters here more than at most Hokkaido attractions.

Kisen, Nakafurano-cho, Sorachi-gun, Hokkaido

A seasonal direct limited express, the Furano Lavender Express, runs from Sapporo Station to Furano Station in summer (about 2 hours), but Furano Station itself is not within easy walking distance of the farm; otherwise transfer via Takikawa to Naka-Furano Station on the JR Furano Line, which is about a 25-minute walk from the farm. During the Furano-Biei Norokko sightseeing train season, the seasonal Lavender Farm Station is much closer, about a 7-minute walk away.
