Kenninji Temple

Kyoto

Kenninji, founded in the early 13th century, is considered the oldest Zen temple in Kyoto and sits at the quieter southern edge of Gion, largely bypassed by the crowds moving through the district's main streets. Its most striking feature is a large painted ceiling in the hatto lecture hall depicting twin dragons, a 21st-century work commissioned to mark the temple's 800th anniversary, along with a well-known reproduction of the Wind and Thunder Gods folding screen, one of the most reproduced images in Japanese art.

The temple's dry rock garden and a separate moss and maple garden give it a more traditional counterpoint to the dragon ceiling, and the layout allows a fairly leisurely, self-paced walk through several connected buildings and gardens. A full visit typically takes around forty-five minutes to an hour. Admission involves a modest fee.

Because it's overshadowed by Gion's more famous sights and Kiyomizu-dera nearby, Kenninji tends to stay noticeably quieter even during peak tourist hours, making it a reliable option for visitors looking for a calmer temple experience without leaving the Higashiyama area. There's little need to plan around specific times of day here, though weekday visits are naturally the quietest. Its central Gion location makes it an easy stop alongside Hanamikoji Street and Yasui Konpiragu.

584 Komatsucho, Higashiyama Ward, Kyoto

7-minute walk from Gion-Shijo Station (Keihan Main Line)
