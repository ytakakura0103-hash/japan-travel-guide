Kanzeonji Temple

Fukuoka

Kanzeonji was established in the seventh century on the order of Emperor Tenji and grew into the leading Buddhist temple of Kyushu during the Nara and Heian periods, serving a role roughly comparable to Todaiji's standing in the historic capital. Fires and the passage of centuries reduced its once-extensive compound considerably, but the grounds still hold a bronze temple bell that scholars regard as among the oldest surviving in Japan, dated to the seventh century, along with a treasure hall containing a notable collection of wooden Buddhist statues.

What remains today is modest in scale compared to the temple's historical peak, and that modesty is part of its appeal: quiet gravel paths, old trees, and a treasure hall that rarely has more than a handful of visitors at a time, even though its contents rival far more famous collections. The adjacent Kyushu National Museum, connected by a short escalator tunnel through the hillside, offers useful context on the wider Dazaifu region's history before or after a visit.

Because it sits a short walk from, but distinctly apart from, the Dazaifu Tenmangu shopping crowds, Kanzeonji stays calm even during the shrine's busiest hours. A separate fee applies for the treasure hall beyond the free temple grounds.
