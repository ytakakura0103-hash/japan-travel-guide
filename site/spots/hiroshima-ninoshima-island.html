Ninoshima Island

Hiroshima

Ninoshima sits close enough to Hiroshima Port that it functions as a genuine local getaway rather than a tourist destination, with a laid-back beach where residents come to swim and watch the sunset over the Inland Sea, well away from the crowds of Miyajima.

The island's more difficult history sits alongside that leisure use: from 1895 it operated as a military quarantine station for the Imperial Army and Navy, and in August 1945, after the bombing of Hiroshima, it became one of the sites where thousands of injured and dying victims were brought for treatment, many of whom did not survive. Ruins of the old quarantine facilities, including an ammunition depot and lookout post, remain on the island, and a memorial marks the mass burial site of bombing victims.

Most visitors come either for the beach and a quiet walk in the hills, or specifically to see the historical remains, and few combine both in one short visit — but either way, Ninoshima offers a side of Hiroshima's story, and a version of its coastline, that the busier tourist circuit doesn't show.
