Local Pick

Yanagidani-kannon Yokoku-ji

Kyoto

Yanagidani-kannon Yokoku-ji is widely credited among Japanese social media users as the origin point of the now-widespread hanachozu trend, where a temple's stone water basin, traditionally used for the simple ritual of washing hands and mouth before approaching a shrine or temple, is filled instead with floating flowers arranged into a deliberate, photogenic display. What started here has since spread to temples and shrines across the country, but Yanagidani-kannon remains a reference point for the style, with several differently themed basins scattered through its grounds, including versions built around dragon, garden, and love motifs, and a moss-covered basin that plays on the temple's wooded setting.

Set into the hills west of central Kyoto, the temple itself has a long history as a site associated with eye ailments and healing, giving it a devotional character beyond its recent social media fame, and the surrounding grounds are quiet and wooded compared to the busier temples closer to the city center. Seasonal illumination events, including an autumn program that has run in previous years, add an evening dimension to what's otherwise a daytime destination.

Because it sits outside central Kyoto in Nagaokakyo, getting there takes more planning than the city-center temples, typically a taxi or local bus connection from the nearest train station, so it suits visitors willing to build a specific trip around it rather than a casual add-on to a central Kyoto day. Checking current basin displays and any seasonal events before visiting is worthwhile, since the flower arrangements change with the season and occasion.

Nagaokakyo, Kyoto Prefecture
