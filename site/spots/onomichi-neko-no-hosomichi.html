Neko no Hosomichi (Cat Alley)

Hiroshima

Threading uphill between Ushitora Shrine and Tenneiji Temple, this narrow stone path earned its nickname after local artist Shunji Sonoyama began tucking small painted 'fukuseki neko' (lucky stone cats) into gaps in the walls in the late 1990s. Decades on, the cats have multiplied into the hundreds, joined by cat-themed knick-knacks left by visitors and shopkeepers.\n\nWhat makes the alley worth the climb isn't just the cats but the buildings around them: converted wooden houses now hold one-room galleries, secondhand bookshops, and coffee counters barely big enough for three stools. Actual neighborhood cats also nap on the warm stone steps, so don't be surprised if a painted one and a real one are sitting side by side.\n\nGo slowly rather than rushing straight up to Senkoji ropeway station — most of the charm is in the side alleys branching off the main path, where laundry lines cross overhead and the sea occasionally flashes into view between rooftops. Morning light before the tour groups arrive is the best time to shoot photos without other people in frame.

Higashitsuchidocho, Onomichi, Hiroshima

About a 5-minute walk uphill from JR Onomichi Station, following signs toward Senkoji Temple/Tenneiji Temple.
