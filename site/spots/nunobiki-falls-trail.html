Nunobiki Falls

Kobe

Nunobiki Falls has been drawing visitors since the Heian period, when it was celebrated in classical poetry, and the trail leading up to it still follows the same stream valley today. The path climbs gently past four named falls, Medaki, Ontaki, Tsuzumigataki and Yunotaki, each with its own small viewing platform, before continuing on toward Nunobiki Herb Garden and the Rokko ropeway if you want to extend the walk.\n\nWhat makes it unusual is the location: most cities bury their best short hikes far from the center, but this one starts a few minutes from a Shinkansen platform. On a weekday morning it's common to have long stretches of the path with only the sound of water for company, despite being in the middle of a city of over a million people.\n\nThe walk to the main falls and back takes roughly an hour at an easy pace, longer if you continue on to the herb garden. Paths are paved or well-maintained dirt with handrails on steeper sections, but they do involve real elevation gain, so wear shoes with grip rather than sandals.\n\nAutumn brings good foliage color along the stream, while summer offers cool shade that's noticeably cooler than the streets below.

Kitano-cho / Shin-Kobe area, Kobe

The trailhead is a few minutes' walk from Shin-Kobe Station (Kobe Municipal Subway, or the Shinkansen); follow signs for Nunobiki Falls from the station's north side.
