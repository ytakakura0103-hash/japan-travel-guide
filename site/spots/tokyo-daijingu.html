Tokyo Daijingu

Tokyo

Tokyo Daijingu earned its nickname as "Tokyo's Ise Shrine" because it was established as a branch shrine of the Ise Grand Shrine, allowing worshippers in the capital to pay respects to the same deities enshrined at Ise without making the long trip themselves. Over time it developed a distinct reputation of its own, becoming one of Tokyo's most visited shrines for prayers related to love, marriage, and good relationships — a focus reflected in the steady queue of mostly young visitors buying its well-regarded en-musubi (relationship-tying) charms and writing wishes on ema plaques.

Tucked into a quiet residential slope near Iidabashi, the shrine occupies a modest footprint compared to Tokyo's larger, more famous shrines, and a visit typically takes twenty to thirty minutes unless you're waiting in line for a popular charm, which can happen on weekends or around days considered auspicious for romance, such as Valentine's Day. Weekday mornings are consistently the quietest time to visit and browse the charm stalls without a wait.

There is no admission fee. Because the shrine is small and easy to combine with other stops, it pairs naturally with a walk around Iidabashi or a visit to nearby Koishikawa Korakuen garden, both an easy walk away, rather than requiring a dedicated trip on its own.

2-4-1 Fujimi, Chiyoda City, Tokyo
