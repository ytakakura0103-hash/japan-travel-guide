Ninnaji Temple

Kyoto

Ninnaji was founded in the late 9th century as a temple closely tied to the imperial family, historically headed by a member of the imperial line, and its grounds today hold a five-story pagoda, several halls, and a UNESCO World Heritage listing as part of the Historic Monuments of Ancient Kyoto. Its most distinctive feature is a grove of Omuro cherry trees, an unusually short variety that rarely grows taller than a person, planted low enough that the blossoms sit roughly at eye level rather than overhead as with most cherry trees elsewhere in the city.

Because Omuro cherries bloom later than most other varieties, often into mid-April, Ninnaji tends to be one of the last places in Kyoto to see cherry blossoms each spring, giving visitors who miss the earlier peak elsewhere in the city another chance. Outside of cherry season, the temple's halls, pagoda, and gardens are worth a visit in their own right, with a walk through the main grounds taking around forty-five minutes to an hour. Admission to the outer grounds is free, with a modest fee for entering specific halls and, during bloom, the cherry grove itself.

The temple is busiest during its cherry blossom weeks but otherwise sees a fairly light, steady flow of visitors, making early mornings pleasant but not strictly necessary outside of peak bloom.

33 Omuroouchi, Ukyo Ward, Kyoto
