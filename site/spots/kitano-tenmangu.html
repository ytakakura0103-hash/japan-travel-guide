Kitano Tenmangu

Kyoto

Kitano Tenmangu is dedicated to Sugawara no Michizane, a 9th-century scholar and official later deified as the god of learning, which makes the shrine a popular stop for students around exam season, who come to buy charms and write wishes for academic success. The shrine's grounds hold roughly 1,500 plum trees, Michizane's favored tree according to tradition, which bloom in a long, staggered season through February into early March, well ahead of Kyoto's cherry blossoms and considerably less crowded to view.

On the 25th of every month, marking the day associated with both Michizane's birth and death, the shrine hosts Tenjin-san, a large antiques and flea market that fills the grounds and surrounding streets with stalls selling everything from antique kimono and pottery to street food, drawing a very different crowd than a typical shrine visit. The grounds outside market days can be walked in about thirty minutes; there's no fee to enter the main precinct, though the plum grove sometimes has a separate modest admission during peak bloom.

Kitano Tenmangu is naturally busiest during exam season, plum blossom season, and especially on the monthly market date, when the surrounding streets can get genuinely crowded from mid-morning on; visiting outside the 25th and outside the February to March plum window gives a much quieter, unhurried visit.

Bakurocho, Kamigyo Ward, Kyoto
