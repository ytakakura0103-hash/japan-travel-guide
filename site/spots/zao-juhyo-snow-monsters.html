Zao Snow Monsters (Juhyo)

Sendai

In the coldest months, moisture-laden winds sweeping up Mount Zao freeze onto the branches of Aomori todomatsu fir trees in successive layers, gradually building up thick, lumpy casings of rime ice that turn entire trees into bulbous, otherworldly shapes locals call juhyo, or 'snow monsters.' Seen from a distance across the slope, especially with fresh snow underfoot, the effect really does look like a frozen crowd of hooded figures marching up the mountainside.\n\nThe phenomenon is best known on the Yamagata side of the mountain, around the Zao Onsen ski resort, where a ropeway carries visitors up close to the formations, including night illumination sessions on select evenings during peak season that light the ice figures up in color. Because the main viewing infrastructure sits across the prefectural border, most Sendai-based visitors join it as an organized winter day tour or bus trip rather than arranging independent transit.\n\nRopeway tickets and any illumination event carry a separate cost from a general day-tour package. The phenomenon is weather-dependent and generally only fully formed from around mid-January through early March, with peak conditions varying year to year, so it's worth checking recent conditions before committing to a specific date. Weekday visits, and earlier ropeway departures, help avoid the biggest ski-season crowds.

Zao Onsen, Yamagata, Yamagata Prefecture
