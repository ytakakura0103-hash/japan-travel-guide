Fudoin Temple

Hiroshima

Almost all of central Hiroshima's older wooden architecture was destroyed in 1945, which makes Fudoin unusual: located roughly 4 kilometers from the hypocenter, its main hall (Kondo), believed to date from the Muromachi period, survived the blast with comparatively minor damage and is now a Nationally Designated Important Cultural Property — one of very few structures in the city that predates the war and remains standing in something close to original form.\n\nThe temple grounds are modest and largely uncrowded, tucked into a residential part of northeastern Hiroshima away from the main Peace Park circuit, with a quiet garden and a bell tower alongside the main hall.\n\nFor visitors who've already spent time at the Peace Memorial Museum and Dome, Fudoin offers a different, less-visited angle on the same history: a building that endured rather than one that was destroyed, standing in an ordinary neighborhood rather than a memorial park, which changes the emotional register of the visit considerably.

5-11 Ushitamachi, Higashi-ku, Hiroshima
