Toganji Temple

Nagoya

Toganji was founded in the mid-1500s by Oda Nobuyuki in memory of his father Oda Nobuhide, taking part of its name from Nobuhide's posthumous Buddhist title. The temple later relocated to its present site in the Moriyama area in the early 1700s, where it has remained ever since as a working Soto Zen temple focused on the bodhisattva Kannon.

Its most striking feature by far is a modern addition: a roughly 10-meter bronze Buddha statue, unveiled in 1987 and supported by ten sculpted elephants, painted in vivid green with gold-leaf accents that make it visible well before you reach the temple gate. The statue reflects a broader Indian connection at Toganji, whose grounds also include a small shrine to Saraswati and a Shiva lingam, unusual additions traced back to a former high priest who studied Buddhism in India. An annual festival honoring Saraswati, known in Japan as Benzaiten, is held on the temple grounds each spring.

Toganji sits well outside the usual downtown temple circuit, which keeps it free of tour groups even though its giant Buddha is genuinely one of the more photogenic religious statues in the greater Nagoya area.

Moriyama-ku, Nagoya, Aichi

About a 15-minute walk from Otobashi Station on the Meijo subway line, or reachable by local bus from Ozone Station.
