Sasaguri 88-Temple Pilgrimage Trail

Fukuoka

In the mid-nineteenth century, a Buddhist nun named Jinin, who had completed the arduous 88-temple pilgrimage across Shikoku, stopped in this valley on her way home and encouraged villagers to bring soil from each of those temples back here, allowing a scaled-down version of the same pilgrimage to be walked here in a single day or two instead of over a month; the 88 halls were completed by local villagers and benefactors in 1854. The full route today loops through the hills around Sasaguri, including a detour past Nanzoin's giant reclining Buddha, connecting dozens of small temples, roadside statues, and mountain shrines.

Most visitors will not walk the entire circuit, but sampling even a few kilometers gives a real sense of the pilgrimage atmosphere, with hand-carved stone markers, small votive statues wrapped in red cloth, and the occasional local pilgrim dressed in traditional white walking gear and a conical hat. Trail conditions vary from paved lanes to forest paths, so footwear and a rough map matter more here than at a single-site temple.

Because the full pilgrimage is a genuine undertaking rather than a tourist loop, foreign visitors are still a rare sight on the trail, and most of the people encountered along the way are local pilgrims or Sasaguri residents. Spring and autumn offer the most comfortable walking weather; the temple town itself is easy to combine with a Nanzoin visit on the same day.

Sasaguri-machi, Kasuya-gun, Fukuoka

About 25-30 minutes by JR Fukuhoku Yutaka Line from Hakata Station to Sasaguri Station, with trailheads spread around the town.
