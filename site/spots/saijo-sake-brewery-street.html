Saijo Sake Brewery Street

Hiroshima

Saijo, part of Higashihiroshima City, ranks alongside Nada in Hyogo and Fushimi in Kyoto as one of Japan's three traditionally recognized centers of sake production, a status built on the area's naturally soft, mineral-balanced groundwater, ideal for brewing. Seven breweries, some with buildings and brick chimneys dating to the 1800s, line a single street running from just outside JR Saijo Station, close enough together to visit several in one afternoon on foot.

Most breweries operate small retail shops selling bottles unavailable outside the region, and several offer tastings or brief tours explaining their specific brewing style, letting visitors compare noticeably different sake within a short walk rather than relying on descriptions alone.

The street was recognized in 2017 as one of Japan's top heritage sites of the 20th century, and unlike more famous sake regions, it remains easy to visit as a half-day trip directly from Hiroshima city by train, without needing a car or an organized tour.
