Fukuya Hacchobori

Hiroshima

Fukuya opened in 1929 as Hiroshima's first-ever department store, and its main Hacchobori building, completed in 1938, earned the nickname "White Palace" for a design considered elegant enough to stand out even among the city's grander prewar architecture. That building carries a weight of history beyond its retail role: it was damaged by the atomic bomb's blast on August 6, 1945, its interior burned but its concrete frame and exterior walls surviving, and it was subsequently restored, making it one of Hiroshima's recognized hibaku, or atomic-bombed, structures still in active use today.

That history sets Fukuya apart from a typical department store visit, giving it a genuine place in Hiroshima's postwar recovery story alongside its role as the city's leading retailer. Today the store spans twelve floors above ground and three below, covering roughly 28,600 square meters of sales space with a full range of fashion, cosmetics, and food offerings typical of a major flagship department store.

A second location, Fukuya Ekimae Store on the Hiroshima Station side of the city, underwent a major renewal and reopened in March 2026 with a blend of department store quality and shopping-mall-style spaces, giving visitors staying near the station an equally convenient Fukuya option without traveling into the Hacchobori city center.
