Sendai Tanabata Festival

Sendai

Held every year from August 6th to 8th, Sendai's Tanabata Festival is one of the 'three great festivals of Tohoku,' alongside the Nebuta Festival in Aomori and the Kanto Festival in Akita, and by most measures the biggest star-festival celebration in Japan. Shopping arcades and side streets across the city center are hung with thousands of fukinagashi, tall paper streamers built around bamboo poles, each one handmade by local shops, schools, and families in a friendly and long-running competition over craftsmanship.

The scale is what surprises first-time visitors: entire arcades vanish under a canopy of color, and looking straight up while walking through is part of the experience. A fireworks display the night before the main festival draws its own separate crowds along the riverbank. Food stalls selling classic festival snacks line the routes, and the whole city center takes on a genuinely festive, slightly chaotic energy for the three days.

There's no admission fee to walk the arcades. The streets are extremely crowded in the evenings, especially on the middle day; visiting in the first hour after the streamers go up in the morning gives much better photos and breathing room, before the after-work crowds arrive. Bring cash for food stalls, and expect hot, humid August weather.

Chuo-dori and Ichibancho arcades, Aoba-ku, Sendai, Miyagi

The main arcades are a 5 to 10 minute walk from Sendai Station's west exit.
