Mount Misen Daisho-in Trail

Hiroshima

Mount Misen, at 535 meters, has been considered sacred since long before Itsukushima Shrine was built, and it's possible to reach the summit either by ropeway or on foot via three marked trails. The Daisho-in course, starting at the temple of the same name, is generally rated the most scenic and slightly less steep than the alternatives, climbing through a nearly unbroken stone staircase shaded by the Misen Primeval Forest, a nationally protected natural monument.

The hike takes most people around ninety minutes to two hours one way, and unlike the ropeway crowd, hikers tend to have long stretches of the trail to themselves, with only birdsong and the occasional wild monkey for company (feeding them is prohibited).

At the summit, the view stretches across the Seto Inland Sea back toward Hiroshima city on clear days, and hikers can either walk back down or take the ropeway down instead, which is a reasonable compromise for tired legs after an honestly earned view.
