Yokohama Christ Church

Yokohama

This Anglican-Episcopal congregation dates back to the earliest years of Yokohama's foreign settlement in 1863, though the building you see today is a later reconstruction, the original was leveled by the Great Kanto Earthquake of 1923, and the current stone structure, a Norman-style building faced in Oya stone and designed by J. H. Morgan, was completed in 1931. Its interior was gutted by fire during the air raids of 1945, and the interior was rebuilt in 1947, but the stone shell standing today dates to that prewar 1931 reconstruction rather than a postwar one.

Unlike the nearby Western-style residences, this is a working parish rather than a museum, holding regular services, so visiting works best as a respectful glimpse from the grounds or a service rather than a self-guided tour through furnished rooms. The stone facade and modest bell tower are worth a look even from outside for anyone tracing the district's religious and architectural history.

Because it sits along the natural walking route between the Yamate heritage houses and Motomachi's shopping street, it is easy to fold in as a two-minute stop rather than a dedicated destination. Photography is generally fine from the exterior, but be mindful during active services and any private events on the grounds.

235 Yamate-cho, Naka-ku, Yokohama

Open in Google Maps

About 7 minutes on foot from Minatomirai Line Motomachi-Chukagai Station (exit 5).
