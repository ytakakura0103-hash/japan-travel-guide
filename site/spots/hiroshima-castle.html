Hiroshima Castle

Hiroshima

Hiroshima Castle was originally built in the 1590s by daimyo Mori Terumoto, whose choice of this river delta site as his base is the reason the city grew where it did. The castle's dark wooden exterior earned it the nickname Rijo, or "Carp Castle," and it served as a regional seat of power through the Edo period and into the Meiji era, when it also housed a military headquarters.

The original main keep was destroyed in the atomic bombing, close enough to the hypocenter that it collapsed almost immediately. The present five-story keep is a 1958 reinforced-concrete reconstruction built to the original exterior appearance. It served for decades as a museum on the castle's history and samurai-era Hiroshima with a rooftop observation level, but the keep closed to visitors on March 22, 2026 after being found not to meet current earthquake safety standards, and its future (repair or a full wooden reconstruction) has not yet been decided; exhibits have been moved to a facility within the castle's outer walls, with a new Sannomaru History Museum planned to open in spring 2027.

The castle grounds retain some original stone foundations and a section of moat with carp and turtles, along with a Shinto shrine and reconstructed turret gates, and remain accessible to visitors even with the keep closed. The site is quieter than Peace Memorial Park and makes for a reasonably short add-on visit, particularly in cherry blossom season when the moat-side trees draw local visitors as much as tourists.

21-1 Motomachi, Naka Ward, Hiroshima, Hiroshima Prefecture

Open in Google Maps

10-minute walk from Kamiyacho-nishi or Kamiyacho-higashi tram stop, or about 20 minutes on foot from Hiroshima Station; also reachable by the Hiroshima Sightseeing Loop Bus (Meipuru-pu).
