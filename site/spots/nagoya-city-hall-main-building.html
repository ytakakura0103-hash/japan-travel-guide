Nagoya City Hall Main Building

Nagoya

Completed in the early 1930s, City Hall's main building pairs a solid Western-style stone-and-concrete base with a distinctly Japanese tiled tower roof on top, an architectural approach from the period known as teikan-yoshiki, or Imperial Crown Style, meant to project modern authority while still nodding to traditional form. It still functions as a working government building, so interior access is limited, but the exterior alone is a notable stop for architecture fans walking between the castle and Sakae.

There's no charge to view or photograph the exterior, and it takes only a few minutes to appreciate from the street, though its neighbor, the similarly styled Aichi Prefectural Government Office next door, makes it worth combining into one short stop.

The building is illuminated at night during certain seasons, which is arguably the most striking time to see it, and since it's simply a building exterior rather than a ticketed attraction, there's no crowd management to think about at any time of day.

3-1-1 Sannomaru, Naka-ku, Nagoya, Aichi
