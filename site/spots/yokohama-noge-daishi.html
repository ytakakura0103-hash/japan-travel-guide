Nogeyama Fudoson

Yokohama

Tucked into the slopes near Nogeyama Park, this modest temple is dedicated to Fudo Myoo, the fierce, flame-wreathed guardian deity commonly petitioned in Japan for protection, discipline, and success in difficult undertakings, which locally translates into a steady stream of students visiting before exams and travelers stopping for safe-journey prayers. Its small scale and unassuming entrance mean it is easy to walk past without registering it as a temple at all if you are not specifically looking for it.

There is no admission fee and no elaborate visitor infrastructure, just a compact worship hall, incense burners, and the kind of quiet, functional religious space that exists to serve a neighborhood rather than draw sightseers. Steps and a short uphill approach add a small physical element to the visit that some worshippers treat as part of the ritual itself.

Because it sits so close to the far more visited Nogeyama Zoo, it makes an easy five-minute detour for visitors already in the area, offering a glimpse of everyday, functional Buddhist practice in contrast to the grander, more monument-like temples found elsewhere in the Yokohama and Kamakura area.

30 Miyazaki-cho, Nishi-ku, Yokohama

Open in Google Maps

About 12 minutes on foot from JR/Yokohama Municipal Subway Sakuragicho Station, near Nogeyama Park.
