Tetsujin 28-go Statue

Kobe

Tetsujin 28-go, created by Nagata-born manga artist Mitsuteru Yokoyama, is widely regarded as one of the earliest and most influential giant-robot characters in Japanese pop culture, a direct forerunner of the mecha genre that later produced Gundam and countless others. Nagata Ward, hit especially hard by the 1995 Great Hanshin earthquake, commissioned this full-scale steel statue as part of a broader community rebuilding effort, both honoring its native creator and giving the neighborhood a distinctive symbol to rally around during recovery.

Standing in a small dedicated park, the statue is genuinely imposing up close, built with a real robot's structural logic rather than as a simplified mascot figure, and it's lit at night for a different visual effect than the daytime view. Its location well outside Kobe's central sightseeing area means it draws a small, dedicated stream of manga and mecha fans rather than general tourists, alongside neighborhood residents who've come to see it as a genuine local landmark.

The surrounding shopping street leans into the connection with themed shop displays and merchandise, giving the visit a sense of grassroots community pride rather than a manufactured tourist attraction.

Nagata Ward, Kobe

Open in Google Maps

From Sannomiya, take the Kobe Municipal Subway (Seishin-Yamate Line) or JR/Hanshin lines to Shin-Nagata Station (about 15-20 minutes); the statue is a short walk from the station.
