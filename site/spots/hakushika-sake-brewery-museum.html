Hakushika Sake Brewery Museum

Kobe

Nishinomiya, adjoining Kobe's Nada sake district to the east, shares the same prized brewing water, known locally as "miyamizu," that made this coastline one of Japan's foremost sake-producing regions from the Edo period onward. Hakushika, one of the area's established producers, maintains a museum inside a preserved wooden brewery hall, displaying traditional brewing tools and equipment alongside exhibits on the discovery and significance of the local water source that put the region on the map centuries ago.

The museum layout walks through each stage of traditional sake production, similar in structure to the better-known Nada museums slightly to the west, but with a stronger focus on the specific history of Nishinomiya's water and its economic importance to the wider region's brewing industry. A tasting corner typically lets visitors sample a few varieties before browsing the attached shop.

Because it sits just outside Kobe proper in neighboring Nishinomiya, it's often skipped by visitors focused solely on Kobe's Nada breweries, despite being easily reachable by the same rail lines, making it a natural extension for anyone already planning a sake-focused day out along this stretch of coast.

From Sannomiya, take the Hanshin Main Line toward Nishinomiya (about 15-20 minutes); the brewery museum (Sake Museum / Hakushika Memorial Sake Brewing Museum) is about a 15-minute walk south from Hanshin Nishinomiya Station toward the coast.
