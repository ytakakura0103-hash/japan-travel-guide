Horikawa Canal Cherry Blossom Walk

Nagoya

The Horikawa Canal was dug in the early Edo period to move goods and building materials into the growing castle town, and while its industrial role has faded, several stretches of its banks were later planted with cherry trees that now form a dense, arching canopy for a couple of weeks each spring. Unlike the moat around the castle, this walk is almost entirely a local secret, with the water's stillness reflecting the blossoms in a way that photographs beautifully in the right light.

There's no cost to walk the canal path, and a scenic stretch can be covered in twenty to thirty minutes, though the full canal runs much longer if you want to explore further.

Because it doesn't appear on most tourist maps, even peak bloom rarely brings the crowds seen at Nagoya Castle or the city's larger parks; a weekday walk during the bloom period offers something close to a private cherry-blossom experience. Outside of the short spring window, the canal reverts to a fairly ordinary, quiet waterway, so timing really is everything for this particular spot.

Horikawa Canal, Naka-ku, Nagoya, Aichi
