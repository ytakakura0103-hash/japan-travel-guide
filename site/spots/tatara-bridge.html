Tatara Bridge

Hiroshima

Tatara Bridge connects Ikuchijima, on the Hiroshima Prefecture side of the Shimanami Kaido, to Omishima across the border in Ehime Prefecture, and it opened to traffic on May 1, 1999. Its central span measures 890 meters, and at the time of completion this made it the longest cable-stayed bridge span in the world, a title it held until being surpassed by China's Sutong Bridge in 2008; it remains one of the longest such spans anywhere.

The bridge's distinctive silhouette, with two tall white support towers and a wide fan of cables, has made it something of a visual signature for the Shimanami Kaido route as a whole, appearing in much of the promotional photography for the crossing. Like the other bridges along the route, it carries a dedicated cycling and walking lane separated from vehicle traffic, and crossing it on a bicycle, with open sea views on both sides and the cable towers rising directly overhead, is often cited by cyclists as one of the highlights of the full Shimanami Kaido ride.

A roadside park near the Ikuchijima end of the bridge offers a place to stop, rest, and photograph the structure from below without needing to cross it.

Setoda-cho, Onomichi, Hiroshima Prefecture (Ikuchijima side)
