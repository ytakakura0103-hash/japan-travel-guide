Komyozenji Temple

Fukuoka

Just a few minutes' walk from Dazaifu Tenmangu's main gate, but far enough off the direct route that most visitors never find it, Komyozenji is a small Zen temple built around two distinct gardens: a dry rock garden raked into wave-like patterns representing the character for "light," and a moss garden with a more naturalistic, layered feel. Sitting on the wooden veranda facing either garden for a few quiet minutes is really the whole point of visiting.

A small entrance fee applies, and visitors are asked to remove shoes before stepping onto the temple's wooden platforms, standard etiquette that's easy to forget after a busy morning at the main shrine. Unlike Dazaifu Tenmangu, there's rarely a line or crowd here even during the shrine's busiest periods.

Autumn brings striking maple color across the moss garden, making it a particularly good detour in November, though the gardens are worth seeing in any season for the contrast between raked stone and living moss. Fifteen to twenty minutes is enough for most visitors, longer if you simply want to sit and take it in.
