Katsuoji Temple

Osaka

Founded in 727 in the hills above what is now Minoh, Katsuoji built its long-standing reputation on "victory luck," a tradition tracing back to an emperor said to have prayed here for success centuries ago, and the temple has since become strongly associated with daruma dolls, the round, weighted good-luck figures bought with one eye blank, filled in once a goal is achieved. Over generations, visitors have left their completed daruma behind rather than taking them home, and the result is a temple where thousands of the red dolls are stacked and scattered across nearly every surface of the grounds, from stone steps to shrine rooftops, creating a visual spectacle that draws almost as many visitors for the sight itself as for religious purposes.

A full visit exploring the grounds, main hall and daruma-covered areas takes around an hour to ninety minutes, and the site sits on a wooded hillside that adds a scenic, slightly secluded quality compared to temples closer to central Osaka. Admission is charged for entry to the temple grounds.

Being situated in the hills near Minoh, the temple also benefits from autumn foliage season, when the surrounding forest colours add to the visit. Reaching it requires a bus connection from Senriyama Station, making it a dedicated outing rather than a casual stop, best combined with a visit to nearby Minoh Falls for a fuller day exploring the Minoh area's nature and temple sites.
