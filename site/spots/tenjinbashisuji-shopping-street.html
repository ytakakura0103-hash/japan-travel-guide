Local Pick

Tenjinbashisuji Shopping Street

Osaka

Running north to south through several districts of Kita Ward, this covered arcade is often cited as Japan's longest shopping street, its roof shielding a dense run of small, independently run shops from rain and summer heat alike. Unlike the more polished arcades near Namba, Tenjinbashisuji keeps a strongly local, workaday feel, with everyday grocers, tailors and hardware shops mixed in among the food stalls and secondhand clothing racks that draw visitors.

Walking the full length end to end takes over half an hour at a steady pace, but most visitors explore just a section of it, dipping into side streets and stopping for takoyaki, skewers or a bowl of noodles along the way. The arcade sits close to Osaka Tenmangu Shrine and the Osaka Museum of Housing and Living, making it easy to fold into a wider walk through the Tenjinbashi area.

Weekday mornings and early afternoons are noticeably quieter than weekends, when both shoppers and tourists fill the narrow lanes, and the stretch closest to Tenjinbashi Station tends to be busiest regardless of day. Most shops keep fairly standard daytime hours and close by early evening, so this suits an afternoon visit better than a late one. There's no cost to walk the street itself, and the appeal lies less in any single stop than in the cumulative, unhurried browsing of hundreds of small storefronts that have operated in the same spots for years.
