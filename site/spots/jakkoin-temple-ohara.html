Jakkoin Temple

Kyoto

Jakkoin is a small, secluded convent in the Ohara valley tied to one of the more melancholy stories in Japanese history: after the Taira clan's defeat at the naval battle of Dan-no-ura in 1185, ending the Genpei War, Kenreimon-in, a Taira noblewoman and mother of the young emperor who died in the battle, survived and retreated here to become a nun, spending her remaining years in mourning and prayer for her family and the emperor she had lost. That story of quiet withdrawal from a violent and consequential period of Japanese history gives the temple a subdued, reflective atmosphere distinct from Kyoto's more grandly scaled sites, reinforced by its small scale and the surrounding forest of Ohara, a rural valley that itself feels removed from the city. The current main hall is a reconstruction, having been rebuilt after a fire in 2000 destroyed the previous historic building, though it was rebuilt faithfully to the earlier design and now hosts a small museum of items salvaged from the fire. A visit typically takes thirty to forty-five minutes. Because Jakkoin sits a bit further into the valley than Sanzen-in, the area's better-known temple, it generally sees fewer visitors, even during the autumn foliage season when Ohara as a whole gets busy. Moss and maples give the grounds seasonal color similar to Sanzen-in, and pairing the two temples into a single Ohara visit makes efficient use of the bus trip required to reach the valley.

676 Oharakusaocho, Sakyo Ward, Kyoto

About 60-minute bus ride from Kyoto Station (bus 17 or 18) to Ohara, then a short walk to the temple
