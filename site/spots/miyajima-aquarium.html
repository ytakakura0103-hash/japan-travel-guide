Miyajima Aquarium

Hiroshima

Miyajima Aquarium, sometimes called Miyajima Public Aquarium, was substantially rebuilt and reopened in 2011 after decades operating in an older facility on roughly the same site, and it focuses heavily on species found in the surrounding Seto Inland Sea rather than exotic or deep-ocean animals from elsewhere. Tanks are organized loosely by habitat, moving from the shallow tidal flats around Miyajima itself through to open water species, giving the collection a fairly coherent regional identity.

Among its residents are finless porpoises, native to the Seto Inland Sea and rarely displayed at other Japanese aquariums, along with sea otters, seals, and sea lions that perform in a scheduled outdoor show through the day. A penguin enclosure and various smaller tanks of local fish and invertebrates round out the collection, and the facility is sized to be manageable in an hour or two, making it a reasonable add-on rather than a full-day destination.

Given that most of Miyajima's other attractions are shrines, temples, and hiking routes, the aquarium is a useful option for families traveling with younger children who may need a change of pace partway through a day on the island.

10-3 Miyajima-cho, Hatsukaichi, Hiroshima Prefecture

Open in Google Maps

About a 20-minute walk from Miyajima pier along the coast, or a short taxi ride; also reachable by local bus.
