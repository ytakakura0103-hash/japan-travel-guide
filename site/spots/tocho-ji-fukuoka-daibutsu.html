Tocho-ji Temple and the Fukuoka Giant Buddha

Fukuoka

Tocho-ji is one of the oldest temples in the city, said to have been founded in the early ninth century by the priest Kukai after his return from study in China, but its main draw for most visitors is far more recent. The seated wooden Buddha statue inside the modern hall stands roughly ten meters tall and is described by the temple as the largest of its kind in Japan, carved from Japanese cypress over several years and completed in 1992. Behind the statue, thousands of small Buddha images line the wall.

The more memorable experience is underneath the statue: visitors descend into a completely unlit corridor representing the path through hell toward paradise, feeling along a handrail in total darkness before emerging near a symbol of enlightenment. It is a simple but genuinely effective piece of religious theater, and it costs only a small entry fee.

Because the temple sits a few minutes' walk from Canal City and Gion Station, it is easy to fold into a Hakata old-town walk alongside Kushida Shrine and Joten-ji without needing a special trip. It rarely gets crowded even when Canal City is busy, since most shopping-mall visitors never wander this far into the temple district. Photography of the statue itself may be restricted in parts of the hall, so check posted signs before taking photos.

2-4 Gokushomachi, Hakata-ku, Fukuoka

About 5 minutes on foot from Gion Station (Kuko Line), or a short walk from Canal City Hakata.
