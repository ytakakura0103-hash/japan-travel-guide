Sunshine Aquarium

Tokyo

Sunshine Aquarium sits on the rooftop of the Sunshine City complex in Ikebukuro, an unusual setting that the aquarium leans into with several tanks designed around the idea of being seen from below or against open sky. Its signature exhibit is a donut-shaped tank suspended overhead, through which rays and small sharks circle continuously above visitors' heads, while a separate open-air enclosure lets penguins swim in a tank positioned so they appear to glide through the sky rather than water when viewed from certain angles. Sea lions, otters, and a range of smaller marine and freshwater displays round out the rest of the collection.

Because it's compact compared to Japan's larger aquariums, a visit typically takes around ninety minutes to two hours, making it manageable as part of a longer day in Ikebukuro rather than requiring the whole afternoon. Weekends and school holidays bring noticeably larger crowds, especially around midday feeding times for the penguins and rays, so a weekday visit or an early entry near opening tends to be calmer.

Admission is paid at the gate and is generally considered reasonable for a rooftop attraction of this size. Families with young children make up a large share of visitors, and the rooftop setting means outdoor sections are more pleasant on mild days than during peak summer heat or heavy rain.

3-1 Higashiikebukuro, Toshima City, Tokyo
