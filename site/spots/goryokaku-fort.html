Goryokaku Fort

Hokkaido

Goryokaku was built in the final years of the Edo period as a Western-style fortification meant to defend Hakodate's newly opened port, its five-pointed star shape designed specifically to eliminate blind spots for cannon fire, a European fortification style essentially unprecedented in Japan at the time. It later became the site of the final battle of the Boshin War in 1869, when former shogunate loyalists made their last stand here before surrendering to the new Meiji government.

On the ground, the star shape is difficult to appreciate; a 107-meter observation tower built beside the fort gives the clearest aerial view of the moat's distinctive five-pointed outline, and is generally considered close to essential for actually seeing what makes the site notable. The fort's interior grounds are planted with over a thousand cherry trees, making it one of Hakodate's best-known hanami spots when they bloom, typically late April to early May, noticeably later than mainland Japan.

Cherry blossom season brings by far the heaviest crowds, both to the grounds and to the tower's observation deck; visiting on a weekday morning during that period, or simply visiting outside blossom season entirely, gives a calmer experience. Winter dusts the star-shaped moat with snow for a starkly different look from the tower, which is worth considering for visitors who won't be in Hakodate during spring.

44 Goryokaku-cho, Hakodate, Hokkaido
