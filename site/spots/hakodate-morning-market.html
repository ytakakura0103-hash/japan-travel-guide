Hakodate Morning Market

Hokkaido

Hakodate's morning market has operated in some form since the years after the Second World War, growing from informal roadside stalls into a dense covered market area right next to the train station, now home to several hundred vendors selling fresh Hokkaido seafood, produce and prepared food. Its best-known novelty is a section where visitors can pay to fish for their own squid from a small tank, which the stall then cleans and serves as sashimi within minutes, though this is a fairly touristy, price-marked experience rather than a bargain.

Many stalls also offer build-your-own kaisendon, a rice bowl topped with a choice of seafood, letting visitors mix crab, salmon roe, uni and other Hokkaido specialties to taste and budget. Prices and quality vary noticeably between stalls, so a bit of comparison before committing to a bowl is worth the time.

The market runs from early morning, and the freshest selection and calmest atmosphere are both found in the first couple of hours after opening; by mid-morning, tour groups arriving off cruise ships or buses can make the narrow aisles considerably more crowded. Since it's largely covered, it's a reasonable stop regardless of weather, though a winter morning here means arriving to a genuinely cold, dark walk from the station before the market itself warms things up with food stalls.
