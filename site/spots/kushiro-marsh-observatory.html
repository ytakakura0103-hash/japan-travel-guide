Kushiro Marsh Observatory

Hokkaido

Kushiro Marsh is Japan's largest wetland and was the country's first site registered under the Ramsar Convention for wetland conservation, protecting a landscape of reed beds, peat bog, and slow waterways that stretches far beyond what's visible from any single viewpoint. The main observatory building sits on the marsh's edge with an exhibition floor explaining the ecosystem and elevated windows looking out over the reeds, useful context before heading onto any of the surrounding trails.

The marsh is one of the last strongholds of the red-crowned crane, a bird that came close to extinction in Japan in the early 20th century and has since recovered specifically around this region; sightings from the observatory or nearby trails aren't guaranteed but are realistic, especially with patience and binoculars. A network of boardwalk trails extends from the observatory into the marsh itself for those wanting a longer walk.

Because Kushiro sits on Hokkaido's far eastern side, well beyond the Sapporo-Furano-Hakodate loop most visitors follow, it requires either a flight or a long train ride, which keeps foot traffic at the observatory modest even relative to its ecological significance.

Winter draws crane-watchers specifically for courtship dances performed against snow, while summer's green reed beds and migratory birds offer a completely different, quieter version of the same marsh.
