Uonotana Market, Akashi

Kobe

Uonotana has operated as a fishmonger's street for several centuries, drawing on Akashi's long tradition as a fishing port at the narrowest point of the strait that separates Honshu from Awaji Island, an area especially known for its octopus. The covered arcade packs in fishmongers, produce stalls, and small eateries along a fairly compact stretch, making it easy to graze through on foot rather than commit to one restaurant.

The market's local specialty is akashiyaki, small dashi-dipped egg-batter dumplings with a piece of octopus inside, distinct from the better-known Osaka takoyaki in both texture and how it is served (dipped in a light broth rather than eaten with sauce), and sampling it at one of the market's stalls or nearby shops is close to essential for a visit. A slow walk-and-eat visit through the market takes roughly an hour.

Mornings are the liveliest and freshest time to visit, since it operates as a working fish market first and a tourist stop second, and many stalls wind down by mid-afternoon; arriving before lunchtime gives the best selection and atmosphere.

1-16 Honmachi 1-chome, Akashi, Hyogo
