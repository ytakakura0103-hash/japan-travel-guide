Path of Literature (Bungaku no Komichi)

Hiroshima

Onomichi has produced and hosted an unusual number of Japanese writers for a small port town, among them novelist Fumiko Hayashi and Shiga Naoya, who both drew on the town's hillside views in their work. This trail collects short passages from two dozen of them, each engraved into a stone marker and placed along the same slope that connects the Cat Alley area to Senkoji Park.

Most visitors don't stop to read every stone, and that's fine — the real appeal is how the trail forces you to slow your pace on a walk you'd otherwise rush through. Between markers, the path opens onto gaps between temple roofs and citrus trees where the Onomichi Channel and the shipyards of Mukaishima appear below.

Because the trail overlaps with routes to several temples, it's easy to combine into a longer loop rather than treating it as a separate errand. Benches near a few of the stones make good rest points if the stairs elsewhere in town have worn you out, and there's rarely a crowd, since most tourists head straight for the ropeway instead.

Higashitsuchidocho area, Onomichi, Hiroshima
