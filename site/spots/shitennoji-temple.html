Shitennoji Temple

Osaka

Traditionally dated to 593 and attributed to Prince Shotoku, an early and influential patron of Buddhism in Japan, Shitennoji is counted among the oldest officially administered temples in the country, and its central compound, though rebuilt after wartime destruction, keeps the original Asuka-period layout, with a five-story pagoda, main hall and gate arranged in a straight line, a design known as the Shitennoji garan style rarely seen at other historic temples.

Walking the central compound and its museum takes roughly an hour, longer if visiting the adjoining garden, Gokurakujodo, a landscaped pond garden built to represent Buddhist paradise, which charges separate admission. The temple's principal grounds are generally uncrowded outside specific event days, making a visit here noticeably calmer than at Osaka's more famous religious sites. A well-known flea market takes place on the temple grounds on the 21st and 22nd of each month, drawing crowds of browsers looking through antiques, kimono and secondhand goods, worth timing a visit around if that interests you, or avoiding if you prefer a quiet temple visit.

Admission to the central compound is modest. The temple is a short walk or train ride from Shitennoji-mae Yuhigaoka Station and sits within reach of Tennoji Park and Abeno Harukas, making it easy to combine with other sights in the Tennoji area. Its long history and distinctive layout make it a worthwhile stop for visitors interested in the earliest era of Buddhism in Japan.
