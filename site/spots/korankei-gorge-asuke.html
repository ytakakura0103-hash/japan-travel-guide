Korankei Gorge

Nagoya

Korankei sits along the Tomoe River, a tributary of the Yahagi River, in the Asuke area of Toyota City, a valley whose reputation for autumn color is tied directly to a specific historical decision. According to local tradition, many of the roughly three thousand maple trees that now fill the gorge, spanning eleven different varieties, were planted starting in 1634 by the eleventh abbot of Kojaku-ji, a Zen temple founded in 1427 that still stands nearby, who deliberately set out to beautify the valley and encouraged later visitors to add more trees of their own.\n\nThat centuries-long planting effort has produced one of the settings regularly cited among Japan's finest autumn foliage destinations, with a network of paths leading through a tunnel of maples toward Taigetsukyo, a red arched bridge that has become the valley's most photographed single spot. Nearby stands Goshiki-Momiji, a single large maple tree notable for displaying five different leaf colors simultaneously as the season progresses. During peak autumn weeks the gorge is illuminated after dark for evening viewing, extending visiting hours well past sunset.\n\nOutside the main autumn season the gorge is a quieter, simply pleasant river valley for walking, though its reputation and crowds are overwhelmingly tied to the few weeks of peak color each November.

Asuke-cho Iimori, Toyota, Aichi
