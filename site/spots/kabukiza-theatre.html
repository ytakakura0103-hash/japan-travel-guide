Kabukiza Theatre

Tokyo

Kabukiza is Tokyo's main dedicated kabuki theater, a large and ornate building in Ginza that has stood in one form or another on this site since the late 19th century, with the current structure a modern rebuild that preserves the traditional facade. Full kabuki programs typically run several hours and are staged in a formal, and to newcomers sometimes demanding, theatrical style with stylized acting, elaborate costumes, and live traditional music.

For visitors who want a taste of kabuki without committing to a full program, the theater sells single-act "hitomakumi" tickets for standing or unreserved upper-level seats, letting you watch just one scene of a longer program for a relatively low cost and a limited time commitment, often under an hour. These tickets are sold same-day on a first-come basis, so arriving with some time to spare before a scene begins is worthwhile, especially for popular programs. English-language audio guides explaining the plot and cultural context are generally available for rent, useful given how difficult the dialogue can be to follow without background.

Full performances are staged most days when the theater is in season, with a different program roughly each month, so what's showing varies considerably by visit date. There's a basement-level shop and food area worth a look even without seeing a performance. Weekday matinees tend to be a little calmer than weekend and evening shows, though single-act tickets in particular can still draw a line.

4-12-15 Ginza, Chuo City, Tokyo
