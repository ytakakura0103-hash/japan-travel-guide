Yamate 111 Bankan

Yokohama

Named simply for its old lot number in the former foreign settlement, this house dates from the mid-1920s and shares some of the Spanish-influenced styling seen at Berrick Hall nearby, with white stucco walls and a red tile roof set behind a small, well-kept rose garden. Its scale is more modest than the grandest Yamate mansions, giving it a more approachable, lived-in feel.

A tearoom inside serves scones and tea, a detail that has made it a modest destination in its own right for visitors doing a slower loop of the bluff rather than rushing between photo stops. Because it sits slightly off the busiest stretch of the Yamate walking route, it tends to be calmer than the marquee houses, even during the rose garden's blooming season in late spring and autumn.

It is an easy add-on for anyone already visiting Berrick Hall or the Italian Garden, close enough to reach on foot within a few minutes, and its tearoom offers one of the more pleasant, unhurried break spots on a bluff walking route that otherwise involves a fair amount of uphill walking between houses.

Yamate-cho, Naka-ku, Yokohama
