Komyozenji Temple

Fukuoka

Nicknamed the "moss temple," Komyozenji is a small Rinzai Zen temple founded in the late thirteenth century, tucked down a side street barely a few minutes' walk from the packed approach to Dazaifu Tenmangu, yet visited by only a fraction of the crowd next door. Its front dry landscape garden arranges fifteen stones to form the character for "light," while the rear garden uses moss to represent land and raked white sand to represent the surrounding sea, both designed by the influential twentieth-century garden designer Shigemori Mirei, making this one of only a few Shigemori gardens in Kyushu.

Visitors sit on the wooden veranda to view both gardens at a slow pace, an experience closer to a Kyoto temple visit than anything else in central Fukuoka. Because of the moss, the grounds are especially vivid after rain and through the humid early summer months, and the temple is also a well-regarded, comparatively quiet spot for autumn maple color.

Given its proximity to Dazaifu Tenmangu, the easiest way to build in a visit is to peel off the main shrine approach for fifteen minutes; the contrast between the crowded shopping street and this hushed garden is itself part of the experience. A small admission fee applies.

2-16-1 Saifu, Dazaifu, Fukuoka
