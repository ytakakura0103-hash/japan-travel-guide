Kanagawa Prefectural Museum of Cultural History

Yokohama

Originally built as the Yokohama branch of a Japanese specie bank in the early 1900s, this building's copper dome and stone facade are as much a reason to visit as the exhibits inside, standing out clearly among the more modern buildings nearby in the Kannai and Nihon-odori area. Inside, the collection ranges widely, from archaeological finds and folk crafts to displays on Kanagawa Prefecture's modern development, which makes it a broader survey than the more narrowly focused Yokohama Archives of History across the street.\n\nThe scale of the exhibits means a thorough visit can take a couple of hours, though visitors short on time can focus on the ground floor and the building's architecture alone and still come away with a sense of the place. It tends to draw fewer foreign tourists than Yokohama's waterfront attractions, so galleries are usually calm even on weekends.\n\nIts location puts it within easy walking distance of the Three Towers and the Archives of History, making it a natural anchor for a morning spent on Meiji and Taisho-era architecture in central Yokohama, and the building is particularly striking when lit at night, though the museum itself keeps standard daytime hours.

Nihon-odori area, Naka-ku, Yokohama
