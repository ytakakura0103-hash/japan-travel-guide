Tenryuji Temple

Kyoto

Tenryuji was founded in the 14th century by the shogun Ashikaga Takauji, in part to pray for the soul of Emperor Go-Daigo after a period of political conflict between them, and its Sogenchi Garden, designed by the temple's first head priest, is regarded as one of the earliest Japanese gardens to be protected as a scenic and historic site under modern preservation law. The pond-centered garden uses the mountains of Arashiyama beyond the temple grounds as a "borrowed scenery" backdrop, a technique that makes the garden appear to extend seamlessly into the surrounding landscape.

The temple sits directly beside the entrance to the bamboo grove, and a walk through its main hall and garden, along with a rear exit that leads straight into the grove itself, makes it a natural anchor for a longer Arashiyama walking route. A visit to the garden and grounds takes around forty-five minutes to an hour; entering the main hall's interior involves an additional modest fee on top of the garden admission.

Tenryuji is popular and can be busy at the same times as the rest of central Arashiyama, particularly in cherry blossom and autumn foliage seasons; visiting at opening, before the bamboo grove crowds build up, tends to keep both stops calmer. The garden's borrowed-scenery view is especially striking in autumn when the surrounding mountainside turns color.

68 Sagatenryuji Susukinobabacho, Ukyo Ward, Kyoto
