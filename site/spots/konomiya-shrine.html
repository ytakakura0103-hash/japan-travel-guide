Konomiya Shrine

Nagoya

Konomiya Shrine's history stretches back well over a thousand years, but it's best known today for the Hadaka Matsuri, or naked festival, held each winter, in which thousands of men wearing only loincloths pack the shrine grounds in a chaotic, physical ritual centered on touching a chosen individual believed to absorb the community's misfortune for the year. It's one of the more startling and memorable festival spectacles in the region, drawing a mix of serious participants and curious spectators.

Outside festival season, the shrine itself is a quieter, fairly typical example of an old regional shrine, free to visit and worth twenty or thirty minutes if you're passing through the Inazawa area for another reason.

On the festival date itself, the grounds and surrounding streets get extremely crowded, and spectators should expect a packed, high-energy atmosphere rather than a contemplative shrine visit; arriving well ahead of the ritual's peak secures a viewing spot. Most other days of the year, it barely registers on typical tourist itineraries, which is part of its appeal for travelers looking for something off the standard route.
