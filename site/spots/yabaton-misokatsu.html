Yabaton

Nagoya

Misokatsu, a breaded and deep-fried pork cutlet served with a thick sauce made from hatcho miso, is one of the dishes most associated with Nagoya's distinct regional food culture, and Yabaton is one of the longest-running and best-known specialists in it, tracing its roots back to a stall near Osu decades ago. The sauce itself, deeply savory with a mild sweetness, is the real signature, and most menus let you choose between different cutlet cuts and thicknesses.

A set meal runs a moderate price by restaurant standards, and eating in typically takes around thirty to forty-five minutes; several branches operate around the city, with the original Osu-area location carrying the most history.

Lunch and dinner peak hours can mean a short wait at the busier branches, particularly the Osu location given its proximity to the shopping district; visiting slightly outside standard meal times, or choosing a branch away from the main tourist flow, generally avoids any real queue.
