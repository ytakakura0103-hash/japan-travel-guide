Nanatsudera Temple

Nagoya

Nanatsudera was originally built in 735, long before Nagoya existed as a city, in what is now Kiyosu. When Tokugawa Ieyasu ordered the construction of Nagoya Castle in the early 1600s, he also ordered much of the population and many institutions of Kiyosu, including several temples and shrines, physically moved to the new castle town in an event known locally as the Kiyosu-goshi. Nanatsudera was one of the temples relocated in that process, making it one of the oldest continuously operating religious institutions in the city even though its current site is comparatively young.

Under the Owari branch of the Tokugawa family, the temple served as an important place of worship and hosted lively festivals throughout the Edo period, drawing crowds from across the growing castle town. Today it sits within walking distance of the far busier Osu Kannon temple and shopping arcade, but it keeps a much lower profile, visited mostly by neighborhood residents rather than tour groups.

It makes a natural, low-effort addition to a visit to the Osu district, offering a quieter historical counterpoint to the shopping and street food a few minutes away.

4 Chome Nishiki, Naka-ku, Nagoya, Aichi
