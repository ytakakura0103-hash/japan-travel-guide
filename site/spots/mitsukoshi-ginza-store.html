Ginza Mitsukoshi

Tokyo

Ginza Mitsukoshi opened in 1930 and occupies one full corner of the Ginza 4-chome crossing, the intersection most people picture when they think of Ginza, alongside the Wako building's clock tower diagonally across the street. Like the Nihombashi flagship, it has its own smaller bronze lion statues at street level, a Mitsukoshi trademark repeated across its stores, though this pair sees far less fanfare than the Nihombashi originals.

A major expansion completed in 2010 roughly doubled the store's floor space, adding an annex connected to the original building and modernizing much of the interior, so while the exterior corner presence still reads as classic Ginza, most of the shopping floors inside are contemporary rather than historic. The rooftop garden is a quieter, less-visited spot worth knowing about, offering a break from the crowds at street level with views over the surrounding district.

As with most of Ginza's department stores, the basement food hall is a strong reason to visit even without a shopping list, and the store's central position makes it a natural stop while exploring the rest of Ginza's boutiques and architecture on foot.

4-6-16 Ginza, Chuo City, Tokyo

Open in Google Maps

About 1 minute on foot from Ginza Station (Tokyo Metro Ginza, Marunouchi, and Hibiya Lines), directly at the Ginza 4-chome crossing exit.
