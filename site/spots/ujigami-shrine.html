Ujigami Shrine

Kyoto

Ujigami Shrine holds a quiet but significant distinction: its main hall is generally regarded as the oldest surviving shrine building in Japan, built in the Heian period using construction techniques that predate the more familiar shrine architecture seen elsewhere in the country. Despite carrying the same UNESCO World Heritage designation as Byodo-in, the far more famous temple just a short walk away and pictured on the ten-yen coin, Ujigami receives only a fraction of the visitors, and its modest scale, a compact worship hall and a smaller inner shrine set among trees, reflects a very different kind of significance: historical and architectural rather than visually spectacular. A visit typically takes fifteen to thirty minutes, making it easy to fit in alongside a longer stop at Byodo-in without disrupting the flow of a Uji day trip. There's no crowd management to think about here, since even weekends rarely bring more than a light trickle of visitors, a rare situation in a city where major sights routinely require some timing strategy. Admission is free or covers only a token fee, and the setting, beside the Uji River and near wooded hills, gives it a calmer, more contemplative feel than shrines in the city center. Any season works reasonably well for a visit, since the shrine's appeal rests more on its age and rarity than on seasonal flowers or foliage, though the walk along the river to reach it is at its best in spring and autumn.

59 Uji Yamada, Uji, Kyoto

15-minute walk from JR Uji Station (JR Nara Line), or 10-minute walk from Keihan Uji Station (Keihan Uji Line)
