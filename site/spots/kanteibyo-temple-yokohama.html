Kanteibyo (Kuan Ti Miao) Temple

Yokohama

Founded by Chinese immigrants not long after Yokohama's port opened, Kanteibyo honors Guan Yu, a general from the era of the Three Kingdoms who was later deified as a guardian of loyalty and, in a twist that suits Chinatown's merchants, financial prosperity. The current building is the temple's fourth incarnation, rebuilt after the 1923 earthquake, wartime bombing, and a later fire, which makes its current condition feel less like a relic and more like a living, actively maintained place of worship.

The facade alone is worth stopping for: bright red and gold woodwork, dragon-carved marble panels, and layered roof ornamentation that reward a slow look rather than a quick photo and walk-on. Incense burns continuously inside, and visitors are welcome to observe or offer a prayer respectfully, following the lead of regular worshippers.

Because it sits directly on Chinatown's main circuit, it can get busy with tour groups around midday, so an early morning or early evening visit gives a calmer look at the interior. It pairs naturally with a stop at the nearby Mazu temple, a smaller and quieter counterpart dedicated to a different deity from the same religious tradition.
