Sengakuji Temple

Tokyo

Sengakuji Temple is the burial site of Asano Naganori and his 47 loyal retainers, the historical figures behind the story of the 47 Ronin — a real early-18th-century episode of vendetta and ritual suicide that has since been retold so many times in kabuki, film, and literature that it ranks among the most recognizable historical dramas in Japan. The retainers' graves, arranged in rows behind the main temple buildings, are still visited daily by people who leave incense sticks in their memory, and the air around the graveyard carries a near-constant trace of smoke as a result.

A small museum on the temple grounds displays artifacts related to the story, including some items said to have belonged to the retainers themselves, and provides useful background for visitors unfamiliar with the historical events before walking through the graves. A full visit, including the museum and graveyard, takes about forty-five minutes to an hour; a quicker look at just the graves takes about fifteen minutes.

Admission to the main temple grounds is free, while the museum charges a modest separate fee. The temple is rarely crowded outside of December 14, when a festival marks the anniversary of the retainers' famous raid and draws a considerably larger crowd than a typical day. Its location a one-minute walk from Sengakuji Station makes it an easy, low-effort stop even for visitors without deep prior knowledge of the story.
