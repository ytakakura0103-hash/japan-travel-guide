Meiji Jingu Shrine

Tokyo

Meiji Jingu is a large forested Shinto shrine dedicated to Emperor Meiji and Empress Shoken, built in the 1920s on land planted with roughly 100,000 trees donated from across Japan. The approach from Harajuku Station winds through this forest for about ten minutes before reaching the shrine buildings, and the effect is striking: within a few minutes of one of Tokyo's busiest train stations, traffic noise gives way to birdsong and gravel underfoot. Near the entrance, a wall of sake and wine barrels donated to the shrine is a popular photo stop.

The shrine's Inner Garden (Gyoen), a separate landscaped area with a pond and iris garden, charges a small entry fee and is worth the detour for anyone with extra time, especially when the irises bloom in June. The main shrine grounds themselves are free to enter. A basic visit to the main hall takes under an hour, but allowing more time for the forest paths and garden makes for a fuller outing.

Early morning is the best time to visit if you want the forest to yourself, since the grounds get busy from mid-morning onward, particularly on weekends. The shrine is also one of the most popular hatsumode (New Year's first shrine visit) destinations in the country, drawing enormous crowds in the first days of January, and traditional weddings are often held here, so keep an eye out for a bridal procession crossing the main courtyard.
