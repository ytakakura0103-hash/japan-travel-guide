Showa-shinzan

Hokkaido

Showa-shinzan formed between 1943 and 1945, when a series of earthquakes and eruptions pushed a new mountain up out of what had been farmland near Mt. Usu. A local postmaster named Masao Mimatsu tracked the mountain's growth obsessively during and after wartime restrictions on scientific observation, producing what's now known as the Mimatsu Diagram, and the mountain remains privately owned by his family to this day, a genuinely unusual arrangement for a natural landmark.

Visitors can't climb the mountain itself, since it's still an active, reddish, steaming dome, but it's viewable up close from the base area, alongside a small memorial hall covering Mimatsu's story and a bear ranch with Ussuri brown bears that's a separate, fee-based attraction next door.

It sits right beside the lower station of the Usuzan Ropeway, so most visitors combine both in one stop; that pairing means Showa-shinzan itself rarely operates as a solo destination, and it's typically busiest whenever ropeway crowds peak, generally midday in summer.

The mountain's reddish, barren slopes contrast sharply with the greenery around it in summer, while winter strips back the surrounding vegetation and makes the volcanic dome's color stand out even more starkly against snow.

184 Showa-shinzan, Sobetsu, Usu District, Hokkaido 052-0102

About 2.5-3 hours from Sapporo to Toya Station, then a Donan Bus to Toyako Onsen and onward (about 20 minutes total from the station) to the Showa-shinzan/Usuzan Ropeway stop; no winter bus service, so a car or taxi is needed in the cold months.
