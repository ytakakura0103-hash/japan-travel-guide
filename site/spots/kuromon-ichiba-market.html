Kuromon Ichiba Market

Osaka

Nicknamed "Osaka's Kitchen," this narrow covered arcade near Nipponbashi has supplied local restaurants and households with fresh seafood, produce and meat for well over a century, though in recent years it has become just as well known for its ready-to-eat stalls as for its wholesale trade. Walking its length takes only a few minutes, but most visitors spend closer to an hour grazing from stall to stall, sampling grilled scallops, skewered wagyu, fresh oysters and seasonal fruit cut and served on the spot.

Prices vary a good deal by stall and by what's in season, so it's worth glancing at a few options before buying rather than stopping at the first one you see. The market is busiest around midday and into the early afternoon, when both tour groups and local shoppers overlap in the narrow aisles; arriving soon after opening in the morning gives a noticeably calmer experience and a better chance to see vendors preparing the day's stock.

Not every stall welcomes eating while walking, so look for seating areas or designated stand-and-eat counters if you buy something hot. Cash remains useful even though more stalls now accept cards. The market connects easily on foot to Namba and Dotonbori, making it a natural stop on a walking route through central Osaka, and a good introduction to Osaka's reputation as a food-first city before heading to sit-down restaurants elsewhere.

Nipponbashi area, Chuo Ward, Osaka
