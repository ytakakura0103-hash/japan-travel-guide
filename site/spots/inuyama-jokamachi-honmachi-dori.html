Inuyama Castle Town, Honmachi-dori

Nagoya

Honmachi-dori runs in a straight line from the base of Inuyama Castle down through the old jokamachi, or castle town, that grew up to serve it. Many of the wooden storefronts lining the street date from the Edo period through the early twentieth century, their narrow frontages and deep interiors a classic layout for merchant houses of the era, and a fair number are still occupied by shops selling exactly the kinds of goods, sake, sweets, pottery, that their predecessors might have sold generations ago.

What makes the street especially good for a slow visit is its street-food culture: stalls and small shopfronts sell gohei-mochi, a grilled rice cake coated in a sweet-savory miso or soy glaze that is something of a local specialty, along with skewered snacks and cups of sake from breweries in the surrounding region, meant to be eaten while strolling rather than sitting down. The street angles gently uphill toward the castle, so the walk builds naturally toward the day's main destination.

Because it connects directly to the castle, Urakuen Garden and the Dondenkan museum, Honmachi-dori works as much as connective tissue as it does a destination of its own, but it rewards visitors who slow down rather than walk straight through.
