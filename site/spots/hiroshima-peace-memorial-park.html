Hiroshima Peace Memorial Park

Hiroshima

The park occupies Nakajima, a former merchant district that was the busiest part of prewar Hiroshima and stood almost at the hypocenter of the 1945 bombing. Architect Kenzo Tange won a 1949 design competition to turn the area into a park dedicated to peace, and his layout still governs the site: the Cenotaph, the Flame of Peace, and the Atomic Bomb Dome across the river are arranged on one axis, so standing at the Cenotaph frames the Dome through its saddle-shaped arch.

Within the roughly 12-hectare grounds are several distinct memorials in addition to the Cenotaph, including the Children's Peace Monument, the Peace Bell, the Memorial Mound containing unidentified ashes, and the Peace Memorial Museum at the park's southern end. The Flame of Peace has burned continuously since 1964 and is intended to stay lit until nuclear weapons are eliminated worldwide.

The park is also simply a public green space used by residents for walking, jogging, and gathering, particularly along the riverbanks planted with cherry trees that draw crowds each spring. Each year on August 6, the anniversary of the bombing, the park hosts Hiroshima's official Peace Memorial Ceremony, and in the evening thousands of paper lanterns are floated on the Motoyasu River in remembrance.

Nakajima-cho, Naka Ward, Hiroshima, Hiroshima Prefecture
