Otaru Music Box Museum

Sapporo

Housed in a former hardware store building dating to 1912, the main hall of the Otaru Music Box Museum stocks a genuinely large inventory of music boxes, from mass-produced souvenir pieces to intricate handmade ones, alongside a small area explaining how music boxes are built. It bills itself as one of Japan's largest music box retailers, and browsing the packed shelves is as much the point as any single item for sale.\n\nOutside the main entrance stands a large steam clock, built in collaboration with the same workshop behind Vancouver's well-known steam clock, which sounds a whistle and releases steam on the hour, drawing a small crowd of photographers each time. The museum sits at the end of Sakaimachi Street, the old merchant road connecting it back to the canal, so it fits naturally into a walking loop of Otaru's historic district.\n\nThe main hall gets busy with tour groups around midday, particularly when cruise ships are in port at Otaru; visiting in the first hour after opening or later in the afternoon tends to be calmer. There's no strong seasonal angle since it's fully indoors, which makes it a reasonable stop on a rainy or snowy Otaru day, though the steam clock display outside is obviously more atmospheric against snow in winter.

4-1 Sumiyoshicho, Otaru, Hokkaido
