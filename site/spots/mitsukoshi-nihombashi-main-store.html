Nihombashi Mitsukoshi Main Store

Tokyo

Mitsukoshi traces its roots to a kimono shop founded in 1673, and its Nihombashi flagship is generally credited as Japan's first Western-style department store, a title it has held since the early 20th century. The current main building was completed in 1935, a Western classical-style structure so large for its time that it was surpassed in scale only by the National Diet Building and the Marunouchi Building; it was designated a national Important Cultural Property in 2016, one of the few working department stores anywhere with that status.

The building's most photographed feature is the pair of bronze lion statues flanking the main entrance, popularly known as the store's guardians and a long-standing meeting spot for Tokyo locals ("let's meet at the lions" is a genuinely common phrase). Inside, the central atrium is dominated by the Tenjo, a large wooden sculpture of a celestial maiden (Magokoro, meaning "sincerity") that rises through multiple floors, along with a working pipe organ used for occasional recitals.

Beyond the architecture, the store functions as a full department store with the basement food hall (depachika) worth a stop on its own, alongside high-end fashion, traditional crafts, and art galleries on the upper floors. Even visitors with no interest in shopping can walk through the ground floor and main atrium for free to see the building itself, which is really the main draw for most first-time visitors.

1-4-1 Nihombashi Muromachi, Chuo City, Tokyo
