Motomachi Historic District Churches

Hokkaido

When Hakodate opened as one of Japan's first treaty ports in the 1850s, foreign traders and missionaries settled on the sloped Motomachi hillside above the harbor, and the district's churches are the clearest surviving trace of that era. The Orthodox Church traces back to Japan's first Russian Orthodox congregation, founded by the Russian consulate around 1858-1860 as a chapel attached to the consulate; the priest later canonized as St. Nicholas of Japan arrived in 1861 and built it into a center of Orthodox missionary work, though the current Russian-Byzantine building dates to a 1916 reconstruction after fire damage. The nearby Roman Catholic and Anglican churches add further variety within an easy walking loop.

The streets connecting them are steep and cobbled in places, lined with other Meiji- and Taisho-era buildings including the pale blue-and-yellow Old Public Hall, whose upper floor operates as a small city history museum. None of the churches typically charge for viewing the exteriors, and some allow limited interior access outside of active services.

Because the district spreads across a genuine hillside neighborhood rather than a single compact block, foot traffic disperses naturally, and even in peak summer it doesn't feel as congested as the harborside morning market area below.

Evening lighting on the church exteriors and the slope's night view over the harbor are a distinct experience from the daytime architecture walk, and snow adds real atmosphere to the same streets in winter.
