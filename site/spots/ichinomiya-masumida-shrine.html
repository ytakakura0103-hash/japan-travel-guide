Masumida Shrine

Nagoya

Masumida Shrine was founded well over 1,400 years ago and held the rank of ichinomiya, or 'first shrine,' of Owari Province, the highest-ranked shrine in the old provincial hierarchy, dedicated to the deity Amenoho-no-Akari-no-Mikoto, described in tradition as a grandson of the sun goddess Amaterasu and the elder brother of Ninigi-no-Mikoto, the deity from whom the imperial line is said to descend. The town that grew up around the shrine took its name directly from that rank, becoming Ichinomiya, and it developed through the Edo period into an important transport hub along the old Gifu highway.

The city's textile industry has roots reaching back to the Heian period, when the area became known for silk and striped cotton weaving, before expanding dramatically from the Meiji era into a major center of woolen textile production that continues, in a smaller form, today. Local tradition draws a direct line between the shrine and that industry, since the mother of the shrine's enshrined deity is associated with the god of textiles, giving the city's manufacturing history a genuinely local mythological explanation rather than a purely economic one.

The shrine grounds themselves are calm and largely local in character, visited far more by Ichinomiya residents than outside tourists, which makes them a good stop for visitors curious about how a specific Shinto shrine can shape an entire city's economic identity.
