Sakura Masamune Kinenkan

Kobe

Sakura Masamune is credited by some historians as the brewery that first produced junmai-style sake in a form close to what's recognized today, and its small memorial hall traces that history alongside the broader story of Nada's rise as a brewing center. The museum is more modest in scale than some of its larger neighbors, with a compact exhibit space and a tasting counter where visitors can sample several grades of sake, often for a small fee.

Because it's smaller and less visited than the district's headline breweries, staff have more time to chat about the sake on offer, and it's a comfortable stop for visitors who find the bigger museums a bit impersonal. A shop sells bottles alongside sake-related snacks and sweets.

The brewery sits within easy walking distance of several other Nada sake producers, so it works well as one stop on a self-guided brewery walk rather than a single destination trip. Hours and tasting availability can vary, so it's worth checking ahead, particularly outside the main brewing season in cooler months when some facilities scale back visitor operations.

Uozaki-nishimachi, Higashinada Ward, Kobe

Open in Google Maps

From Sannomiya, take the Hanshin Main Line (or the Rokko Liner) to Uozaki Station; Sakura Masamune Kinenkan is about a 5-minute walk from the station.
