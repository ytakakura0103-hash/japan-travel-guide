Mount Misen Ropeway

Hiroshima

Mount Misen is the highest point on Miyajima and has been treated as sacred ground for centuries, associated with the Buddhist monk Kobo Daishi, who is said to have practiced ascetic training on the mountain in the early 9th century. A small hall near the summit contains a flame traditionally said to have burned continuously since his time, and it is sometimes described as one of the sources used to light the Flame of Peace in Hiroshima's Peace Memorial Park.

The ropeway climbs the mountain in two connected stages from a lower station reached by bus or a walk from the ferry pier, cutting what would otherwise be a hike of roughly two hours each way down to about a fifteen-minute ride. From the upper ropeway station, it is still a further walk of around 20 to 30 minutes over uneven trail to reach the actual summit and its observation deck, which looks out over the forested island, the Great Torii far below, and the wider Seto Inland Sea toward Honshu.

The forest along the ropeway route and summit trail is part of a protected natural area and is home to wild macaques and deer, and the whole mountain is a well-known spot for autumn foliage, typically peaking in mid-to-late November.

Momijidani Park, Miyajima-cho, Hatsukaichi, Hiroshima Prefecture

About 15 minutes on foot or a short bus ride from Miyajima pier to the Momijidani ropeway station, then a two-stage cable car ride of roughly 15 minutes to the upper station.
