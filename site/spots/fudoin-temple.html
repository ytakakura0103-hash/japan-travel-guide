Fudoin Temple

Hiroshima

Fudoin's main hall, the Kondo, is thought to have originally been built in Yamaguchi around 1540 and was relocated to this site roughly fifty years later. Constructed in the Karayo, or Chinese-influenced Zen style, it is considered the largest surviving example of that architectural style in Japan and was designated a National Treasure in 1952, making it the only National Treasure building within Hiroshima city.\n\nWhat sets Fudoin apart in the context of Hiroshima's history is that it stood close enough to the hypocenter to have been expected to burn, yet the hall survived the 1945 bombing with only moderate damage, unlike almost every other prewar wooden structure in the city center. It is included on Hiroshima's official list of buildings that survived the atomic bombing, and this survival is part of what draws visitors interested in the city's history beyond Peace Memorial Park.\n\nThe temple grounds also include a large two-story gate built in 1594, and because Fudoin sits away from the main tourist circuit in the Ushita area northeast of the city center, it tends to be genuinely quiet, with few visitors compared to the sites closer to the river delta.

5-11-8 Ushita-shinmachi, Higashi Ward, Hiroshima, Hiroshima Prefecture

Open in Google Maps

About 10 minutes by bus from Hiroshima Station's north exit, then a short walk; alternatively about 20 minutes on foot from Hiroshima Station.
