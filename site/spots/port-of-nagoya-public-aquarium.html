Port of Nagoya Public Aquarium

Nagoya

This aquarium is best known for its orcas -- until 2025 home to Japan's only active killer whale breeding program -- though as of 2025 only a single female, Lin, remains after the death of the country's last male orca, Earth, in August 2025 (the female Stella was earlier returned to another facility in 2024). Dolphin shows, a re-created Antarctic environment with penguins, and tanks tracing the migratory life cycle of species that swim from the open ocean up through Japan's waters round out the collection. It is genuinely large, split across two main buildings connected by an overhead walkway, and the sheer scale means a visit takes a good half day if you see everything.

Admission is priced on the higher side for the region, reflecting the scale of the facility, and show times for the orca and dolphin performances are worth checking on arrival since they run on a fixed schedule rather than continuously. The retired icebreaker Fuji, once used on Antarctic research missions, is moored nearby and can be boarded separately.

Weekends and school holidays bring heavy crowds, especially around show times; arriving right at opening lets you see the main tanks before tour groups fill the walkways. Combining the visit with the nearby Legoland Japan makes for a full family day out in the port area.

1-3 Minato-cho, Minato-ku, Nagoya, Aichi
