Kamogawa River

Kyoto

The Kamogawa runs shallow and wide through the center of Kyoto, and unlike most of the city's famous sights, it isn't really a destination with an entrance or a fee so much as a piece of everyday life that visitors are welcome to join. In the evenings, especially from spring through early autumn, locals sit spaced out along the stone banks near Shijo and Sanjo at oddly regular intervals, a pattern often noted by visitors and explained, half-jokingly, as an unspoken rule about not sitting too close to strangers or existing couples. Stepping stones shaped like turtles and birds cross the river at a few points further north, near the Demachiyanagi delta, offering a popular photo spot and shortcut for pedestrians and cyclists. Restaurants along the river's west bank, particularly in the Pontocho and Kiyamachi areas, build seasonal outdoor decks called yuka over the water from May to September, giving diners a cooler spot to eat during Kyoto's hot, humid summers. A walk or bike ride along the paved paths on either bank costs nothing and can be extended as far as time allows, from a short stroll near downtown to a longer route north toward the university district. Cherry blossoms line stretches of the riverbank in spring, and the water level and mood shift noticeably after summer rains. Early morning and just before sunset tend to be the most pleasant, least crowded times to walk along it.
