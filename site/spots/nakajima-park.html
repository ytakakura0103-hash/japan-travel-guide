Local Pick

Nakajima Park

Hokkaido

Where Odori Park is Sapporo's civic stage, Nakajima Park functions more like the city's actual backyard: a genuinely large green space with a pond, a Japanese garden, and a Meiji-era Western-style guesthouse called Hoheikan that once hosted imperial visitors and can still be toured. It sits directly above its own subway station, but because it isn't positioned as a headline sightseeing stop, it tends to draw a mostly local crowd of joggers, families and picnickers rather than tour groups.

Spring brings cherry blossoms around the pond that rival Maruyama's for beauty with a fraction of the crowd, and autumn foliage in October is similarly understated but rewarding. A small concert hall and the Sapporo Art Park's sister venue occasionally host events on the grounds, adding a cultural layer beyond the greenery itself.

Because it rarely appears on rushed first-time itineraries, almost any time of day here is relatively calm compared with downtown's marquee sights; weekend mornings before local joggers give way to a lunchtime crowd are especially pleasant. Winter turns the pond area into a quiet snowscape worth a slow walk, particularly for visitors who want a break from the more crowded festival sites downtown during Snow Festival season.

Nakajima Koen, Chuo-ku, Sapporo, Hokkaido
