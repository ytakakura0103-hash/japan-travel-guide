Kanagawa-juku Historic Walking Trail

Yokohama

Before Yokohama's waterfront settlement was built up, Kanagawa-juku was already an established post station on the Tokaido, the great highway linking Edo and Kyoto, and it briefly became the initial base for several foreign consulates in the years right after the 1859 port opening, before diplomats and merchants shifted to the newer settlement across the bay. A scattering of temples along this stretch, including some that briefly served as consulate buildings, still stand as physical traces of that transitional period.

The city has marked a walking route through the area with signage explaining the old post-town layout, former inn sites, and the temples' consular history, though the trail is understated compared to a formal museum experience, more a set of historical markers woven into an ordinary residential and commercial neighborhood than a curated attraction. Patience and a willingness to read plaques carefully pay off here.

Few visitors make the connection that Yokohama's diplomatic history technically started in this quieter district before moving to the port, which keeps the trail almost entirely free of tourist foot traffic. It suits history-minded travelers more than casual sightseers, and works well as a slower, half-day alternative to the busier central Yokohama sites.

Kanagawa-cho / Higashi-Kanagawa area, Kanagawa-ku, Yokohama

Open in Google Maps

About 10-15 minutes on foot from JR Kanagawa Station or Keikyu Kanagawa Station.
