Kiyomizu-dera Temple

Kyoto

Kiyomizu-dera takes its name, meaning "pure water temple," from the Otowa waterfall that flows beneath its main hall, and the temple's founding legend is tied to that spring. The building most visitors come to see is the main hall's wooden veranda, constructed using a traditional joinery method without nails, which extends out from the hillside on a lattice of pillars and offers a wide view over the maple and cherry trees below and the city beyond. The full temple grounds spread across the hillside and include several other halls, a three-story pagoda, and the approach street lined with shops.

A visit to the main hall and grounds can be done in under an hour, though allowing more time to also walk the surrounding paths and nearby Jishu Shrine makes for a fuller morning or afternoon. Admission to the inner grounds involves a modest fee. The temple is at its most crowded in the late morning and early afternoon and during cherry blossom and autumn foliage weeks, when evening illuminations are sometimes held; visiting soon after opening is the most effective way to see the veranda without a wall of people in front of it.

Because it sits at the top of the Higashiyama slopes, most people combine it with a walk down through Ninenzaka and Sannenzaka afterward rather than visiting it in isolation.

1-294 Kiyomizu, Higashiyama Ward, Kyoto
