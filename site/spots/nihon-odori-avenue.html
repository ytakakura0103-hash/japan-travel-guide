Nihon Odori Avenue

Yokohama

After a devastating fire swept through the foreign settlement in 1866, Scottish engineer Richard Henry Brunton was brought in to redesign the district, and this wide, tree-lined avenue running from Yokohama Park to the harbor was his firebreak-and-boulevard solution, making it one of the earliest deliberately planned streets in modern Japan. The ginkgo trees planted along its length turn a deep gold in November, and the avenue is often compared, a little grandly, to a miniature Champs-Élysées.

Walking its length today takes you past a run of Taisho and early Showa-era buildings, former consulates, banks, and newspaper offices, many still in active use, that give a sense of the street's role as Yokohama's civic and diplomatic spine for over a century. It connects several of the area's key historic sites without requiring a detour.

Because it functions as a through street rather than a destination in itself, most visitors walk it without really noticing they are on a landmark. Slowing down for the architecture along the way, and timing a visit for the ginkgo color in autumn, turns an ordinary walk into one of the better free architecture routes in central Yokohama.

Nihon-odori, Naka-ku, Yokohama
