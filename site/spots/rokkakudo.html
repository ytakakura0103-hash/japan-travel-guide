Rokkakudo

Kyoto

Rokkakudo, formally Chohoji, is a small hexagonal-roofed temple set incongruously in the middle of a modern shopping district in central Kyoto, its low wooden hall surrounded on most sides by department stores and office buildings rather than the greenery typical of the city's larger temples. Tradition credits the temple, founded well over a thousand years ago, as the birthplace of ikebana, the art of formal flower arranging, tracing back to a resident priest who began arranging flowers as an offering before the temple's altar.

That connection is still active today: the Ikenobo school of ikebana, one of the oldest and most established in Japan, maintains its headquarters and a teaching school directly on the temple grounds, and flower arrangements are often on display near the main hall reflecting the ongoing practice rather than just its history. A visit to the compact grounds takes fifteen to twenty minutes for most people; there's no significant fee to enter.

Because it sits inside a shopping district rather than a temple neighborhood, Rokkakudo makes an easy, unplanned stop between shopping or errands rather than a dedicated destination, and it's rarely crowded even during the day. It's a short walk from the Museum of Kyoto and Nishiki Market, making it simple to combine with either.

248 Donomaecho, Nakagyo Ward, Kyoto
