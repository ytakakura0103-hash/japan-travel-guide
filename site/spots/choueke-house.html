Choueke House

Kobe

The Choueke family arrived in Kobe from Aleppo by way of Ottoman trade networks and built this two-story wooden residence in Kitano's foreign settlement in the early 20th century. Unlike most of the district's ijinkan, which were emptied out and refurnished as generic museum displays, this house kept its original owners' furniture, rugs and household objects, so the rooms read less like an exhibit and more like a family simply stepped out.

The ground floor mixes European and Middle Eastern decorative touches that hint at the family's trading routes, while upstairs bedrooms give a sense of daily domestic life rather than formal reception rooms. It's a smaller, quieter stop than the ticketed "Ijinkan set courses" most tour groups follow.

Practically, admission is modest and combination tickets covering several Kitano houses are usually available at the entrance booths; opening hours can shift seasonally so it's worth checking before making a special trip. Because most visitors head straight for the Weathercock House and Moegi House at the top of the hill, arriving in the early morning or detouring onto the side lane where Choueke House sits will usually mean having the rooms nearly to yourself.

The surrounding lanes are steep and cobbled, so comfortable shoes help, and the walk up rewards you with glimpses of Kobe port between the houses.

Kitano-cho, Chuo Ward, Kobe

Open in Google Maps

From Sannomiya Station, walk or take the City Loop bus north uphill into the Kitano Ijinkan district (about 15-20 minutes on foot); the house sits on a side lane just off the main Kitano tourist street.
