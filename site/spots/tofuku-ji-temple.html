Tofuku-ji Temple

Kyoto

Tofuku-ji is one of Kyoto's larger Zen temple complexes, and its main draw is a wooded ravine that cuts through the grounds, planted with roughly 2,000 maple trees and crossed by Tsutenkyo, a covered wooden bridge built specifically as a vantage point over the valley. In autumn the ravine turns a deep, saturated red that's considered among the best foliage displays in the city, while in other seasons the same valley offers a quieter, deeply green walk through mature woodland.

The temple also has a well-regarded rock garden at the Hojo hall, designed in the 20th century but drawing on much older garden principles, worth the added time if the ravine alone doesn't fill out the visit. A walk covering the bridge, valley, and Hojo garden takes around an hour to ninety minutes. Separate modest admission fees apply to the bridge crossing and to the Hojo garden.

Tofuku-ji sees real crowds during peak autumn foliage weeks in November, but even then tends to be noticeably less congested than Kiyomizu-dera or Arashiyama at the same time of year, since it draws fewer casual sightseers relative to its size. Visiting on a weekday morning, or slightly before or after the absolute peak of the leaf color, is the most reliable way to see the bridge without a solid line of people on it.

15-778 Honmachi, Higashiyama Ward, Kyoto
