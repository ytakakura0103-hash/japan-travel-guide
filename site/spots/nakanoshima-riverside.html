Local Pick

Nakanoshima

Osaka

A narrow strip of land squeezed between the Dojima and Tosabori rivers, Nakanoshima sits at the historic heart of Osaka's business district yet feels distinctly separate from the office towers around it, thanks to a run of parks, rose gardens and riverside promenades along its length. The island mixes eras visibly: Meiji- and Taisho-period stone buildings, including the domed Osaka City Central Public Hall and the Nakanoshima Library, stand within view of newer museum architecture, giving the walk a sense of Osaka's architectural range in one stretch.

A full walk from one end of the island to the other takes roughly thirty to forty minutes at an easy pace, longer with stops at cafes or museum exhibitions along the way. The rose garden is at its best in a couple of short windows each year, typically mid-spring and again in autumn, when the beds along the water are in full bloom and worth timing a visit around.

Weekday mornings tend to be the quietest time to stroll, since the island fills with office workers on lunch breaks and, in the evening, with people relaxing by the water after work. Most outdoor areas are free to enter, though the museums charge separate admission. Combine a walk here with a stop at the Osaka Museum of History or Osaka Castle, both a short distance away, for a fuller half-day route through the city centre.
