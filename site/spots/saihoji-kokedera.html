Saihoji (Kokedera, the Moss Temple)

Kyoto

Saihoji, better known as Kokedera or the "moss temple," is a UNESCO World Heritage garden covering its grounds in more than 120 varieties of moss, grown so densely over the centuries that it forms a soft, rolling green carpet under the trees around the temple's pond. The garden's current character is partly accidental: repeated flooding in the early 20th century killed off other plantings and allowed moss to take over the grounds, producing the look the temple is now famous for.

Unlike most Kyoto temples, Saihoji can't simply be walked into; visitors must apply for a reserved time slot in advance by mail, or, more recently, through a web-based reservation system that has supplemented the traditional postal process, and entry involves participating in a short Buddhist practice, typically copying or chanting a sutra at a low table, before being allowed out into the garden itself. This adds real friction to visiting compared to Kyoto's open-access temples, along with a distinctly higher admission fee reflecting the limited, controlled entry.

Once inside, a walk through the moss garden itself takes about thirty to forty-five minutes, on top of the sutra session beforehand. Because entry is capped by reservation rather than open to walk-ins, Saihoji never feels crowded in the way major sights do, regardless of season, which is largely the point for visitors willing to plan ahead for it.

56 Matsuo Jingatanicho, Nishikyo Ward, Kyoto
