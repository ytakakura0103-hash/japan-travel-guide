Shiroi Koibito Park

Hokkaido

Shiroi Koibito, a white-chocolate sandwich cookie, has been one of Hokkaido's default souvenirs since the 1970s, and its maker built this half-museum, half-theme-park around the factory that still produces it. Large windows let visitors watch parts of the cookie production line in motion, while the surrounding building leans into a storybook European aesthetic, complete with a clock tower courtyard, that reads as more playful than historically accurate.

The free zone includes a shop and cafe; the paid zone adds cookie-making workshops, exhibits on chocolate and confectionery history, and photo-friendly interiors. It's aimed squarely at families and cookie enthusiasts rather than being a must-see historical sight, and works best as a relaxed two-hour stop rather than a full-day plan.

The courtyard gets a seasonal illumination display in winter that draws a steady stream of evening visitors, so daytime hours are calmer if the goal is the factory tour rather than the lights. Weekends and school holidays bring more families with children; a weekday morning visit tends to be the least crowded option. It sits a little outside the main downtown cluster, so it pairs reasonably well with a wider loop through Sapporo's western neighborhoods rather than as a same-day add-on to Odori Park.

The park reopened after a major renewal in July 2025, adding an immersive video and sound attraction themed around the park's famous cookie alongside a renovated factory-tour viewing area and refreshed staff uniforms, giving the long-running attraction a more contemporary edge without losing its core factory-tour appeal.

2-11-36 Miyanosawa 2-jo, Nishi-ku, Sapporo, Hokkaido

Open in Google Maps

Take the Tozai subway line to its western terminus, Miyanosawa Station, then walk about 10 minutes.
