Tokoname Pottery Footpath (Yakimono Sanpomichi)

Nagoya

Tokoname is counted among Japan's Six Ancient Kilns, a group of pottery-producing regions with continuous production histories stretching back roughly a thousand years, and the Yakimono Sanpomichi, or Pottery Footpath, is the most direct way to experience that history on foot. The shorter of its two marked routes, Course A, covers about 1.6 kilometers through the old kiln district, passing brick chimneys, working studios and small galleries selling Tokoname-yaki ceramics.

The route's signature stretch is Dokan-zaka, a sloped lane paved with old clay pipes and ceramic jars set on their sides into the ground, creating one of the most photographed textures in the region. Nearby, Maneki Neko-dori, or Lucky Cat Street, is lined with dozens of Tokoname-made beckoning cat statues, a nod to the city's status as Japan's largest producer of maneki-neko, and the street culminates in Tokonyan, a roughly three-meter-tall cat face and paw emerging from an embankment, one of the largest lucky cat figures in the country.

The walk is compact enough to complete in an hour or two, making Tokoname an easy half-day trip that pairs naturally with the nearby INAX Live Museum.
