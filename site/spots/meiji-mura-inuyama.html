Museum Meiji-Mura

Nagoya

Meiji-mura opened in 1965 with a specific mission: to rescue architecturally significant buildings from Japan's rapid modernization, roughly the Meiji, Taisho and early Showa periods, before they were demolished and lost entirely. Over the decades since, more than sixty structures have been physically relocated here and reassembled across a sprawling, hilly site beside Lake Iruka, with eleven of them now designated Important Cultural Properties and most of the rest registered as Tangible Cultural Properties.

The collection's centerpiece is the reconstructed entrance hall and lobby of Frank Lloyd Wright's original Imperial Hotel, which stood in Tokyo from 1923 until the main building was demolished in 1967 to make way for a larger replacement; this fragment is the closest most visitors will ever get to experiencing Wright's original design. Elsewhere on the grounds are Lafcadio Hearn's summer house, a Meiji-era Christian church designed by James McDonald Gardiner, and Kyoto's old St. Francis Xavier Catholic Cathedral, alongside buildings imported from as far away as Hawaii, Seattle and Brazil.

The site is large enough that a thorough visit takes most of a day, with a vintage steam locomotive and old Kyoto trams running between distant sections to help visitors cover the ground.
