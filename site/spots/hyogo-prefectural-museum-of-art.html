Hyogo Prefectural Museum of Art

Kobe

Designed by Kobe-born architect Tadao Ando, whose signature exposed concrete and geometric forms are on full display here, the museum sits directly on the waterfront in a building conceived partly as a symbol of recovery following the 1995 Great Hanshin earthquake. The structure itself, with its interlocking cylindrical and rectangular volumes and long sightlines toward the bay, draws almost as much attention from visitors as the art inside, and architecture enthusiasts often visit as much for the building as for any specific exhibition.

The collection includes work by regional Hyogo-connected artists alongside broader modern and contemporary Japanese and Western art, and the museum regularly hosts major touring special exhibitions that can draw larger crowds than the permanent collection alone. A visit covering the permanent galleries takes roughly an hour, considerably longer if a special exhibition is running.

Outside of headline special exhibitions, the museum is rarely crowded, and its waterfront terraces and outdoor sculpture areas make for a pleasant, uncrowded walk even for visitors who decide to skip the paid galleries and just take in the building and its setting.
