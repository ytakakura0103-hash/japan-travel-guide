Fukuya Mentaiko Main Store

Fukuoka

Mentaiko, seasoned and marinated pollock roe, is inseparable from Fukuoka's food identity, and Fukuya is generally recognized as the company that first developed and commercialized the modern version of the product in the years after World War II, adapting a Korean-influenced recipe into what's now sold across Japan. Visiting the main store gives a chance to buy it close to the source, often with a wider range of grades and spice levels than you'll find at an airport gift shop.

Mentaiko is sold vacuum-packed and chilled or frozen, which travels reasonably well for a day or two, making it a realistic souvenir for visitors heading home rather than something that has to be eaten immediately. Staff can usually advise on which grade or packaging suits a longer trip home versus quick local eating.

Branches and mentaiko counters from various producers, including Fukuya, are also found inside Hakata Station and major Tenjin department stores, which is a simpler option if the main store's exact location doesn't fit your route. Either way, comparing a couple of shops' offerings is worth it, since spice level and saltiness vary noticeably between producers.
