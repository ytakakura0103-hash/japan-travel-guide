Onikobe Onsen

Sendai

Onikobe sits within a massive ancient caldera in the low mountains of northwestern Miyagi, and its name, meaning roughly "demon's neck," reflects the raw volcanic character of the area. A walking trail through the so-called Jigokudani, or Hell Valley, cuts through a rugged canyon where hot spring water bursts from the ground with real force, and a natural geyser erupts at fairly predictable intervals, sending a jet of hot water into the air.

Several of the area's ryokan offer day-bathing for a modest fee, and the geyser and canyon trail can be combined into a visit of two to three hours including travel time from Naruko.

Despite being only about thirty minutes from Naruko Onsen by car or bus, Onikobe sees noticeably fewer visitors than its better-known neighbor, giving it a more rugged, undeveloped feel. The geyser and steaming canyon are particularly striking against cold winter air, when the contrast between hot steam and snow is at its most dramatic.
