Kinkaku-ji (Golden Pavilion)

Kyoto

Kinkaku-ji's top two floors are covered in gold leaf, a design chosen by the shogun Ashikaga Yoshimitsu when he had the building converted from a retirement villa into a Zen temple in the late 14th century; the current structure is a mid-20th-century reconstruction after the original was destroyed by fire. The pavilion stands at the edge of Kyokochi pond, positioned so its gilded reflection appears in the still water, which is the view nearly every visitor comes to photograph.

Kinkaku-ji is a walking route rather than a building to enter — visitors follow a set path around the pond and up through the temple's gardens, passing the pavilion at a fixed viewing distance rather than going inside. The full loop takes roughly thirty to forty-five minutes at an unhurried pace. Admission is a modest fixed fee, and the ticket itself is a small paper talisman rather than a printed stub, which many visitors keep as a souvenir.

Because it's one of the most visited sites in Kyoto, the temple is busy for most of the day; the first hour after opening is noticeably calmer, and weekday mornings are best if the schedule allows. Snow in winter and the surrounding maples in autumn are especially popular times to see the pavilion, though both bring their own crowds.

1 Kinkakujicho, Kita Ward, Kyoto
