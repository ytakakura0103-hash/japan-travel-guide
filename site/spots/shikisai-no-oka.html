Shikisai-no-Oka

Hokkaido

Shikisai-no-Oka, whose name roughly translates to "hill of four seasons' colors," spreads bands of different flowers across a sloped hillside so that from a distance the fields read almost like brushstrokes of color rather than individual rows. Unlike Farm Tomita's lavender-centric fame, this farm rotates through a wider range of flowers across a longer season, generally offering something in bloom from spring through mid-autumn, which makes it a slightly more flexible choice for visitors whose travel dates don't line up with peak lavender week.

A small paddock of alpacas near the entrance has become an unexpected draw of its own, particularly for families traveling with children, and golf-cart-style buggies can be rented to cover the sloped grounds without a long uphill walk.

Because it sits a little further off the main Biei town loop than the Patchwork Road's roadside trees, it doesn't get quite the same density of tour buses as Farm Tomita in peak summer, though weekend afternoons in July and August are still its busiest window; weekday mornings are noticeably calmer. Winter closes most of the flower fields, so this is very much a warm-season destination, best planned for anywhere between late spring and early autumn.

Shinsei, Biei-cho, Kamikawa-gun, Hokkaido

Open in Google Maps

Reachable by rental car, or via the seasonal Biei-Furano sightseeing bus loop; the nearest train station is Bibaushi on the JR Furano Line.
