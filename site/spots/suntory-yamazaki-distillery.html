Suntory Yamazaki Distillery

Osaka

Founded in 1923, Yamazaki was Japan's first dedicated whisky distillery and remains the most historically significant, credited with developing the techniques and blending philosophy that eventually diverged from the Scotch traditions it was originally modelled on to create a distinctly Japanese style of whisky, now internationally recognised and, at times, difficult to find given global demand. The distillery runs tours that walk visitors through the production process, from malting and distillation to the barrel-filled ageing warehouses, typically finishing with a tasting session that includes several expressions not always available for purchase elsewhere.

Tours generally run for an hour to ninety minutes and often require advance reservation, particularly for English-language sessions or tastings that include rarer bottlings, so booking well before a visit is strongly advised rather than assuming walk-in availability. A small museum and shop are also open to visitors without a tour booking, offering a shorter, self-guided alternative for anyone unable to secure a reserved slot.

The valley setting near the Yodo River, chosen originally for its water quality and misty climate believed to aid maturation, gives the grounds a scenic backdrop distinct from the distillery's industrial function. The site is a short walk from Yamazaki Station on the JR Kyoto Line, making it a feasible day trip from either Osaka or Kyoto, and it's worth noting that whisky tastings mean this isn't a stop suited to designated drivers on the day.
