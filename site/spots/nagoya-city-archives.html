Nagoya City Archives

Nagoya

Completed in 1922, this building served for decades as Nagoya's court of appeals along with a local and district court, functioning as the judiciary center for the greater Chubu region until the courts moved to a new facility in 1979. A decade later the vacated building was converted into the Nagoya City Archives, tasked with preserving and making public the city's official historical documents, a role it still holds today.

Architecturally, the building is a striking example of early twentieth-century Western-influenced design, its neo-baroque facade combining red brick with white granite trim in a style that was fashionable in Japan during the Taisho era. Only two Court of Appeals buildings from this design lineage survive in Japan, this one and a counterpart in Sapporo, and the Nagoya building has been designated a National Important Cultural Property as a result. Inside, the restored marble staircase, meeting rooms and a preserved courtroom give a strong sense of the building's original judicial function even though its purpose has completely changed.

It sits within the Bunka no Michi Cultural Path district, making it easy to combine with a visit to the nearby Futaba Museum and Shumokukan.

1-3 Shirakabe, Higashi-ku, Nagoya, Aichi
