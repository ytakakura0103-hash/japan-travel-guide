Umeda Sky Building Floating Garden Observatory

Osaka

Designed by architect Hiroshi Hara and completed in 1993, the twin 40-storey towers are connected near their peak by a ring-shaped, partially open-air walkway known as the Floating Garden Observatory, reached by a glass-sided escalator that crosses the gap between the towers before arriving at the top. The design was unusual enough at completion that it's often cited among notable modern buildings, and it still stands out sharply against Osaka's otherwise low-rise Kita skyline.

The observation deck itself takes most visitors under an hour to enjoy fully, though the wait for tickets and escalators can extend that, particularly around sunset when the deck is at its most popular. Because the view stretches uninterrupted toward the Yodo River, the bay and the mountains beyond on clear days, arriving thirty to forty minutes before sunset for a seat near the railing is worth the trade-off of slightly longer lines. Weekday visits, or an early evening arrival before the golden-hour rush, tend to be noticeably quieter than weekend visits. Admission is charged, with pricing broadly comparable to other city observatories in Osaka.

Below the towers, the basement level houses a recreated Showa-era retro dining street, a pleasant contrast to the modern architecture above and a good spot for a meal before or after the observatory visit. Night views are equally worthwhile, when the illuminated city grid spreads out in every direction.

1-1-88 Oyodonaka, Kita Ward, Osaka
