MoN Takanawa: The Museum of Narratives

Tokyo

MoN, short for the Museum of Narratives, opened in March 2026 as the cultural centerpiece of Takanawa Gateway City, the large mixed-use development built around Tokyo's newest JR station. The building itself, designed by Kengo Kuma, spirals upward around a green, planted core, and is as much a piece of the exhibit as anything inside it, wrapping visitors through a route rather than a conventional room-by-room floor plan.

The museum's identity centers on immersive, technology-driven storytelling rather than a fixed permanent collection. Its best-known feature so far is a large-scale installation built around Osamu Tezuka's Phoenix (Hinotori), using LED theater staging to put visitors inside the story rather than displaying it as static art, alongside other rotating narrative-based exhibits that change more like theater programming than a traditional museum schedule.

Because it opened so recently, exhibits and hours are still evolving, and it's worth checking current programming before a visit, especially if a specific installation is the draw. It pairs naturally with a walk through the rest of Takanawa Gateway City's new public spaces, and its station-direct access makes it an easy add-on for anyone passing through Shinagawa or Tamachi rather than a destination requiring a special trip.

Takanawa Gateway City, Minato City, Tokyo
