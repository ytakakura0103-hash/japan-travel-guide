Local Pick

Toyokoro Jewelry Ice

Hokkaido

Each midwinter, ice that forms upriver on the Tokachi River breaks apart and washes down to the Otsu coastline near Toyokoro, where wave action polishes the fragments into clear, glassy shapes scattered across the beach's black volcanic sand. The contrast between the dark sand and the translucent ice, catching light like scattered jewels, has made this one of Hokkaido's most distinctive winter natural phenomena, drawing photographers specifically for the brief seasonal window when conditions align.

The phenomenon typically appears from mid-January through late February, and even within that window, the quantity and quality of ice on any given day depends heavily on weather, tides, and river conditions upstream, making it considerably less predictable than a fixed seasonal attraction like cherry blossoms. A local viewing support facility, "Jewelry House," has previously operated seasonally near the site, closing again each year once the ice season ends.

Given its remote coastal location in the Tokachi region, several hours from Sapporo, and the weather-dependent nature of the phenomenon itself, this is a destination for visitors specifically chasing the experience rather than a casual add-on to a broader Hokkaido itinerary. Checking current-year conditions and viewing facility status close to a planned visit is essential, since both the ice itself and any support infrastructure vary from year to year.
