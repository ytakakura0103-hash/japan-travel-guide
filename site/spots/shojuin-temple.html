Shojuin Temple

Kyoto

Shojuin is a small temple on a hillside south of Uji that has become disproportionately popular relative to its size, largely thanks to a single striking feature: a window cut into the shape of a heart, framed against the surrounding garden and, in season, against maple leaves turning color just outside. The temple's main hall also has a ceiling made up of around 160 individually hand-painted panels, each depicting a different flower, animal, or seasonal scene, created by contributing artists in recent years rather than as a historical relic, which gives the space a distinctly contemporary, almost gallery-like feel unusual among Kyoto's older temples. Because it sits away from the main tourist routes and requires a taxi or bus rather than a direct train connection, Shojuin has kept something of a secret, off-the-beaten-path atmosphere, though word has spread enough that its best-known corners can draw a short queue for photos during peak color season. A visit itself is brief, often twenty to thirty minutes, but getting there and back adds meaningfully to the time commitment. Autumn, when the maples frame the heart window in red, is by far the most popular and busiest period; visiting earlier in the day helps avoid the wait for photos. Admission is modest. Because of its size and limited access, it works best as a deliberate side trip rather than a spontaneous stop.

149 Okuyamada Kawakami, Ujitawara, Tsuzuki District, Kyoto
