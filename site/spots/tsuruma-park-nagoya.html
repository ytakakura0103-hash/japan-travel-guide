Tsuruma Park

Nagoya

Tsuruma Park dates back to 1909 and was the site of a major regional exposition the following year, which is when its centerpiece, a stone-and-marble fountain tower, was first built. Designed by engineer Teiji Suzuki as a deliberate blend of Eastern and Western styles, the tower became one of the park's defining landmarks. It was later dismantled during construction of the Tsurumai subway line and rebuilt in 1983, and it now carries protected status as a municipal cultural property.

The park is one of Nagoya's most reliable seasonal destinations. Cherry trees line the paths in spring and rank among the city's better-known hanami spots, while a Western-style garden on the western side fills with roses in May and June during the park's annual rose festival, and irises follow shortly after in the park's pond areas.

Unlike the manicured formality of some Japanese gardens, Tsuruma Park has the loose, well-used feel of a genuine neighborhood park, with joggers, food stalls during festival weekends and families picnicking under the trees rather than a ticketed, curated experience.
