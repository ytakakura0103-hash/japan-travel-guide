Gekkeikan Okura Sake Museum

Kyoto

Located in Fushimi, one of Japan's most important sake-producing districts thanks to its natural spring water, the Gekkeikan Okura Sake Museum occupies a preserved Edo-period brewery building that gives a tangible sense of how sake was made before modern industrial equipment took over. Exhibits walk through the stages of traditional brewing, from rice polishing and steaming to fermentation in large wooden vats, with old tools, photographs, and equipment displayed along the original working spaces. Signage includes English explanations, making it accessible without a guide, and a self-guided visit typically takes well under an hour. The highlight for most visitors comes at the end, where a small tasting lets you sample a few different styles of Gekkeikan sake, sometimes including a version not widely sold outside the brewery; a modest admission fee generally covers the tasting as well. The surrounding neighborhood, with its canal, willow trees, and rows of other historic sake breweries, rewards a slower walk afterward rather than a rushed in-and-out visit. Boat rides along the old canal are available seasonally nearby, adding another way to see the district. Because it's a smaller, specialized museum, it rarely gets crowded, though weekend afternoons draw more visitors than weekday mornings. The area pairs naturally with a stop at Teradaya, the nearby inn connected to Sakamoto Ryoma, since both sit within easy walking distance of Chushojima Station.

247 Minamihamacho, Fushimi Ward, Kyoto

Open in Google Maps

5-minute walk from Chushojima Station (Keihan Uji Line)
