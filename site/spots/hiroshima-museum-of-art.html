Hiroshima Museum of Art

Hiroshima

The museum was founded by a local bank in 1978, explicitly framed as a memorial to children lost to war, including the atomic bombing, and dedicated to the idea that art could contribute to peace. Its core collection focuses on French painting from the 19th and early 20th centuries, with works by artists such as Monet, Renoir, Van Gogh, Cezanne, and Picasso, alongside a smaller collection of Japanese Western-style painting from the same period.

The building itself is a domed structure set around a small courtyard garden, designed to feel more intimate than a large national museum, and the permanent collection is supplemented by rotating special exhibitions throughout the year. Because the collection is relatively focused rather than encyclopedic, a visit is manageable in under two hours, which makes it an easy pairing with the neighboring Shukkeien Garden.

The museum sits a short walk from Hiroshima Station, making it one of the more convenient art stops in the city for visitors without much time, and English-language descriptions are provided for the core collection.

3-2 Motomachi, Naka Ward, Hiroshima, Hiroshima Prefecture

Open in Google Maps

12-minute walk from Hiroshima Station's south exit, or a short ride on the Hiroshima Sightseeing Loop Bus.
