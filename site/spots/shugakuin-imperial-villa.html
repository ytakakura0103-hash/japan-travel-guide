Shugakuin Imperial Villa

Kyoto

Built in the 17th century as a retirement retreat for Emperor Go-Mizunoo, Shugakuin Imperial Villa spreads across three separate garden terraces on rising ground at the base of the Higashiyama hills, connected by paths through rice paddies and pine groves that are themselves considered part of the designed landscape rather than incidental farmland. The upper garden, reached last on the guided route, centers on a large pond with a view that borrows the surrounding mountains as part of its composition, a technique known as shakkei that makes the garden feel far larger than its actual boundaries suggest. As with Katsura, Kyoto's other major imperial garden property, visiting requires advance reservation through the Imperial Household Agency, and tours run on a fixed schedule with a guide leading the group through all three terraces on a set route rather than free exploration. The full tour typically runs around ninety minutes and covers a fair amount of walking and some gentle climbing between the terrace levels, so comfortable shoes are worth wearing. Because access is limited to scheduled tour groups, the gardens stay uncrowded regardless of the season, a rare guarantee in Kyoto. Autumn brings strong maple color across the terraced hillsides, and this is typically the most requested and hardest-to-book season, so applying well ahead of a planned autumn visit is worth doing. Spring and the rice-planting season in the surrounding paddies offer a different, greener character to the same route.
