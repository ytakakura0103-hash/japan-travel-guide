Tokyo Midtown

Tokyo

Tokyo Midtown, built on the site of the former Japan Defense Agency headquarters in Akasaka, is one of the more polished mixed-use developments in central Tokyo, combining office towers, high-end shops, restaurants, and museums (including 21_21 Design Sight and the Suntory Museum of Art) around a central plaza. The complex's real draw for casual visitors, though, is Midtown Garden, a broad lawn edged by trees that turns into one of the city's most relaxed cherry blossom picnic spots each spring — less crowded and more spacious than famous parks like Ueno, and easier to find a patch of grass on a weekday. In winter the same lawn and the plaza in front of the towers host an illumination display, usually running from mid-November through the year's end, with strings of small lights draped over the trees.

Budget an hour or two just to wander the shops and gardens, longer if you plan to stop into one of the museums. Weekday mornings are the quietest time to walk the lawn or grab a coffee at one of the cafes facing the garden; weekends and the illumination season bring noticeably bigger crowds, especially in the evening. Because Midtown connects directly to Roppongi Station, it also works well as a covered rest stop between Roppongi's nightlife and its museums, and its basement food hall is a reasonable option for a quick meal without needing to go back outside.
