Kobe City Museum

Kobe

Housed in a stately 1935 former bank building in the old foreign settlement district, Kobe City Museum focuses on the city's identity as a gateway between Japan and the outside world. Its standout holdings are Nanban screens and maps from the 16th and 17th centuries, painted when Japan was first encountering Portuguese ships, missionaries, and traders, alongside old maps of Kobe itself and archaeological finds from the region.

The permanent collection is compact enough to see thoroughly in under an hour, though special exhibitions, which rotate a few times a year and sometimes bring in major loaned artworks, can extend a visit considerably and may carry a separate admission fee. The building itself, with its classical stone columns, is worth photographing even from the outside.

Because it sits a block off the busier Motomachi shopping street, the museum rarely feels crowded except during a headline special exhibition. It pairs naturally with a walk through the nearby former foreign settlement (Kyu-Kyoryuchi), where several other Meiji and Taisho-era buildings from Kobe's international trading past still stand as bank branches, boutiques, and cafes.

24 Kyomachi, Chuo-ku, Kobe
