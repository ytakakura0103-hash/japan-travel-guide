Kokoen Garden

Kobe

Kokoen occupies the site of what was once the western residential quarter of Himeji Castle's samurai retainers, and rather than a single continuous garden, it's divided into nine separate walled sections, each designed in a distinct historical Edo-period garden style, from a formal pine garden to a bamboo grove and a tea ceremony garden with an active teahouse. Walking between the sections through connecting gates gives a sense of variety that a single sprawling garden rarely offers.

Because it sits just outside Himeji Castle's main entrance, many visitors treat it as an afterthought once they've toured the castle keep, which keeps it noticeably calmer despite the shared proximity; the high stone walls separating each garden section also help absorb crowd noise, so even a moderately busy day rarely feels crowded inside.

A combination ticket covering both the castle and Kokoen is usually available and represents good value if you're visiting both. The teahouse garden offers matcha and a seasonal sweet for an additional fee, a pleasant way to rest partway through a Himeji day trip. Maple and cherry sections make spring and autumn especially rewarding visits.

68 Honmachi, Himeji, Hyogo Prefecture

Open in Google Maps

From Sannomiya or Kobe Station, take the JR Special Rapid service to Himeji (roughly 40-50 minutes), then walk about 15-20 minutes from Himeji Station toward the castle; Kokoen is adjacent to the castle's west side.
