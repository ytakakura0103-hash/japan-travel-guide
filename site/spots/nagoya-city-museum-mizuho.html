Nagoya City Museum

Nagoya

Opened in 1977 to mark Nagoya's population passing two million, the Nagoya City Museum sits inside Mizuho Park and functions as the city's primary institution for the history and culture of both Nagoya and the wider Aichi region. Its holdings run to hundreds of thousands of items, from archaeological finds of the Jomon and Yayoi periods, including the oldest known boat-shaped wooden coffin in Japan, through to roof tiles and Buddhist images from the Nara and Heian eras.

The permanent exhibition traces local history from the Paleolithic period to the present day, with particular attention paid to the two warlords most closely associated with the Nagoya area, Oda Nobunaga and Toyotomi Hideyoshi, and to the subsequent rise of the Owari branch of the Tokugawa family that ruled the region for over two centuries. A rotating program of special exhibitions throughout the year covers everything from specific cultural-heritage topics to broader themes in Owari regional history.

As with any large municipal museum, it is worth checking current opening status and any renovation schedule before visiting, since exhibition halls occasionally close for extended maintenance.

1-27-1 Mizuho-dori, Mizuho-ku, Nagoya, Aichi
