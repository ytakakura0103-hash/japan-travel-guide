Mitaki-dera Temple

Hiroshima

Founded in the early 9th century and named for the three small waterfalls (mitaki) running through its grounds, this temple climbs a forested hillside in northwestern Hiroshima, far enough from downtown that it draws only a fraction of the visitors who pass through Peace Memorial Park on the same day.\n\nRather than a single central hall, the site is spread across the slope as a loose collection of smaller structures, stone Buddhist statues (including rows of moss-covered Jizo figures), and a multi-story pagoda relocated here from Wakayama Prefecture after the war as a memorial. The combination of running water, dense forest cover, and weathered stone carvings gives Mitaki-dera a noticeably different atmosphere from Hiroshima's flatter, more open memorial sites.\n\nAutumn foliage draws the largest local crowds, when the maples along the stream turn color, but the temple is worth visiting in any season for the quiet alone — a genuine option for anyone who wants a forested, contemplative stop without leaving the city limits.

411 Mitakisan-cho, Nishi-ku, Hiroshima

Open in Google Maps

About a 10-minute walk from JR Mitaki Station; roughly 15 minutes from Hiroshima Station by JR train.
