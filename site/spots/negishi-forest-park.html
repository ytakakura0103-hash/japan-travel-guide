Negishi Forest Park

Yokohama

This park occupies the site of the Negishi Racecourse, established in the 1860s for the foreign settlement's horse-racing community and one of the earliest Western-style racecourses built in Japan. Horse racing here ended decades ago, but the abandoned concrete grandstand, an imposing, ivy-creeping ruin from the early twentieth century, still stands at the park's edge, fenced off but strikingly visible, a genuinely unusual sight against the otherwise ordinary open lawns around it.

Today the grounds function as a straightforward public park, with wide grassy fields good for picnics and kite flying, a running course, and a modest stretch of cherry trees that draws local hanami crowds each spring without ever getting as crowded as bigger-name blossom spots. A small horse-racing history museum nearby adds context for the grandstand ruin, for visitors curious about that chapter of the site's past.

Because it sits on the border between Naka and Isogo wards, off the main sightseeing corridors, the park attracts overwhelmingly local visitors, joggers, families, dog walkers, rather than tourists, and the grandstand ruin in particular is the kind of striking, slightly eerie photo subject that rewards travelers willing to go a bit off the beaten path.

1 Negishidai, Naka-ku, Yokohama
