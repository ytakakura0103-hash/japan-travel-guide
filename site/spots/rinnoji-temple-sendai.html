Rinnoji Temple

Sendai

Rinnoji began under the patronage of the Date family and later served as a family temple, and its main draw today is less the temple buildings than the classical strolling garden wrapped around a central pond. Paths loop past stone lanterns, a small waterfall, and clusters of trees chosen for how they change through the year, in the tradition of Japanese kaiyu-shiki (circuit) garden design.\n\nInside the temple hall are wooden statues and Date-era artifacts, but most visitors spend the bulk of their time simply walking the garden loop, which takes roughly thirty to forty minutes at an unhurried pace. It is a smaller, quieter alternative to Sendai's bigger historical sites, appealing to anyone who wants a slower, more contemplative stop between castle ruins and shrines.\n\nAdmission is modest. The garden is at its most photogenic in early June, when irises bloom around the pond edges, and again in mid-to-late November, when the maples turn deep red against the temple's dark wood. Weekday mornings are noticeably quieter than weekend afternoons, when local garden clubs sometimes organize visits.

1-14-1 Otamayashita, Aoba-ku, Sendai, Miyagi
