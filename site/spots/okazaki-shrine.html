Okazaki Shrine

Kyoto

Okazaki Shrine is a small, local-feeling shrine in the Okazaki cultural district, historically associated with fertility, safe childbirth, and protection for pregnant women, and, as a result, dotted throughout its grounds with small rabbit statues, since rabbits, known for prolific breeding, are considered the shrine's sacred messenger in local tradition. Rabbit motifs turn up everywhere here, from stone carvings scattered among the shrine buildings to charms and seals sold at the shrine office, making the hunt for all of them something of a small game for visitors, especially families with children.

A visit to the compact grounds takes about fifteen to twenty minutes for most people, longer if you stop to look for every rabbit statue on the property. There's no significant fee to enter the shrine itself, though charms and seasonal goshuin stamps involve a small cost.

Because it's overshadowed by the larger Heian Shrine and the museums nearby, Okazaki Shrine tends to stay quiet even when the surrounding district is busy, and there's little need to plan a visit around a particular time of day. It sits within easy walking distance of Heian Shrine, the Kyoto City KYOCERA Museum of Art, and the zoo, making it a light, easy addition to a day spent in the Okazaki area.

51 Okazaki Higashitenno-cho, Sakyo Ward, Kyoto
