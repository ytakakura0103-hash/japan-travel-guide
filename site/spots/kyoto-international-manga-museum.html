Kyoto International Manga Museum

Kyoto

Housed in a former elementary school building that closed decades ago and was later repurposed rather than demolished, the Kyoto International Manga Museum holds a collection of roughly 300,000 manga volumes, one of the largest of its kind anywhere. The building's long hallways still carry traces of its original use, and the most distinctive feature is a continuous wall of open shelving, sometimes called the Wall of Manga, stretching across multiple floors and packed with volumes that visitors are free to pull down and read on the spot, seated on the floor, in reading nooks, or out on the building's lawn in good weather. Most of the collection is in Japanese, though a rotating selection of translated volumes and English-language exhibits makes the museum accessible to visitors who don't read Japanese, and rotating special exhibitions cover specific artists, genres, or manga history topics in more depth. A single admission fee covers the whole day, and visitors frequently spend two to three hours or more simply browsing, making it one of the better rainy-day options in central Kyoto. Weekends and school holidays bring more local families and younger readers; weekday afternoons tend to be calmer. Because it sits in the middle of downtown, a short walk from the Nijo Castle and Nishiki Market areas, it fits easily into a day that mixes historical sightseeing with something more contemporary.
