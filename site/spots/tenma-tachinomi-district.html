Local Pick

Tenma Standing Bar District

Osaka

Tenma sits at the northern end of Tenjinbashi-suji, Japan's longest covered shopping arcade, but its real draw after dark is the tangle of streets on either side of JR Tenma Station, home to well over a hundred standing bars packed into just a few blocks. This is senbero territory in its purest form, cheap enough that a couple of drinks and a few small plates rarely runs past a thousand or two thousand yen, and it's consistently ranked in Japanese media as one of the country's best neighborhoods for hashigo-zake, hopping between three or four different counters in a single night.

The bars themselves are mostly tiny, standing-room affairs run by a single owner, specializing in anything from yakitori and horumon to sashimi or oden, and the crowd is overwhelmingly local office workers rather than tourists. Unlike Dotonbori's polished, English-friendly food stalls, Tenma rewards curiosity: menus are handwritten and Japanese-only more often than not, and half the fun is pointing at whatever looks good or watching what the regulars order. Prices are posted and honest, and nobody expects a lengthy stay, which is exactly what makes it so easy to bar-hop here.

The best time to go is early evening on a weekday, when the after-work crowd fills in and the narrow lanes fill with the smell of grilling skewers. It's an easy add-on to a visit to the Tenjinbashisuji Shopping Street or Osaka Tenmangu Shrine, both a few minutes' walk south, and it offers a much more local, unfiltered version of Osaka's drinking culture than the bigger-name nightlife districts in Minami.

Tenma, Kita Ward, Osaka

Open in Google Maps

Immediately around JR Tenma Station (Osaka Loop Line), also a short walk from Minami-Morimachi Station (Osaka Metro Tanimachi/Sakaisuji Lines).
