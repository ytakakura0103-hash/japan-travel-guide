Ishinomori Manga Museum

Sendai

Shotaro Ishinomori, one of the most prolific and influential figures in postwar manga, was born in the Ishinomaki area, and this museum, built in a deliberately flying-saucer-shaped building on a small river island, celebrates his sprawling body of work, from the tokusatsu icon Kamen Rider to the science-fiction series Cyborg 009. Inside, exhibits recreate scenes from his manga in three dimensions, display decades of Kamen Rider mask designs side by side, and let visitors pose as a transformed hero at a dedicated photo setup.\n\nThe museum flooded badly in the March 2011 tsunami and closed for repairs, and its eventual reopening became a genuine point of local pride, a visible marker of the city working its way back. Walking from Ishinomaki Station to the museum, visitors pass a series of bronze character statues along what's been dubbed Manga Road, and a specially decorated train, wrapped in Ishinomori character art, sometimes runs the route as well.\n\nAdmission is a moderate, standard museum fee, and a visit runs about ninety minutes for fans, less for casual browsers. It's a comfortable day trip from Sendai and pairs naturally with a stop at Ishinomaki's waterfront or a ferry out to Tashirojima. Weekday visits are calmer than weekends, when families with kids fill the transformation photo area.

2-7 Nakase, Ishinomaki, Miyagi
