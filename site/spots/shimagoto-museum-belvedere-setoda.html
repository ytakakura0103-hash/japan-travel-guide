Shimagoto Museum & Belvedere Setoda

Hiroshima

Shimagoto Museum treats the entire island of Setoda as an open-air gallery, scattering sculptures and installations across the town as part of an ongoing biennale rather than confining art to a single building. The approach turns an ordinary walk or cycle through Setoda into something closer to a scavenger hunt for public art, with pieces tucked into everyday spots around the island rather than curated behind museum walls.

The most photographed pieces sit at Belvedere Setoda and the island's tourist information center, where oversized lemon and mikan-themed sculptures play on Setoda's identity as one of Japan's most productive citrus-growing regions, a playful, colorful counterpoint to the more contemplative art found elsewhere on the trail. That citrus branding ties neatly into Setoda's broader reputation for lemon products, including the gelato covered elsewhere in this guide.

Because the installations are spread across the island rather than concentrated in one place, exploring the full trail works best by bike, fitting naturally into a Shimanami Kaido cycling stop rather than requiring a dedicated separate visit. A map of current installation locations is generally available at the tourist information center itself.
