Local Pick

Kamagaki no Komichi

Nagoya

For generations, potters in Seto's old Hora district built up the walls, fences and foundations around their homes and workshops using worn-out kiln furniture, the saggars, supports and firing tools that wear out through repeated use and get discarded, rather than buying conventional building materials. Over time this practical habit produced Kamagaki no Komichi, a lane running nearly 400 meters where discarded kiln tools have been stacked into genuinely distinctive walls, a sight unique to this one street in Seto.

The lane itself follows what was once a main route for moving finished pottery through the town, and it is still lined with the homes of kiln-owning families along with small studios and galleries selling contemporary Seto ware. A gallery inside a converted late-Meiji-era kiln sits partway along the route, occasionally hosting local festivals where residents sell original pottery pieces and serve simple seasonal food to visitors.

Seto itself, one of Japan's Six Ancient Kilns and the origin of the generic Japanese word for ceramics, seto-mono, rarely appears on first-time Nagoya itineraries, which makes this walk feel like a genuine discovery rather than a checklist stop.

Hora-cho, Seto, Aichi
