Yamato Museum

Hiroshima

Kure was one of Imperial Japan's four main naval arsenals, and its shipyards built the Yamato, the largest and most heavily armed battleship ever completed, sunk by American aircraft in April 1945 with the loss of most of its crew. This museum, officially the Kure Maritime Museum, centers on a meticulously detailed 26.3-meter, 1:10-scale model of the ship, built using original blueprints, alongside exhibits on Kure's broader shipbuilding history and Japan's naval technology through the 20th century.\n\nThe exhibits don't shy away from the human cost of the war, with sections devoted to the lives of sailors and the scale of losses, giving the museum a more reflective tone than a simple showcase of engineering achievement.\n\nIt sits next door to the JMSDF Kure Museum with its decommissioned submarine, and the two make a natural pair — most visitors budget half a day for both, especially if arriving from Hiroshima city, which is a manageable day trip by train.

5-20 Takaramachi, Kure, Hiroshima

About a 5-minute walk from JR Kure Station; roughly 25–35 minutes from Hiroshima Station by direct JR train.
