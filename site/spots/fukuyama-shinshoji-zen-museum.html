Shinshoji Zen Museum and Gardens

Hiroshima

Shinshoji is a Rinzai Zen temple founded in 1965, but rather than presenting as a single historic structure, its roughly 230,000-square-meter grounds function more like a curated cultural park: a 17th-century temple hall relocated whole from Shiga Prefecture, a reconstructed teahouse based on the design principles of tea master Sen no Rikyu, and a temple administration building designed by architect Terunobu Fujimori around a red-pine theme, sitting alongside spaces for contemporary art exhibitions.

Visitors can join Zen meditation sessions or try sutra copying (shakyo), options that make this one of the more accessible places in the region to experience Buddhist practice directly rather than only viewing a temple from outside.

The mix of eras and disciplines — Edo-period architecture, modern design, contemporary art — makes Shinshoji unusual among temple visits in Hiroshima Prefecture, and its hillside setting outside central Fukuyama keeps it well clear of the crowds around the city's more famous castle.

91 Nakashinden, Fukuyama, Hiroshima
