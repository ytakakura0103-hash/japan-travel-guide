Adashino Nenbutsuji

Kyoto

The thousands of small stone Buddha statues and pagodas covering the grounds of Adashino Nenbutsuji weren't originally placed here as a single monument; they were gathered over centuries from unmarked graves scattered across the surrounding hills, remains of ordinary people from Kyoto's past who were buried here without markers or memorials because they couldn't afford them. The temple's founding is tied to this history, established as a place to console the countless unclaimed dead of the area, and the roughly 8,000 weathered stone figures now arranged across the grounds, some worn nearly featureless by time, stand as a collective memorial rather than individual grave markers. The effect is quietly different from Kyoto's more decorative temples, more contemplative than photogenic, though the site does draw photographers, particularly when moss covers the stones in the rainy season or when a candlelit evening event illuminates the statues each August. A visit typically takes half an hour to an hour. Because Adashino Nenbutsuji sits a fair walk beyond the busier core of Arashiyama, past the bamboo grove and the more crowded temples, it tends to see noticeably fewer visitors, and the walk there along the Saga-Toriimoto preserved street, lined with thatched-roof houses, is worthwhile in its own right. Photography of the statues is asked to be handled respectfully, given their function as memorials rather than decoration. Weekday visits outside the August illumination event are generally quiet.

17 Adashinocho, Ukyo Ward, Kyoto
