Kamigamo Shrine

Kyoto

Kamigamo Shrine, along with its sister shrine Shimogamo, is among the oldest shrines in Kyoto, with roots predating the city's founding as the imperial capital in the late 8th century, and it holds UNESCO World Heritage status as part of the wider Historic Monuments of Ancient Kyoto listing. Its most distinctive visual feature is a pair of cone-shaped sand mounds set in front of the main worship hall, said to represent the sacred mountain the shrine's deity is believed to have descended from, and rebuilt fresh from raked sand periodically.

The shrine is the setting for one leg of the Aoi Matsuri each May, one of Kyoto's three major annual festivals, when a procession in Heian-period court dress moves between the Imperial Palace and Kamigamo and Shimogamo shrines; the shrine grounds themselves also host archery and horseback rituals connected to the festival earlier in the month. Outside festival dates, the compact core grounds can be walked in around thirty minutes, with a wider precinct of streams and greenery worth a slower walk if time allows. Entry to the main grounds is free.

Being farther from central Kyoto than most sightseeing routes, Kamigamo Shrine draws a noticeably lighter crowd than the city's headline sights, aside from festival days, making it a comparatively peaceful stop for visitors interested in Kyoto's older Shinto history.

339 Kamigamo Motoyama, Kita Ward, Kyoto
