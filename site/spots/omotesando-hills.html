Omotesando Hills

Tokyo

Omotesando Hills was designed by architect Tadao Ando to sit at roughly the same height as the elegant 1927 Dojunkai Aoyama Apartments it replaced, a deliberate choice meant to preserve the low-rise, tree-shaded character of Omotesando's avenue even as the complex added several stories of retail space below ground. Inside, a gently sloped spiral ramp winds down through the building's atrium, connecting shop floors in a single continuous walk rather than separate levels reached by escalator — a layout that is as much the attraction as the boutiques themselves, and one of Tadao Ando's more widely cited commercial works.

The shops lean toward international luxury and contemporary fashion brands, so browsing rather than heavy purchasing is the norm for most visitors; thirty minutes to an hour covers a walk through the main spiral, longer with a coffee stop at one of the small cafes tucked into the complex. Weekday mornings are the calmest time to appreciate the architecture without crowds, since Omotesando's main avenue outside gets progressively busier through the afternoon and into the evening.

The building sits directly on Omotesando's famous zelkova-tree-lined street, which turns golden in autumn and is worth timing a visit around if you're drawn to seasonal color, and it is an easy stop to combine with Cat Street or a wider walk through Harajuku and Aoyama.
