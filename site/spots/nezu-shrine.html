Nezu Shrine

Tokyo

Nezu Shrine is one of Tokyo's older Shinto shrines, with a history that local tradition traces back many centuries, and its current buildings date from a major early 18th-century reconstruction commissioned by a shogun, giving it a well-preserved, largely uncommercialized atmosphere compared to some of the city's more famous shrines. The signature feature is a tunnel of roughly a hundred small vermilion torii gates leading up a slope, similar in spirit to the more famous gates at Kyoto's Fushimi Inari but on a much smaller and less crowded scale.

The shrine's azalea garden is the other main draw, planted with thousands of bushes across dozens of varieties that bloom in a concentrated window in April, when the shrine holds a dedicated azalea festival and the grounds fill with color, and with more visitors than usual, though even then it rarely reaches the intensity of Tokyo's most famous flower spots. Outside that window, the shrine is a quiet, contemplative visit.

A walk through the grounds, including the torii tunnel and garden, takes well under an hour, making it easy to combine with a walk through the nearby Yanaka and Nezu backstreets, an area known for its old-town atmosphere. The shrine is free to enter, though the azalea garden may charge a small fee during the peak blooming period. Early mornings and weekdays are the calmest times to visit, even during the festival.
