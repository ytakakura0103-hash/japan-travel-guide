Museum of Kyoto

Kyoto

The Museum of Kyoto traces the city's history from its founding as the Heian-era capital through to the present day, using a mix of models, reconstructed street scenes, and artifacts to cover everything from court culture and craft traditions to the city's transformation in the modern era. Part of the museum occupies a preserved former bank building completed in the early 20th century, its heavy red-brick and stone facade recognized as an Important Cultural Property and one of the more distinctive pieces of Meiji and Taisho-era architecture still standing in central Kyoto.

The permanent historical galleries are paired with a separate program of special exhibitions, which change periodically and can range from art and design shows to more focused historical topics, usually requiring a separate ticket from the permanent collection. A visit to the permanent exhibits alone takes around an hour to ninety minutes; the historical streetscape reconstruction area, built to resemble an Edo-period Kyoto street, is a particular highlight for visitors interested in the city's older townscape rather than just its temples.

The museum is centrally located near Karasuma-Oike and tends to be considerably quieter than Kyoto's headline sights, making it a comfortable option at almost any time of day. It's a useful stop for getting historical context before or after visiting the city's older neighborhoods and temples.

Sanjo-Takakura, Nakagyo Ward, Kyoto
