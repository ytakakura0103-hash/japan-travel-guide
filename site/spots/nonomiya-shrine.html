Nonomiya Shrine

Kyoto

Nonomiya Shrine sits just before the entrance to the Arashiyama bamboo grove, marked by an unusual black torii gate made of unpainted wood rather than the vermilion typical of most Shinto shrines, a style tied to its historical role. Before the Heian period and for centuries after, unmarried imperial princesses selected to serve as high priestess at Ise Shrine would spend a period of ritual purification here before departing for Ise, and the shrine still carries associations with purity, marriage, and safe childbirth as a result of that history.

The shrine itself is small, tucked into a mossy, tree-shaded plot that feels notably quieter and cooler than the open paths nearby, and can be seen in about ten minutes. There's no fee to enter, and its position directly at the bamboo grove's entrance means it's easy to visit almost by accident on the way through rather than as a separate stop.

Because most visitors are moving quickly toward the bamboo grove itself, Nonomiya tends to see less lingering foot traffic even when the grove beyond it is crowded, making it a small, calm pause near a busy part of Arashiyama. It pairs naturally with the bamboo grove and Tenryuji Temple, both immediately adjacent.

Sagano area, Ukyo Ward, Kyoto
