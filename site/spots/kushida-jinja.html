Kushida Shrine

Fukuoka

Kushida Shrine has watched over the merchants of Hakata for well over a thousand years, and it remains the spiritual anchor of the neighborhood rather than a museum piece. Its most photographed feature is a towering, elaborately decorated float called a kazariyamakasa, kept on permanent display near the entrance so visitors can appreciate the craftsmanship behind Hakata Gion Yamakasa even outside festival season.

That festival, held every July, culminates in teams racing one-ton floats through the streets at dawn, and the shrine is the finish line. If a trip happens to land in early July, the practice runs and float-building activity around the shrine are worth seeking out; outside that window, the grounds are calm enough for an unhurried look at the old ginkgo tree said to be over a thousand years old and the small shrine buildings tucked around the main hall.

The shrine sits inside the old Hakata merchant district, a short walk from Canal City and Gion Station, and pairs naturally with a visit to Tocho-ji and the nearby Hakata Machiya Folk Museum. Unlike Dazaifu Tenmangu, it rarely feels overwhelmed by tour groups, making it a comparatively relaxed stop even on weekends. New Year's Eve and the first days of January are the busy exception, when locals queue for hatsumode.

1-41 Kamikawabata-machi, Hakata-ku, Fukuoka

Open in Google Maps

About 5 minutes on foot from Gion Station (Kuko Line), or a short walk from Canal City Hakata and Nakasu-Kawabata Station.
