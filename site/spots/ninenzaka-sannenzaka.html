Ninenzaka & Sannenzaka

Kyoto

These two adjoining stone-paved lanes descend the hillside below Kiyomizu-dera, their names referring to their approximate lengths in old Japanese units. Wooden machiya townhouses that once housed local residents now hold tea shops, pottery stores, and stalls selling regional snacks, and the low buildings and lack of overhead wires give the streets one of the more convincing "old Kyoto" atmospheres left in the city, which is why they're used often in period dramas and photography.

The lanes aren't sights with a single defined stop; the point is to walk them slowly, ducking into shops or a tea house along the way, so a visit can be as short as fifteen minutes or stretch to an hour or more depending on how many stops you make. There's no admission fee to walk the streets themselves. Because they sit directly on the route up to Kiyomizu-dera, they're busiest at the same times as the temple, particularly late morning through mid-afternoon; visiting early, or coming down from the temple in the late afternoon as day-trip crowds thin, tends to be calmer.

The area is also popular for kimono rental, so visitors dressed in rented kimono are a common sight here, adding to the old-town feel. Cherry blossom and autumn foliage seasons bring some of the heaviest crowds of the year.
