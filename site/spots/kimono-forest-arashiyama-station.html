Kimono Forest at Randen Arashiyama Station

Kyoto

Randen Arashiyama Station's approach is lined with more than six hundred acrylic poles, each wrapped in a different Kyo-yuzen dyeing pattern sourced from Kyoto's textile industry, creating a dense corridor of color that's become one of Arashiyama's most consistently photographed spots regardless of season. Unlike most of the district's famous sights, which depend on specific light or foliage timing, the Kimono Forest is lit and accessible at all hours, making it as striking after dark as during the day.

A small reflecting pond, Ryu no Atago Pond, sits within the installation and is specifically positioned to catch symmetrical reflections of the surrounding poles, a detail that's driven much of its recent social media popularity as visitors line up specific shots. Because it's built directly into a working train station rather than a separate paid attraction, there's no admission fee and no set visiting hours, and it's genuinely free to walk through even without boarding a train.

Its location right at the station makes it a natural first or last stop on an Arashiyama visit, easily combined with the nearby Bamboo Grove, Togetsukyo Bridge, or Tenryuji Temple. Weekday mornings tend to be quieter for photos, while the approach can get crowded during peak cherry blossom and autumn foliage season when Arashiyama draws its largest visitor numbers.
