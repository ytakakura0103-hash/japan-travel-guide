Local Pick

Sendai Asaichi (Morning Market)

Sendai

Sendai's morning market grew out of informal postwar street stalls and has stayed in roughly the same spot ever since, a narrow covered arcade packed with maybe fifty small shops selling fresh produce, seafood, pickles, dried goods, and ready-to-eat snacks. It's a working market rather than a curated tourist attraction, so the atmosphere is brisk and unpolished: vendors calling out prices, regulars chatting with shopkeepers they've known for decades, crates of vegetables stacked on the narrow walkway.

For visitors, it's a good place to try Sendai specialties in bite-sized, affordable form, from fresh zunda (sweet mashed edamame) sweets to grilled seafood skewers and simple rice bowls, without committing to a sit-down restaurant. Some stalls sell breakfast sets aimed at market workers and early risers, which are usually filling and cheap.

There's no admission cost, obviously, and most food items are inexpensive. The market's energy is highest right after opening in the early morning, when the produce is freshest and the crowd is mostly locals rather than other tourists; by late morning some stalls start closing up. It's an easy add-on to a Sendai Station visit if you're an early riser.

Chuo 3-chome, Aoba-ku, Sendai, Miyagi

A 5-minute walk from Sendai Station's west exit.
