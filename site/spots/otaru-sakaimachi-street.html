Sakaimachi Street

Sapporo

Sakaimachi was Otaru's main commercial street during the city's early 20th-century boom years as a banking and trading hub, sometimes nicknamed the Wall Street of Hokkaido at the time. Many of the stone and brick buildings from that era survive and have been converted into shops selling Otaru's traditional specialties: hand-blown glassware, fresh seafood and dried goods, and a cluster of well-known confectionery shops including LeTAO, known for its cheesecake, whose flagship store anchors much of the street's dessert-shopping reputation.

Walking the full length of the street from the canal to the music box museum takes about 15-20 minutes without stops, though most visitors take considerably longer given how many small shops invite browsing along the way. It's a good source of souvenirs that feel more specific to Otaru than generic Hokkaido gift-shop items.

Weekend afternoons and any day cruise ships are docked nearby bring the heaviest crowds; a weekday morning walk is noticeably calmer and lets shops be browsed without jostling. Many of the glass and confectionery shops offer warm indoor spaces, which makes the street a comfortable option even on a cold Otaru winter day, when the canal walk outside can be a bracing experience.
