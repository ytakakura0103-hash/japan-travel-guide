Takashimaya Osaka (Namba)

Osaka

Takashimaya traces its origins to a Kyoto kimono shop founded in 1831, and its Namba store, opened in 1932, grew into the largest-scale store in the entire Takashimaya Group, occupying a substantial 78,000-square-meter site directly connected to Namba Station. The building survived the firebombing of Osaka during the Second World War and later served as a logistics center during the postwar occupation period, giving it a layered history beyond its role as a retail flagship.

A full renovation completed in March 2011 modernized the interior while preserving the store's early-Showa architectural character, making it, alongside Daimaru Shinsaibashi, one of Osaka's clearest examples of a historic department store building still in active daily use. Inside, expect the full range typical of a flagship Takashimaya: extensive luxury and fashion floors, a well-regarded basement depachika food hall, and broad household and gift departments.

Its direct connection to Namba Station, one of the busiest transit hubs in the city, makes it an easy stop before or after exploring Dotonbori, Shinsaibashi, or the bar-hopping alleys of Ura-Namba, all within easy walking distance. The scale of the building rewards setting aside real time if department-store browsing or the food hall is a priority, rather than treating it as a quick stop.

5-1-5 Namba, Chuo Ward, Osaka
