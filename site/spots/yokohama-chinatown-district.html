Yokohama Chinatown

Yokohama

Chinatown took shape after Yokohama's port opened to foreign trade in 1859, when Chinese merchants and their staff settled just outside the formal foreign concession and built what is now one of the largest Chinatowns in the world. Today it packs several hundred restaurants and shops into a compact grid of streets, ranging from high-end banquet halls to stand-up counters selling pork buns and small skewers to eat on the move.

Because the food ranges so widely in price and formality, it helps to have a rough plan rather than wandering in hungry at peak lunchtime, when the most famous restaurants can mean long waits. Side streets one or two blocks off the main road tend to have shorter lines and comparable food to the busiest storefronts, and late afternoon on a weekday is generally the calmest time to walk through.

Beyond eating, the district holds several working temples, including one dedicated to the warrior deity Guan Yu and another to the sea goddess Mazu, both worth a look even for visitors who came only for the food. Lunar New Year, usually in late January or February, brings lion dance parades and much larger crowds than a typical weekend.
