Daisho-in Temple

Hiroshima

Daisho-in is traditionally considered one of the oldest and most important temples on Miyajima, associated with the same Kobo Daishi tradition tied to Mount Misen, and it once served as a temple of significant standing within Japanese Shingon Buddhism. It sits at the base of Mount Misen, a short uphill walk from Itsukushima Shrine, and unlike the shrine's open, waterborne layout, Daisho-in's grounds are dense and layered, built up the hillside with stairways, halls, and garden paths.

One of its most recognizable features is a stone stairway lined with metal prayer wheels inscribed with sutras, which visitors can spin by hand while climbing, said to confer the same merit as reading the text inside. The temple grounds also include a cave hall containing statues representing the 88 temples of the Shikoku pilgrimage, letting visitors symbolically complete that famous pilgrimage route in miniature within a single building, along with numerous smaller Jizo statues, some dressed in knitted caps left by visitors.

Because it draws far fewer visitors than Itsukushima Shrine despite being only a short walk away, Daisho-in tends to be a noticeably calmer stop, and its varied, close-quarters layout rewards slower exploration rather than a quick pass-through.

210 Miyajima-cho, Hatsukaichi, Hiroshima Prefecture

About a 20-minute walk from Miyajima pier, near the base of the Mount Misen ropeway route.
