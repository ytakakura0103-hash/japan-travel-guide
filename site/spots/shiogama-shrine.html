Shiogama Shrine

Sendai

Shiogama Shrine held the top religious ranking, ichinomiya, within the historical Mutsu Province, a huge region covering most of what is now northeastern Japan, and it's still treated as one of Tohoku's most important shrines for matters of safe seafaring and, somewhat unusually, safe childbirth. The main approach is a long, steep flight of 202 stone steps, a workout that most visitors feel in their legs by the top but that also thins out the more casual crowd before the main hall.

The shrine complex actually houses two shrines under one roof, dedicated to different deities connected to navigation and the sea, reflecting Shiogama's long history as an important port town. Beyond the religious significance, the grounds offer a solid elevated view over the harbor and toward Matsushima Bay in the distance, a good orientation point for the wider area.

There's no admission fee to visit. The stairway and grounds are especially popular in early-to-mid April, when the cherry trees lining the steps bloom, drawing local crowds who come specifically for hanami; visiting outside cherry season, or early on a weekday, gives a much quieter climb and a calmer main hall.
