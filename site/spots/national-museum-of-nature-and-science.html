National Museum of Nature and Science

Tokyo

This is Tokyo's main natural history and science museum, spread across two connected buildings inside Ueno Park, and it's built to appeal as much to curious families as to anyone interested in the history of Japanese science. The Japan Gallery covers the country's natural environment and the development of science and technology domestically, while the Global Gallery focuses on natural history more broadly, including a well-known collection of dinosaur skeletons and other large fossil specimens that tend to be the highlight for younger visitors.

Exhibits are generally hands-on where possible, with interactive displays and models aimed at making scientific concepts approachable for children without being too simplified for adults. Because much of the museum is indoor and covers a lot of varied ground, it's a reliable choice on a rainy day in Ueno when outdoor sightseeing is less appealing.

A visit through both buildings at a reasonable pace takes a couple of hours, and families with younger children often end up spending longer given how much there is to interact with. The museum can get busy with school groups on weekday mornings during the academic year, so a weekday afternoon or a calmer weekend morning may actually work better depending on the season. Its position inside Ueno Park makes it easy to pair with the zoo or other nearby museums.

7-20 Uenokoen, Taito City, Tokyo
