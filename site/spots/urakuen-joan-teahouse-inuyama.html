Urakuen Garden and Joan Teahouse

Nagoya

The teahouse at the heart of Urakuen Garden, known as Joan, was built around 1618 for Oda Urakusai, a younger brother of Oda Nobunaga and a disciple of the legendary tea master Sen no Rikyu. It originally stood at Kennin-ji temple in Kyoto and is regarded as one of the three finest surviving teahouses in Japan, a status confirmed when it was designated a National Treasure in 1951. Over the following decades the structure was relocated first to Tokyo and then to Kanagawa before finally settling in Inuyama in 1972, just east of the castle.

Joan is a study in deliberate restraint: a low wooden-shingle roof, walls of clay-covered bamboo lattice, and a tiny crawl-through entrance called a nijiriguchi designed to strip away rank and formality before guests enter the two-and-a-half-tatami tea room inside. The garden surrounding it was laid out to complement that same understated aesthetic, with stone paths and plantings meant to be experienced slowly rather than photographed in passing.

Because the teahouse itself is generally viewed from outside rather than entered, a visit here is more about the garden setting and the history behind the structure than a hands-on tea ceremony, though seasonal tea events are sometimes held on the grounds.
