Nihombashi Takashimaya

Tokyo

Takashimaya opened its Tokyo Nihombashi store on the current site in 1933, and in 2009 it became the first department store building in Japan to be designated a national Important Cultural Property, recognized as the largest and best-preserved store of its era still standing largely as originally built. Both the exterior and much of the interior retain their early Showa-period character, a rarity among Japanese department stores, most of which have been rebuilt or heavily renovated since.

Details worth looking for include the heavy iron doors at the main entrance and, on certain floors, elevators still operated manually by uniformed attendants rather than automated buttons, small touches that give the store a noticeably different feel from the more modern annexes surrounding it. The complex has since expanded into a larger shopping center incorporating newer buildings, but the original 1933 structure remains the centerpiece and the reason architecture-minded visitors make a point of stopping in.

As with the other historic department stores in this guide, a visit doesn't require any intention to shop; walking the original building's ground floor and stairwells is free and gives a sense of pre-war Japanese commercial architecture that's increasingly hard to find intact elsewhere in central Tokyo.
