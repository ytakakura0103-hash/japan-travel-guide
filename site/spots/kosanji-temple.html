Kosanji Temple and Hill of Hope

Hiroshima

Kosanji was built starting in 1936 by a local businessman as a memorial temple dedicated to his mother, and construction continued for more than three decades, with individual halls modeled after or inspired by famous temples and shrines elsewhere in Japan, including structures echoing Nikko's Yomeimon gate and Kyoto's Byodo-in. The result is less a single traditional temple than an assembled collection of replica and original halls, gates, and a museum of Buddhist art, all on one hillside site in the small town of Setoda.

Behind the temple, a separate and more recent addition called the Hill of Hope, or Miraishin no Oka, spreads across roughly 5,000 square meters of white marble sculpture and terracing, all carved from around 3,000 tonnes of Carrara marble imported from Italy. Designed and built over sixteen years by Hiroshima-born sculptor Kazuto Kuetani, the garden was opened to the public in 2000 and has no formal religious function, existing purely as a large-scale sculptural artwork with sweeping views over the town and the Seto Inland Sea from its highest terraces.

The combination of dense, colorful traditional-style temple halls followed by an almost entirely white, modern marble garden makes for a fairly unusual visit, and it has become one of the signature stops for cyclists and drivers passing through Setoda on the Shimanami Kaido.

553-2 Setoda-cho Sawa, Onomichi, Hiroshima Prefecture

Open in Google Maps

About 5-minute walk from Setoda port on Ikuchijima island; roughly 1 to 1.5 hours from Onomichi by direct bus, or reachable via the Shimanami Kaido cycling route.
