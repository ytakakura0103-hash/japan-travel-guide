Local Pick

Ken and Mary Tree

Hokkaido

This lone poplar earned its name after appearing in a Nissan Skyline television commercial in the 1970s featuring a couple nicknamed Ken and Mary, and the tree has stayed a minor pilgrimage stop for fans of the ad and for photographers drawn to its solitary shape against the rolling Biei farmland. Unlike the Blue Pond or Farm Tomita, there is essentially nothing here beyond the tree itself and a small roadside pull-off, which is precisely the appeal for visitors who have already seen Biei's bigger, more developed attractions.

It sits along the same general loop as the Patchwork Road's other famous trees, so it's usually visited as one stop among several rather than a dedicated destination on its own, best reached with a rental car or bicycle given the lack of public transit this far into the farmland.

Because it has no ticket booth, gift shop or parking lot to speak of, crowds are rarely a real issue here even in peak summer, though a handful of other cars may be pulled over at the same time on a nice day. The tree, like the surrounding fields, looks markedly different by season, from full summer leaf cover to a bare winter silhouette against snow, and it remains on private farmland, so photographs should be taken respectfully from the road rather than by walking into the field.

Biei area, Kamikawa-gun, Hokkaido
