Local Pick

Iseyama Kotaijingu Shrine

Yokohama

When Yokohama's port opened and the city grew rapidly around it, civic leaders felt the young settlement needed a dedicated guardian shrine, and this hilltop complex, established in 1870 and dedicated to the sun goddess Amaterasu, was built explicitly as a Yokohama counterpart to the great Ise Grand Shrine, hence its lasting nickname among locals. It has served as the city's principal Shinto shrine ever since, hosting major seasonal festivals and New Year's visits drawing large local crowds.

The elevated site gives it a pleasant, breezy setting away from street noise, with a tree-lined approach and a modest but well-kept shrine hall, and cherry trees along the grounds draw a small hanami crowd each spring that is almost entirely local rather than tourist. Unlike Kamakura's famous shrines, there is no entrance fee and no ticketed inner sanctuary to queue for.

Because it sits a short but uphill walk from Sakuragicho and Nogeyama, most visitors bundling a Minato Mirai and Chinatown itinerary skip it entirely, even though it offers a more authentic sense of everyday Shinto practice than the shrines inside Chinatown built for a different community. Early morning visits catch it at its quietest and most contemplative.

56 Miyazaki-cho, Nishi-ku, Yokohama

Open in Google Maps

About 10 minutes on foot from JR/Yokohama Municipal Subway Sakuragicho Station.
