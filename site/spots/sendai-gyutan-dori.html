Sendai Gyutan-dori (Beef Tongue Street)

Sendai

Grilled beef tongue, or gyutan, became Sendai's signature dish almost by accident: after the war, a local restaurateur started grilling the tongue cuts that Western-style butchers left over, seasoning them simply with salt, and the style stuck as a distinctly Sendai specialty. Today, a concentration of gyutan restaurants sits in and around the underground shopping level near Sendai Station, often collectively referred to as Gyutan-dori, alongside standalone shops scattered through downtown.\n\nA typical set meal pairs thinly sliced grilled tongue with barley rice, oxtail soup, and pickles, a combination that's become the standard way to order it almost everywhere in the city. The texture is springier and leaner than most people expect from tongue, and the char from the grill is central to the flavor.\n\nExpect a moderate price for a proper set meal, more than a quick lunch but not extravagant. The most famous shops near the station routinely have queues at standard lunch and dinner hours; going at an off-peak time, such as mid-afternoon between 2 and 4pm, or choosing a slightly less famous shop a few blocks from the station, cuts the wait dramatically without much drop in quality.
