Higashi Honganji

Kyoto

Higashi Honganji sits so close to Kyoto Station that it's often the first temple visitors see on arrival, and the scale of its Founder's Hall can still catch people off guard even after photos. At 76 by 58 meters, the hall ranks among the largest wooden structures anywhere, and stepping inside past the wide wooden veranda gives a real sense of the size, particularly the huge roof beams overhead. The temple, headquarters of the Otani-ha branch of Jodo Shinshu Buddhism, was rebuilt in the late 19th century after a series of fires, and some of the massive support beams were reportedly hauled into place using ropes woven from human hair donated by devoted parishioners, a detail still recounted by temple guides. Admission to the main halls is free, which makes it an easy, low-commitment stop, and most visitors spend well under an hour here, more if they also walk through the Shosei-en garden a short distance to the east. Because it sits right off the main station approach, tour groups tend to pass through in the late morning; arriving soon after opening or later in the afternoon usually means a quieter hall to yourself. There's little shade directly around the main buildings, so midday visits in summer can be uncomfortably hot. With its central location, Higashi Honganji works well as a first or last stop when connecting to other sights near the station, including Kyoto Tower and Nishi Honganji a bit further west.
