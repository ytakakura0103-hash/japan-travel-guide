Cup Noodles Museum Osaka Ikeda

Osaka

Instant ramen was invented in Ikeda by Momofuku Ando in the late 1950s, and this museum, built on the site associated with that history, tells the story through exhibits on Ando's development process before moving into its main draw: a make-your-own cup noodle workshop where visitors design a custom cup, choose from a wide range of soup bases and toppings, and walk away with a sealed, personalised product. A separate area lets visitors make fresh noodles from scratch by hand for those wanting a more involved, longer activity beyond the cup noodle customisation.

The full experience, including both the historical exhibits and a workshop session, typically takes around two hours, and workshop participation may require a small material fee along with same-day ticketing that can run out on busy days. Weekends and school holidays draw considerably larger crowds, particularly for workshop slots, so arriving close to opening time improves the chances of securing a spot without a long wait.

Regular museum admission covering the historical exhibits is free, with the workshops charged separately. This is one of the more engaging family-oriented museums in the Osaka area, appealing to both children and adults with an interest in product design or Japanese food history. The museum sits a short walk from Ikeda Station and can be combined with a visit to the nearby Duskin Museum, which runs a similar hands-on format focused on doughnuts, for a fuller half-day of workshop-style museums in Ikeda.

8-25 Masumicho, Ikeda, Osaka
