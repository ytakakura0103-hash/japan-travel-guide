Atomic Bomb Dome (Genbaku Dome)

Hiroshima

Before the bombing, this building was the Hiroshima Prefectural Industrial Promotion Hall, a European-style exhibition space designed by Czech architect Jan Letzel and completed in 1915. Because the blast struck almost directly overhead, the force pushed down rather than sideways, leaving the central dome's iron frame and sections of brick wall standing while nearly everyone inside was killed instantly.

In the years after the war, opinion in Hiroshima was divided over whether to demolish the ruin or preserve it. The city eventually chose preservation, and the structure has been stabilized with reinforcing work rather than restored, so it still reads as a wound rather than a monument. It became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1996, listed specifically as a reminder of the destructive power of nuclear weapons rather than as conventional architectural heritage.

Most visitors view it from across the Motoyasu River on the Peace Memorial Park side, where the ruin, the river, and the modern city skyline behind it sit in the same frame. There is no admission and no set viewing hours, since the site is a preserved ruin rather than a museum, but it is lit at night and forms part of the same walking route as the Peace Memorial Museum and Children's Peace Monument.

1-10 Otemachi, Naka Ward, Hiroshima, Hiroshima Prefecture

5-minute walk from Genbaku Dome-mae tram stop (Hiroshima Electric Railway lines 2 and 6), or about 25 minutes by tram from Hiroshima Station.
