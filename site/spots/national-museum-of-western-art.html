National Museum of Western Art

Tokyo

The National Museum of Western Art occupies a building designed by the modernist architect Le Corbusier, one of a small group of his works recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The collection began as the private holdings of Kojiro Matsukata, a Japanese industrialist who acquired a large number of European paintings and sculptures in the early 20th century; after a complicated history that saw part of the collection seized in France during the war, it was eventually returned to Japan and formed the basis of this museum.

The permanent collection focuses on European art from the medieval period through the early 20th century, with particular strength in Impressionist and Post-Impressionist painting, including works by Monet and Renoir, and an extensive group of Rodin sculptures, some of which stand in the museum's forecourt, including a cast of The Thinker visible before you even buy a ticket. Special exhibitions, often major loan shows from European institutions, are held in a separate space and ticketed separately from the permanent collection.

The permanent collection can be seen at a reasonable pace in about an hour to ninety minutes; special exhibitions add more time and tend to draw larger crowds, especially on weekends. Because it sits inside Ueno Park, it combines easily with the park's other museums and the zoo for a longer cultural day out.

7-7 Uenokoen, Taito City, Tokyo
