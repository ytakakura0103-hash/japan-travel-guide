Tsukishima Monja Street

Tokyo

Tsukishima's main street, often called Monja Street, is lined with roughly eighty restaurants specializing in monjayaki, a Tokyo dish related to Osaka's better-known okonomiyaki but with a much runnier batter that's cooked and eaten directly off a hot iron griddle built into the table. The cooking process is part of the appeal: ingredients are mixed and cooked tableside, often with staff on hand to help first-timers get the technique right, since monjayaki is trickier to cook than its firmer okonomiyaki cousin and is eaten with a small flat spatula rather than a fork.

Because monjayaki is inherently a shared, hands-on dish best enjoyed slowly, meals here tend to run longer than a typical casual restaurant visit, and the street is especially popular with groups of friends or coworkers rather than solo diners. Restaurant quality and specialties vary block by block, with some shops known for classic recipes and others for more elaborate ingredient combinations, so it's worth a little research or a look at a few menus before committing to one.

The street is busiest in the evening, particularly on weekends, when reservations or a short wait are common at popular shops; a weekday evening or an earlier dinner time is more likely to get you seated quickly. Tsukishima itself is a quieter, mostly residential island neighborhood, making the food street feel like a distinct destination rather than part of a larger sightseeing route.
