Daisho-in Temple

Hiroshima

Daisho-in predates Itsukushima Shrine's current fame and was historically the more important religious center on the island, serving as a training temple within the Shingon Buddhist sect. Its entrance staircase is lined with metal sutra wheels that visitors spin as they climb — turning one is said to carry the same merit as reading the sutra inside — and the grounds beyond hold a maze of smaller halls, statues, and a cave hall lit by hundreds of lanterns.\n\nBecause most day-trippers head straight from the ferry to the floating torii gate and back, Daisho-in stays comparatively uncrowded even in peak season, despite being only a short walk away and free to enter.\n\nIt also functions as the starting point for the Daisho-in hiking course up Mount Misen, generally considered the most scenic of the mountain's three trails, so it's worth visiting either as a standalone stop or as the first leg of a longer hike.

210 Miyajimacho, Hatsukaichi, Hiroshima

About a 20-minute walk from Miyajima Pier, past Itsukushima Shrine.
