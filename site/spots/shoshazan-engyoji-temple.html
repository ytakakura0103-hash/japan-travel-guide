Shoshazan Engyoji Temple

Kobe

Founded in the late 10th century, Engyoji spreads across the wooded summit of Mt. Shosha outside Himeji, its main hall and surrounding structures built on raised wooden platforms in a style reminiscent of Kyoto's Kiyomizudera, though on a far quieter scale. The temple's atmospheric halls, connected by covered walkways through the forest, were used as filming locations for the 2003 film "The Last Samurai," which brought a wave of international attention without fundamentally changing the site's low-key character.

A ropeway carries visitors most of the way up the mountain, with a further walk of twenty to thirty minutes through cedar forest to reach the main temple buildings, a walk that itself feels like a step back into feudal-era Japan given the dense tree cover and worn stone paths. The Maniden main hall's elevated wooden stage offers views out over the forested valley below.

Because it requires a train ride from Kobe followed by a bus and ropeway, Engyoji sees a fraction of the visitors of Himeji Castle just down the mountain, and even fewer make the extra effort to walk further into the complex's inner halls, which stay genuinely peaceful most days.

Mt. Shosha, Himeji, Hyogo Prefecture

From Sannomiya or Kobe Station, take the JR Special Rapid service to Himeji (roughly 40-50 minutes), then a bus to the Shosha ropeway base station, followed by the ropeway and a walk to the temple.
