Byodo-in Temple

Kyoto

Byodo-in was built in the mid-11th century as a nobleman's retreat before being converted into a Buddhist temple, and its Phoenix Hall is one of the few wooden structures from the Heian period still standing largely as originally built. Its symmetrical wings and rooftop bronze phoenixes are recognizable enough that the building appears on Japan's 10-yen coin, and the same reflecting pond in front of it, designed to represent the Buddhist Pure Land, remains the temple's signature view. The hall's interior, including a large seated Amida Buddha statue and painted wooden doors, can be seen on a separate timed, limited-capacity tour for an additional fee beyond general admission.

The temple grounds also include a modern museum built partly underground that displays some of the original bell and phoenix ornaments alongside detailed models, giving useful context that's easy to miss if you only see the exterior. A visit covering the grounds and museum takes around an hour, longer with the hall interior tour.

Uji is a day-trip destination from central Kyoto, and Byodo-in tends to be quieter than the city's headline sights simply due to the extra travel time involved; weekday mornings are especially calm. The temple and surrounding area are also known for tea, since Uji is one of Japan's most historic green tea-growing regions.

116 Uji Renge, Uji, Kyoto
