JMSDF Kure Museum (Iron Whale Museum)

Hiroshima

The JMSDF Kure Museum, widely known by its nickname "Iron Whale Museum," opened in 2007 to explain the history and work of the Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force, with particular focus on submarine operations and coastal minesweeping, both closely tied to Kure's role as a naval base city. Its centerpiece is the Akishio, a Yushio-class diesel-electric submarine launched in 1985 and decommissioned in 2004, which was brought ashore and installed as a walk-through exhibit rather than simply displayed from the outside.

Visitors can walk through much of the submarine's original interior, including crew berths, the captain's quarters, and operational spaces, largely preserved as they were when the vessel was in active service, making it one of the only places in Japan where the public can enter a former Japanese navy submarine. Indoor galleries cover the JMSDF's postwar formation and its minesweeping work clearing wartime ordnance from Japanese waters, a lesser-known but significant part of the country's postwar reconstruction.

Admission is free, reflecting its status as a public defense-related facility rather than a private museum, and an on-site cafe serves a curry recipe based on the type served aboard JMSDF vessels. It sits directly across from the Yamato Museum, and the two are typically visited together.

5-32 Takaramachi, Kure, Hiroshima Prefecture
