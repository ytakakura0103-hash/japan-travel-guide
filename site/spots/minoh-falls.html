Minoh Falls

Osaka

Minoh Falls sits at the far end of a gently sloped forest trail that follows a stream out of Minoh town, a walk of around forty minutes that passes waterside teahouses, small shrines and, in autumn, some of the most reliably vivid maple foliage in the Osaka area, which has helped the falls earn a place among Japan's officially recognised top one hundred waterfalls. The 33-metre drop itself is not enormous by national standards, but the setting, a narrow gorge shaded by dense forest, gives it an atmosphere that feels well outside a metropolitan area of Osaka's size, despite being reachable within an hour of the city centre.

The walk to the falls and back takes roughly ninety minutes at a comfortable pace, not counting time at the falls themselves or stops along the way, and the path is largely flat and paved, suitable for most fitness levels though not ideal for wheelchairs given some uneven sections. Autumn weekends, particularly when the leaves peak in November, draw significant crowds along the trail and at the falls, so a weekday visit or an early morning start is more comfortable during that season.

Outside autumn, the walk is far quieter and pleasant in its own right, with cooler shade in summer. There's no admission fee to walk the trail or view the falls, though some teahouses along the way charge for food and rest stops. The walk begins a short distance from Minoh Station, making the whole outing accessible without a car.
