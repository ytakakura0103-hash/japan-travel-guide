Kurodake Ropeway

Sapporo

Mt. Kurodake is one of the more accessible peaks within Daisetsuzan National Park, and the ropeway from Sounkyo Onsen, followed by a connecting chairlift, carries visitors most of the way up without requiring technical mountaineering experience, leaving a manageable hiking trail to the actual summit for those who want to continue on foot. The terrain at the top opens into genuine alpine tundra, a stark contrast to the forested gorge below.

Daisetsuzan is known among hikers as home to some of Japan's earliest autumn foliage, since the alpine zone changes color well before lowland Hokkaido, often by early-to-mid September, which draws a dedicated but relatively niche crowd of foliage chasers rather than the general tourist traffic that floods Biei or Jozankei later in the season.

Because reaching Sounkyo already requires a longer trip out from Sapporo, and the ropeway itself serves a genuinely outdoor-oriented crowd rather than casual sightseers, lines and crowding are generally much lighter than at more famous ropeways like Mt. Hakodate or Mt. Moiwa, even during the short peak foliage window.

Summer offers wildflower blooms across the alpine terrain and the longest hiking window, while the ropeway also runs in winter for backcountry skiing and snowboarding access, though weather closures are common in the coldest months.

Sounkyo Onsen, Kamikawa-cho, Kamikawa District, Hokkaido 078-1701
