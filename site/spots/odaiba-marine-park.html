Odaiba Marine Park & Rainbow Bridge

Tokyo

Odaiba Marine Park sits on Tokyo Bay's artificial waterfront, with a stretch of imported sand built up to resemble a beach, facing directly across the water toward central Tokyo. A scaled-down replica of the Statue of Liberty stands near the shoreline, originally installed as a temporary exhibit decades ago and kept on permanently after it proved popular, and it's become one of the area's signature photo spots, framed against the Rainbow Bridge and the city skyline behind it.

Swimming in the bay itself is generally discouraged due to water quality, so the beach functions more as a promenade and viewpoint than a swimming spot, with paths running along the waterfront good for walking or cycling at any time of day. The most popular time to visit is dusk, when the sun sets behind the city skyline and the Rainbow Bridge's lights switch on, giving the bay a completely different character from its daytime look; this transition period tends to draw the largest crowds, particularly on weekends and around holidays.

The park is free to enter, with no fixed time needed for a visit; many people combine a stop here with dinner or shopping at one of Odaiba's nearby malls before or after watching the sunset. Because the area is spread out and connected by the elevated Yurikamome train line, the ride itself offers good bay views even before arriving.
