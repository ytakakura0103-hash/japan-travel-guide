Akihabara Electric Town

Tokyo

Akihabara grew out of a cluster of postwar electronics shops into what is now widely considered the center of Japan's otaku (geek) culture, and the district still layers both identities on top of each other. Multi-storey buildings packed with everything from vintage computer parts to the latest gadgets sit alongside equally tall buildings devoted to anime, manga, trading cards, and collectible figures, with stores like Mandarake spread across several connected floors. Retro video game arcades, some still running decades-old cabinets alongside newer rhythm and crane games, are tucked into side streets throughout the area.

Maid cafes, where costumed staff serve food with a theatrical, exaggerated politeness, are one of Akihabara's best-known novelties; they range from tourist-friendly introductory experiences to more niche, long-running establishments, and prices and formats vary a lot between them, so it's worth checking what a particular cafe offers before committing. The main street, Chuo-dori, closes to cars on Sunday afternoons, similar to Ginza, which makes for an easier walk through the district.

Akihabara is busy most days but especially on weekends, when both shoppers and cosplay photographers fill the main street. A weekday visit, particularly in the morning, is noticeably quieter and better suited to actually browsing the shops rather than just moving with the crowd. Budget at least two to three hours if you plan to go into more than a couple of the specialty stores, since many reward a slow browse rather than a quick pass.
