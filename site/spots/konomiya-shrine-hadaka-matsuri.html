Konomiya Shrine and the Hadaka Matsuri

Nagoya

Konomiya Shrine, formally Owari Okunitama Shrine, traces the origins of its most famous rite back to the year 767, when the governor of Owari Province is said to have come here to pray for relief from a spreading epidemic on imperial orders. That ritual survives today as the Hadaka Matsuri, or Naked Festival, held on the thirteenth day of the old lunar calendar, typically falling in February.

On the day of the festival, thousands of men wearing only white loincloths and headbands converge on the shrine grounds in a ritual meant to drive out disease and misfortune. At the center of the event is the shin-otoko, or 'lucky man,' a young local man chosen for the role who runs through the crowd completely naked; participants believe that touching him transfers their own bad luck onto him, and the resulting crush of thousands of men chasing a single runner is one of the more physically intense festival experiences in Japan.

Outside of the festival itself, Konomiya Shrine functions as a fairly typical, quiet Shinto shrine, so visitors hoping to see the Hadaka Matsuri in person need to plan their trip specifically around its date each year.
