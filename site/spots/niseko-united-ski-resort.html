Niseko United

Sapporo

Niseko United links four separate ski resorts, Grand Hirafu, Hanazono, Niseko Village and Annupuri, under a combined lift pass that lets skiers and snowboarders move between them across the same mountain, Mt. Niseko Annupuri. The area's reputation rests on unusually consistent, dry powder snow driven by cold air masses sweeping in from Siberia across the Sea of Japan, a weather pattern that has made it one of the most internationally known ski destinations in Asia, with a large seasonal population of overseas visitors and staff.

Hirafu, the largest and busiest of the four base areas, has the widest range of restaurants, bars and English-language services, reflecting its long-standing popularity with Australian and other international skiers in particular; Annupuri and the newer Hanazono zone tend to feel quieter and more low-key by comparison, for skiers who'd rather avoid the busiest base village.

Late December through February is peak powder season and also peak crowds and prices, particularly around the New Year holiday; March offers milder weather with generally still-good snow and noticeably thinner crowds. Outside winter, the area shifts to hiking, mountain biking and outdoor activities, though the powder snow that made it famous is obviously a winter-only draw.

Niseko area (Hirafu, Niseko Village, Annupuri), Abuta-gun, Hokkaido
