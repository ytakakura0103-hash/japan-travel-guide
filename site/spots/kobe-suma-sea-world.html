Kobe Suma Sea World

Kobe

This aquarium succeeds the long-running Suma Aqualife Park, which closed in 2023 and was rebuilt as a larger, privately operated resort that reopened in 2024 right on Suma Beach. Its headline feature is an orca stadium where visitors can watch performances while dining, something not offered anywhere else in western Japan, alongside a dedicated orca education zone and the usual range of aquarium tanks covering local Osaka Bay marine life through to tropical species.

Because it now includes an attached hotel and dining areas built into the same waterfront complex, a visit can range from a couple of focused hours around the main tanks and a show to a full day if paired with the beach right outside. Show times are scheduled through the day, so checking the current schedule before arriving helps in planning around the orca performance.

As a rebuilt, heavily promoted facility, it draws strong weekend and holiday crowds, particularly for the orca shows, so arriving well before a showtime or visiting on a weekday reduces the wait considerably. The beachfront location also makes it a natural pairing with a walk along Suma Beach itself, especially in the warmer months.

Wakamiyacho area, Suma-ku, Kobe
