Toyota Automobile Museum

Nagoya

Opened in April 1989 to mark Toyota Motor Corporation's fiftieth anniversary, the Toyota Automobile Museum takes a deliberately wider view than its name might suggest, covering the global history of the automobile from the earliest gasoline-powered cars of the late 1800s through to modern electric vehicles, rather than focusing narrowly on Toyota's own product line. Spread across a 46,700-square-meter site, the museum's Automobile Gallery displays over a hundred roadworthy vehicles, including genuinely rare pieces like a 1922 Grand Prix Sunbeam alongside classic American, European and Japanese models.

A separate Cultural Gallery pairs the vehicles themselves with roughly four thousand related cultural artifacts, vintage advertisements, toys, design sketches and other material that situates the cars within the broader design and consumer history of their eras. The museum's research collection runs even deeper, holding around two hundred thousand reference items including books, magazines and manufacturer catalogs, a resource used by researchers as much as casual visitors.

Since it sits in Nagakute, close to Ghibli Park and the Aichi Prefectural Ceramic Museum's general area, car enthusiasts visiting for Ghibli Park can reasonably combine both stops in a single day.

41-100 Yokomichi, Nagakute, Aichi

Higashiyama subway line from Nagoya Station to Fujigaoka Station, then the Linimo maglev line to Geidai-dori Station, about 40 minutes total from central Nagoya, followed by a roughly 5-minute walk to the museum.
