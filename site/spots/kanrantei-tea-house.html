Kanrantei Tea House

Sendai

Kanrantei is believed to have originated as a teahouse within Toyotomi Hideyoshi's Fushimi Castle, before Date Masamune had it relocated to Edo and his son, Date Tadamune, later moved it once more to Matsushima sometime in the late 1620s or early 1630s. Its name translates roughly to "a place to view ripples on the water," a fitting description given its position looking out over Matsushima Bay. Inside, twenty-one painted fusuma sliding panels from the Edo period have been designated an Important Cultural Property.\n\nAdmission is modest and typically includes access to a small adjoining museum of Date-family artifacts, with a full visit taking around thirty minutes.\n\nThe building's connection to the Date clan's private entertaining and moon-viewing parties gives it a more intimate feel than Matsushima's larger temple complexes, and it tends to draw fewer visitors than the main sightseeing pier nearby. It's worth timing a visit for late afternoon light, and autumn moon-viewing remains a traditional draw for the site.

Matsushima, Matsushima-machi, Miyagi-gun, Miyagi
