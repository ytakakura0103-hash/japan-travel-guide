Nijo Castle

Kyoto

Nijo Castle was built at the start of the 17th century as the Kyoto residence of the Tokugawa shoguns, a display of authority positioned deliberately close to the Imperial Palace. Its best-known feature is the "nightingale floor" in the Ninomaru Palace, corridors built with fittings that produce a chirping sound underfoot, traditionally explained as an intentional alarm against anyone trying to sneak up on the shogun, though some historians argue the squeak may simply be an unintended result of aging construction. The palace's interior rooms, decorated with elaborate screen paintings, are viewed by walking through in socks along a fixed route, since shoes aren't worn inside.

Beyond the palace, the castle grounds include a moat, stone walls, and a separate garden with a pond, along with cherry and plum groves that draw visitors in spring. A full visit covering both the palace interior and the outer grounds takes roughly ninety minutes to two hours. Admission is charged, with a modest surcharge for the palace beyond the base grounds fee.

Nijo Castle is busiest in the late morning through early afternoon and during peak cherry blossom weeks; visiting close to opening time, or later in the afternoon on a weekday, tends to be noticeably calmer, particularly inside the palace corridors where crowd noise competes with the sound of the floors themselves.

541 Nijojocho, Nakagyo Ward, Kyoto
