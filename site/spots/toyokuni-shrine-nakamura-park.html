Local Pick

Toyokuni Shrine, Nakamura Park

Nagoya

Toyotomi Hideyoshi, one of the three great unifiers of feudal Japan and a figure whose rise from humble origins to supreme power is one of the country's best-known historical narratives, is said to have been born in this part of Nagoya, and Toyokuni Shrine was established here in his honor. The surrounding Nakamura Park also includes a memorial hall covering both Hideyoshi and fellow local-born warlord Kato Kiyomasa, giving the area a concentrated dose of Sengoku-period history rarely found outside dedicated history museums.

The shrine and park are free to visit, and covering both the shrine grounds and memorial hall takes about an hour; the memorial hall charges a small separate admission.

Sitting outside the usual downtown sightseeing loop, this park draws mostly neighborhood visitors rather than tourists, so it's a reliably quiet spot even on weekends. History enthusiasts with a specific interest in the Sengoku period will get the most out of a visit, though the park itself is pleasant for anyone wanting a green break off the beaten path.

3-35 Nakamura-cho, Nakamura-ku, Nagoya, Aichi

Open in Google Maps

About 7-minute walk from Nakamura-koen Station (Higashiyama subway line)
