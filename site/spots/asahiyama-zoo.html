Asahiyama Zoo

Sapporo

Asahiyama Zoo nearly closed in the 1990s after visitor numbers collapsed, and its turnaround became something of a case study in Japanese tourism: rather than expanding enclosures for size alone, the zoo redesigned exhibits to show off animals' natural behaviors, including an underwater viewing tunnel where visitors watch polar bears and seals swim directly overhead, and a similar setup for the orangutan enclosure that lets the animals move at height across walkways above visitors' heads.

Its best-known seasonal feature is the penguin walk, when king and gentoo penguins are let out to waddle around the snowy grounds, typically running twice daily from mid-December through February when there's enough snow on the ground, though it's worth checking the zoo's own schedule since the walk depends on actual snow cover and doesn't run every day. In warmer months, the underwater and elevated viewing exhibits remain the zoo's core draw regardless of the season.

Being in Asahikawa rather than Sapporo makes this a genuine day-trip commitment, which naturally filters out casual same-day visitors and keeps crowds somewhat more manageable than a comparably famous zoo closer to a major city center, though winter weekends during penguin walk season still draw a real crowd; arriving for the first walk of the day tends to beat the lunchtime rush.
