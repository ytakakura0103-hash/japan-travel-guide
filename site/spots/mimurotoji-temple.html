Mimurotoji Temple

Kyoto

Mimurotoji has earned the nickname "hydrangea temple" for the sloped garden that fills with roughly 20,000 hydrangea plants across some 50 varieties each June, turning the hillside into a dense patchwork of blue, purple, and white blooms that draws visitors even in the middle of Japan's rainy season, when many other gardens look their least appealing. That rainy-season timing is part of the appeal: the flowers are often at their best on overcast or lightly rainy days, and the temple sets aside a small area where visitors can walk among the hydrangeas up close for an additional fee during peak bloom. The grounds don't rely on a single season, either. A lotus pond fills much of the lower garden in summer, and maples turn the temple a deep red in November, giving it three distinct high points across the year. Outside these peak weeks, the temple is considerably quieter and can be seen at a relaxed pace in under an hour. During hydrangea season, weekday mornings are noticeably calmer than weekend afternoons, when the narrow garden paths can bottleneck. General admission is modest, with a separate, slightly higher fee during the hydrangea walk-in period. Mimurotoji sits in the Uji area, a bit removed from the more crowded circuit of temples in central Kyoto, making it a good match for visitors extending a Uji day trip beyond Byodo-in.

21 Todo Shigatani, Uji, Kyoto
