Arashiyama Bamboo Grove

Kyoto

The bamboo grove is a short, straight path running between Tenryuji Temple and the Okochi Sanso villa, walled in on both sides by tall, densely packed bamboo stalks that block out most direct sunlight even at midday. It's a natural landmark rather than a garden, so there's no entrance fee to walk through it, and the walk itself takes only about ten to fifteen minutes if you don't stop, though most visitors linger longer for photos.

Because it sits at the center of one of Kyoto's most popular sightseeing districts, the grove gets extremely crowded from mid-morning onward, especially on weekends; arriving shortly after sunrise, before the tour groups and rickshaw traffic build up, is by far the best way to experience it with any sense of quiet. Early light filtering through the canopy is also when the setting photographs best.

The grove connects naturally to other Arashiyama sights nearby, including Tenryuji Temple, Nonomiya Shrine at its entrance, and the Togetsukyo Bridge a short walk south, so it works well as one stop on a longer morning loop through the area rather than a destination on its own. There's little shade or shelter along the path, so summer visits are best done early to avoid the heat as well as the crowds.

Sagaogurayama area, Ukyo Ward, Kyoto
