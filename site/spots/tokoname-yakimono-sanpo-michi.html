Tokoname Pottery Footpath (Yakimono Sanpo Michi)

Nagoya

Tokoname has produced ceramics for close to a thousand years, and its most famous product, the reddish-brown Tokoname-yaki teapot, is still made by workshops along this walking course through the old kiln district. What makes the route distinctive is the walls themselves: many were built by embedding retired clay pipes and sake bottles directly into the surface, creating a textured, almost sculptural streetscape you won't find in a typical pottery town. A trail of small ceramic maneki-neko statues tucked along the path has become a minor attraction of its own for visitors working through the route.

The walk itself is free and takes roughly an hour at an easy pace, longer with stops at the small shops and galleries selling local ceramics along the way.

Because the town sits near Chubu Centrair International Airport, it increasingly draws visitors on layovers or heading to or from flights, so mid-morning on a weekday tends to be calmer than weekend afternoons. Autumn light on the aged brick kiln chimneys is particularly photogenic.

Partway along the footpath, a giant ceramic maneki-neko statue nicknamed Toko-nyan, standing over six meters wide and three meters tall, watches over the path from a hillside perch, joined by dozens of smaller ceramic cats set into the surrounding walls. It's become one of the district's most photographed spots, a playful counterpoint to the more subdued kiln-and-pottery scenery along the rest of the walk.

Sakae-cho, Tokoname, Aichi
