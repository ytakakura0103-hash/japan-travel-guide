Bashamichi Street

Yokohama

The name Bashamichi translates roughly to horse carriage road, a reference to the foreign merchants who once traveled this route between the old foreign settlement and the port, and the street holds the distinction of being among the earliest in Japan fitted with gas streetlights, back when such lighting was still a novelty nationwide. Replica gas lamps and brick paving along the sidewalk today are a nod to that history, even though the street itself has long since modernized into a mix of banks, galleries, cafes, and boutiques.

Unlike the dense crowds of nearby Chinatown, Bashamichi has a calmer, more workaday atmosphere, closer to a pleasant urban shopping street than a tourist destination, which makes it a good place to browse without the jostling found elsewhere in central Yokohama. A scattering of preserved early 20th-century bank and office buildings along the route rewards visitors who look up rather than just at shop windows.

It connects conveniently to both the Red Brick Warehouse and the Kannai government district, so it works best as a walking route between those two areas rather than a dedicated stop, and its station, sharing the street's name, makes it easy to start or end a Minato Mirai day here.
