Berrick Hall

Yokohama

Designed by American architect J.H. Morgan for British trader B.R. Berrick, this 1930 residence is generally considered the biggest of the prewar foreign homes still standing on the Yamate bluff. The Spanish Colonial styling shows up in the tiled roof, the quatrefoil windows, and especially the three-arched entrance porch, a Morgan signature that appears again, in simplified form, at the nearby Yamate 111 Bankan he also designed.\n\nInside, the restored rooms include a wide living room, a palm-motif sunroom, and a dining room with decorative beam ceilings; upstairs, a child's bedroom retains a hand-painted fresco that has been carefully brought back after decades of wear. Admission is free, and the interior can be toured at your own pace in well under an hour.\n\nBecause it sits slightly apart from the main cluster of Yamate houses, foot traffic here tends to be lighter than at the more heavily marketed spots near Harbor View Park, making it a comfortable choice if you want to appreciate the architecture without dodging tour groups. Spring and autumn bring pleasant garden light for photos through the tall windows.

72 Yamate-cho, Naka-ku, Yokohama

Open in Google Maps

About 10 minutes on foot from JR Negishi Line Ishikawacho Station.
