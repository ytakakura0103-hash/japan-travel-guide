Maiko Park

Kobe

Maiko Park stretches along the coast right where the Akashi Kaikyo Bridge lands on the Kobe side, its name referring to the pine trees (matsu) that have historically lined this stretch of shore and given the area its scenic reputation even before the bridge existed. Walking paths wind through the pines and along the water, with the towering bridge structure visible overhead for much of the route, making it one of the more dramatic free viewpoints in the city.

The park connects directly to the paid Maiko Marine Promenade facility for anyone who wants the closer, glass-floor view, but the park itself costs nothing to enter and offers plenty of good photo angles of the bridge from ground level. A walk through the main sections of the park, including some time at the water's edge, takes roughly 30 minutes to an hour.

The park is pleasant in any season, but it gets a particular boost in spring when the pines are joined by blossoming trees in some sections, and sunset draws steady crowds for bridge photos. Weekday mornings are the quietest time to have the pine paths mostly to yourself.

Higashi Maiko-cho, Tarumi-ku, Kobe
