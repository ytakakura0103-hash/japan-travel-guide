Koshien Stadium

Kobe

Opened in 1924, Koshien is one of the oldest and most storied stadiums in Japanese sports, home to the professional Hanshin Tigers as well as the biannual national high school baseball tournaments that dominate national television and produce some of the country's most emotionally charged sporting moments, complete with the tradition of losing teams scooping up handfuls of the stadium's dirt to take home. The ivy that once famously covered its outer walls has been replanted following renovations, continuing a decades-old visual signature of the ground.

A museum adjacent to the stadium covers its history in depth, including memorabilia from both professional and high-school tournament history, and is worth visiting even outside of game days for anyone with an interest in Japanese baseball culture. On game days, the stadium atmosphere, especially Hanshin Tigers matches, is genuinely raucous, with organized cheering sections, balloon releases and a fan intensity that rivals anything in Japanese sport.

Because Koshien sits between Kobe and Osaka rather than in either city center, it's an easy stop if timed around a game or the high school tournament season in spring and summer; outside game days, the stadium exterior and museum are the main draws.

Koshien-cho, Nishinomiya, Hyogo Prefecture
