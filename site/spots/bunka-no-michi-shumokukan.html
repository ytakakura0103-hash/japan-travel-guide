Local Pick

Cultural Path Shumokukan

Nagoya

The Bunka no Michi district was, in the Edo period, a row of samurai residences serving the Owari domain; by the Meiji and Taisho eras it had become home to a new class of businessmen, journalists and public figures who built mansions blending imported Western style with traditional Japanese construction. Shumokukan is one of the surviving examples of that transition, a merchant family's residence whose rooms alternate between Japanese tatami spaces and Western-style parlors with imported furniture and detailing.

Compared to the Futaba Museum a short walk away, Shumokukan tends to draw a fraction of the visitors, which makes it a good place to actually take in the architectural details, the garden, and the layout of a wealthy household from a century ago without navigating a crowd. Admission is inexpensive and the building can be seen thoroughly in well under an hour.

Walking between Shumokukan and the Futaba Museum, along streets still lined with older houses and small gardens, is arguably the best way to experience the Cultural Path district as a whole rather than as a single stop.

3-23 Shumoku-cho, Higashi-ku, Nagoya, Aichi

Open in Google Maps

About a 10-minute walk from Exit 1 of Takaoka Station on the Sakuradori subway line, close to the Futaba Museum.
