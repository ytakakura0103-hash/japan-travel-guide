Local Pick

Nenbutsu-ji Temple, Arima

Kobe

Arima's temple district traces its roots to the priest Gyoki and later the monk Ninsai, both credited in local tradition with reviving and developing the hot spring town in earlier centuries after it had fallen into disrepair, and several small temples in the area, including Nenbutsu-ji, preserve that connection through modest halls and memorial markers rather than grand architecture. It sits away from the main hot spring street, in the quieter residential lanes that most day-trippers never wander into.

A visit here is brief, generally 10 to 15 minutes, and there is no admission fee, so it works well as a short stop woven into a longer walk through Arima's back streets rather than a dedicated destination. The temple's real appeal is less about grand architecture and more about the sense of stepping away from the tourist street into the town's quieter, older residential fabric.

Because it is a minor stop well off Arima's main thoroughfare, it essentially never sees a crowd, making it a good spot for visitors who want a few minutes of quiet reflection between bathhouse visits.
