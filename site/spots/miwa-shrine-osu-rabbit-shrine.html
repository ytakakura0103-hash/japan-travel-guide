Miwa Shrine (Rabbit Shrine)

Nagoya

Miwa Shrine, tucked into the Osu shopping district, has long been known as a matchmaking shrine, its associated deity traditionally invoked for good relationships, but its recent social media popularity comes from a more specific detail: rabbit-shaped ema, the wooden plaques worshippers write wishes on before hanging at a shrine. Rabbits are considered the deity's messenger here, and the shrine leans into the motif with rabbit statues and rabbit-print goods scattered around the compact grounds.

That rabbit branding has intersected with Japan's fan culture in an unplanned way: visitors from idol and character fandoms have taken to painting their favorite performer's or character's face directly onto a blank ema plaque before hanging it, a practice that's spread largely through short-form video showing the finished results lined up among hundreds of others. Whether or not that specific use was intended, it's become one of the shrine's main draws for younger visitors.

Because it sits directly within the Osu shopping district, a visit here folds naturally into a broader afternoon browsing Osu's shops and street food rather than requiring a separate trip. The shrine itself is small enough to see properly in ten or fifteen minutes, making it an easy add-on rather than a destination requiring its own block of time.

Osu, Naka Ward, Nagoya

A short walk from Osu Kannon Station (Nagoya Municipal Subway Tsurumai Line), within the Osu shopping district.
