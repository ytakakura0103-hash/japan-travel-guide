Moegi House

Kobe

Built in the 1900s as the residence of an American consul, Moegi House takes its name from its soft yellow-green (moegi) exterior, a colonial-style wooden building with a broad veranda that wraps around the ground floor. Compared to the heavier brick and dark wood interior of its neighbor, the Weathercock House, Moegi House feels lighter and more relaxed, with parquet floors, a fireplace in nearly every room, and furniture typical of an American household of the era.

The house is small enough to walk through in about 15 to 20 minutes, and it is most often visited as a combined ticket alongside the Weathercock House right across the small square. The veranda and front lawn are popular for photos, particularly because the house catches good afternoon light.

Since it shares a plaza with the district's most famous building, Moegi House absorbs some of that overflow crowd, but it is usually noticeably calmer than its neighbor, especially just before closing time in the late afternoon when day-trippers have started heading back down the hill.

3-10-11 Kitanocho, Chuo-ku, Kobe
